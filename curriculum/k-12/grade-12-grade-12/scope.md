# Grade 12 Scope

## How to read this scope

Each `unit_id` in the frontmatter resolves to a unit file under `units/`.
`start_day`/`end_day` map the unit onto the 180-day school year; the list is a
continuous sequence from day 1 to day 180 with no gaps or overlaps. `domain` and
`pillar` name the primary domain(s) and pillar(s) each unit serves. `domain_weighting`
expresses the intended balance of instructional time across the ten domains, in
percent of the year. `lesson_count` counts the core lessons; the days between lessons
carry flex time, assessment, integration, and celebration. `assessment_plan`
describes how a learner's growth is seen and recorded at this level.

## Year at a glance

Grade 12 is the **capstone of the K–12 spiral**: the year everything learned becomes
an integrated sense of who the learner is, what they value, and how they will act in
the world. Where Grade 11 *articulated, committed, and stood for* a developing
purpose — a values-and-purpose statement, empathy extended to distant and future
others, full authorship of a personal project — Grade 12 *consolidates* the whole arc
into final synthesis, real-world agency, and the hand-off to adult life. Learners are
treated as co-authors and citizens throughout, never as students. The year opens with
three inward units that consolidate the integrated self, the lifelong body, and the
collective-care community.

*Feelings, Identity & Purpose* (U.12.001, D01) opens the year where Grade 11's
articulated-and-committed purpose left off. Learners now *integrate* it into a single,
stable sense of who they are: they integrate emotional understanding into ethical
judgment, recognizing how emotion informs and can bias moral reasoning (D01.S1.12.01),
and respond to complex, emotionally charged personal and global situations with
insight, care, and emotional wisdom (D01.S1.12.02). They articulate an integrated
sense of who they are, what they value, and how they will act, held together across
contexts (D01.S2.12.01), and explain how they author their identity within global,
cultural, and technological forces rather than being defined by them (D01.S2.12.02).
They exercise self-directed agency by setting, planning, and acting on a meaningful
life goal with autonomy and accountability (D01.S3.12.01), and sustain self-regulation
and purpose through setbacks and systemic constraints, naming what sustains them
(D01.S3.12.02). Every self is honored as a whole in the making, never a label, and
every question is held as a question.

*Body, Wellness & Lifelong Movement* (U.12.002, D03) reads the body as the somatic
ground of thought and feeling, now integrated into a lifelong framework and understood
as a shared public concern. Learners integrate the body–mind connection into a
personal framework for lifelong awareness and wellbeing and explain it to others
(D03.S1.12.01), and articulate how to sustain their own and others' embodied health
and dignity across a lifetime, including aging and change (D03.S1.12.02). They
integrate movement into a lifelong, adaptable plan that supports physical, mental, and
social health (D03.S2.12.01), and explain how environments — cities, workplaces,
natural spaces — shape people's ability to move and propose changes that widen access
(D03.S2.12.02). The body is taught with dignity and joy, honoring the wide range of
bodies and abilities and the whole arc of a life.

*Relationships, Community & Collective Care* (U.12.003, D02) turns outward into care
at its most mature — intimate trust, integrated empathy, and collective responsibility
for the whole living world. Learners sustain intimate, trusting relationships through
honest communication, repair, and mutual respect (D02.S1.12.01), and reflect on their
own communication and relationship habits, committing to grow as a listener and
partner (D02.S1.12.02). They integrate empathy and perspective-taking into ethical
reasoning about complex social problems (D02.S2.12.01), and act on empathy to reduce
suffering and promote dignity for others, including the more-than-human world
(D02.S2.12.02). They articulate their own place within nested communities, from family
to the global community of all life (D02.S3.12.01), and weigh belonging, solidarity,
and collective responsibility in deciding how to act for the common good
(D02.S3.12.02). They articulate a personal ethic of care, cooperation, and mutual aid
for a just and thriving world (D02.S4.12.01), and act on collective responsibility for
people and the Earth, connecting local action to global solidarity (D02.S4.12.02).

The heart of the year is given to the three great tools of school, now wielded at full
maturity: language, number, and science. *Language, Literature & Deliberation*
(U.12.004, D04) grows from Grade 11's multilingual, critical, and persuasive voice into
*deliberation, integration, and language justice*. Learners engage in complex,
sustained deliberation on contested issues, hearing many voices and reaching reasoned
positions (D04.S1.12.01), and speak with clarity, care, and integrity in public and
professional settings, adapting to diverse audiences and purposes (D04.S1.12.02). They
read, evaluate, and integrate scholarly, technical, and literary texts across
registers and disciplines (D04.S2.12.01), and reason across multiple writing systems
and languages, recognizing how each encodes meaning differently (D04.S2.12.02). They
produce a sustained, polished piece of writing that demonstrates command of structure,
style, and evidence for a chosen audience (D04.S3.12.01), and write with clarity and
care across disciplines and registers, using language ethically and precisely in a
multilingual world (D04.S3.12.02). They advocate for language justice, making
communication accessible to people of all languages in public life (D04.S4.12.01), and
move confidently and respectfully across languages and cultures, hearing many voices
and speaking with clarity and care in a multilingual world (D04.S4.12.02), always home
language first, with multilingualism held as wealth. *Mathematics: Reasoning about
Large Systems* (U.12.005, D05) grows from Grade 11's sequences, vectors, and
statistical design into the summit of the mathematical ladder. Learners apply the
ideas of limits and continuity informally to describe change, approximation, and the
behavior of quantities (D05.S1.12.01), and reason quantitatively about large systems —
populations, energy use, carbon — using orders of magnitude and estimation, and
critique the numbers used in public claims (D05.S1.12.02). They model systems of
relationships with equations or matrices and interpret the meaning of a solution in a
real context (D05.S2.12.01), and describe an algorithm as a step-by-step procedure for
a real problem, analyzing whether it works correctly and fairly (D05.S2.12.02). They
use coordinates and vectors to analyze geometric situations and prove results
(D05.S3.12.01), and apply geometric reasoning to real-world design — architecture,
mapping, the layout of shared spaces — considering access and sustainability
(D05.S3.12.02). They use inferential reasoning, including margin of error, confidence,
and significance, to evaluate claims made from data (D05.S4.12.01), and detect and
critique misleading statistics, data visualizations, and algorithmic bias in media and
public life, defending a conclusion with honest data (D05.S4.12.02). Mathematics
remains sense-making with real things, drawn from many traditions, for everyone.

*Science: Systems, Evidence & Our Origins* (U.12.006, D06) is the culminating pass
through Grade 12 science, carrying Grade 11's discipline of investigation into the
discipline of *weighing competing explanations and the ethics of knowledge itself*.
Learners evaluate competing scientific explanations of the same phenomenon using
criteria of evidence, coherence, and falsifiability (D06.S1.12.01), and reflect on the
ethics of scientific practice — who asks the questions, who benefits, and how knowledge
is used — proposing a more just practice (D06.S1.12.02). They explain how ecosystems,
economies, and human health are coupled and weigh trade-offs in sustaining living
systems (D06.S2.12.01), and hold scientific accounts of life's origin and diversity
alongside cultural and spiritual accounts, articulating how they relate (D06.S2.12.02).
They explain the fundamental forces and particles that make up matter and how they
unify physical explanations (D06.S3.12.01), and weigh the promise and risk of physical
technologies — energy, materials, computing — for a just and sustainable future
(D06.S3.12.02). They weigh cosmological models and geological evidence and the
relationship between scientific and other ways of knowing about origins (D06.S4.12.01),
and articulate a considered position on the origins of the cosmos and life that holds
scientific evidence and other ways of knowing in respectful relation (D06.S4.12.02).
Origins are held as evidence-grounded accounts among many others — never as dogma.

*Creative & Aesthetic Expression: A Capstone Voice* (U.12.007, D07) gives the
co-author's deliberate, ethical voice its mature form: a *sustained, coherent body of
work* and a *critically discerning eye*. Learners produce a sustained body of artwork
that demonstrates technical skill and a coherent personal vision, presented with a
written artist statement (D07.S1.12.01), and critique artwork across cultures and eras
with insight, honoring diverse traditions while forming and defending an independent
judgment (D07.S1.12.02). They produce and perform or present a sustained body of
musical work that shows skill and a coherent personal voice, with a written statement
(D07.S2.12.01), and critique music across cultures and eras with insight, honoring
diverse traditions while forming and defending an independent judgment (D07.S2.12.02).
They produce and perform a sustained piece that shows technical skill and a coherent
creative vision, with a written artist statement (D07.S3.12.01), and critique
performance across cultures and eras with insight, honoring diverse traditions while
forming and defending an independent judgment (D07.S3.12.02). They design and realize
a substantial project that demonstrates craft and a coherent purpose, presented with a
written rationale (D07.S4.12.01), and evaluate design across cultures and eras with
insight, honoring diverse traditions while forming and defending an independent
judgment about good design (D07.S4.12.02). The arts of other cultures are met as
living traditions to honor with respectful questions, never as specimens.

*Health, Nutrition & Care* (U.12.008, D03) returns to the body at the threshold of
adult life, now with independence and care for others. Learners synthesize the science
of nutrition with ethical, cultural, and environmental considerations to make lifelong
food decisions (D03.S3.12.01), and advocate for food systems and policies that support
health and dignity for all people, linking individual and collective wellbeing
(D03.S3.12.02). They manage their own health independently, integrating sleep,
movement, nutrition, mental health, and medical care (D03.S4.12.01), and explain how
to care for the health and safety of others, including basic emergency response and
caregiving, and when to step in (D03.S4.12.02). Every body, every pace, and every
family's way of living is honored; the body is taught with dignity, never shame.

The final third steps into the full adult questions of the unfinished human story, the
just society, and the living Earth. *Global History: Change, Knowledge & the
Unfinished Story* (U.12.009, D08) gives Grade 12 its culminating pass through the
long, many-voiced human story, deepening Grade 11's hardest and most careful
questions. Learners weigh competing accounts of change and continuity across global
history and form a reasoned synthesis of the evidence (D08.S1.12.01), and reflect on
their own place in the long, unfinished human story, connecting the past to the
present and to possible futures (D08.S1.12.02). They weigh multiple knowledge
traditions side by side and articulate a respectful, non-dogmatic relationship among
them (D08.S2.12.01), and reflect on how honoring many knowledge traditions contributes
to a more just and flourishing shared human story (D08.S2.12.02). They trace global
movements of peoples and power across deep time and synthesize how migration has
shaped the human story (D08.S3.12.01), and reflect on their own place in the ongoing
movement of people and ideas and their responsibility to a shared and changing Earth
(D08.S3.12.02). They weigh competing accounts of power and resistance in a contested
historical event and form a reasoned judgment (D08.S4.12.01), and reflect on their own
role and responsibility in the ongoing, unfinished struggle for freedom and dignity
for all people (D08.S4.12.02). History is told from many perspectives, never as a
single nation's triumph, and the voices of those too often left out are centered with
care and accuracy.

*Ethics, Justice & Civic Life* (U.12.010, D09) deepens Grade 11's structures, justice,
and solidarity into the *habits of a citizen*. Learners reason through a complex,
real-world moral dilemma with no clean answer, weighing multiple frameworks and
consequences, and defend a nuanced position (D09.S1.12.01), and reflect on their own
evolving values, how they formed, and how they will test them in the world, holding
questions open rather than settling them as creed (D09.S1.12.02). They weigh a real,
contested question of justice and rights, locally and globally, and defend a reasoned
position that accounts for multiple perspectives (D09.S2.12.01), and reflect on their
own responsibilities to fairness and justice in their communities and in the wider
world, and how they will act (D09.S2.12.02). They practice the habits of an engaged
citizen by researching an issue, building coalitions, listening across disagreement,
and acting collectively toward a fairer common life (D09.S3.12.01), and reflect on
their own role in public life, crafting a considered vision of how they will
participate to build a more just and sustainable world (D09.S3.12.02). They practice
the habits of peacemaking by listening across deep disagreement, taking responsibility
for harm, and repairing relationships and communities (D09.S4.12.01), and reflect on
how they can contribute to peace and repair in a divided world, locally and globally,
with both honesty and hope (D09.S4.12.02). Questions are held as questions, never
answered as dogma.

*Technology, Media & the Living Earth* (U.12.011, D10) gathers the outward year into
stewardship — neither worship nor fear. Learners analyze technology as a system
shaping society, weighing design, governance, and their own choices, and argue for a
humane direction (D10.S1.12.01), and reflect on their own relationship with technology
— what it gives and what it costs — and how they will use it as a steward rather than
a consumer (D10.S1.12.02). They practice responsible media creation by making and
sharing a message that is accurate, fair, and honest about its sources (D10.S2.12.01),
and reflect on how they will stay informed in a flood of information, holding claims
open to evidence rather than settling into certainty (D10.S2.12.02). They explain
Earth as one interconnected system and how human activity is pushing against the
planetary boundaries that sustain all life (D10.S3.12.01), and reflect on their place
within the living world and how understanding interdependence shapes how they choose
to live (D10.S3.12.02). They design and defend a stewardship plan for a real place or
resource, weighing fairness to present and future generations (D10.S4.12.01), and
reflect on their own responsibility as a steward of the Earth and how they will act —
individually and with others — for all life (D10.S4.12.02).

The year closes in *All of Me, All of Us* (U.12.012, D01 + D02), gathering the
integrated self, the solidary community, and the living Earth into a considered life
direction, and handing each learner off to adult life. There the integrated sense of
who they are, what they value, and how they will act (D01.S2.12.01) meets
self-directed agency toward a meaningful life goal (D01.S3.12.01), and the weighing of
belonging, solidarity, and collective responsibility (D02.S3.12.02) meets the act of
collective responsibility for people and the Earth, connecting local action to global
solidarity (D02.S4.12.02). The capstone portfolio and celebration gathers each
learner's own work as a co-author of their education and their life, and hands them
off to adult life as a whole person and a citizen of the living Earth.

Throughout, the four pillars are interwoven. Intellectual awareness (P3) carries the
most weight — the deliberative multilingual voice, the summit of quantitative and
algorithmic reasoning, the evidence-and-ethics discipline of science, and the
sustained, critically discerning creative voice — while contextual and ecological
awareness (P4) rises further, for Grade 12 faces the deepest questions of the
unfinished human story, the just society, and the living Earth: competing accounts of
change and continuity, the weighing of knowledge traditions and of power and
resistance, the habits of a citizen, and Earth as one interconnected system pressing
against planetary boundaries. Emotional and social awareness (P1) remains the ground:
identity and purpose are integrated, and intimate trust, integrated empathy, and
collective responsibility matter more than ever. A known, cared-for, and capable
body-and-self (P2) is the foundation all of it stands on, now carried into a lifetime
of embodied wellbeing and care for others. Science (D06), global history (D08), ethics
(D09), and technology and environment (D10) each now carry a full unit of the year,
and each culminates the full scientific, historical, and ethical ladders of the
spiral. The hardest truths of the world are faced honestly — inequality, ecological
crisis, injustice, moral ambiguity — always met with intellectual tools and a sense of
agency, never nihilism. No high-stakes tests anywhere: a learner's growth is seen
through explanations that state their limits, arguments that weigh conflicting
evidence and critique hidden assumptions, reasoning across more than one framework and
across cultures, modeling and proof with real things, made and critiqued works, and a
capstone portfolio of what they say, make, and do as co-authors of their own education
and their own lives.
