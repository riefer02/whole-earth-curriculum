# Lesson 5 — A Sustained Body of Music: Skill and Voice

## Summary

Learners meet the musical parallel of Lesson 1: a **body of music** is several pieces held
together by one **voice** — a recurring **motif**, a shared **framework**, a consistent feel.
They study how a single melodic framework can generate many pieces across time (the **raga**),
then make three short pieces of their own that share a voice, using voice, body, or
instrument.

## Objectives

- Produce and perform or present a sustained body of musical work that shows skill and a
  coherent personal voice, with a written statement. _(D07.S2.12.01)_

## Connection

Think of a lullaby, a work-song, a chant, or a tune you know from home — one melody that
comes back in different versions, faster or slower, sung by different people, each time
recognizably the same. That is a body of music: not one performance but a *voice* that
returns. Everywhere people make music, they do this — repeat and vary a pattern until it
becomes theirs.

## Materials

- Music-capstone journal
- Motif-to-body sheet
- Low-tech: voice, body, and found sound; a listening partner
- Enriched: BandLab or any recorder

## Preparation

- Copy or draw the motif-to-body sheet (three pieces + a "shared voice" line).
- Retrieval: from Grade 11 (D07.S2.11.01), composing a piece that responds to an issue;
  from Grade 10 (D07.S2.10.01), composing and recording an original piece with a clear
  idea. Today we *begin* the sustained musical body for this unit's capstone.
- Prepare a short recorded or sung example of a motif varied three ways to model (S-011).

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **a body of music is
several pieces united by one voice — a recurring motif, a shared framework, a consistent
feel — showing skill that develops across the set.** Teach motif-and-variation explicitly,
then let learners make three pieces (S-011). Remind learners that "skill" here is audible
*intention* — a repeated rhythm, a chosen scale, a returning phrase — not only virtuosity.

The **ethics** lens: a musical voice is a responsibility — when a body of work borrows from
a tradition, the maker must credit it and its living keepers. The **egalitarianism** lens:
a body of music can be made with nothing but the voice and body — clapped, hummed, tapped —
so no instrument and no money is a barrier. The **global** lens: the raga of Indian
classical music is a melodic framework with a scale, characteristic phrases, and an
associated mood and time, from which performers have generated countless pieces across
centuries — a living tradition, honored with respectful questions, never imitated carelessly
(S-366). The **technology** lens: recording tools let a maker hear their own voice back and
build a body over time; free tools such as BandLab put this in any learner's hands (S-443).
The **environment** lens: found sound — rain, wind, footsteps, work — can be the material of
a body of music, tying the voice to a place. Preview: Lesson 6 puts the body into words.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Grade 11: name a piece that responded to a social or
   environmental issue. Today we ask what it takes to make not one piece but a *body* of
   music with one voice.
2. **Meet the idea (8 min).** A **body of music** is several pieces held together by a
   **voice**: a recurring **motif** (a short, remembered phrase), a shared **framework**
   (a scale or mode), and a consistent feel. One melody, varied many ways, is the oldest
   form of this.
3. **Worked example — the raga (12 min).** In Indian classical music, a **raga** is a
   melodic framework: a particular scale, a typical order of notes, and characteristic
   phrases, each raga with its own mood and even its own time of day or season (S-366).
   Across centuries, performers have improvised and composed countless pieces within one
   raga — one framework, many works, one voice. Listen to (or have described) two pieces in
   the same raga, and name what ties them together: the scale and its phrases.
4. **Name the move (5 min).** A body of music is a decision: *I will keep returning to this
   motif or mode.* The voice is what stays recognizable when everything else changes.
5. **Make three pieces (18 min).** Choose one short motif — a rhythm you clap, a phrase you
   hum, a few notes you play. Make **three short pieces** from it, each a little different in
   speed, mood, or decoration but the same at the core. Record or notate them if you can.
   Then write one line: *what stays the same across all three.*
6. **Share and check (5 min).** With a partner, play or describe your three pieces and read
   your line. Ask: *could a listener tell these three belong together, and how?* Give one
   specific improvement.
7. **Close (2 min).** Say — or write, sign, gesture, or use AAC to express — the difference
   between "three tunes" and "a body of music."

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use the motif-to-body sheet with a given motif; the learner varies it three
   ways (faster, slower, higher) rather than inventing a motif cold.
- **Extension:** Name the single element (rhythm, melody, timbre, or mode) that most carries
   the voice, and note where the skill improves across the three pieces.
- **Number access (dyscalculia):** No arithmetic is required; "three pieces" is a simple
   count the sheet already structures with three boxes, and a learner may make any number of
   pieces while still meeting "several pieces, one voice." Notation is optional; the voice
   can be captured by ear, gesture, or description.
- **Communication access:** Every spoken step — singing, playing aloud, discussing, sharing,
   or closing — can be done in sign, gesture, writing, notation, or AAC instead. Learners who
   are d/Deaf, hard-of-hearing, non-speaking, or who use AAC make and describe their pieces
   through felt vibration, visual notation, gesture, or written description; no step requires
   producing or hearing sound. A learner who is blind or has low vision works by ear, touch,
   and verbal description.

## Assessment

- **Formative (peer + self):** Can the learner state the difference between a single piece
  and a body of music, make three pieces from one motif, and name what stays the same?
- **Portfolio artifact (unit):** The three pieces (recorded, notated, or described) with the
  one-line "shared voice" statement, opened in the music-capstone journal.

## Home connection

Ask someone at home to sing or hum a tune they know in two different ways — and describe
what stays the same. Write one sentence naming their "motif" and one naming what you would
keep and vary in your own music.
