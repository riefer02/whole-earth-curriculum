# Lesson 9 — A Sustained Performance: Skill and Vision

## Summary

Learners meet the performance parallel of Lessons 1 and 5: a **sustained performance** is
not a string of movements but one piece with a beginning, middle, and end, held together by
a single concept or **through-line** and shaped by **rehearsal**. They study **capoeira** — a
form where movement, music, and meaning are one — then devise a short piece with a
through-line of their own.

## Objectives

- Produce and perform a sustained piece that shows technical skill and a coherent creative
  vision, with a written artist statement. _(D07.S3.12.01)_

## Connection

Think of a gesture everyone knows — a greeting, a dance step, a way of carrying something, a
ritual of welcome or farewell. A performance is many such movements woven into one piece with
a beginning, a middle, and an end, all saying one thing. Everywhere people mark joy, grief,
work, and welcome through the moving body — dance, ritual, and drama are a first language,
older than writing.

## Materials

- Performance-capstone journal
- Performance-arc sheet
- Low-tech: body, voice, and found space; a rehearsal partner or mirror
- Enriched: a camera or phone to record rehearsals

## Preparation

- Copy or draw the performance-arc sheet (beginning / middle / end + through-line).
- Retrieval: from Grade 11 (D07.S3.11.01), a performance responding to a social or
  environmental issue; from Grade 10 (D07.S3.10.01), devising an original piece with craft
  in movement and expression. Today we *begin* the sustained performance for this unit's
  capstone.
- Prepare a short live or described example of a piece with a clear through-line (S-011).

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **a sustained performance is
one piece — beginning, middle, end — held together by a single concept (the through-line) and
shaped, through rehearsal, into visible skill and intention.** Teach the through-line
explicitly, then let learners devise and rehearse (S-011). "Skill" here is *readable
intention* — clear gesture, deliberate rhythm, controlled stillness — not virtuosity.

The **ethics** lens: the body on stage speaks for the performer, so the concept must be one
they can stand behind and represent honestly. The **egalitarianism** lens: a sustained
performance needs no stage, costume, or prop — only a body, a space, and an idea. The
**global** lens: capoeira, an Afro-Brazilian art of movement, music, and community that grew
among enslaved Africans and carries strength and identity, is a living tradition honored with
respectful questions, never a specimen to imitate carelessly (S-325, S-056). The
**technology** lens: recording a rehearsal lets a performer watch their own work from the
outside and revise — a mirror that no earlier generation had. The **environment** lens: the
performer's material is the body *in* a place — a field, a room, a street — and the piece can
answer the space it is in. Preview: Lesson 10 puts the performance into words.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Grade 11: name a performance that responded to a social or
   environmental issue. Today we ask what it takes to make not a string of movements but one
   *sustained* piece with a single vision.
2. **Meet the idea (8 min).** A **sustained performance** is one piece with a **beginning**, a
   **middle**, and an **end**, held together by a **through-line** — the single idea or
   feeling that runs through every moment. Skill means the idea reads clearly: each gesture,
   each pause, each shape is deliberate.
3. **Worked example — capoeira (12 min).** In capoeira, movement, music, and community are one
   form: two people move in a circle (*roda*) around song and instruments, playing a game of
   attack and escape that is also dance (S-325). The through-line — a game of bodies in
   dialogue — runs through every step, and the skill is the controlled, musical precision of
   the movement. It is a living art carried by practitioners now, not a museum relic (S-056).
   Name what holds it together: one idea, in every movement.
4. **Name the move (5 min).** A sustained performance is a decision: *I will say this one
   thing with my whole body, from first moment to last.* The through-line is what stays true
   when the movements change.
5. **Devise your piece (18 min).** Choose one idea you want to express without words — a
   feeling, a question, a memory, a protest, a welcome. Shape it into a **beginning**, a
   **middle**, and an **end** of movement, gesture, or signed/spoken text. Rehearse it once,
   then once more, sharpening the moment where the idea is clearest. Write one line: *the one
   idea that runs through all three.*
6. **Share and check (5 min).** With a partner, perform or describe your piece and read your
   line. Ask: *could a viewer tell the three moments belong to one idea, and how?* Give one
   specific improvement.
7. **Close (2 min).** Say — or write, sign, gesture, or use AAC to express — the difference
   between "a string of movements" and "a sustained performance."

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use the performance-arc sheet with a given through-line ("a welcome"); the
   learner shapes beginning-middle-end around it rather than choosing a concept cold.
- **Extension:** Identify the moment where the piece's meaning is clearest and the moment it
   is muddiest, and revise the muddy moment to carry the through-line more plainly.
- **Number access (dyscalculia):** No arithmetic is required; "three moments" is a simple
   count the sheet already structures with three labeled points. A learner may shape any
   number of moments and still meet "one piece, one idea."
- **Communication access:** Every spoken step — performing, describing, discussing, sharing,
   or closing — can be done in sign, gesture, writing, or AAC instead. Learners who are
   non-speaking, d/Deaf, hard-of-hearing, or who use AAC complete every task in their
   preferred mode; performance may be entirely silent, signed, or gestured, and no step
    requires producing or hearing sound. A learner with limited mobility devises the piece from
    what their body does — a finger, an eye, the breath, a wheelchair's turn — which is
    performance, not a lesser version of it. A learner who is blind or has low vision devises
    the piece from felt movement, spatial touch, and spoken or signed description, and gives
    and receives feedback through a partner's verbal description of the movement rather than
    by watching it.

## Assessment

- **Formative (peer + self):** Can the learner state the difference between a string of
  movements and a sustained piece, shape a beginning-middle-end around one through-line, and
  revise once in rehearsal?
- **Portfolio artifact (unit):** The performance arc (devised, rehearsed, recorded or
  described) with the one-line through-line, opened in the performance-capstone journal.

## Home connection

Watch someone at home do an everyday thing — preparing food, greeting a guest, working — and
name the "through-line" of their movement. Write one sentence about what you would make a
performance about, and why.
