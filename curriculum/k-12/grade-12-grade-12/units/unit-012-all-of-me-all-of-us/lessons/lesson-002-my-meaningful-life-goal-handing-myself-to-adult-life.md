# Lesson 2 — My Meaningful Life Goal: Handing Myself to Adult Life

## Summary

Learners close the self-and-agency arc by handing their meaningful life goal to the adult
they are becoming. They retrieve the goal and plan they set earlier in the year, renew them
with one honest update, and name the two handles that make the handoff real: **autonomy**
(it is mine, not borrowed) and **accountability** (a named check and a date). They sign the
goal over in a short promise and celebrate it — with no pressure to have a perfect goal.

## Objectives

- Exercise self-directed agency by setting, planning, and acting on a meaningful life goal
  with autonomy and accountability. _(D01.S3.12.01)_

## Connection

When you hand something you have made — a key, a letter, a tool — to the person who will
carry it next, you trust them with it, and you also promise them something about how it will
be kept. Today you are both people at once: the one who made the goal and the adult who will
carry it. The handoff is small but it is what turns a wish into a life: *it is mine*, and
*there is a check on it.*

## Materials

- Goal-handoff sheet
- Capstone journal
- Paper and pencil (low-tech); voice, body, pen, screen, or hands (low-tech)
- Goal-setting page (enriched)

## Preparation

- Copy or draw the goal-handoff sheet (my goal / why it matters / next step + date / who or
  what will hold me accountable / signature line).
- Retrieval: from earlier in the year, the chosen meaningful goal and the plan (milestones,
  an if-then for the hard moment, a first step, an accountability check) built in the
  self-and-agency arc (D01.S3.12.01). Today they are renewed and handed forward.
- Prepare a model handoff (worked example first: Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, S-011), and
  hold the goal explicitly as *the learner's own, never prescribed* — a goal may be modest,
  unfolding, or still being found, and "I am still finding mine" is a complete and honored
  handoff.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **a meaningful life goal is
handed to adult life with two handles — autonomy (it is genuinely mine, not borrowed from a
parent, a feed, or a fear) and accountability (a named check and a date) — and the handoff
is a promise, not a grade.** There is no new heavy content; this is consolidation and a
closing ceremony. Model once, then let each learner renew their own (S-011); the renewal is
retrieval across months, the spacing payoff in action (Dunlosky et al., S-039). Specific,
checkable goals with feedback outperform vague wishes (Locke & Latham, S-223) — that is the
evidence behind the "next step + date + named check."

The **ethics** lens: choosing a goal worth the cost of pursuing it is a moral act, not just
a practical one — the learner names what they will give their life toward. The
**egalitarianism** lens: every path is honored — no goal outranks another, and a learner
with a modest, uncertain, or still-forming goal is celebrated exactly as fully as one with a
grand plan; the pressure to have a "perfect" life goal is refused, not required. The
**global** lens: what a meaningful life looks like differs across cultures and lives — a
goal of care for family, land, craft, or community is as valid as any ambition, and the
lesson does not import one region's template of "success." Some traditions name the good
life as living *well* rather than endlessly accumulating: Andean *buen vivir* (*sumak kawsay*,
"living well") holds a meaningful life as one lived in harmony with community and the living
Earth (S-509) — a different shape of "meaningful" from a self-made ambition, and equally
honored. The **technology** lens: name one
tool that will serve the goal and one that will tempt it — the handoff includes a decision
about technology, as the earlier carried-ethic lessons did. The **environment** lens: a
meaningful goal may serve the living world, and the lesson leaves room for it — the full
return to people and the Earth arrives in Lesson 3.

**Safeguarding:** the goal is the learner's own and may be private; no one is required to
name a goal tied to family, money, or a marginalized identity, and "still finding it" is a
complete answer. The promise is shared only in the part the learner chooses.

## Procedure

1. **Retrieve the goal and plan (8 min).** Without opening your journal, write your
   meaningful goal, its next step, and your accountability check from memory. Then open your
   journal and see what you recalled and what drifted (retrieval: S-039).
2. **Meet the handoff (6 min).** Read the model handoff (S-011). It has four lines — **my
   goal, why it matters, my next step (with a date), who or what will hold me accountable** —
   and a signature. The two handles are named: *autonomy* (it is mine) and *accountability*
   (it is checked).
3. **Renew the plan (12 min).** Update one thing honestly: a milestone, a date, an if-then
   for the hard moment, or the next step. A living goal is revised, not frozen — and "still
   finding it" can be the whole entry.
4. **Write the handoff (12 min).** Fill the sheet in your own words, and sign it over from
   "me now" to "me becoming." Keep it specific and checkable (S-223).
5. **Make the promise (10 min).** With a partner, speak, write, or sign your goal and your
   next step. Your partner listens and asks one question: *what will you do first, and when?*
   Then trade places.
6. **Celebrate (7 min).** In a circle, each person who wishes shares one line of their
   handoff. Receive every path with the same joy — the grand, the modest, and the still
   being found.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Name a goal and one next step with a date; write a two-line handoff; share one
  line — spoken, written, or signed.
- **Extension:** Write a one-page letter to your future self, sealed to reopen in a year,
  naming where this goal will be tested and what you will do then.
- **Communication access:** Every step — recalling, renewing, writing, signing, promising, or
  celebrating — can be done in writing, sign, gesture, typing, or AAC instead of speech.
  Learners who are non-speaking, d/Deaf, hard-of-hearing, or who use AAC complete every task
  in their preferred mode; no step requires hearing or producing sound. A learner who is
  blind or has low vision completes the handoff through dictation or a spoken promise rather
  than a printed sheet.
- **Number access (dyscalculia):** No arithmetic is required. A date can be named in words
  ("before the next harvest," "this week") rather than numbers, and the sheet already
  structures the four lines, so no learner needs to hold numbers in mind to complete the
  handoff.

## Assessment

- **Summative + portfolio (unit):** The signed goal-handoff sheet, filed as a closing capstone
  artifact — assessed for autonomy (is it genuinely theirs?) and accountability (is there a
  named check and a date?), never for the size or impressiveness of the goal.
- **Self + peer:** Did the learner renew one element, state a specific next step, and name an
  accountability check? Shared without ranking.

## Home connection

Share your handoff with someone at home — read it aloud, or show them the written or signed
version — and ask them to check in with you at the date you named. Write one sentence on what
they said.
