# Lesson 3 — Belonging, Solidarity, and Collective Responsibility: Local to Global

## Summary

Learners close the unit — and the year — by weighing belonging, solidarity, and collective
responsibility, and acting on them for people and the Earth. They weigh the three against one
another to decide how to act for the common good, trace one real act from local to global,
and gather the integrated self, the goal handoff, and this act of belonging into a single
carried whole. The lesson ends in a celebration of each whole person, the solidary community,
and the living Earth.

## Objectives

- Weigh belonging, solidarity, and collective responsibility in deciding how to act for the
  common good. _(D02.S3.12.02)_
- Act on collective responsibility for people and the Earth, connecting local action to
  global solidarity. _(D02.S4.12.02)_

## Connection

A single seed, planted on one street, is a local act; the shade, the soil, the bees, and the
trees people plant because they saw yours are the global one. Belonging is what holds you
close; solidarity is what holds you together with others; collective responsibility is what
you owe to people you will never meet and to the Earth itself. Today you weigh the three, act
on them, and — because this is the last lesson of the year — you gather everything you have
become into one whole and hand it to the world.

## Materials

- Belonging-solidarity-responsibility weighing sheet
- Local-to-global chain sheet
- Capstone portfolio cover
- Paper and pencil (low-tech); voice, body, pen, screen, or hands (low-tech)
- Mutual-aid page (enriched)

## Preparation

- Copy or draw the weighing sheet (three-pan balance + decision line) and the chain sheet
  (four links + a link to the living Earth).
- Retrieval: from the community-and-care arc, the nested rings, the weighing of solidarity,
  the ethic of care, mutual aid, and the local-to-global chain (D02.S3.12.02, D02.S4.12.02).
  Today they are gathered, weighed, and acted on once more, as the year's close.
- Prepare a model weighing and chain (worked example first: Kirschner, Sweller & Clark,
  S-011), and hold the whole as a *celebration, not a test* — the closing gathers every
  learner's path with joy and dignity, never a ranking.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **belonging, solidarity, and
collective responsibility are three different pulls, and acting for the common good means
weighing them — then turning the decision into one real act that connects here to everywhere,
and to the Earth.** This is the year's final lesson: a celebration and a handoff, not new
content. Model once, then let each learner weigh and trace their own (S-011); the gathering
is retrieval across the whole year, the spacing payoff in full (Dunlosky et al., S-039).

The **ethics** lens: weighing what holds me, what holds us, and what we owe is the year's
moral core, now completed. The **egalitarianism** lens: solidarity is shared deciding, not a
one-way gift — the lesson distinguishes mutual aid from charity, where people meet one
another's needs as equals (Spade, S-345), and names mutual aid as a documented, widespread
human practice (Kropotkin, S-163), held here as description, not as a claim that helping is
good. The **global** lens: the lesson honors the world's named traditions of pulling together
— harambee, gotong royong, mink'a/minga, barn-raising (S-164) — as living ways communities
already connect local action to a shared life, never as specimens. The **technology** lens:
tools can carry solidarity across distance or narrow it into an echo; the chain's "global"
link can travel by hand, by letter, by screen — the learner chooses the carrier and names its
limit. The **environment** lens: the outermost ring is the living Earth, not a backdrop — the
ethic of reciprocity with the more-than-human world (Kimmerer, S-427) and the idea that a
life's work can return to the land rather than only take from it (circular economy, S-452)
close the year where flourishing is mutual.

**Safeguarding:** this is a celebration, not a competition. Every learner's act — grand,
modest, or still being found — is honored; no one is required to name a family, a cause, or a
trauma, and a learner may share only the part they choose. The closing circle holds each
whole person with joy and dignity.

## Procedure

1. **Retrieve the year (8 min).** Without opening your journal, write the tools of the
   community-and-care arc from memory: the nested rings, the weighing of solidarity, the
   ethic of care, mutual aid, the local-to-global chain. Then check against your journal
   (retrieval: S-039).
2. **Meet the three pulls (6 min).** Read the model weighing (S-011). **Belonging** — what
   holds me close. **Solidarity** — what holds us together. **Collective responsibility** —
   what we owe people we will never meet and the Earth itself. Acting for the common good
   weighs all three; none cancels the others.
3. **Weigh and decide (12 min).** On the three-pan balance, name one real pull for each, and
   write one decision: *so I will act by ___*. Say whether your act shares the deciding with
   others or only gives — and lean it toward solidarity (S-345).
4. **Trace local to global (12 min).** On the chain, write your local act, its immediate
   effect, its connection to distant people and ecosystems, and its contribution to a global
   movement — then one link to the living Earth. One act from a tradition you know or learn
   (S-164) may stand beside your own.
5. **Gather the whole (10 min).** On the portfolio cover, bring together this week's three
   artifacts — the integrated self, the goal handoff, this act of belonging — and write one
   closing line: the person and citizen of the living world you are handing to adult life.
6. **Celebrate (7 min).** In a closing circle, each person who wishes shares one line of the
   whole they will carry — spoken, written, or signed. Receive every path with the same joy;
   end in the shared silence or sound of having arrived together.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Name "belonging" and one local act, trace the chain in two links, and write a
  one-line closing; share one line — spoken, written, or signed.
- **Extension:** Write a one-page "solidarity plan" — one act you will begin this month, with
  who shares the deciding and one way you will check the effect on people and the Earth.
- **Communication access:** Every step — weighing, tracing, gathering, sharing, or celebrating
  — can be done in writing, sign, gesture, typing, or AAC instead of speech. Learners who are
  non-speaking, d/Deaf, hard-of-hearing, or who use AAC complete every task in their preferred
  mode; no step requires hearing or producing sound. A learner who is blind or has low vision
  completes the balance and chain through spoken description, dictation, or a tactile
  organizer.
- **Number access (dyscalculia):** No arithmetic is required. "Three pulls" and "four links"
  are simple counts the sheets already structure with labeled boxes, so no learner needs to
  hold numbers in mind to complete the weighing or the chain.

## Assessment

- **Summative + portfolio (unit):** The completed weighing sheet, the local-to-global chain,
  and the capstone portfolio cover with the closing line — assessed for a real weighing
  (does the decision weigh all three pulls?), a real connection (does the act actually reach
  beyond the local?), and honesty, never for the size of the act or agreement with any view.
- **Self + peer + performance:** The shared closing line and the celebration circle, received
  without ranking.

## Home connection

Share your closing line with someone who matters to you — read it aloud, or show them the
written or signed version — and ask them to remind you of it in a year. Write one sentence on
what they said, and one act you will begin this month.
