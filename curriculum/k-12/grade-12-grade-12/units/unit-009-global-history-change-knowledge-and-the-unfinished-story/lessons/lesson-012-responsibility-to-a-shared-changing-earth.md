# Lesson 12 — Responsibility to a Shared, Changing Earth

## Summary

Learners turn their place in the movement of people into a *responsibility* to a shared, changing
Earth. They fill three nested circles — care for themselves and their people, **justice** for those
most affected by a changing climate, and care for the living Earth — each with one concrete,
within-reach action. The lesson holds care and justice together, and keeps agency at the center,
never guilt or despair.

## Objectives

- Reflect on their own place in the ongoing movement of people and ideas, and their responsibility
  to a shared and changing Earth. _(D08.S3.12.02)_

## Connection

A shared well, a shared river, a shared sky — what one person does to them reaches everyone else.
The Earth is that shared thing at its largest: a changing climate is already moving people, most of
all those who did least to cause it (S-006, S-383). Responsibility here is not a heavy verdict; it
is three ordinary circles — care for my own, justice for the most affected, care for the living
Earth — each with a step I can actually take.

## Materials

- Responsibility circle sheet
- Capstone history journal
- Low-tech: paper and any writing tool; a partner-listener
- Enriched: a shared document or local climate data

## Preparation

- Copy or draw the three nested circles with action lines.
- Retrieval: from Lesson 11, the learner's place in the movement. Today we ask what *responsibility*
  follows from that place.
- Prepare a model reflection to show first (S-011).

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **a changing climate is already
moving people, and the places that emitted least often feel it worst; responsibility means three
concrete circles — care, justice, and care for the Earth — each with a step within reach.** Teach the
circles explicitly (S-011). Hold the weight with honesty and hope: name the facts (climate change is
already reshaping where people can live — S-383, S-005; the people least responsible often bear the
heaviest burden — S-006), and always attach agency, never guilt. The learner is not asked to solve
the climate alone; they are asked to name the next small, real thing they can do (facilitation,
holding difficult topics safely).

The **ethics** lens: responsibility is not distributed evenly — those who emitted least owe no debt,
and those who emitted most owe more; naming this is justice, not blame-gaming (S-006). The
**egalitarianism** lens: the lesson refuses to put the whole burden on any one learner; a
responsibility that only asks individuals to recycle while systems go unchanged is not justice —
both the personal circle and the justice circle matter. The **global** lens: climate movement is
global — within and across borders, and the responses must be rooted in care and justice, not in
walls (S-383, S-005). The **technology** lens: tools cut both ways — the same machines that warmed
the planet also measure it and connect the people organizing to protect it; technology is a choice,
not a fate. The **environment** lens: this is the lesson's home — the living Earth is the one shared
home, and caring for it is caring for the people on it (S-004). Preview: Lesson 13 turns to power
and resistance — the struggles over who gets to decide what happens to the shared Earth and its
people.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Lesson 11, name what you carry and where it came from. Today: what
   *responsibility* follows from being in a movement of people on a shared, changing Earth?
2. **Meet the three circles (8 min).** Write them: **(1) Care** — for myself and my people; **(2)
   Justice** — for those most affected by a changing climate, who often did least to cause it (S-006);
   **(3) Care for the living Earth** — the shared home that carries us all. Responsibility is a step
   in each circle, not one grand fix.
3. **Worked example (10 min).** Read or hear a model: a learner who (1) learns to grow food with
   less water, (2) joins a group pressing for those displaced by drought to be treated with dignity
   (S-383), and (3) plants for pollinators on a shared patch. Notice each step is small, real, and
   within reach.
4. **Fill your circles (18 min).** Write one concrete action in each circle — something you can
   actually do this year. Then write one sentence: *which action is genuinely within my power, and
   which one needs others to act with me?*
5. **Share and check (5 min).** Share one circle with a partner. Ask: *is this action real and
   within reach, or a wish?* Give one gentle suggestion.
6. **Close (2 min).** Say — or write, sign, gesture, or use AAC to express — one step you will take,
   and one step you will ask others to take with you.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use the circles with starters ("I will care for my people by…; I will stand for
  justice by…; I will care for the Earth by…"); fill one circle at a time.
- **Extension:** Name the justice gap — who caused the change and who feels it (S-006) — and write an
  action that answers it specifically rather than a general "be better."
- **Number access (dyscalculia):** No arithmetic is required; the three circles are a structure, not
  a computation, and no figures need be reproduced. A learner may complete every circle in spoken or
  signed words without any numerals.
- **Communication access:** Every spoken step — naming, sharing, or closing — can be done in writing,
  sign, gesture, or AAC instead. Learners who are non-speaking, d/Deaf, hard-of-hearing, or who use
  AAC complete every task in their preferred mode; no step requires producing or hearing sound.
  Sharing is always optional.

## Assessment

- **Formative (peer + self):** Can the learner name one concrete, within-reach action in each circle
  — care, justice, and care for the Earth — and say which needs others to act with them?
- **Portfolio artifact (unit):** The completed responsibility circles, opened in the capstone history
  journal — the strand's "responsibility to a shared Earth" reflection.

## Home connection

With someone at home, name one change in your local environment and one shared step your household
could take. Write the step, and who you would need to act with you to make it real.
