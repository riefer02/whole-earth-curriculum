# Lesson 9 — Movements of Peoples and Power Across Deep Time

## Summary

Learners trace the movements of peoples — and of **power** — across deep time. They map the human
**dispersal** out of Africa, the long trade networks of the Silk Roads and Indian Ocean, the
**forced movement** of the transatlantic slave trade, and today's chosen and forced **displacement**,
and they name for each *who moved, why, and who held the power to decide*. The lesson connects
deep-time movement to the living fact of migration.

## Objectives

- Trace global movements of peoples and power across deep time and synthesize how migration has
  shaped the human story. _(D08.S3.12.01)_

## Connection

Almost everyone alive is somewhere because someone moved — a great-grandparent who crossed a
border, a family that fled, a trade that drew people to a port, or a dispersal so ancient no name
survives. Movement is not an interruption of the human story; it *is* the human story. The question
history asks is not *whether* people moved but *who chose to move, who was forced, and who held the
power to decide*.

## Materials

- Movements map-or-timeline (pre-printed)
- Capstone history journal
- Low-tech: a hand-drawn map; string or pebbles
- Enriched: a digital map tool

## Preparation

- Prepare the arrowed, non-numeric world map or timeline with the four movements labeled.
- Retrieval: from Grade 10 (D08.S3.10.01/02), colonization's reshaping of populations and modern
  displacement; from Grade 8 (D08.S3.08.01), the Silk Roads and Indian Ocean routes. Today we trace
  the whole arc in one picture.
- Prepare the worked example (tracing one movement) to model first (S-011).

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **peoples have moved across the
Earth since our species began, and the power to decide where and how people live has moved with
them — so migration must be read through the question of who chose and who was forced.** Teach the
tracing explicitly (S-011). Hold the hard parts with care and accuracy, and never reduce the
transatlantic slave trade to a statistic: at least 12 million Africans were forcibly transported,
in a trade built on the denial of human dignity (S-322, S-274); today, tens of millions of people
are forcibly displaced by conflict, persecution, and violence (S-493). Name these as facts, with
the feeling named first, and connect them to the agency of the people who endured and resisted —
never to despair.

The **ethics** lens: the difference between *chosen* and *forced* movement is a moral difference,
and blurring it hides the harm of forced migration. The **egalitarianism** lens: the map is not
"explorers discovering" — peoples moved and knew these routes before any empire claimed them; the
Silk Roads and Indian Ocean were made by many peoples, not by any single power (S-265, S-207). The
**global** lens: this is one human story — our shared ancestry began in Africa (S-506) and fanned
out to every continent, so no place and no people is "more native" than another on the scale of
deep time. The **technology** lens: the astrolabe, the sail, the rail line, and the jet each
changed *how far and how fast* people and power could move — and who could afford to (S-379). The
**environment** lens: movement has always been partly driven by environment — drought, flood, and
climate shift push people, past and present (S-383). Preview: Lesson 10 synthesizes *how* this
movement shaped the human story.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Grade 8 or 10, name one trade network or one migration you studied and
   what moved along it. Today we trace the *whole* arc in one picture.
2. **Meet the frame (8 min).** Write the three questions you will ask of every movement: **who
   moved? why — chosen or forced? who held the power to decide?** Movement is the human story; these
   three questions tell you what kind of story it was.
3. **Worked example — out of Africa (12 min).** Trace the first movement: anatomically modern humans
   evolved in Africa roughly 300,000 years ago and, over tens of thousands of years, dispersed to
   every habitable continent (S-506). Ask the three questions: who (all our ancestors), why (climate,
   following food and land, over generations), who held power (no single ruler — it was gradual, not
   commanded). Mark it on your map.
4. **Trace the rest (18 min).** With a partner, trace and label three more movements: the Silk
   Roads and Indian Ocean trade (goods, ideas, beliefs, and people moved both ways — S-265, S-207,
   S-323); the transatlantic slave trade (forced, not chosen — S-322, S-274); and today's
   displacement (chosen and forced, driven by conflict, economy, and climate — S-493, S-383). For
   each, answer the three questions.
5. **Share and check (5 min).** Show one route to another pair. Ask: *did we name who chose and who
   was forced?* Give one correction.
6. **Close (2 min).** Say — or write, sign, gesture, or use AAC to express — the three questions you
   now ask of any migration, past or present.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use the map with routes pre-drawn and labeled; the learner answers only "chosen or
  forced?" for each with a mark or word.
- **Extension:** Mark where *power itself* moved — ports, borders, capitals — and write one sentence
  on how a route of goods can also be a route of empire.
- **Number access (dyscalculia):** The map is non-numeric and arrowed; no dates or figures are
  computed. The few figures ("300,000 years," "12 million," "tens of millions") are offered on the
  pre-printed map and read aloud, never required to be reproduced, ordered, or compared numerically;
  a learner may complete the whole task by tracing and labeling with words.
- **Communication access:** Every spoken step — naming, reading aloud, sharing, or closing — can be
  done in writing, sign, gesture, or AAC instead. Learners who are non-speaking, d/Deaf,
  hard-of-hearing, or who use AAC complete every task in their preferred mode. For learners with low
  or no vision, the map can be a tactile surface (raised routes, string) and the three questions
  spoken or signed.

## Assessment

- **Formative (peer + self):** Can the learner trace each movement and answer, for each, who moved,
  whether it was chosen or forced, and who held the power to decide?
- **Portfolio artifact (unit):** The labeled movements map with the three-questions notes, opened in
  the capstone history journal — the strand's "deep-time migration" artifact.

## Home connection

Ask someone at home about one move in your family's past — who moved, why, and whether it was chosen
or forced. Write those three answers, and one sentence on what the move set in motion.
