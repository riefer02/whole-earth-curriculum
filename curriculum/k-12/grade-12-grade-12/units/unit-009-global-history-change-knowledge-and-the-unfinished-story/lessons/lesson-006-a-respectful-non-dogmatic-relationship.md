# Lesson 6 — A Respectful, Non-Dogmatic Relationship

## Summary

Learners articulate the *relationship* between knowledge traditions explicitly. They learn that a
respectful, **non-dogmatic** relationship does two things at once: it honors what each tradition
offers *and* refuses to treat any tradition as beyond question. Using the idea of **two-eyed
seeing**, they write a relationship statement — where the traditions **complement**, where they
**differ**, and what they will keep **open** rather than force shut.

## Objectives

- Weigh multiple knowledge traditions side by side and articulate a respectful, non-dogmatic
  relationship among them. _(D08.S2.12.01)_

## Connection

Two elders can disagree about the same medicine and still respect each other completely — because
respect is not "agree with me," and disagreement is not "you are wrong about everything." A
non-dogmatic relationship is that same stance, scaled up: I honor your way of knowing, I do not
have to adopt it, and I do not treat my own as beyond question either. Both traditions stay open;
neither becomes a law.

## Materials

- Relationship statement sheet
- Capstone history journal
- Low-tech: paper and any writing tool; a partner-listener or voice recorder
- Enriched: a shared document

## Preparation

- Copy or draw the relationship statement frame.
- Retrieval: from Lesson 5, the side-by-side chart and its relation sentence. Today we write the
  relationship *fully* — complement, difference, and what stays open.
- Prepare a model relationship statement to show first (S-011).

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **a respectful, non-dogmatic
relationship honors each tradition and treats none as beyond question — it names complement,
difference, and the open questions it refuses to force shut.** Teach the statement explicitly
(S-011). Two dogmatisms to avoid, and to name aloud: *scientific dogmatism* (only one tradition is
real knowledge) and *relativist dogmatism* (all claims are equal, so nothing can be checked). The
mature stance refuses both: traditions are held with dignity *and* claims are weighed with evidence.

The **ethics** lens: the refusal to rank is not a refusal to judge — we can still ask of any
tradition, "does this claim hold up, and does this practice do harm?" The **egalitarianism** lens:
"two-eyed seeing" — seeing with Indigenous and scientific eyes together — is itself a teaching from
Mi'kmaq Elder Albert Marshall, carried into science by Indigenous scholars such as Robin Wall
Kimmerer (S-427), which is a quiet reversal of the idea that only one tradition gets to define the
relationship. The **global** lens: complement-and-difference relationships exist worldwide — a
doctor who is also a herbalist, a fisherman who is also a hydrologist — and each culture words the
relation its own way. The **technology** lens: tools let one tradition borrow from another (a
satellite map of a landscape an elder knows by foot), which is *relation*, not replacement. The
**environment** lens: when fire science and cultural burning work together, the result is better
land care than either alone (S-320, S-321) — a working model of the non-dogmatic relationship.
Preview: Lesson 7 turns from the relationship to the act of *honoring* many traditions.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Lesson 5, name one place two knowledge traditions complement and one
   place they differ. Today we write the *whole* relationship.
2. **Meet the stance (8 min).** Write it down: **non-dogmatic** means no tradition — including my
   own — is treated as beyond question. A respectful relationship therefore says three things:
   **where they complement, where they differ, and what I keep open.**
3. **Worked example (12 min).** Read or hear a model relationship statement about fire science and
   cultural burning: they complement (both keep a landscape healthy), they differ (one measures
   fuel loads, the other carries law and kinship), and the writer keeps open the question of how a
   single place can hold both systems of authority at once (S-320, S-321, S-427). Mark the three
   parts.
4. **Write your statement (18 min).** Using the frame, write your own relationship statement for
   the traditions you charted in Lesson 5. Name complement, difference, and one open question you
   will not force shut. Guard against both dogmatisms: do not rank, and do not pretend sameness.
5. **Revise with a partner (8 min).** Read your statement to a partner. Ask: *does it honor both
   traditions, and does it avoid both dogmatisms?* Revise one sentence.
6. **Close (2 min).** Say — or write, sign, gesture, or use AAC to express — the three things a
   respectful, non-dogmatic relationship always says.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use the frame with starters ("They complement when…; they differ when…; I will keep
  open…"); fill the blanks rather than composing free.
- **Extension:** Name the two dogmatisms (only-one-tradition vs all-claims-equal) and write how
  your statement steers between them with a concrete example.
- **Number access (dyscalculia):** No arithmetic is required; the three parts are a list, not a
  computation. The sheet is pre-numbered so the learner can work one part at a time in any order
  without counting.
- **Communication access:** Every spoken step — reading, drafting aloud, discussing, or closing —
  can be done in writing, sign, gesture, or AAC instead. Learners who are non-speaking, d/Deaf,
  hard-of-hearing, or who use AAC complete every task in their preferred mode; no step requires
  producing or hearing sound.

## Assessment

- **Formative (peer + self):** Does the statement name complement, difference, and an open
  question, while avoiding both ranking and pretending sameness?
- **Portfolio artifact (unit):** The written relationship statement, paired with the Lesson 5
  chart, in the capstone history journal.

## Home connection

Write a two-sentence "relationship statement" between a way of knowing you grew up with and one you
met at school. Name one place they complement and one open question you will not force shut.
