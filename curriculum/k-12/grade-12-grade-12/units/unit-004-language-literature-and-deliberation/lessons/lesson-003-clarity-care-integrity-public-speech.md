# Lesson 3 — Clarity, Care, and Integrity in Public Speech

## Summary

Learners plan and deliver one message adapted to different **audiences** and **purposes**,
naming three commitments that travel unchanged — **clarity, care, and integrity** — and
the **register** shifts that must change. Adapting a voice is not losing a self; but
adaptation without integrity becomes manipulation, and this lesson draws that line.

## Objectives

- Speak with clarity, care, and integrity, adapting register, formality, and delivery to
  different audiences and purposes. _(D04.S1.12.02)_

## Connection

You already do this daily: you do not tell a younger sibling what you tell an employer,
and you do not speak to a room the way you speak to one friend. That shifting is not
fakeness — it is **register**, the way any skilled speaker tunes voice to audience and
purpose. The danger is not that the voice changes; it is that *something in the message*
changes in order to please or to win. The skill is to hold three things steady — say it
clearly, say it with care for the people it affects, and say what you actually believe —
while everything else about the delivery adapts.

## Materials

- Audience-and-purpose map
- Deliberation journal
- Speaking slips (low-tech: paper)

## Preparation

- Copy or draw the audience-and-purpose map; prepare the speaking slips.
- Retrieval: from Lesson 2, the parts of a reasoned position; from Grade 11
  (D04.S1.11.02), a persuasive speech that anticipates counterarguments. Today we add
  the capstone turn: *adaptation with integrity* (worked example first: Kirschner,
  Sweller & Clark, S-011).
- Prepare a plain definition: **register** is the level and style of language a setting
  calls for (formal to informal, technical to everyday); **delivery** is how the voice
  and body carry it. The classical Greek tradition named three means of persuasion —
  character (*ethos*), emotion (*pathos*), and argument (*logos*) (Aristotle, S-346) —
  as one tradition among many.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land, held as **description**:
public speech is a *craft* — word choice, tone, and delivery are analyzed and practiced
— and one ancient tradition named three appeals (ethos, pathos, logos) as the means of
persuasion (Aristotle, S-346). Hold that tradition as **one among many**: other cultures
have their own arts of public speaking, and no single one is the default. The lesson's
three commitments — clarity, care, integrity — are **values**, not facts; the craft of
register-shifting is a describable skill.

The **ethics** lens: integrity is the line between *adapting* a message and *distorting*
it; care asks who is affected by what we say. The **egalitarianism** lens: public speech
is not reserved for the fluent or the formally educated — clarity is a form of respect
for every listener, including those for whom the language is not their first. The
**global** lens: what counts as respectful or persuasive delivery differs across
cultures (eye contact, volume, directness), so no single style is the default. The
**technology** lens: a microphone, a recording, a livestream all *amplify* — they carry
one voice to many, and they change how care and integrity must be exercised because the
audience can no longer be seen. The **environment** lens: use an environmental
announcement as one purpose (a warning, a stewardship appeal) so speech is practiced on
questions where clarity can matter for safety and for the living world. The
**critical-thinking** lens: ask of any speaker, "what is being said clearly, and what is
being smoothed over?" Preview: Lesson 4 turns from speaking to reading across registers.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Lesson 2: what parts hold a reasoned position together? A
   speech is that position, *delivered* — today we work on the delivery and its limits.
2. **Meet register (10 min).** Work a model example first (S-011): one message — *a
   dangerous storm is coming; fill water now* — said three ways: to a village meeting
   (loud, direct, repeated), to a professional briefing (calm, technical, cited), and to
   a young child (gentle, concrete, reassuring). Name what changed (register, delivery)
   and what did not (clarity, care, integrity).
3. **Map one message (12 min).** With a partner, take a speaking slip and fill the
   audience-and-purpose map: the same core message adapted to three audiences and three
   purposes. Write the *one sentence* that stays true in every version.
4. **Deliver and listen (12 min).** Each partner delivers one version — aloud, or in
   writing, sign, typing, or AAC — and the other receives it in their preferred mode. The
   other checks it against the three commitments: *Is it clear? Is it careful about who it
   affects? Does it say what you actually believe?*
5. **Draw the line (8 min).** Discuss: at what point does adapting become misleading?
   Write one sentence stating where the line sits for you, and why.
6. **Close (5 min).** Say — or write, sign, type, or use AAC for — in one sentence, what
   changes when you adapt a voice, and what must never change.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Adapt the message to only two audiences and check it against one
  commitment (clarity) before adding the others.
- **Extension:** Analyze a recorded public address for where adaptation served the
  audience and where it served the speaker's own interest instead.
- **Communication access:** "Delivering" a message here means *conveying* it — aloud, in
  writing, in sign, or via AAC. Learners who are non-speaking, d/Deaf, hard-of-hearing, or
  who use AAC plan, adapt, and deliver in their preferred mode, and receive partners'
  versions in written or signed form. The performance assessment is the adaptation and its
  integrity — not the voice that carries it.

## Assessment

- **Formative (peer + self + performance):** Can the learner adapt one message across
  audiences and purposes while keeping it clear, careful, and honest — and name the
  line where adaptation becomes distortion? The performance may be delivered aloud, in
  writing, in sign, or via AAC; what is assessed is the adaptation and its integrity,
  not the voice that carries it.
- **Portfolio artifact (unit):** The completed audience-and-purpose map with the
  one-sentence "unchanging core," filed in the deliberation journal.

## Home connection

Tell — or write to, sign to, or type to — someone at home about this lesson and ask how
they change the way they speak to different people. Write one sentence about what they
change and one about what they keep the same.
