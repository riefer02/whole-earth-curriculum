# Lesson 14 — A Steward of the Earth

## Summary

Learners close the unit by naming their own responsibility as a **steward of the Earth**. They
gather the unit's threads — technology as a system to steward, media to create and read
honestly, the living world they belong to, and action taken with others — into a single
**steward's compass**, and write a short, honest vision of how they will act, individually and
with others, for all life. Hope here is not optimism; it is commitment.

## Objectives

- Reflect on their own responsibility as a steward of the Earth and how they will act —
  individually and with others — for all life. _(D10.S4.12.02)_

## Connection

A steward is not a hero and not an owner — a steward is someone who accepts a role of care and
keeps it. The Earth does not need a few perfect guardians; it needs many ordinary people who
each choose, in their own corner, to keep something alive — a tool, a truth, a place, a
promise. This unit has given you the pieces; this lesson is where you say, in your own words,
what you will do with them.

## Materials

- Steward's compass sheet
- Capstone technology & Earth journal
- Low-tech: blank paper; the unit's portfolio artifacts
- Enriched: a quiet space or a recording device

## Preparation

- Copy or draw the four-direction compass.
- Retrieval: lay out the unit's artifacts — the system map, the argument, the ledger, the
  message, the media diet, the confidence sheet, the cycle map, the boundary organizer, the web,
  the ripple map, and the stewardship plan. Today we gather them into one role.
- Frame hope as commitment, not optimism: a steward acts because it is right, not because
  success is guaranteed.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **my responsibility as a
steward of the Earth is to act — individually and with others — for all life, and I can say how,
because the unit has given me four directions: care for technology, honest media, the living
world, and shared action.** This is the unit's capstone reflection. Do not grade the grandness
of the vision; grade the *specificity and honesty* of it. Honor each learner's vision as their
own, never rank them, and keep the door open: stewardship is a practice begun, not a test
passed.

The **ethics** lens: stewardship is an ethics of relationship and reciprocity — because I am
given to, I give back, and my responsibility extends to beings and generations I will never
meet (S-427). The **egalitarianism** lens: a steward acts *with* others, not above them —
solidarity and mutual aid, where people meet one another's needs as equals, is the shape
stewardship takes among humans (S-345). The **global** lens: the four directions are lived
differently everywhere — a tree-planting movement in Kenya, river protectors, commons keepers —
but they are the same four compass points the world over (S-059, S-416). The **technology**
lens: the unit's whole stance returns here — technology neither worshiped nor feared, but
stewarded, chosen with clear eyes for what it gives and costs (S-281). The **environment** lens:
the compass's center is the unit's deepest claim — that human flourishing and the flourishing of
the living Earth are not rivals but the same project, and acting for all life is acting for
ourselves too (S-004, S-395).

## Procedure

1. **Recall (10 min).** Lay out the unit's artifacts in order — from the system map in Lesson 1
   to the stewardship plan in Lesson 13. Notice the arc: *seeing* a system, *judging* it,
   *choosing* stewardship, *creating* honestly, *staying* informed, *knowing* your place, and
   *designing* a response.
2. **Draw the compass (10 min).** Four directions: **care for technology** (choose, repair,
   share, steward), **honest media** (accurate, fair, sourced, open to evidence), **the living
   world** (know your place in the web), and **shared action** (act with others). Write one line
   under each direction: one thing you will actually do.
3. **Name the center (5 min).** In the middle, write **for all life** — the people and beings
   your stewardship serves, present and future. Say — or write, sign, gesture, or use AAC to
   express — why "all life" and not "just mine" is the center.
4. **Write the vision (15 min).** Write a short, honest paragraph: *"As a steward of the Earth,
   I will…"* — naming one act in each direction, and naming who you will do it *with*. Keep it
   specific and true to your life.
5. **Share (10 min).** In a pair or small group, share only what you choose. Listen without
   ranking; note one thing in another's vision that strengthens your own.
6. **Close (5 min).** Say — or write, sign, gesture, or use AAC to express — the one line from
   your vision you are most certain you will keep.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use the compass sheet with the four directions pre-labeled and starters ("For
  technology, I will…; for honest media, I will…; for the living world, I will…; with others, I
  will…"); the learner fills one line each.
- **Extension:** For each direction, name the ally or group you will act with, and one honest
  limit you accept — what you cannot do — so the vision is real, not a wish.
- **Number access (dyscalculia):** The lesson requires no arithmetic. The compass is a word
  organizer, doable in speech, writing, sign, or gesture; nothing must be counted, ordered, or
  computed.
- **Communication access:** Every spoken step — saying, sharing, reading aloud, or closing — can
  be done in writing, sign, gesture, or AAC instead. Learners who are non-speaking, d/Deaf,
  hard-of-hearing, or who use AAC complete every task in their preferred mode; no step requires
  producing or hearing sound.

## Assessment

- **Formative (self):** Can the learner name their responsibility as a steward and write an
  honest, specific vision that includes action in all four directions and names who they will
  act with?
- **Portfolio artifact (unit):** The completed steward's compass and the written vision, opened
  in the capstone technology & Earth journal as the unit's closing artifact.

## Home connection

Read your vision to someone you trust, or share it in writing, sign, or a recording. Ask them
to remind you of it in a month. A promise shared is a promise kept.
