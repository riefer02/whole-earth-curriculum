# Lesson 6 — Independent Health Management: Sleep, Movement, Nutrition

## Summary

Learners build the first half of an **independent health plan**: **sleep**, **movement**, and
**nutrition**. They learn evidence-based **ranges** (teenagers about 8–10 hours of sleep;
regular movement that includes aerobic and muscle-strengthening activity; varied eating), and
they fit those ranges to their own body, pace, and life through **self-observation** — treating
guidance as ranges, never rigid rules, and never grading any body.

## Objectives

- Manage their own health independently, integrating sleep, movement, nutrition, mental
  health, and medical care — beginning with the first three. _(D03.S4.12.01)_

## Connection

Leaving home, you become the person who decides when you sleep, how you move, and what you
eat. Independence is not doing whatever feels good in the moment; it is tending these three
things like a garden you now own. The good news is that you already know more than you think —
your body has been telling you what works. Today you learn to listen to it, with a little
science to guide you.

## Materials

- Three-pillar plan sheet
- Health & care journal
- Low-tech: paper and any writing tool; one's own body and a clock or daylight
- Enriched: an optional sleep/movement log

## Preparation

- Copy or draw the three-column sheet (sleep / movement / nutrition, each with "what I know,"
  "what my body tells me," "my small step").
- Retrieval: from Grade 8 (D03.S4.08.02), a personal self-care routine covering sleep,
  hygiene, movement, and connection; from Grade 10 (D03.S2.10.01), a safe and sustainable
  movement plan. Today we make the plan *independently ours*.
- Prepare the worked example (one pillar filled in) to model first (S-011).

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **independent health
management means tending sleep, movement, and nutrition yourself — guided by evidence-based
ranges, adapted by self-observation, and free of rigid rules or any judgment of a body.** Teach
one filled-in pillar as a worked example, then let learners complete their own (S-011). Lead
with dignity: a learner with a disability, an illness, an eating-disorder history, or a life
that makes "8 hours" impossible must never be graded on the targets. Ranges describe tendencies,
not verdicts; the goal is *tending*, not *scoring*.

The **ethics** lens: we treat a person's body and habits with the respect we owe a person —
describing, not prescribing shame. The **egalitarianism** lens: the same ranges serve every
body, but every body meets them differently — a person who uses a wheelchair moves differently,
a person with insomnia sleeps differently — and none is worth less. The **global** lens: sleep,
movement, and eating look different across cultures and climates — a midday rest in one place,
a dawn run in another, a shared late meal elsewhere — and all can be healthy. The **technology**
lens: trackers and apps can help you *notice* patterns, but they can also turn health into a
scoreboard that feeds anxiety; use them as a mirror, never as a judge. The
**environment** lens: where you live shapes how you can move and sleep — a walkable
neighborhood, a safe green space, a quiet room — and the plan must fit the place. Preview:
Lesson 7 adds mental health and medical care; Lesson 8 weaves all five into one lifelong plan.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Grade 8: name one self-care routine you built for sleep, hygiene,
   movement, or connection. Today we make those routines *ours* to run, not just to follow.
2. **Meet the idea (8 min).** Write it down: **independent health management** is tending your
   own wellbeing with knowledge and attention — starting with three pillars: **sleep**,
   **movement**, **nutrition**. The science gives you *ranges*; your own body and life tell you
   where within them you belong.
3. **Worked example — the ranges (15 min).** Model each range and note it is a range, not a
   rule: **sleep** — teenagers about 8–10 hours a night (S-050); **movement** — regular
   activity that includes both aerobic and muscle-strengthening work, done at your own capacity
   (S-069); **nutrition** — varied eating across food groups, with water as the everyday drink
   (S-038, S-049). For each, write what the *evidence* says (a range) on your sheet.
4. **Name the move — range, not rule (8 min).** A **range** says "most people in this group
   thrive around here"; a **rule** says "everyone must." Your job is to find your place in the
   range by **self-observation**: notice how you actually feel after sleeping, moving, and
   eating. Write one thing your body has already told you about each pillar.
5. **Guided practice (15 min).** With a partner, complete your three-pillar sheet — a range, a
   body-signal, and a small step for each. Exchange and check each other: is each small step
   small enough to actually take, and kind rather than punishing? Say — or write, sign,
   gesture, or use AAC to show — your one smallest step and when you will try it.
6. **Write it (5 min).** In your journal, write your three small steps as a single sentence of
   intent: *I will ____, ____, and ____ this week, and I will notice how I feel.*
7. **Close (2 min).** Say — or write, sign, gesture, or use AAC to express — the difference
   between a *range to find yourself in* and a *rule to obey*.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use the sheet pre-filled with the three ranges; the learner adds only one body
  signal and one small step rather than all three.
- **Extension:** For one pillar, distinguish what is a measurable fact (a sleep range, an
  activity guideline) from what is a personal value (how much to prioritize each), and note
  where the two could conflict and how you would resolve it.
- **Number access (dyscalculia):** The ranges (8–10 hours) are read as "about this much, more
  or less," never computed; provide the three-column sheet pre-printed with the ranges already
  written, and allow every step to be expressed in words, drawings, or gesture with no
  arithmetic at all.
- **Communication access:** Every spoken step — saying, naming, reading aloud, discussing, or
  closing — can be done in writing, sign, gesture, or AAC instead. Learners who are
  non-speaking, d/Deaf, hard-of-hearing, or who use AAC complete every task in their preferred
  mode; no step requires producing or hearing sound.

## Assessment

- **Formative (peer + self):** Can the learner state each range as a range, name a body signal
  for each pillar, and set one kind, small step for each?
- **Portfolio artifact (unit):** The completed three-pillar sheet plus the sentence of intent,
  opened in the health & care journal.

## Home connection

At home, notice — without judgment — how you felt after one night's sleep, one bout of
movement, and one meal. Write one sentence for each: what helped, and one thing you might do
the same or differently next time.
