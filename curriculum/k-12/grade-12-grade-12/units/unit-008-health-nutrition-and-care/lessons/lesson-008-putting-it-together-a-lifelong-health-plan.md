# Lesson 8 — Putting It Together: A Lifelong Health Plan

## Summary

Learners **synthesize** sleep, movement, nutrition, mental health, and medical care into a
single, adaptable **lifelong health plan** — the capstone artifact for independent health
management. They **retrieve** and weave together the unit's earlier pieces (a spaced-recall
payoff), write what they will **keep** and what they will **adjust**, and commit to
**revisiting** the plan as their body and life change. The plan is personal, revisable, and
free of any judgment of a body.

## Objectives

- Manage their own health independently, integrating sleep, movement, nutrition, mental
  health, and medical care into one lifelong, adaptable plan. _(D03.S4.12.01)_

## Connection

A plan is not a prison — it is a map you draw and redraw. The point of everything in this unit
is not a perfect score but a life you can keep tending, decade after decade, as your body and
circumstances change. Today you gather the pieces you have already built and set them down as
one plan — yours, for life, and yours to revise.

## Materials

- Lifelong-health wheel sheet
- Health & care journal
- Low-tech: paper and any writing tool; your own completed sheets from Lessons 6 and 7
- Enriched: a one-page plan template

## Preparation

- Copy or draw the five-spoke wheel (sleep / movement / nutrition / mental health / medical
  care around "my health, my pace," each with "keep" and "adjust").
- Retrieval: this lesson is the unit's spaced-recall pass — pull forward Lessons 6 and 7 (and
  the nutrition through-line from Lessons 1–3). Recalling across days is itself a
  science-of-learning strength (S-039).
- Prepare the worked example (one spoke's keep/adjust) to model first (S-011).

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **independent health
management is not five separate rules but one integrated, revisable plan — and revisiting it is
part of the plan, not a failure of it.** Model one spoke's keep/adjust as a worked example,
then let learners assemble their own wheel, retrieving the unit's earlier work (S-011, S-039).
Celebrate the plan as a living thing: a learner who revises is succeeding, not slipping. Never
grade a body, a number, or a habit; grade the *integration* — that all five areas are present
and thoughtfully connected.

The **ethics** lens: a plan you make for your own life is a promise to yourself — make it one
you can keep and one that treats you with kindness rather than punishment. The
**egalitarianism** lens: every body, every ability, every budget can hold a lifelong plan —
the plan adapts to the person, never the person to some fixed ideal. The **global** lens: a
lifelong plan looks different in every culture and climate — a plan that works in one place
may not in another, and that variety is health, not failure. The **technology** lens: tools
can *log* your plan, but the plan is yours — a tool should serve it, not run it, and no
tracker knows your life better than you do. The **environment** lens: your environment —
housing, air, green space, safety — shapes what plan is even possible, so the plan must
include tending the *conditions* of health, not only the habits. Preview: Lessons 9 and 10
turn outward, to caring for others and emergency response.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From memory — without looking — name the five areas of independent
   health you have built in this unit. Then check yourself against Lessons 6 and 7. This
   *retrieval* is how the learning sticks (S-039).
2. **Meet the idea (8 min).** Write it down: a **lifelong health plan** is one integrated
   picture of how you will tend **sleep**, **movement**, **nutrition**, **mental health**, and
   **medical care** — plus a promise to **revisit and adjust** it as your life changes.
3. **Worked example — one spoke (10 min).** Model the "keep and adjust" pair for one area. For
   example, **sleep**: *keep* — a roughly consistent bedtime that fits your life; *adjust* —
   shift it when work, study, or family need it, and notice how you feel. Write it on the
   wheel as the model.
4. **Weave your wheel (15 min).** For each of the five spokes, write one thing you will
   **keep** and one thing you will **adjust**. Draw them onto your wheel around the hub *my
   health, my pace.* Pull from your own earlier sheets — this is *your* plan, not a template.
5. **Name the move — revisit (5 min).** The plan is never finished. Write the commitment: *I
   will revisit this plan when my body, my work, or my life changes, and adjust it without
   shame.*
6. **Share and check (8 min).** With a partner, show only the *structure* of your wheel (all
   five spokes present, a keep and an adjust each). Check each other: is every area tended, and
   is the plan kind rather than punishing? Say — or write, sign, gesture, or use AAC to show —
   one thing you will adjust first and when.
7. **Close (2 min).** Say — or write, sign, gesture, or use AAC to express — what it means for
   a plan to be "for life, and yours to revise."

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use the wheel pre-filled with one "keep" per spoke; the learner adds one
  "adjust" per spoke rather than building both from scratch.
- **Extension:** Choose a life-change scenario (leaving home, an illness, aging, a move to a
  new climate) and revise one spoke in light of it, writing what stays and what changes.
- **Number access (dyscalculia):** This lesson requires no arithmetic; the five spokes are
  handled by the pre-printed wheel, and every spoke may be completed in words, drawings, or
  gesture with no numerals at all.
- **Communication access:** Every spoken step — saying, naming, reading aloud, discussing, or
  closing — can be done in writing, sign, gesture, or AAC instead. Learners who are
  non-speaking, d/Deaf, hard-of-hearing, or who use AAC complete every task in their preferred
  mode; no step requires producing or hearing sound.

## Assessment

- **Formative (peer + self):** Can the learner name all five areas from memory, and produce a
  wheel with a "keep" and an "adjust" for each, connected into one kind, revisable plan?
- **Summative / portfolio artifact (unit):** The completed lifelong-health wheel with the
  revisit-and-adjust commitment — the S4.12.01 capstone, opened in the journal.

## Home connection

At home, share the *shape* of your plan (the five areas and your one first adjustment) with
someone you trust — or, if you prefer, with yourself in writing. Write one sentence about how
you will remember to revisit it.
