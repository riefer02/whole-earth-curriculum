# Lesson 12 — A Vision for a More Just, Sustainable World

## Summary

Learners craft a considered vision of a more just *and* sustainable world, holding fairness to
people and care for the Earth as one project rather than rivals. They learn the widely accepted
meaning of **sustainable development** — meeting the needs of the present without compromising the
ability of future generations to meet their own — and the idea that environmental harm falls
unequally on those who did least to cause it. They write the world they want to help build and the
concrete role they will play, closing the strand's reflection.

## Objectives

- Reflect on their own role in public life and craft a considered vision of how they will
  participate to build a more just and sustainable world. _(D09.S3.12.02)_

## Connection

Some people speak as if we must choose: take care of people *or* take care of the Earth. But the
two are the same project seen twice — the people who suffer most from a damaged river or a heating
planet are usually the people with the least power, and a world that is fair to people would not
hand its children a broken Earth. A just world and a livable world are not rivals; they are two
names for the same wish. A vision worth having holds both, and names the role you will play in it.

## Materials

- Just-and-sustainable vision sheet
- Capstone ethics & civic journal
- Low-tech: blank paper; a picture or object from a place you love
- Enriched: two short texts (Brundtland definition; an environmental-justice account)

## Preparation

- Copy or draw the vision sheet; prepare the short texts.
- Retrieval: from Lesson 11, the learner's pathways and role; from Grade 11 (D09.S3.11.01),
  global collective-action problems. Today we synthesize into a vision.
- Frame the vision as *the learner's own*, not a platform to adopt; the skill is coherence —
  holding justice and sustainability together — not agreement with any given blueprint.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **a more just world and a
sustainable world are the same project, not rivals; sustainable development is meeting the needs
of the present without compromising future generations' ability to meet their own, and
environmental harm falls unequally on those who did least to cause it. A considered vision holds
justice and sustainability together and names the concrete role the learner will play.** Hold the
facts accurately: the definition of sustainable development comes from the 1987 Brundtland Report
(S-395); environmental harm is unevenly distributed, with low-income and marginalized communities
often bearing the most (S-483, S-484); climate change is real and human-caused, summarized by the
IPCC (S-005). Present these as evidence; the *vision* that responds to them is a value, and it is
the learner's own.

The **ethics** lens: a vision is a claim about what *ought* to be, and the lesson keeps that
distinct from what *is* — evidence (climate, inequality, environmental harm) supports the vision
but does not dictate it (S-005, S-464, S-483). The **egalitarianism** lens: environmental justice
names exactly the unfairness that the vision must answer — the burden of harm falls on those with
the least power to refuse it (S-483, S-484, S-485), so a just vision is not "environment-first"
or "people-first" but *both at once*. The **global** lens: the vision must account for the whole
world, including future people and distant places not in the room — sustainability is
intergenerational and global by definition (S-395). The **technology** lens: technology is
two-edged here — it can help meet needs with less harm, and it can also deepen the gaps — so the
vision should say how tools will serve justice, not just efficiency. The **environment** lens:
this is the strand's most direct meeting with the living Earth — a livable planet is the floor
beneath every other justice, and the vision treats it as such (S-005). Preview: Lesson 13 turns
to peacemaking — listening, taking responsibility, and repairing.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Lesson 11, your pathways into public life. Today we gather them into
   a *vision*.
2. **Meet the definition (10 min).** Write it: **sustainable development** is meeting the needs of
   the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own (S-395).
   Note the two parts it holds together — needs *now*, and the future's needs. A vision that drops
   either half is not sustainable.
3. **See the unfairness (8 min).** Write the fact: environmental harm falls unequally — the
   people with the least power often bear the most of it (S-483, S-484). That is why justice and
   sustainability are one project: you cannot build a fair world on a damaged Earth, or a healthy
   Earth on an unfair world.
4. **Write the vision (15 min).** In the overlapping space, write the world you want to help
   build — one or two sentences each for "justice for people" and "a livable Earth," and then the
   place where they meet. Be specific: a world where what, for whom, and with what Earth?
5. **Name the role (12 min).** In the bottom space, write the concrete role you will play — the
   one or two things you, specifically, will do to move that world closer, drawing on the pathways
   you circled in Lesson 11.
6. **Share and check (3 min).** With a partner, ask: *does the vision hold both justice and
   sustainability, and is the role concrete enough to act on?* Give one specific suggestion.
7. **Close (2 min).** Say — or write, sign, gesture, or use AAC to express — one line of your
   vision you are willing to stand behind today.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use a vision sheet with starters ("A fair world is one where…; a livable Earth is
  one where…; where they meet is…; the role I will play is…").
- **Extension:** Name one place where fairness to people and care for the Earth genuinely pull
  apart (for example, a job that feeds a family but harms a place), and argue how you would hold
  both — without pretending the tension away.
- **Number access (dyscalculia):** This lesson requires no arithmetic. Any figures (dates, data)
  are pre-printed and read aloud; nothing must be counted, computed, or ordered. The vision is a
  word organizer, doable in speech, writing, sign, or gesture.
- **Communication access:** Every spoken step — naming, reading aloud, discussing, sharing, or
  closing — can be done in writing, sign, gesture, or AAC instead. Learners who are non-speaking,
  d/Deaf, hard-of-hearing, or who use AAC complete every task in their preferred mode; no step
  requires producing or hearing sound.

## Assessment

- **Formative (peer + self):** Can the learner craft a vision that holds justice for people and a
  livable Earth together, grounded in the evidence (sustainable development, unequal environmental
  harm), and name a concrete role they will play (in speech, writing, sign, or AAC)?
- **Portfolio artifact (unit):** The completed vision sheet, added to the capstone ethics & civic
  journal.

## Home connection

Ask someone at home: "What is one thing the world needs to be both fairer to people and kinder to
the Earth?" Write their answer and yours — one line each.
