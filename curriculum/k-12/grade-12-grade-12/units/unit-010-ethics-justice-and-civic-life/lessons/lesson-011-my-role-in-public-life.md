# Lesson 11 — My Role in Public Life

## Summary

Learners reflect on their role in public life by mapping the many pathways into it — voting,
organizing, testifying, creating, witnessing, caring, protesting, running, teaching, reporting —
and by noticing that power is unevenly distributed and must be held accountable. They mark the
pathways they are most likely to take, and where they have a voice and where they do not, crafting
the first sketch of a considered public self.

## Objectives

- Reflect on their own role in public life and craft a considered vision of how they will
  participate to build a more just and sustainable world. _(D09.S3.12.02)_

## Connection

Public life is not only elections and politicians. It is the meeting where a street is decided,
the person who speaks up for a neighbor, the artist who makes a problem visible, the volunteer who
keeps a shared place alive, the witness who records what happened. Everyone already has a small
place in public life; the question is whether we take it up on purpose. Mapping the ways in —
and noticing where power sits — is the first step to choosing, rather than drifting into, a public
role.

## Materials

- Pathways-into-public-life sheet
- Capstone ethics & civic journal
- Low-tech: blank paper; a list of local ways to participate
- Enriched: a democracy-and-participation data page

## Preparation

- Copy or draw the constellation sheet; prepare the local list of ways to participate.
- Retrieval: from Lesson 10, acting collectively. Today we widen the frame from one action to a
  *role* in public life.
- Frame participation as many pathways, none ranked above the others; every pathway is real, and
  learners differ in which are open to them.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **public life has many pathways —
voting, organizing, testifying, creating, witnessing, caring, protesting, running, teaching,
reporting — and no single one is the "real" form of citizenship. Power is unevenly distributed and
must be held accountable, and the learner's role begins by noticing which pathways are open to
them and where their own voice is strong or weak.** Hold the facts lightly: participation in
public decisions is uneven across and within societies (S-461); the Universal Declaration affirms
a right to take part in government, directly or through freely chosen representatives (S-149,
Article 21); peace, justice, and accountable institutions are a shared global goal (S-463). Do
not prescribe any political path or party; the lesson is about *how* to enter public life, not
*which* cause to serve.

The **ethics** lens: a public role is a set of responsibilities — to be truthful, to be fair, to
listen — and the lesson treats taking up a role as taking up those duties. The **egalitarianism**
lens: power is not only force; it is also the ability to set the agenda and to be heard (S-275),
so the lesson asks each learner to notice where their own voice is loud and where it is quiet —
and what that means for who else's voice needs lifting. The **global** lens: the pathways exist
across societies, but they look different everywhere — participation is uneven worldwide (S-461),
and no single country's way is the model; the right to take part is a shared, universal claim
(S-149). The **technology** lens: digital tools have opened new pathways (organizing, witnessing,
reporting) while also concentrating who gets seen and who is silenced — and the same tools spread
both truth and falsehood, so a public role now includes the duty to check and to be checkable
(S-481, S-482, S-278, S-217, S-063). The **environment** lens: public life includes care for the
shared, living world — the person who speaks for a river or a green space is taking part in public
life as surely as the person who votes. Preview: Lesson 12 turns the role into a *vision* of a
more just, sustainable world.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Lesson 10, one collective action. Today we widen the frame: from one
   action to a *role* in public life.
2. **Meet the pathways (10 min).** Write them around yourself on the sheet: **vote, organize,
   testify, create, witness, care, protest, run, teach, report.** Public life is all of these, not
   only one. No pathway is the "real" one; they are different doors into the same shared life.
3. **Notice the power (8 min).** Write: **power** is the ability to decide — and to be heard
   (S-275). In public life, power is uneven: some people's voices are loud, others are quiet. A
   role in public life includes asking *whose voice is missing here?*
4. **Mark your entries (15 min).** Circle the two pathways you are most likely to take, and write
   one reason for each. Then write honestly: *where do I already have a voice, and where is my
   voice weak or missing?* (S-461, S-149)
5. **Name one duty (10 min).** For the pathway you circled first, write one *duty* it brings — a
   thing you would owe others if you took it up (truthfulness, fairness, listening, care). A role
   is not a costume; it is a set of responsibilities.
6. **Close (7 min).** Say — or write, sign, gesture, or use AAC to express — one way you will take
   part in public life in the next year, and why that one.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use a sheet with the pathways pre-printed; the learner circles two and answers with
  a starter ("I am most likely to… because…; a voice I would need to listen for is…").
- **Extension:** Name one pathway where power is concentrated and one concrete way ordinary people
  hold it accountable, and write which you would take up and why.
- **Number access (dyscalculia):** This lesson requires no arithmetic. Participation data is
  offloaded to a pre-printed sheet and read aloud; nothing must be counted, computed, or ordered.
  The constellation is spatial and word-based, doable in speech, writing, sign, or gesture.
- **Communication access:** Every spoken step — naming, reading aloud, discussing, or closing —
  can be done in writing, sign, gesture, or AAC instead. Learners who are non-speaking, d/Deaf,
  hard-of-hearing, or who use AAC complete every task in their preferred mode; no step requires
  producing or hearing sound.

## Assessment

- **Formative (peer + self):** Can the learner map the pathways into public life, notice where
  power and voice are uneven, mark their own likely entries, and name a duty each brings (in
  speech, writing, sign, or AAC)?
- **Portfolio artifact (unit):** The completed constellation sheet, opened in the capstone ethics
  & civic journal — the strand's "public role" artifact.

## Home connection

Ask someone at home: "What is one way you have taken part in public life — a meeting, a vote, a
protest, a kindness that changed a shared thing?" Write their answer and yours — one line each.
