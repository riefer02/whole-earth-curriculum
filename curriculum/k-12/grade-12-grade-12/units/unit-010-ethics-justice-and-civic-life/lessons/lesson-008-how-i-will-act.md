# Lesson 8 — How I Will Act

## Summary

Learners turn a responsibility they accepted in Lesson 7 into a concrete, doable action: they name
the responsibility, choose one specific action, list **allies and resources**, name the honest
**limits** (time, power, risk), and set a check-back date for accountability. They learn that
agency — the real capacity to act — grows when it is shared, and that a commitment with named
limits is stronger, not weaker, than a vague promise.

## Objectives

- Reflect on their own responsibilities to fairness and justice in their communities and in the
  wider world, and how they will act. _(D09.S2.12.02)_

## Connection

"I care about fairness" becomes real only when it becomes a verb. The gap between caring and
acting is usually not a lack of feeling but a lack of *shape*: no specific action, no allies, no
sense of what it will cost. A commitment that names a single doable thing, the people who will do
it with you, and the honest limits of what you can manage is one you can actually keep. Justice,
like any craft, is practiced — and practice needs a plan.

## Materials

- Responsibility-to-action sheet
- Capstone ethics & civic journal
- Low-tech: blank paper; a local community contact list
- Enriched: a searchable directory of local groups

## Preparation

- Copy or draw the flow sheet; gather a short list of local groups that already act on common
  issues (a food share, a cleanup, a mutual-aid circle, a student council).
- Retrieval: from Lesson 7, one responsibility at each scale. Today we choose *one* and make it
  concrete.
- Frame limits as honesty, not failure: naming what you cannot do protects the commitment you *do*
  make.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **a responsibility becomes real
when it becomes an action — name the responsibility, choose one specific, doable action, list
allies and resources, name the honest limits, and set a check-back date. Agency grows when it is
shared, and a commitment with named limits is stronger than a vague promise.** This is the
strand's "how I will act" move, and it pairs the *value* (what I owe) with *agency* (what I can
actually do). Do not grade learners on the size of their action; a small, real, kept commitment is
worth more than a grand, abandoned one.

The **ethics** lens: a responsibility without an action is a good intention, and ethics is about
what we *do* — the move from "I ought" to "I will" is where the strand's reasoning becomes
practice. The **egalitarianism** lens: agency is not equal — a learner with money, time, and
safety can do more than one without, so the lesson never ranks actions by size; it asks only that
the action be *real* and the limits be *named* honestly (S-460). The **global** lens: acting for
fairness looks different everywhere — a mutual-aid tradition like *harambee*, *gotong royong*, or
*mink'a* is one way humans have always turned shared responsibility into shared work (S-164); a
learner's action can be local and still join an old, worldwide pattern. The **technology** lens:
an action can use tools — a shared document, a group chat, a petition — but the tool is not the
action; the lesson keeps the *doing* first and the technology in service of it. The
**environment** lens: many of the most doable actions of fairness are also acts of care for a
shared place — cleaning a common space, sharing water, protecting a green corner — where justice
and stewardship meet. Preview: Lesson 9 scales up — researching an issue, building coalitions, and
listening.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Lesson 7, your circles of responsibility. Choose *one* responsibility
   you actually accept — the one you are most willing to act on now.
2. **Name one concrete action (12 min).** Turn it into a single, doable action. Make it specific:
   not "help the poor" but "join the weekend food-share at the market" or "help run the school
   fairness club." Write it as a verb with a where and a when.
3. **List allies and resources (10 min).** Write who will do it *with* you and what you will
   need. Agency grows when it is shared (S-147); a lone promise is harder to keep than a shared
   one. Include groups already doing the work.
4. **Name the honest limits (8 min).** Write what you honestly cannot do — the time, the power,
   the risk. Naming a limit protects the part you *can* keep; it is honesty, not failure.
5. **Set the check-back (8 min).** Write a date — one to two weeks out — when you will look back
   at whether you did it, what got in the way, and what to adjust. A commitment is kept by
   checking, not by hoping.
6. **Pair-check (8 min).** With a partner, read your plan. Ask: *is the action specific and
   doable? are the allies real? are the limits honest? is there a check-back date?* Give one
   specific suggestion.
7. **Close (4 min).** Say — or write, sign, gesture, or use AAC to express — one thing you will
   actually *do* this week.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use a flow sheet with the five boxes and starters ("I will… by…, with…; I cannot…;
  I will check back on…"); the learner fills one line per box.
- **Extension:** Anticipate one concrete obstacle, write how you will work around it without
  dropping the commitment, and name the ally who can help with that specific obstacle.
- **Number access (dyscalculia):** This lesson requires no arithmetic. Dates are chosen from a
  calendar and can be circled rather than computed; nothing must be counted, ordered, or
  calculated. The flow is a word organizer, doable in speech, writing, sign, or gesture.
- **Communication access:** Every spoken step — naming, reading aloud, discussing, or closing —
  can be done in writing, sign, gesture, or AAC instead. Learners who are non-speaking, d/Deaf,
  hard-of-hearing, or who use AAC complete every task in their preferred mode; no step requires
  producing or hearing sound.

## Assessment

- **Formative (peer + self):** Can the learner turn a responsibility into a specific, doable
  action with allies, resources, honest limits, and a check-back date (in speech, writing, sign,
  or AAC)?
- **Portfolio artifact (unit):** The completed action plan, added to the capstone ethics & civic
  journal.

## Home connection

Tell someone at home your one action and your check-back date, and ask them to ask you about it
on that day. Accountability is a gift, not a test.
