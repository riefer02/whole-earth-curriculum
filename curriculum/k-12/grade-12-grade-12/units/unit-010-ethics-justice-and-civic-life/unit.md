# Unit 10 — Ethics, Justice & Civic Life

## Overview

This unit deepens Grade 11's structures, justice, and solidarity into the habits of a
citizen. Learners reason through a complex, real-world moral dilemma with no clean
answer, weighing multiple frameworks and consequences, and defend a nuanced position,
and reflect on their own evolving values, how they formed, and how they will test them
in the world, holding questions open rather than settling them as creed. They weigh a
real, contested question of justice and rights, locally and globally, and defend a
reasoned position that accounts for multiple perspectives, and reflect on their own
responsibilities to fairness and justice in their communities and in the wider world,
and how they will act. They practice the habits of an engaged citizen by researching
an issue, building coalitions, listening across disagreement, and acting collectively
toward a fairer common life, and reflect on their own role in public life, crafting a
considered vision of how they will participate to build a more just and sustainable
world. They practice the habits of peacemaking by listening across deep disagreement,
taking responsibility for harm, and repairing relationships and communities, and
reflect on how they can contribute to peace and repair in a divided world, locally and
globally, with both honesty and hope. Questions are held as questions, never answered
as dogma.

## Essential questions

- How do I reason through a real moral dilemma with no clean answer, and how have my values formed and how will I test them in the world?
- How do I weigh contested questions of justice and rights, locally and globally, and what are my responsibilities to fairness and justice?
- How do I practice the habits of an engaged citizen and a peacemaker — researching, building coalitions, listening across disagreement, and repairing harm?
