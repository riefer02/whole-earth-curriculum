# Lesson 14 — The Whole Arc: A Life in a Body

## Summary

Learners assemble the unit's **four threads** — the body–mind framework, embodied
dignity across a lifetime, the lifelong movement plan, and environments that widen
movement — into one whole, and write a **single sentence** that ties them into a life.
They gather the unit's **four capstone artifacts** into a portfolio and reflect, through
**spaced retrieval**, on what they now carry forward (Dunlosky et al., S-039).

## Objectives

- Integrate the unit's four threads — body–mind, dignity, movement, and environments —
  into one lifelong whole. _(D03.S1.12.01, D03.S1.12.02, D03.S2.12.01, D03.S2.12.02)_

## Connection

A life is not four separate projects. The way you read your body's signals (Lesson 2)
shows up in how you move (Lesson 10); the dignity you protect in caregiving (Lesson 7)
shows up in the ramp you fight for (Lesson 13). What this unit has been building is not
a checklist but a *way of living in a body* — one that notices, that respects every
body's worth, that moves on purpose, and that makes the world easier for others to move
through. Today you gather the pieces and see that they were always one thing.

## Materials

- Whole-arc map
- Portfolio checklist (enriched)

## Preparation

- Copy or draw the whole-arc map; prepare the portfolio checklist.
- Retrieval (spaced): the four threads, each reached across the unit. Today is the
  capstone retrieval pass — recalling and *integrating* is what makes the learning
  durable (Dunlosky et al., S-039).
- Gather the four artifacts: the body–mind framework (Lesson 4), the dignity
  reflection (Lesson 7), the movement plan (Lesson 10), and the access proposal
  (Lesson 13).

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **the four threads are
one thing** — a way of living in a body that notices (body–mind), honors every body's
worth (dignity), moves on purpose (movement plan), and widens the world's doors
(environments). The capstone is a **retrieval-and-integration pass**, which is the
highest-value kind of review (Dunlosky et al., S-039). Keep the tone open, not
summative-in-the-punitive sense: this is a gathering, not a grading. Honor every body
to the last line — the unit's closing claim is that dignity and joy belong to the whole
arc of a life, at every age and every ability, and that the learner now carries the
tools to protect that.

The **ethics** lens: the four threads are all, at bottom, about care — for oneself, for
others, for the shared world. The **egalitarianism** lens: the unit has insisted that
every body is a whole body and every stage is a whole stage, and the capstone restates
it as a commitment, not a slogan. The **global** lens: the learner may carry this
framework into a life that looks like any life on Earth — a city, a farm, a coast, a
mountain — and it is built to travel. The **critical-thinking** lens: the integration
sentence is a claim about your own life, offered to be tested and revised as the life
unfolds. The **technology & environment** lens: the four threads come together in a body that lives in a particular place and time — among devices, buildings, and climates that help or hinder — so the whole arc is carried not in a vacuum but in a world, and part of the task is making that world easier for every body to move through. Close with a forward-looking note: this is the beginning of carrying it, not
the end of learning it.

## Procedure

1. **Recall the four threads (8 min).** Without looking back, write the four threads of
   the unit in one line each — body–mind, dignity, movement, environments — and what
   each means to you now.
2. **Map the whole (10 min).** On the whole-arc map, place the four threads as arcs
   meeting at a center. At each thread, write its one essential idea in your own words.
3. **Write the tying sentence (12 min).** In the center, write a single sentence that
   ties all four into one life — the sentence you would carry forward and hand to
   someone else. Make it true to you.
4. **Assemble the portfolio (10 min).** Gather the four artifacts and set them beside
   the map: the framework, the dignity reflection, the movement plan, the access
   proposal. Check each against the checklist.
5. **Share the sentence (8 min).** In pairs, read your tying sentence aloud. Each
   listener says one word they would not change and one they want to hear more about.
6. **Close (7 min).** Say, in your own words, what this unit has changed about how you
   will live in your body — and in the world you move through.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Write the four threads with a word or two each and the tying sentence as
  a single clause, using the map's labels as a scaffold.
- **Extension:** Write a one-paragraph "whole-arc statement" that explains the tying
  sentence to a stranger, then read it aloud and revise after hearing how it lands.

## Assessment

- **Summative (portfolio):** The completed whole-arc map with the tying sentence, and
  the assembled four-artifact portfolio — the unit's capstone.
- **Self:** Can the learner name all four threads and say how they connect in their own
  life, in one honest sentence?

## Home connection

Read your tying sentence to someone at home and ask them what it makes them think about
their own body and life. Write one sentence about what they said.
