# Lesson 10 — Designing My Own Movement Plan

## Summary

Learners learn the **FITT levers** — **frequency, intensity, time, type** — and use
them to design a **weekly movement plan** that fits their own body, life, and
circumstances. They learn that energy needs and capacities differ among bodies (FAO,
S-285), so every plan is individual. They add **adaptation triggers** — injury, season,
a new job, a new stage of life — so the plan bends instead of breaking. This produces
the unit's third capstone artifact: the **lifelong, adaptable movement plan**.

## Objectives

- Integrate movement into a lifelong, adaptable plan fitted to one's own body and life,
  supporting physical, mental, and social health. _(D03.S2.12.01)_

## Connection

A plan you cannot keep is worse than no plan, because it teaches you to quit. The best
movement plan is not the most impressive one — it is the one you can actually do next
week, and next year, and in a different season, with a different body. That is why a
good plan is built on four levers you can turn — how often, how hard, how long, and what
kind — and on a promise to *adapt* rather than quit when life changes. Today you build
a plan that is honest to your own life, not someone else's.

## Materials

- Movement-plan template
- Energy-needs summary (enriched)

## Preparation

- Copy or draw the movement-plan template.
- Retrieval: from Lesson 9, the four building blocks; from Lesson 8, the three kinds of
  health movement feeds. Today we turn blocks and benefits into a *personal plan*.
- Prepare a plain definition: **FITT** names four levers — frequency (how often),
  intensity (how hard), time (how long), type (what kind). A plan is **adaptable** when
  it names the triggers that would change it.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land, held as **skill plus
value**: a plan is built from four levers (frequency, intensity, time, type), and it is
only good if it is **individual and adaptable** — energy needs and capacities differ
among bodies by age, size, sex, and activity level (FAO, S-285), and a balanced routine
draws on aerobic and muscle-strengthening activity with balance and flexibility across
the lifespan (WHO, S-341). Teach the FITT scaffold explicitly (worked example first:
Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, S-011), then release to design. No body-shaming, no
calorie-talk, no single "ideal" plan: the criterion is *does it serve this learner's
health and joy across time*, not *does it look impressive*.

The **ethics** lens: a plan is a promise to yourself, and an honest promise beats a
heroic one you will break. The **egalitarianism** lens: plans must fit real lives — no
money for equipment, no safe place to run, long work hours — and a good plan works with
what is actually there. The **global** lens: movement forms differ everywhere, and the
plan should use the forms the learner's own life already contains (walking, dancing,
farming, games). The **critical-thinking** lens: "you should exercise X minutes" is a
general guide, not a law for every body — read it, then adapt it. The **technology** lens: apps and trackers can scaffold the FITT levers — they count frequency, intensity, time, and type — but access to them is uneven, and a plan that depends on a device fails the moment the device is gone; the plan must live in the life, not in the app. Preview: Lesson 11
makes the plan stick and teaches it to bend.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Lesson 9: what are the four blocks? Today we turn them into
   *your* plan.
2. **Meet FITT (10 min).** Work a model example first (S-011): a person with long work
   hours and no gym. **Frequency** — three times a week; **intensity** — enough to
   breathe harder but still talk; **time** — twenty minutes; **type** — brisk walk or
   dance, plus a carry for strength. Show how each lever turns to fit a real life.
3. **Draft your week (15 min).** On the template, fill all four columns for one real
   week, using activities your life already contains. Keep it honest to what you can
   actually do.
4. **Peer-check (8 min).** Swap with a partner. Each of you checks the other's plan for
   two things: does it hit all four blocks across the week, and is it *actually
   doable*? Suggest one improvement.
5. **Add adaptation triggers (8 min).** Write the triggers that would change your plan:
   an injury, a new season, a move, a busier job, a different stage of life. Write what
   you would switch to in each case.
6. **Close (5 min).** Say, in one sentence, what makes your plan *yours* rather than a
   copy of someone else's.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Fill one row (type and time) for three days and leave intensity aside;
  build the plan with a partner.
- **Extension:** Write a low-tech, no-equipment version of your plan and a fully
  equipped version, and name what you gain and lose in each.

## Assessment

- **Summative (portfolio):** The completed FITT plan with adaptation triggers — the
  unit's third capstone artifact, the lifelong, adaptable movement plan.
- **Peer:** Could a partner identify all four blocks and confirm the plan is doable?

## Home connection

Show your plan to someone at home and ask them what they would change to make it fit
*your* real week. Write one sentence about their suggestion and one about whether you
will take it.
