# Lesson 12 — The Plan and the Promise

## Summary

Learners turn yesterday's chosen goal into a plan with autonomy and accountability.
They study a worked-example plan, then write their own: the goal, three milestones, an
if-then plan for the hard moment, a first step due this week, and a named
accountability check. The lesson treats planning as an explicit, teachable skill —
modeled, then practiced.

## Objectives

- Set, plan, and take the first step on a meaningful life goal with autonomy and
  accountability. _(D01.S3.12.01)_

## Connection

A goal without a plan is a wish. Wishes feel good and evaporate; plans have a first
step, a date, and a way to know whether you did it. The difference between the two is
rarely talent — it is whether, in the hard moment, you already know what you will do.
The trick that works is deceptively small: decide in advance, *"if this happens, then I
will do this."* That one sentence turns intention into action when willpower alone
would fail.

## Materials

- Goal plan frame
- Worked-example plan
- Paper and pencil (low-tech)
- If-then planning page (enriched)

## Preparation

- Copy or draw the goal plan frame; prepare the worked-example plan (a goal unlike most
  learners', so it models the *form* without prescribing the *content*).
- Retrieval: from Lesson 11, the chosen goal. Today it becomes a plan.
- Prepare plain definitions (model once, then guided practice: Kirschner, Sweller &
  Clark, S-011): a **milestone** is a reachable marker on the way; an **if-then plan** is
  a pre-decided response to a specific hard moment ("If I feel like skipping, then I
  will ___") — a well-evidenced situational self-control strategy (Duckworth, Gendler &
  Gross, S-097); **accountability** is a named way to notice whether you did what you
  said; **autonomy** is that the plan is *yours*.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **a goal becomes real
through a first step, a date, and an if-then plan for the hard moment — and the plan
stays autonomous even while it is accountable.** Planning is a *skill*, so it is taught
explicitly: model once, then guided practice (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, S-011).
If-then planning is a well-evidenced self-control strategy — with the honest caveat
that willpower is not a muscle you can simply grow (Duckworth et al., S-097).

The **ethics** lens: accountability to oneself and to others is an ethical commitment —
a promise, and promises are the material of ethics. The **egalitarianism** lens: a
learner's plan must match their real constraints — time, money, care duties, access —
so "autonomy" means a plan authored within one's actual conditions, never a generic
template imposed on all. The **global** lens: what a "life goal" looks like — a trade,
a calling, a family role, a craft — varies across cultures; the plan's *form* is
universal, its *content* is the learner's own. The **critical-thinking** lens: the
accountability check is a way to verify one's own claim against what actually happens.

**Safeguarding:** goals are the learner's own and may be modest or private. No one is
required to share a goal that touches family precarity, health, or safety. A learner
may plan a *short-term* goal if a life goal feels too exposed.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Lesson 11: what goal did you choose, and which test did it
   pass? Today it becomes a plan.
2. **Study the worked example (10 min).** Read the model plan (S-011). Find its five
   parts: the **goal**, three **milestones**, the **if-then plan**, the **first step
   with a date**, and the **accountability check.** Say what each part *does*.
3. **Draft your plan (15 min).** On the frame, write your goal and three milestones.
   Then write one if-then plan for the specific hard moment you can already foresee.
4. **Set the first step and the check (8 min).** Write a first step due this week, and
   name the accountability check — a person you will tell, a calendar mark, a log you
   will keep.
5. **Peer-check (8 min).** Exchange with a partner. Ask: *Is the first step small
   enough to do this week? Does the if-then plan name a real hard moment? Is the check
   something that will actually happen?*
6. **Close (5 min).** Say, in one sentence, the difference between a wish and a plan —
   and why the if-then plan is the part most likely to matter.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Set a short-term goal with one milestone, a first step, and a single
  if-then plan; skip the second and third milestones.
- **Extension:** Write a second if-then plan for a *different* hard moment, and a
  fallback if the first step fails.

## Assessment

- **Formative (peer + self):** Can the learner produce a plan with a goal, milestones,
  a real if-then plan, a dated first step, and a named accountability check?
- **Portfolio artifact (unit):** The completed goal plan, kept in the capstone journal
  (feeds Lesson 13's setback work).

## Home connection

Tell your first step and its date to someone at home, and ask them to be your check.
Write one sentence on what you agreed.
