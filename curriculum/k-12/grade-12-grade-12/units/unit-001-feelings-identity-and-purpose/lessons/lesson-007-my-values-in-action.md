# Lesson 7 — My Values in Action

## Summary

Learners move from *claiming* values to *living* them. They learn the concept of the
**value-action gap** — the distance between what we say we stand for and what we
actually do when it costs us — and they name, honestly, one gap in their own life and
one concrete commitment to close it. Integrity is reframed not as perfection but as the
habit of shrinking the gap.

## Objectives

- Articulate values and align them with action, holding an integrated self together
  under real pressure. _(D01.S2.12.01)_

## Connection

It is easy to be honest when honesty costs nothing, and easy to be fair when fairness
is free. The test of a value is what you do when it *costs* — when the deadline tempts
you to copy, when the group is unkind and silence is safer, when a good opportunity
asks you to hide a part of who you are. Everyone has a gap between what they claim and
what they do. Integrity is not having no gap; it is noticing it and deliberately
closing it, a little, every time you choose.

## Materials

- Value-action map
- Pressure scenarios
- Paper and pencil (low-tech)
- Values-to-behavior page (enriched)

## Preparation

- Copy or draw the value-action map; prepare the pressure scenarios.
- Retrieval: from Lesson 6, the through-line ("in every room I am still the person
  who ___"). Today we test whether actions live up to that line.
- Prepare plain definitions (model once: Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, S-011): **integrity**
  is consistency between claimed values and real action; a **value-action gap** is the
  distance between them, which widens when the cost of living the value rises. Hold
  these as description, never as a moral score on any learner.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **values are tested by
their cost, and integrity is the habit of noticing and shrinking the gap — not a state
of perfect consistency.** Even strongly held values can fail to appear in behavior when
the situation makes them expensive, which is why the lesson asks for honesty about the
gap rather than shame about it.

The **ethics** lens: living a value is an ethical act, and the gap is where ethics
becomes real. The **egalitarianism** lens: the *cost* of living a value is not the same
for everyone — speaking up can cost a privileged person comfort and a marginalized
person their safety; honor that difference rather than moralizing. The **global** lens:
values are named and ranked differently across cultures and traditions; a learner's
value set is theirs to articulate, never to be corrected toward any single canon. The
**critical-thinking** lens: the honest naming of a gap is a check on self-flattery —
exactly the kind of check this unit trains.

**Safeguarding:** scenarios are one step removed and chosen to avoid demanding
disclosure of trauma or family distress. If a learner names a real gap that is
genuinely risky (e.g., safety, abuse), do not treat it as a classroom exercise —
redirect to appropriate support.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Lesson 6: what was your through-line sentence? Today we
   test whether your actions would keep that promise under pressure.
2. **Meet the gap (10 min).** Work a model example (S-011): a student who "values
   honesty" faces a deadline and a friend's completed paper. The *claimed* value is
   honesty; the *matching* action is doing their own work; the *gap* is the temptation,
   and its *cost* is the grade risk. Name the parts, then the honest question: where
   does *my* gap open?
3. **Map a scenario (12 min).** In pairs, take a pressure scenario. Name the value it
   tests, the action that would match it, and the cost that opens the gap.
4. **Name your own gap (10 min).** Privately, in your capstone journal, write one value
   you claim and one place your action falls short of it — and name the real cost that
   makes it hard.
5. **Make a commitment (10 min).** Write one concrete, small commitment that shrinks
   that gap this week — specific enough that a friend could check it.
6. **Close (5 min).** Say why integrity is a *direction you keep choosing*, not a score
   you either have or lack.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Match one value to one action, and name the gap only for the easiest
  scenario.
- **Extension:** Write the commitment as an if-then plan ("If I face ___, then I will
  ___") and test it against a second scenario.

## Assessment

- **Formative (peer + self):** Can the learner name a value, the action that matches
  it, a real gap and its cost, and a concrete commitment?
- **Portfolio artifact (unit):** The value-action map with the commitment, kept in the
  capstone journal (feeds Lesson 10).

## Home connection

Share one value you hold with someone at home and ask them where they have seen you
live it — and where they have seen the gap. Write one sentence on what you heard.
