# Lesson 13 — Local Action, Global Solidarity

## Summary

Learners connect local action to global solidarity, and plan one real act of collective
responsibility for people and the Earth. They trace a **chain** — a local act, its
immediate effect, its connection to distant people and ecosystems, and its contribution to
a global movement — and study documented cases where local action scaled worldwide.

## Objectives

- Act on collective responsibility for people and the Earth, connecting local action to
  global solidarity. _(D02.S4.12.02)_

## Connection

No action stays where it happens. A tree planted on one street shades one block and,
multiplied across a movement, becomes millions of trees on another continent — Wangari
Maathai began with seedlings in a Nairobi backyard and the Green Belt Movement crossed
borders. A neighborhood that defends its water is part of a worldwide pattern: the people
who bear environmental harm are rarely the people who caused it, and the same fight is
fought in a thousand places. This lesson asks you to see your local act as one link in a
chain that reaches the whole Earth — and to plan it that way.

## Materials

- Local-to-global chain map
- Local-to-global case cards
- Paper and pencil (low-tech)
- Environmental justice and the commons page (enriched)

## Preparation

- Copy or draw the local-to-global chain map; prepare the case cards, kept factual.
- Retrieval: from Lesson 12, mutual aid and shared power; from Lesson 10, the commons and
  the common good. Today we scale the commons to the planet.
- Prepare plain definitions (model once: Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, S-011): **local
  action** is what I do where I am; **global solidarity** is acting with distant others
  across difference for a shared good. **Environmental justice** is the fair treatment of
  all people in environmental decisions — because burdens fall unevenly (Bullard, S-484).
  The **commons** are shared resources governed for the whole (Ostrom, S-416).

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **local action and global
solidarity are one chain, not two separate things — and collective responsibility means
planning the link, not just the act.** The cases are documented: the Green Belt Movement
began locally and planted over thirty million trees across Africa (S-059); environmental
burdens fall unevenly — the field's founding finding (Bullard, S-484); climate is a
planetary commons harmed by many local acts at once (IPCC, S-005). Hold "we should act"
as a value, distinct from these facts about how the world is connected.

The **ethics** lens: responsibility does not stop at the edge of my street — the chain is
the ethical fact, and ignoring the far end of it is a choice. The **egalitarianism** lens:
who causes harm and who bears it are often different people, in different places — the
environmental-justice fact (Bullard, S-484) — so solidarity must cross the gap. The
**global** lens: this is the global lens in action — one chain from a Nairobi backyard to
a continent (S-059), from a North Carolina community to a worldwide movement (Bullard,
S-484). The **environment** lens: the Earth is the largest commons, and every local act
is a vote in how it is kept or spent (IPCC, S-005; Ostrom, S-416). The **technology**
lens: digital networks are what let a local act *find* its global kin — a neighborhood
group can join a movement on three continents in an afternoon (Britannica, S-481) — the
tool that shortens the chain, for better and for worse. Preview: Lesson 14 gathers the
whole unit into the ethic you will carry forward.

**Safeguarding:** the action is small, real, and optional; no one is asked to take on
danger or to fix the world alone — one link is enough.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Lesson 12: what makes mutual aid different from charity? Today
   we scale that "we" to the planet.
2. **Meet the chain (10 min).** Work a model example (S-011): planting a tree in a Nairobi
   backyard (local act) shades a family (effect), and joined with millions of others
   (movement) rebuilds watersheds across Africa — the Green Belt Movement (S-059). Name
   the chain's links.
3. **Study local-to-global cases (10 min).** Read the cards: a community defending its
   water against a pipeline; a neighborhood mutual-aid network that others copied; a
   community that bore environmental harm and organized. Note what each case *connected*.
4. **Plan your local action (15 min).** On the chain map, plan one real, small act you can
   take where you are. Then fill each link: its immediate effect, its connection to
   distant people or ecosystems, and the global movement or pattern it joins.
5. **Check the far end (8 min).** Does your local act *harm* anyone or anything far away —
   a product you buy, a resource you use, a stream downstream? If so, revise one link to
   avoid it. Solidarity means not exporting the harm.
6. **Close (4 min).** Say (or write, sign, type, or gesture), in one sentence, how your
   local act is one link in a global chain — and which link you will actually take first.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Name one local act and one person or ecosystem far away it might touch, in
  one sentence.
- **Extension:** Write a short account of one documented local-to-global movement (the
  Green Belt Movement, a water defense, an environmental-justice struggle), naming each
  link of the chain and what made it scale.
- **Communication access:** Tracing and naming the chain is a written (or signed, typed,
  gestured, or AAC) task; the chain map is the answer. Learners who are non-speaking, d/Deaf,
  hard-of-hearing, or AAC users complete every step in their preferred mode.

## Assessment

- **Formative (peer + self):** Can the learner trace the local-to-global chain, plan one
  real local action, connect it to distant people/ecosystems, and check the far end for
  harm?
- **Portfolio artifact (unit):** The local-to-global chain map, in the care journal.

## Home connection

Choose one thing your household buys or uses, and trace its chain back — where it came
from and whom its making affected. Write one sentence on what you found.
