# Lesson 4 — The Listener I Want to Become

## Summary

Learners honestly audit their own listening and relationship habits and commit to one
concrete step of growth as a listener and partner. They learn active listening as a
learnable skill — **attend, paraphrase, check** — practice it in pairs, and write a
private when-then commitment that is small enough to keep.

## Objectives

- Reflect on one's own communication and relationship habits, and commit to one concrete
  step of growth as a listener and partner. _(D02.S1.12.02)_

## Connection

Listening is not a talent you are born with or without; it is a set of habits you can see
and change. Most of us, honestly, half-listen — we wait for our turn, we glance at a
screen, we rush to fix. Naming that truth, without shame, is where growth begins, because
a habit you can see is a habit you can change. This lesson asks you to look at your own
listening squarely and pick one small, real step to take — not to become a perfect
listener tomorrow, but to become a slightly better one by next week.

## Materials

- Listening self-audit
- Care journal
- Paper and pencil (low-tech)
- Active listening page (enriched)

## Preparation

- Copy or draw the listening self-audit.
- Retrieval: from Lessons 1–3, the four blocks of trust, the four moves of honest talk,
  and the three questions of repair. Today we turn the lens on *ourselves*.
- Prepare plain definitions (model once: Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, S-011): **active
   listening** is attending fully, **paraphrasing** (saying back what you heard — in
   speech, sign, writing, or text), and **checking** (asking if you got it right) — a
   learnable skill (CASEL, S-001). Listening is not only auditory: it can be visual
   (reading, watching posture and gesture) and text-based too.
   **Half-listening** is the familiar state of hearing while preparing to speak or
   attending to a device.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **listening is a
learnable habit, and honest self-observation is the engine of growth — not shame, but
"not yet."** Keep the audit private and judgment-free; a learner who cannot yet name a
habit is not failing, they are at the start of the spiral.

The **ethics** lens: listening well is a form of respect — it treats the other's inner
world as worth attending to. The **egalitarianism** lens: who gets truly listened to, and
who gets talked over or ignored, is uneven — by status, gender, age, and ability; better
listening is a quiet leveller. The **global** lens: cultures signal listening differently
— eye contact, silence, a nod, a repeated phrase, a sign, a written note — so "good
listening" is not one posture or one sense; it is attending in the way the speaker can
receive, in the mode that reaches them. The **technology** lens: the
device is the modern rival of attention — notifications and the pull to check split
attention in ways face-to-face conversation never had to fight (Suler, S-343; WHO notes
adolescent digital habits shape wellbeing, S-311). The **environment** lens: deep
listening is also a way of attending to the living world — noticing a bird's alarm call,
a stream's change, a season's shift — the same skill of attention turned outward.
Preview: Lesson 5 moves from intimate listening to empathy in ethical reasoning.

**Safeguarding:** the audit is private and not collected; learners share only what they
choose, and the when-then commitment may be about any relationship, including with
themselves or the living world.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Lessons 1–3, name the blocks of trust, the four moves of
   honest talk, and the three questions of repair. Today: what kind of *listener* holds
   all of that together?
2. **Audit yourself honestly (8 min).** Privately, fill in the listening self-audit:
   do I interrupt? rehearse my reply while they speak? check my phone? paraphrase back?
   ask before advising? No one else sees it.
3. **Meet active listening (10 min).** Work a model example first (S-011): a partner
   tells you a small frustration. Half-listening interrupts with advice ("You should
   just…"). Active listening attends, then paraphrases ("So it sounds like you felt
   dismissed when…"), then checks ("Did I get that right?"). The same three moves work
   without sound — read or watch a message, write or sign back what you understood, and
   check. Name the three moves in any mode.
4. **Practice in pairs (12 min).** One partner shares a small, ordinary story (no private
   disclosure) — told aloud, written, or signed. The other practices attend, paraphrase,
   check, then they swap. Work in the mode that fits you: speak and be heard, or exchange
   written/signed/text messages and paraphrase back in writing or sign. Notice what
   changed when you held off on advising.
5. **Name your next step (10 min).** In your care journal, write one habit you saw
   honestly, and one small **when-then** commitment: "When I feel the urge to interrupt,
   then I will pause and paraphrase first." Make it small enough to keep this week.
6. **Close (5 min).** Say (or write, sign, type, or gesture), in one sentence, the
   listener you want to become — and the one habit that is your next step.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Practice a single move (wait, then say back — in speech, sign, or
  writing) and write a one-line commitment.
- **Extension:** Notice *why* half-listening is so common — speed, distraction, the pull
  to advise — and design a counter-habit for one specific situation, then test it for a
  day and note the result.
- **Communication access:** Listening in this lesson is not only auditory. d/Deaf and
  hard-of-hearing learners attend visually (reading, watching posture and gesture) and
  paraphrase in writing, signing, typing, or AAC; non-speaking learners paraphrase in
  their preferred mode. No step requires hearing or producing sound — the audit, the
  when-then commitment, and the pair practice are all available in written or signed form.

## Assessment

- **Formative (self + peer):** Can the learner name the three moves, practice
  paraphrase-and-check in a pair (in speech, sign, writing, or text), and write one
  honest, concrete growth commitment?
- **Portfolio artifact (unit):** The private audit (kept by the learner) and the written
  when-then commitment, dated in the care journal.

## Home connection

This week, practice attend-paraphrase-check once with someone at home — in speech, sign,
writing, or text — without telling them you are practicing. Write one sentence on what
you noticed.
