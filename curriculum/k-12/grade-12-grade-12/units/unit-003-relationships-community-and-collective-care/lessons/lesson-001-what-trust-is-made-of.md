# Lesson 1 — What Trust Is Made Of

## Summary

Learners name what trust is actually made of — **honesty, reliability, repair, and
mutual respect** — and see it as something **built and rebuilt by repeated small acts**,
not a single promise. They work a model example, sort real moments by which block they
show, and write one example of their own without disclosing anything private.

## Objectives

- Analyze what sustains intimate, trusting relationships — honesty, repair, and mutual
  respect — and name the small repeated acts that build them. _(D02.S1.12.01)_

## Connection

Think of someone you trust enough to tell the truth to. You did not decide to trust
them in one dramatic moment; you built it, quietly, over many small moments — times they
kept a promise, owned a mistake, and treated you as your equal. The same is true in
reverse: you are trusted because of small repeated acts, not one grand declaration. This
unit begins with those small acts, because that is what intimate trust is actually made
of — and what lets it be repaired when it cracks.

## Materials

- Trust-building-blocks diagram
- Care journal
- Paper and pencil (low-tech)
- Trust in relationships page (enriched)

## Preparation

- Copy or draw the trust-building-blocks diagram.
- Retrieval: from Grade 11 (D02.S1.11.02), building and maintaining close, trusting
  relationships; from Unit 1 this year (D01.S1.12.01), how feeling shapes judgment.
  Today we name the *ingredients* of trust.
- Prepare plain definitions (model once: Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, S-011): **trust**
  is the confidence that another will act with your wellbeing in mind, built by repeated
  **honest** (truthful), **reliable** (keeps promises), and **respectful** (treats you as
  an equal) acts — and restored by **repair** after harm.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **trust is a structure
of repeated small acts — honesty, reliability, respect — that survives because it can be
repaired when it cracks.** This is a description of how trust works, not a demand that
learners trust more or trust specific people. Keep the lesson about the *pattern*, not
about any learner's private relationships.

The **ethics** lens: what we owe someone who trusts us, and what we owe when we break
trust, is the open question this lesson plants. The **egalitarianism** lens: respect —
treating the other as an equal — is the block that keeps trust from curdling into
control or dependence. The **global** lens: how trust is built and signaled differs
across cultures — a promise kept may be shown by a word, a gift, a shared meal, or
showing up; none is the default. The **technology** lens: digital life changes the
*evidence* of trust — a delayed reply, a read receipt, a message stripped of tone all
carry different weight than a face-to-face promise, so the same small acts read
differently online (Suler, S-343). The **environment** lens: we also build a kind of
trust with the living world — a garden that is tended, a river kept clean — a
relationship of reliability whose breakage (a dry season, a poisoned stream) we can also
feel as a betrayal. Preview: Lessons 2–4 take up honesty, repair, and growth as a
listener.

**Safeguarding:** no learner is asked to name or disclose a real, private relationship.
All examples are fictional or from observation/story, and learners may always write "a
character" instead of a person they know.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Grade 11: what makes a relationship close and trusting
   rather than merely friendly? Today we open the box and name the ingredients.
2. **Meet the four blocks (10 min).** Work a model example first (S-011): two friends,
   one breaks a promise to keep the other's secret. Name what kept the friendship
   standing: **honesty** (they finally told the truth), **reliability** (they usually kept
   promises), **repair** (they owned it and made it right), and **mutual respect** (they
   treated each other as equals, not as boss and follower).
3. **Sort real moments (12 min).** With a partner, sort small everyday moments — keeping
   a promise, admitting a lie, listening without interrupting, showing up on time,
   blaming someone else — by which block each one builds or chips away. Argue (aloud or
   in writing) the borderline ones.
4. **Argue the hardest block (8 min).** Which block is hardest to *rebuild* once it
   breaks — honesty, reliability, or respect? Choose one, argue (aloud or in writing),
   then agree on a place.
5. **Draw your own blocks (12 min).** In your care journal, draw the four blocks and
   write one small, real act under each — from a story, a film, or your own observation
   (no private names needed). Mark with a small crack the one that is hardest to repair.
6. **Close (4 min).** Say (or write, sign, type, or gesture), in one sentence, what
   trust is made of — and why a single promise is not enough to build it.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Name just two blocks and one small act for each, leaving repair and
  respect for later.
- **Extension:** Compare how one of the four blocks behaves in a face-to-face friendship
  versus an online one, and write two sentences on the difference.
- **Communication access:** Every step that asks a learner to *say* or *argue* can be
  completed in writing, signing, typing, AAC, or gesture — including pointing to a block
  or handing over a written note. Learners who are non-speaking, d/Deaf, or hard-of-hearing
  complete every task in their preferred mode.

## Assessment

- **Formative (peer + self):** Can the learner name the four blocks, sort moments with
  reasons, and write one real example of each without disclosing anything private?
- **Portfolio artifact (unit):** The four-block diagram with examples, opened in the care
  journal.

## Home connection

Ask someone at home what one small repeated act makes them trust a person — and what it
takes to earn back trust after it breaks. Write one sentence on each.
