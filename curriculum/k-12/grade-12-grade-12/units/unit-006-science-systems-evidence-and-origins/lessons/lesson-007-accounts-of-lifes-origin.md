# Lesson 7 — Accounts of Life's Origin and Diversity

## Summary

Learners get the scientific account of life's origin and diversity straight: **evolution by
natural selection** and **common ancestry** are strongly evidenced, while life's very first
origin from non-living chemistry remains an **open area of research**. They learn to hold
"what the evidence shows" and "what is still open" in the same frame — and to treat the
account as evidence-grounded, never creed.

## Objectives

- Hold scientific accounts of life's origin and diversity alongside cultural and spiritual
  accounts, and articulate how they relate. _(D06.S2.12.02)_

## Connection

Every living thing you have ever met — the dog, the mosquito, the wheat, the fungus on the
bread — shares a family story written in its body and in the rocks. Some parts of that story
are as well-evidenced as anything we know; other parts, especially the very first step, are
still open questions scientists are working on. Knowing which is which is what it means to
hold the account honestly, as evidence rather than as a story you must simply accept.

## Materials

- Origin-and-diversity timeline sheet
- Science journal
- Low-tech: a deep-time strip, scratch paper

## Preparation

- Copy or draw the timeline sheet; prepare a deep-time strip scaled so 4.5 billion years
  fits a meter (1 cm ≈ 45 million years).
- Retrieval: from Grade 11 (D06.S2.11.01), evidence for evolution and common ancestry. Today
  we separate the *diversity* account (well evidenced) from the *first origin* question
  (still open).
- Prepare the worked example (one line of evolution evidence) to model first (S-011).

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **the scientific account of
life has two parts with different status — diversity by evolution and common ancestry is
strongly evidenced; the first origin of life from non-living chemistry is an open research
question — and we name both honestly.** Teach the evidence explicitly, then let learners sort
settled from open (S-011). Be precise about scope: Darwin's *On the Origin of Species* is
about the origin of *diversity*, not the origin of the first life (S-303); the first origin
remains unresolved, with hypotheses such as an RNA world and with life's building blocks
found in meteorites (S-499). Origins are held as evidence-grounded accounts among many —
never as dogma (philosophy §11).

The **ethics** lens: honesty about what is open and what is settled is an ethical discipline —
overclaiming certainty about the first origin, or denying the well-evidenced diversity
account, are both failures of the same honesty. The **egalitarianism** lens: the evidence is
public — anyone can examine a fossil, a finch beak, or a genome; it does not require a title
to be weighed (S-305, S-304). The **global** lens: the evidence was gathered worldwide — the
Galápagos finches, English moths, and rocks of every continent — and the account is offered
alongside, not above, the many cultural accounts people hold (S-303, S-308). The
**technology** lens: DNA sequencing and radiometric dating are the instruments that made
common ancestry and deep time checkable in ways earlier centuries could not (S-308). The
**environment** lens: the account is environmental at its root — organisms are shaped by
their surroundings, which is why a changing climate reshapes the tree of life (S-303).
Preview: Lesson 8 places this account beside cultural and spiritual accounts and works out
how they relate.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Grade 11, name one piece of evidence for evolution. Today we
   separate what is settled from what is still open.
2. **Build deep time (10 min).** Mark a line from Earth's formation, about 4.5 billion years
   ago, to today (S-308). Place milestones: first simple life, then complex life, then the
   diversity we see now. Feel how *long* the line is before any life appears — deep time is
   the scale the story needs.
3. **Worked example — the diversity account (15 min).** Darwin proposed that species change
   by **natural selection**: individuals vary, some variations survive and reproduce more,
   and the population shifts (S-303). Evidence: the beaks of Darwin's finches changed shape
   from season to season as food changed (S-305); the peppered moth shifted from light to
   dark as industrial soot darkened trees (S-304); and shared features and DNA point to
   **common ancestry**. Write this as "what the evidence shows."
4. **Name the open part (8 min).** The *first* origin — how non-living chemistry became the
   first living thing (**abiogenesis**) — is not yet settled. Building blocks of life
   (amino acids, nucleobases) are found in meteorites and comets, and hypotheses like an RNA
   world are tested, but the exact path remains unknown (S-499). Write this under "still an
   open question."
5. **Sort the account (12 min).** With a partner, make a two-column list: *what the evidence
   shows* versus *still open*. Say — or write, sign, gesture, or use AAC to show — one entry
   in each column and why it belongs there.
6. **Close (2 min).** Say — or write, sign, gesture, or use AAC to express — why a strong
   account can still contain an open question, and why naming both is honesty.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Sort pre-written cards into the two columns rather than generating entries
   from scratch.
- **Extension:** State one prediction the common-ancestry account makes (e.g., where a
   transitional fossil or a shared gene should be found) and how it could be tested.
- **Number access (dyscalculia):** Deep time is felt, not computed — use the paper strip and
   relative positions ("long before," "later") rather than years; provide the two-column
   list as a pre-printed organizer, and allow every entry to be placed and explained in
   words or by pointing, with any dates read aloud or offloaded to a partner.
- **Communication access:** Every spoken step — saying, naming, reading aloud, discussing,
   or closing — can be done in writing, sign, gesture, or AAC instead. Learners who are
   non-speaking, d/Deaf, hard-of-hearing, or who use AAC complete every task in their
   preferred mode; no step requires producing or hearing sound.

## Assessment

- **Formative (peer + self):** Can the learner place the diversity account and the first-
  origin question correctly on the settled-versus-open columns, and explain one line of
  evidence for each?
- **Portfolio artifact (unit):** The two-column list with the deep-time milestones, opened in
  the science journal.

## Home connection

Look at two living things at home — a pet, a plant, an insect. Write one sentence on what
they share that hints at a common story, and one sentence naming something about life's
beginning that is still unknown.
