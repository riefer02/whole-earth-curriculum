# Lesson 14 — A Considered Position on Origins

## Summary

The unit's capstone: learners articulate their **own considered position** on the origins of
the cosmos and of life, in four honest parts — **what the evidence supports, what other
accounts offer, where I stand and why, and what remains open**. They review the unit's
artifacts, draft and refine the position, and share only what they choose — holding evidence
and other ways of knowing in respectful relation, never as creed.

## Objectives

- Articulate a considered position on the origins of the cosmos and life that holds
  scientific evidence and other ways of knowing in respectful relation. _(D06.S4.12.02)_

## Connection

You have spent this unit weighing explanations, tracing who asks and who benefits, mapping
coupled systems, holding science and culture side by side, and reading the forces, the
particles, and the night sky. Now the question turns to *you*: given all of it — what is your
position? Not a final answer to be defended forever, but a considered position you can state
honestly today, knowing it may grow. That is what a free, thinking person does with the
oldest question there is.

## Materials

- Position-statement frame
- Science journal
- Low-tech: the unit's artifacts laid out, scratch paper

## Preparation

- Copy or draw the position-statement frame.
- Retrieval: the whole unit — the weigh (L1–L2), the ethics of science (L3–L4), coupled
  systems and trade-offs (L5–L6), accounts of life and culture side by side (L7–L8), forces
  and particles (L9–L10), technology's promise and risk (L11–L12), and cosmological and
  geological evidence (L13). Today we compose from all of it.
- Prepare the four-part frame to model first (S-011).

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **a considered position on
origins has four honest parts — what the evidence supports, what other accounts offer, where
I stand and why, and what remains open — and it holds evidence and other ways of knowing in
respectful relation, never as creed.** Model the frame, then let learners compose (S-011).
This lesson is emotionally and culturally delicate: every learner's tradition is to be held
with dignity; no position is imposed, ranked, or mocked (philosophy §11). Sharing is always
optional; use written or anonymous sharing if speaking up is not safe for some learners
(contexts, cultural power dynamics). Distinguish evidence from values one last time: *what
the evidence supports* is a factual claim; *where I stand and why* is a value, and each has
its own kind of honesty.

The **ethics** lens: the position is an ethical act — it refuses both dogmatism (pretending a
model is certain truth) and contempt (pretending another's account is worthless), and chooses
honesty and respect instead. The **egalitarianism** lens: every learner's position, from
every tradition and no tradition, stands on equal footing — the exercise is not to find the
one right answer but to let each person speak their considered mind with dignity (S-202,
S-203). The **global** lens: origins have been held by every people, in every place — and a
considered position honors that the scientific account is one among many, standing beside,
not above (S-002, S-427). The **technology** lens: our knowledge of origins is mediated by
instruments — telescopes, dating methods, sequencers — which is why the position names
*what the evidence supports* as distinct from *what I believe*. The **environment** lens: to
hold a position on origins is also to hold a position on the living world that is here now —
awe at the cosmos is incomplete without care for the planet it produced (S-361, S-437).
Preview: the position closes the unit and opens the lifelong question.

## Procedure

1. **Gather the unit (10 min).** Lay out your artifacts — the weigh sheets, the traces, the
   proposals, the coupling diagram, the side-by-side chart, the forces table, the models-and-
   evidence sheet. Re-read your own words. What did *you* conclude at each step?
2. **Meet the four parts (8 min).** The position has four parts: **(1) what the evidence
   supports** (the facts, e.g., the Big Bang and evolution are well-evidenced, S-002, S-303);
   **(2) what other accounts offer** (meaning, duty, belonging — what the stories carry,
   S-202, S-203, S-427); **(3) where I stand and why** (your value, named openly); and
   **(4) what remains open** (the first origin of life, the nature of what came before —
   S-499). Write the four as headings.
3. **Model the frame (10 min).** Read a model position aloud — or follow one in writing:
   "The evidence supports an expanding universe and life's shared ancestry. The stories of my
   people offer meaning and duty. I stand with both, because they answer different questions.
   I leave open how the first life began, and I remain in wonder." Notice: no part is
   dogma, and no part is mocked.
4. **Compose your position (20 min).** Write your own four-part position. Say — or write,
   sign, gesture, or use AAC to show — each part, especially what you leave open. You may
   share it or keep it private; both are complete.
5. **Refine with a peer (5 min).** If you choose, exchange with a partner and check one
   thing: *is the evidence part actually evidence, and the value part honestly labeled as
   value?* Give one specific, respectful improvement.
6. **Close (2 min).** Say — or write, sign, gesture, or use AAC to express — one sentence of
   your position, and one thing you leave open.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Complete only parts 1 and 4 ("what the evidence supports" and "what remains
   open") and answer part 3 aloud or in a single sentence.
- **Extension:** State the limit of your position — the point at which the evidence stops and
   your belief begins — and write how your position would change if new evidence appeared.
- **Number access (dyscalculia):** The position is prose, not arithmetic; provide the
   four-part frame as a pre-printed scaffold with sentence starters, and allow every part to
   be spoken, signed, or recorded rather than written, with no numbers required.
- **Communication access:** Every spoken step — saying, naming, reading aloud, discussing,
   or closing — can be done in writing, sign, gesture, or AAC instead. Learners who are
   non-speaking, d/Deaf, hard-of-hearing, or who use AAC complete every task in their
   preferred mode; no step requires producing or hearing sound.

## Assessment

- **Formative (self + peer, optional sharing):** Can the learner state a four-part position
  that separates what the evidence supports from what they value, names what other accounts
  offer, and leaves the open questions open — with dignity for all accounts?
- **Portfolio artifact (unit capstone):** The four-part position statement, added to the
  science journal as the unit's closing artifact.

## Home connection

If you wish, read your position to someone at home, and ask them what their own four parts
would be. Listen without arguing, and write one sentence about where you differ and one about
where you agree.
