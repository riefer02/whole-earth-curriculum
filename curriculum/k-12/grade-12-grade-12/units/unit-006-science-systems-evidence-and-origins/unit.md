# Unit 6 — Science: Systems, Evidence & Our Origins

## Overview

This unit is the culminating pass through Grade 12 science, carrying Grade 11's
discipline of investigation into the discipline of weighing competing explanations and
the ethics of knowledge itself. Learners evaluate competing scientific explanations of
the same phenomenon using criteria of evidence, coherence, and falsifiability, and
reflect on the ethics of scientific practice — who asks the questions, who benefits,
and how knowledge is used — proposing a more just practice. They explain how
ecosystems, economies, and human health are coupled and weigh trade-offs in sustaining
living systems, and hold scientific accounts of life's origin and diversity alongside
cultural and spiritual accounts, articulating how they relate. They explain the
fundamental forces and particles that make up matter and how they unify physical
explanations, and weigh the promise and risk of physical technologies — energy,
materials, computing — for a just and sustainable future. They weigh cosmological
models and geological evidence and the relationship between scientific and other ways
of knowing about origins, and articulate a considered position on the origins of the
cosmos and life that holds scientific evidence and other ways of knowing in respectful
relation. Origins are held as evidence-grounded accounts among many others — never as
dogma.

## Essential questions

- How do I evaluate competing scientific explanations, and reflect on the ethics of science — who asks the questions, who benefits, and how knowledge is used?
- How are ecosystems, economies, and human health coupled, and how do I hold scientific and cultural accounts of life side by side?
- What are the fundamental forces and particles of matter, and how do I weigh cosmological and geological evidence — and my own considered position — on origins?
