# Grade 5 Scope

## How to read this scope

Each `unit_id` in the frontmatter resolves to a unit file under `units/`.
`start_day`/`end_day` map the unit onto the 180-day school year; the list is a
continuous sequence from day 1 to day 180 with no gaps or overlaps. `domain` and
`pillar` name the primary domain(s) and pillar(s) each unit serves. `domain_weighting`
expresses the intended balance of instructional time across the ten domains, in
percent of the year. `lesson_count` counts the core lessons; the days between lessons
carry flex time, assessment, integration, and celebration. `assessment_plan`
describes how a child's growth is seen and recorded at this level.

## Year at a glance

The Grade 5 year is a spiral that deepens what Grade 4 began, and it opens with the
question at the heart of every science: how do we know? *How Do We Know?*
(U.05.001, D06) sets the year's through-line. Where Grade 4 measured and repeated
trials, learners now design the test and separate evidence from inference from opinion —
a distinction that echoes through reading, history, media, and the arts. The year then
turns inward. *My Feelings, My Values & My Growing Self* (U.05.002, D01) asks learners
to name their values and live by one, and *My Body, My Movement & My Growth*
(U.05.003, D03) reads the body with new precision — the mind–body connection, and how
bodies grow at different rates, every pace normal.

*Teams, Empathy & Belonging* (U.05.004, D02) turns outward into the practice of care:
giving and receiving feedback kindly, taking another culture's perspective, and dividing
resources so everyone has enough. The heart of the year belongs to the two great tools
of school, now working with real independence. *Reading, Writing, Research & Many
Languages* (U.05.005, D04) grows comprehension and research: presenting a clear main
idea, explaining how an author uses structure, telling fact from opinion, and gathering
several sources into a short report in one's own words. Many writing systems are
recognized, and multilingualism is held as wealth. *Decimals, Fractions, Shapes & Data*
(U.05.006, D05) reaches decimals to the thousandths and works with fractions and
decimals in real contexts, graphing relationships on a coordinate plane — mathematics as
sense-making with real things, for everyone.

From there the year looks out at the world. *Food Webs, Forces, Earth & Sky*
(U.05.007, D06) deepens the science the year opened with — matter and energy moving
through a food web, traits passing to offspring, gravity and magnetism as forces at a
distance, the Earth–Moon–Sun system, and slow and fast processes shaping Earth's
surface. *Making, Music & Movement* (U.05.008, D07) gives craft to the imagination — an
artwork with a clear idea, a composition with a beginning, middle, and end, and a design
that solves a real problem — meeting other cultures' arts as living traditions.
*Healthy, Safe & Strong* (U.05.009, D03) returns to the body: how nutrients support it,
how to respond in an emergency, and consent for one's own body and others' boundaries.

The final third steps into the wider human story, told in many voices. *People, Cultures
& Long Ago* (U.05.010, D08) compares two civilizations on a timeline, shows how a
technology changed daily life, compares origin stories from different peoples, meets an
achievement of a non-European civilization, and traces a migration's push and pull.
*Rights, Fairness & Shared Decisions* (U.05.011, D09) explains what a right is and where
rights such as the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child come from, and plans a small
collective action. *Tools, Media & Our Earth Home* (U.05.012, D10) asks how a technology
has changed daily life, how to check a claim against more than one source, and how to
use a resource carefully so it lasts. The year closes by celebrating each whole person,
the caring community, and the living Earth, and looking ahead to Grade 6.

Throughout, the four pillars are interwoven. Intellectual awareness (P3) carries the
most weight — fact-and-opinion reading, research writing, decimals and fractions, and
designed tests are the year's central work — but emotional and social awareness (P1) and
physical and somatic awareness (P2) remain strong, for a known, cared-for, and capable
body-and-self is the ground all formal learning stands on. Contextual and ecological
awareness (P4) keeps growing: history toward comparing civilizations, civics toward
rights and collective action, and ecology toward food webs and stewardship. No
high-stakes tests anywhere: growth is seen through reading, writing, number work with
real things, designed fair tests, and a portfolio of what they make, say, and do.
