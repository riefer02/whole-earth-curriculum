# Lesson 7 — Across Land and Sea: Trade Connected Peoples

## Summary

Learners explore how trade and travel connected peoples across the **Indian Ocean**, riding
the **monsoon** winds in **dhows** with lateen sails. They sort what moved along the route
into four kinds — **goods, ideas, plants, and animals** — and see that trade carried far more
than things: it carried ways of thinking, ways of farming, and ways of life, between peoples
who met as equals.

## Objectives

- Explain how trade and travel connected peoples across land and sea and what moved with
  them, such as goods, ideas, plants, and animals. _(D08.S3.05.02)_

## Connection

Think of the last thing you ate or wore that came from somewhere else — a spice, a fruit, a
cloth. It did not grow in your pocket; it traveled. Long ago, before planes and trucks,
people still moved things across oceans and deserts — and with the goods came **ideas**,
**plants**, and **animals**. A single sailing route could carry a religion, a new crop, and a
new way of counting, all at once. Today you see how much travels with trade.

## Materials

- Indian Ocean trade map
- What-moved sorting cards
- What-moved chart
- Pencils

## Preparation

- Print or draw the Indian Ocean trade map with the coasts connected by monsoon routes.
- Copy and cut the what-moved sorting cards, and copy a what-moved chart for each learner.
- Arrange four corners or circles for the sorting activity.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land is that **trade connected
peoples as equals and moved goods, ideas, plants, and animals — not only things.** Frame the
Indian Ocean network, the Silk Roads, and the Trans-Saharan gold-salt trade as networks of
**mutual exchange between peoples who met as trading partners**, never as a story of one
people "discovering" others. Model the four-category sort aloud (guided practice). Keep the
technology lens honest: the monsoon winds and the dhow's lateen sail are real technologies
that made the route possible. See `docs/philosophy.md` §7 and `docs/facilitation.md`.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** You have traced a migration across the sea. Today you meet the *other*
   reason people crossed oceans and deserts: **trade**.
2. **Meet the monsoon route (15 min).** Look at the map. Sailors in **dhows** — boats with a
   triangular **lateen sail** — rode the **monsoon winds**, which blow one way for part of the
   year and the other way for the rest. This connected the coasts of **East Africa, Arabia,
   India, Southeast Asia, and China**. People learned the winds' rhythm, and for centuries
   ships moved back and forth, carrying goods and much more.
3. **Sort what moved (20 min).** Take the cards and sort each into one of four columns:
   **goods** (spices, cloth, timber, gold); **ideas** (religions, ways of counting, sailing
   skills); **plants** (coconuts, bananas, rice); **animals** (cattle, horses). Check with a
   partner: does each card fit its column, and could some fit two? Notice how much is *not* a
   thing you can hold.
4. **See the meeting (10 min).** With a partner, talk about what happened when these peoples
   met: they traded, but they also **learned from each other** — new numbers, new faiths, new
   crops. No one was "behind" anyone; each people brought something the others valued.
5. **Close (5 min).** Trade is exchange between equals. What moved was not only goods but
   ideas, plants, and animals — the whole life of peoples, flowing across land and sea.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide picture cards for the sort and a four-column chart with images; scribe
  the learner's sentences.
- **Extension:** Compare a sea route (the Indian Ocean) with a land route (the Silk Roads) or a
  desert route (the Trans-Saharan gold-salt trade) and name what each moved.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner sort what moved into goods, ideas, plants, and
  animals, and explain that trade carried more than goods?
- **Self-check:** The learner asks, "Did I sort the items correctly, and did I name at least
  one idea, one plant, and one animal that moved — not only goods?"

## Home connection

Look at one food or object in your home that came from another place, and ask someone at home
how it got here — then name whether it is a good, an idea, a plant, or an animal.

## Resources

- The Indian Ocean trade network, driven by the monsoon winds and sailed by dhows with lateen
  sails, connected East Africa, Arabia, India, Southeast Asia, and China for centuries and
  moved goods, ideas (including religions and number systems), plants, and animals — a widely
  documented pattern (UNESCO, "The Indian Ocean, a shared heritage"; and historical work on
  the dhow and monsoon sailing). The Silk Roads (overland) and the Trans-Saharan gold-salt
  trade (desert, by camel) are parallel networks of exchange. These are told as networks of
  mutual exchange between peoples, in line with the curriculum's global and non-colonial
  framing.
