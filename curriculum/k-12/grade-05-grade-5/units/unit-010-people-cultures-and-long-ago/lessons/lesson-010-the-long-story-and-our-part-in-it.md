# Lesson 10 — The Long Story, and Our Part in It

## Summary

The capstone gathers the unit's threads into **one long human story**. Learners assemble
their portfolio artifacts — the comparing timeline, the origin-story comparison, the
migration note, the achievement page, and the fairness-and-dignity example — and, in a
gallery walk and closing circle, connect each to the big question and name **their own part**
in the unfinished story of fairness and dignity.

## Objectives

- Explain what fairness and dignity mean using a concrete example of when they were honored or
  denied. _(D08.S4.05.02)_
- Revisit the unit's threads — timelines, tools, origin stories, migration, and trade — and
  connect them into one shared story. _(D08.S1.05.01, D08.S2.05.01, D08.S3.05.01)_

## Connection

A story is not one page but a whole book — and the human story is the longest book of all. You
have been collecting its pages all unit: a timeline of two rivers, a tool that changed life, a
story of beginnings, a great achievement, a voyage, a trade route, and people who stood up for
fairness. Today you bind those pages together, walk through them like a museum, and find where
**your** page fits — because the story is still being written, and you are part of it.

## Materials

- Portfolio pages (the learner's own artifacts)
- Long-story web poster
- Capstone card
- Pencils and art supplies

## Preparation

- Have each learner gather their own artifacts from this unit (timeline, origin-story
  comparison, migration note, achievement page, fairness-and-dignity example).
- Print or draw the long-story web poster.
- Copy a capstone card for each learner, and arrange a gallery wall or story path.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). This is the unit's **capstone and summative
assessment**, and also a moment of **retrieval and synthesis** — learners recall the whole unit
and connect it, rather than re-reading it. Keep the tone celebratory and collective: success is
shared, mastery is individual, and no one is ranked. The closing should land on **hope and
agency** — the learner's own part in the unfinished story. If a learner's artifacts are
incomplete, let them finish one now rather than be judged "done" or "not done." This lesson
completes the unit's portfolio. See `docs/facilitation.md` on assessing without ranking.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Look back with a partner: **what did we learn this unit?** Take turns
   recalling one thing each — a timeline, a tool, a story, an achievement, a voyage, a trade
   route, a stand for fairness. This is retrieval: pulling it out of memory, not re-reading it.
2. **Assemble your pages (15 min).** Lay out your own artifacts in order: your two-civilization
   timeline, your origin-story comparison, your migration note, your achievement page, and your
   fairness-and-dignity example. Check each is complete; finish any that need it.
3. **Walk the museum (20 min).** Display the artifacts and walk the gallery. At each page,
   notice what it shows about the long human story. Then, on your capstone card, answer three
   prompts: **Choose one artifact** you are proud of. **Say how it connects** to the big
   question — how peoples lived, knew, moved, traded, and stood for fairness. **Name one thing
   you can do** to honor fairness and dignity in your own life.
4. **Close the circle (15 min).** Gather. Each learner shares their chosen artifact and their
   one action for fairness — one sentence is enough. Listen to how many different pages make up
   this one story.
5. **Look forward (5 min).** The story is not finished. You are not only reading the human
   story — you are writing the next line of it.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Choose one artifact with the learner and scribe their capstone card; celebrate
  completion of a single page rather than all five.
- **Extension:** Write a one-paragraph synthesis linking two or more threads (time, story,
  movement, trade, fairness) with an example from the unit and one from your own life.

## Assessment

- **Summative (portfolio):** The learner's assembled artifacts — the comparing timeline, the
  origin-story comparison, the migration note, the achievement page, and the
  fairness-and-dignity example — together show mastery of the unit's standards.
- **Peer/self:** In the circle, learners listen and each names one connection they heard and one
  action they can take for fairness.
- **Self-check:** The learner asks, "Did I connect my artifact to the big question, and did I
  name a real thing I can do to honor fairness and dignity?"

## Home connection

Share your whole portfolio with someone at home — walk them through your pages like a museum —
and tell them the one action for fairness you chose.

  the story" follows the curriculum's aim that learners become agents of care and justice, not
  only readers of the past.
