# Lesson 8 — Standing Up: People Who Resisted Unfairness

## Summary

Learners meet three historical examples of people treated unfairly who **resisted peacefully
and helped one another**: the **1930 Salt March** in India, the **1929 Aba Women's War** in
southeastern Nigeria, and the **1966 Wave Hill walk-off** by Gurindji stockworkers in
Australia. They record one story with an **unfairness–action–mutual aid** frame, centering
the people's **dignity, agency, and courage** — never victimhood.

## Objectives

- Describe a historical example of people treated unfairly, such as workers or people denied
  their rights, who resisted or helped one another. _(D08.S4.05.01)_

## Connection

Think of a time a rule was unfair — everyone knew it, but one person spoke up, and others
joined them, and together they changed it. That is what people have done in every time and
place. Being treated unfairly does not make a person weak; resisting it — peacefully, and
helping one another while doing so — is one of the bravest things humans do. Today you meet
three groups of people who did exactly that.

## Materials

- Resistance story cards (Salt March, Aba Women's War, Wave Hill walk-off)
- Standing-up chart
- Pencils

## Preparation

- Copy the three resistance story cards, written through the people's courage and agency.
- Copy a standing-up chart for each learner.
- Review the note below on holding difficult topics with dignity and hope.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). This is a **difficult topic, held with dignity
and hope** — never victimhood. The point is not to dwell on suffering but on **agency**: real
people saw unfairness, organized, helped one another, and acted. Tell each story with the
people as the heroes of their own history. Name the unfairness briefly and factually (an
unjust tax, an unjust rule, unfair pay and land) and spend most of the time on **what they
did** and **how they helped one another**. Connect difficulty to hope and to something the
learner can do (speak up, support, be fair). Respect a learner's right to pause. This lesson
produces the unit's **resistance example** portfolio artifact. See `docs/facilitation.md` on
holding difficult topics safely.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** You have seen how people moved and traded. Today you meet something
   harder: what people did when they were treated **unfairly** — and how they stood up,
   together, with dignity.
2. **Meet three stories (20 min).** Read the cards. *The Salt March (India, 1930):* a law said
   people had to buy salt only from the government, and taxed it — even though salt could be
   gathered free from the sea. Mohandas Gandhi and others walked for weeks to the coast and
   made their own salt, peacefully breaking an unjust law, with thousands joining and helping
   one another along the way. *The Aba Women's War (southeastern Nigeria, 1929):* women
   organized against an unfair tax and unjust rule, gathering in the thousands, speaking out,
   and supporting each other until the rule was changed. *The Wave Hill walk-off (Australia,
   1966):* Gurindji stockworkers, led by Vincent Lingiari, walked off a cattle station to
   demand fair pay and their own land, and held on together for years until their land was
   returned.
3. **Record one story (15 min).** Choose one story. On your chart, write: **What was the
   unfairness?** **What did the people do?** **How did they help one another?** Notice that in
   every story the people did not give up — they organized, acted, and held each other up.
4. **Find the courage (5 min).** With a partner, name one thing all three stories share: the
   people acted **peacefully**, they acted **together**, and they kept their **dignity**.
5. **Close (5 min).** Being treated unfairly is not the end of a story. People resist, help
   one another, and change things — and that is part of the human story too.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use one story at a time with a fill-in chart and picture prompts; scribe the
  learner's words and keep the tone calm and hopeful.
- **Extension:** Compare two stories — the unfairness, the action, and how the two acts of
  courage resemble each other across different places and times.

## Assessment

- **Formative (portfolio/observation):** Can the learner name the unfairness, what the people
  did, and how they helped one another, in one story — framing the people with dignity and
  agency?
- **Self-check:** The learner asks, "Did I center the people's courage and helping, not only
  the unfairness — and did I hold the story with respect?"

## Home connection

Ask someone at home about a time people stood up against something unfair and helped one
another — in your community or family, or in a story they know.

## Resources

- The 1930 Salt March, in which Mohandas Gandhi led a nonviolent march to the sea at Dandi to
  make salt in defiance of the salt tax, is documented by the Nobel Prize site and historical
  scholarship (<https://www.nobelprize.org/>). The 1929 Aba Women's War, in which thousands of
  women in southeastern Nigeria organized against unfair taxation and unjust rule, is
  documented in Nigerian history (see the work of historians such as Adiele Afigbo and
  records of the time). The 1966 Wave Hill walk-off, led by Vincent Lingiari with Gurindji
  stockworkers seeking fair pay and land, is documented by the National Museum of Australia
  (<https://www.nma.gov.au/>) and led to the return of land. These are told as stories of
  dignity, agency, and mutual aid, in line with the curriculum's commitment to center
  resistance and liberation, not victimhood.
