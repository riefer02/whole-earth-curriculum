# Lesson 2 — Protect and Regulate: Vitamins, Minerals, and Water

## Summary

Learners meet the nutrients that **protect** and **regulate** the body: **vitamins** (a shield
that protects against sickness and helps healing), **minerals** (calcium that builds bones and
teeth, iron that carries oxygen in the blood), and **water** (the river that carries everything
and keeps the body cool). They match foods from many regions to these jobs and record what each
nutrient does.

## Objectives

- Explain how nutrients such as vitamins, minerals, and water support different parts of the
  body. _(D03.S3.05.01)_

## Connection

A village has more than builders and cooks — it has **guards** who protect it, **repair
workers** who keep the walls and roads strong, and a **river** that carries water everywhere it
is needed. Your body works the same way. **Vitamins** are the guards: they protect you from
sickness and help cuts and scrapes heal. **Minerals** are the repair workers: **calcium** keeps
your bones and teeth strong, and **iron** carries oxygen through your blood so your whole body
can breathe. **Water** is the river: it carries nutrients to every cell, washes away waste, and
keeps you cool when you sweat. From the mango and leafy greens to the sesame and beans to the
cup of water — these are your body's protectors and regulators.

## Materials

- Protect and regulate poster
- Protect–build–carry page
- Pencils and crayons

## Preparation

- Print or draw the protect and regulate poster (shield for vitamins, bone and blood cell for
  minerals, river/water drop for water).
- Copy a protect–build–carry page for each learner.
- Recall Lesson 1: carbohydrates fuel, proteins build, fats store and build — today's nutrients
  protect and regulate.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land is that **vitamins protect,
minerals build and regulate, and water carries** — three more jobs, all needed. Keep it concrete
with a shield, a builder, and a river. Use food examples from many regions (citrus, guava, mango,
leafy greens, tomatoes, sesame, beans, lentils, fish, dairy or its plant alternatives) and honor
every way of eating. Water is available differently in different places; frame access to clean
water as a shared fairness issue, never a family's failing. See `docs/facilitation.md`.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Last time you met the nutrients that **fuel and build** your body. Today,
   the nutrients that **protect and regulate** it: **vitamins, minerals, and water**.
2. **Meet the three helpers (10 min).** Look at the poster. **Vitamins** are a **shield**: they
   protect you from sickness and help wounds heal — vitamin C in citrus and guava, vitamin A in
   mango, sweet potato, and dark leafy greens. **Minerals** build and regulate: **calcium**
   builds strong bones and teeth (in leafy greens, sesame, dairy, and fortified foods), and
   **iron** carries oxygen in your blood (in beans, lentils, and fish). **Water** is the river:
   your body is more than half water, and water carries nutrients in, waste out, and keeps you
   cool.
3. **Act the jobs (10 min).** In a group, act out the three jobs. One of you is a **shield**
   (arms up, guarding), one is a **builder** (stacking motions for bones), one is a **river**
   (flowing arms, carrying). Say each job aloud: protect, build, carry.
4. **Record on your page (15 min).** Draw or write one **vitamin** and what it protects, one
   **mineral** and what it builds or carries, and **water** and what it does. Use real foods
   you eat or know.
5. **Close (5 min).** Fuel and build (Lesson 1), protect and regulate (today) — now you know all
   six nutrients and the jobs they do. Next time: what happens when the body gets too little of
   one of them, over time.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a picture bank of foods and sentence starters ("Vitamin C helps my body
  ___"), and scribe as needed.
- **Extension:** Name a specific vitamin (A, C, D) or mineral (calcium, iron, iodine) and the
  exact body part it supports, and one food rich in it.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name one vitamin, one mineral, and water, and say
  what each does (protect, build/regulate, carry)?
- **Self-check:** The learner checks that their page matches each nutrient to its real job and
  uses at least one real food.

## Home connection

Find one fruit or leafy green, one bean or seed, and a glass of water at home, and tell someone
what each does for the body.

## Resources

- Vitamins and minerals each have specific jobs — vitamin A for vision and immunity, vitamin C
  for healing, calcium for bones, iron for carrying oxygen — and water makes up a large share of
  the body and carries nutrients and waste. See the World Health Organization's micronutrient
  fact sheets (<https://www.who.int/health-topics/micronutrients>) and its guidance on water as
  essential to health. These describe what nutrients do — never a judgment about any body or
  way of eating.
