# Lesson 7 — My Body, My Consent

## Summary

Learners learn that **their body belongs to them** and that **consent** is permission they give
or do not give for someone to touch them — a hug, a handshake, a high-five, a pat. They practice
saying **"no"** and **"stop"** with a firm voice, learn to **ask before touching** someone, and
name a **trusted adult** to tell. The lesson is warm, age-appropriate, and free of any sexual
content.

## Objectives

- Explain the idea of consent for one's own body. _(D03.S4.05.02)_

## Connection

Your body is yours. You get to decide who touches you and how — a hug, a handshake, a pat on the
head, a high-five — and you can say **yes** or **no** to any of them. **Consent** is that
permission: it is given or not given, and only *you* can give it for your own body. "**No means
no**" and "**stop means stop**" — when you say them, they must be respected, right away. And it
is **always okay** to say no, even to someone you love, even to a grown-up, even to a hug that
is meant kindly. If anyone touches you in a way that feels wrong, or keeps going after you say
stop, tell a **trusted adult** — always, and it is never your fault.

## Materials

- Consent poster
- My body, my choice page
- Pencils and crayons

## Preparation

- Print or draw the consent poster (a child with a personal-space bubble, a "stop" hand, and the
  phrases "ask first," "no means no," "stop means stop," "always okay to say no").
- Copy a my-body-my-choice page for each learner.
- Prepare a calm, private-feeling space; this lesson can raise feelings, so keep the tone warm
  and unhurried.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land is that **a child's body is
their own, "no" and "stop" must always be respected, and a trusted adult is there to help**. It
is developmentally appropriate and **contains no sexual content**: we talk only about everyday
touch (hugs, handshakes, high-fives, pats) and personal space. Do **not** introduce sexual
topics at this grade. Keep it empowering, never frightening: the child's right to say no, and to
get help, is the whole point. Reassure learners that if something ever feels wrong, telling a
trusted adult is always the right thing and never their fault. Some learners may disclose
something difficult — respond calmly, thank them for telling you, and follow your local
child-protection/safeguarding procedure. See `docs/facilitation.md` on holding difficult topics.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** You have learned to keep your body well and to call for help in an
   emergency. Today, a different kind of safety: the rule that **your body is yours**.
2. **Meet consent (10 min).** Look at the poster. **Consent** means permission. For your own
   body, only *you* give it. A hug, a handshake, a high-five, a pat on the head — you can say
   **yes** or **no** to each. The words are simple: "**No means no**" and "**stop means stop**."
   When you say them, they must be respected **right away**. And it is **always okay** to say no
   — even to someone you love, even to a grown-up.
3. **Practice the voice — with words or without (10 min).** Say it out loud with a firm, clear
   voice: **"No."** **"Stop, please."** **"No, thank you."** Now say a friendly yes: **"Yes, a
   high-five!"** A learner who does not speak can say the same thing without words: a **raised
   "stop" hand** (as on the poster), a head shake, a "stop"/"no" card, or a sign — any clear
   signal is a real "no," and any clear "yes" (a nod, a thumbs-up) works too. Notice: a yes can
   change to a no at any time, and that is always allowed. Practice the bubble too — stretch your
   arms out, or trace the bubble small with your hands from a chair, to feel your **personal
   space**, the space around you that belongs to you.
4. **Ask before you touch (10 min).** Consent goes both ways — before *you* touch someone, even
   for a hug, you **ask first**: "May I hug you?" And if they say no, you say **"Okay!"** and
   that is the end of it, with no pouting and no pushing.
5. **Name a trusted adult (5 min).** On your page, finish these: *"It is always okay to say no
   to ___." "Before I touch someone, I ___." "A trusted adult I can tell is ___."* That adult is
   someone who will listen and help, no matter what.
6. **Close (5 min).** Your body is yours, and everyone else's is theirs. No means no, stop means
   stop, ask first, and always tell a trusted adult. Next time, you will practice the other side:
   **respecting** everyone else's boundaries.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a word bank (hug, handshake, high-five, pat) and a picture of a trusted
  adult; practice the voice together as a group.
- **Accessibility:** "No" and "stop" may be expressed without speech — a raised stop hand, a head
  shake, a card, or a sign — and "yes" with a nod, thumbs-up, or card. The personal-space bubble
  can be practiced seated. Partners honor any clear signal as a full answer.
- **Extension:** Role-play a full ask-and-answer ("May I hug you?" → "No, thank you" → "Okay!")
  and write one sentence about why a "no" must always be accepted, never argued with.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner state that their body is their own, say "no" and
  "stop" with a firm voice, name asking-first, and identify one trusted adult to tell?
- **Self-check:** The learner reads their three completed sentences and checks they can name an
  adult and say "no means no" and "stop means stop."

## Home connection

At home, talk about who your trusted adults are, and practice saying "no" and "stop" — and
hearing a "no" — with a firm, kind voice.

## Resources

- Teaching children body autonomy and consent in age-appropriate, non-sexual terms — that their
  body is their own, that "no" and "stop" must be respected, and that they should tell a trusted
  adult — is standard in child-safety education and reflected in the UN Convention on the Rights
  of the Child's protection of children from harm (Art. 19). See also child-protection guidance
  from UNICEF and the Red Cross / Red Crescent. This lesson stays at everyday touch and personal
  space; sexual content is not appropriate at this grade.
