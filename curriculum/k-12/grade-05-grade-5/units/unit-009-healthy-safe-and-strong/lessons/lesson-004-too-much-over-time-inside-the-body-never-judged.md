# Lesson 4 — Too Much Over Time: Inside the Body, Never Judged

## Summary

Learners discover what **"too much, over time"** means for the **inside** of the body — too
much **added sugar**, **salt**, or **saturated fat** in a repeated pattern — while holding one
central rule: this is about the body's **inside systems**, **never** about a body's **size or
shape**. They sort statements into "inside the body" and "never the point," and practice saying
what is true without judging any body or any way of eating.

## Objectives

- Describe how having too much of certain foods affects the body over time, without judging any
  body. _(D03.S3.05.02)_

## Connection

Stir a pinch of salt into a cup of water and it tastes a little salty — the body can handle
that. Stir in spoonful after spoonful, day after day, and you are asking a lot of the body.
That is what **"too much, over time"** means: not one treat or one salty meal, but a **pattern**
kept up for months and years. Too much **added sugar**, over time, can rot **teeth** and send
your energy **spiking up and crashing down**. Too much **salt**, over time, can **strain the
heart and blood vessels**. Too much **saturated fat**, over time, can **build up inside blood
vessels**. Notice every one of these is about the **inside** of the body — never about how a
body looks. Bodies come in every size and shape, and every one of them deserves respect.

## Materials

- Inside, never judged poster
- Inside or outside page
- Pencils

## Preparation

- Print or draw the inside-never-judged poster (three inside-the-body panels: added sugar →
  teeth and energy; salt → heart and vessels; saturated fat → vessel build-up).
- Copy an inside-or-outside page for each learner.
- Recall Lesson 3 (too little, over time); today is the other side — too much, over time.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land is **"too much, over time"
affects the inside of the body — and this must never be used to judge a body's size or shape**.
This is the lesson's moral heart, and it is non-negotiable. Never link food to weight, shape, or
attractiveness; never shame any body or any family's food culture. Keep the language strictly
about **systems**: teeth, blood sugar, heart, blood vessels. A "treat" or a salty meal is fine
in a varied pattern; the concern is only a repeated excess over time. Affirm that bodies of all
sizes are healthy and worthy. Model the worked example once, then guide the sort. See
`docs/facilitation.md` and the unit's "without judging any body" commitment.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Last time you saw what too little of a nutrient does over time. Today,
   the other side: what can **too much** of some things do, **over time** — and one big rule to
   carry the whole way.
2. **Meet the big rule (5 min).** Here is the rule, said out loud and written down: **this is
   about the inside of the body, never about size or shape.** Bodies come in every size and
   shape, and every one deserves respect. Say it back to a partner.
3. **Meet the three effects (10 min).** Look at the poster. *Too much **added sugar**, over
   time:* it can rot **teeth** and send **energy** spiking up and crashing down. *Too much
   **salt**, over time:* it can **strain the heart and blood vessels**. *Too much **saturated
   fat**, over time:* it can **build up inside blood vessels**. All three are **inside** effects.
   None of them is about how a body looks.
4. **Sort (15 min).** On your page, sort each statement into "about the inside of the body" or
   "about size or shape — never the point." *"Too much salt can strain the heart"* → inside.
   *"This food makes a body look wrong"* → never the point; cross it out. Then write one true
   sentence about too much over time that names an **inside** effect only.
5. **Close (5 min).** You can talk about food and the body honestly **and** kindly. Too much,
   over time, has real effects — on the inside. And every body, at every size, is worthy of
   care and respect. Next time, we leave food for a while and learn to be **safe**: what to do
   in an emergency.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a two-column sort with pictures and sentence starters; scribe the closing
  sentence.
- **Extension:** Explain why a **pattern over time** matters more than any single meal, and write
  one way to talk about food without ever mentioning a body's size or shape.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner describe one inside-the-body effect of too much
  added sugar, salt, or saturated fat over time — using only inside-the-body language, never
  size or shape?
- **Self-check:** The learner re-reads their sentence and checks that it names an inside effect
  and says nothing about how any body looks.

## Home connection

With someone at home, name one thing that is fine sometimes but hard on the inside of the body
when there is too much, over time — and say it the kind way, about the inside only.

## Resources

- Repeatedly consuming too much added sugar is linked to tooth decay and, over time, to spikes
  and crashes in energy; too much salt (sodium) over time can raise blood pressure and strain
  the heart; and too much saturated fat over time is linked to build-up in blood vessels. See
  the World Health Organization "Healthy diet" fact sheet
  (<https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/healthy-diet>), which advises limiting free
  sugars, salt, and saturated fat — as evidence about the body's systems, never as a judgment
  about any body's size or shape.
