# Lesson 8 — Respecting Others' Boundaries (Capstone)

## Summary

The unit capstone turns consent outward: learners practice **respecting others' boundaries**
with a three-step flow — **ask first**, **listen to words and body cues**, and **respect the
answer**, accepting a "**no**" as a **full answer**. They then review the whole unit — the six
nutrients and their jobs, too little and too much over time, the four emergency steps, and the
consent rule — into a single portfolio page that ties **health, safety, and respect** together.

## Objectives

- Explain the idea of consent for one's own body and respect for the boundaries of others.
  _(D03.S4.05.02)_
- Explain how nutrients such as carbohydrates, proteins, fats, vitamins, minerals, and water
  support different parts of the body. _(D03.S3.05.01)_

## Connection

Consent has two sides. Last time you learned that **your** body is yours and you can say no.
Today, the other side: **everyone else's body is theirs**, too — and you are the one who must
respect it. That means **ask first** ("May I hug you?"), **listen** to their words *and* their
body — a step back, a frown, a shake of the head all say "no" without a sound — and **respect
the answer**. A "no" is a **full answer**: you do not argue, push, or pout; you say "Okay!" and
that is that. Respecting boundaries is how we honor the dignity of every person, whatever their
size, shape, or way of living.

## Materials

- Ask–listen–respect poster
- Boundary role-play cards
- Unit review page

## Preparation

- Print or draw the ask–listen–respect poster (three steps: ask first, listen to words and body
  cues, respect the answer).
- Copy a set of boundary role-play cards and a unit review page for each learner.
- Recall the whole unit: nutrients (Lessons 1–2), too little and too much over time (Lessons
  3–4), emergency response (Lessons 5–6), and consent (Lesson 7).

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It has two jobs: (1) land **respect for others'
boundaries** — ask, listen, respect, and accept a "no" as full — and (2) **review the unit**
into one portfolio artifact. Keep the consent content at the same age-appropriate level as
Lesson 7 (everyday touch and personal space; no sexual content). Model the ask–listen–respect
flow as a worked example before pairs role-play, and watch that every "no" in role-play is met
with a graceful "Okay!" — that is the skill. The review page is a chance to catch any learner
who has not yet got a concept and let them revisit it ("not yet" is information, never a
verdict). See `docs/facilitation.md` and `docs/contexts.md`.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** You have traveled through this whole unit — fuel and build, protect and
   regulate, too little and too much over time, emergencies, and your own consent. Today you
   finish the last piece: **respecting others' boundaries** — and then you tie it all together.
2. **Meet the three steps (10 min).** Look at the poster. *One — **ask first**:* before you touch
   someone or join their game or borrow their things, ask. *Two — **listen**:* hear their words,
   and watch their **body cues** — a smile and open arms say yes; a step back, a frown, a shake
   of the head say no. *Three — **respect the answer**:* a "**no**" is a **full answer**. Say
   "Okay!" and stop. No arguing, no pushing, no pouting.
3. **Watch the worked example (5 min).** Two learners show it. *"May I hug you?"* — the other
   steps back a little and shakes their head. *"No, thank you."* The first smiles and says
   *"Okay!"* — and does not hug. That is respect, in one moment.
4. **Role-play (15 min).** With a partner, practice the three steps with the cards — a hug, a
   high-five, borrowing a pencil, joining a game. Take turns asking and answering. Every time
   someone says no, the other answers "Okay!" and truly stops.
5. **Review the unit (15 min).** On your review page, write or draw: **one nutrient** and its
   job; **one too-little** and **one too-much** effect over time (about the inside, never size or
   shape); the **four emergency steps** and the **three things** to say; and the **consent rule**
   (no means no, stop means stop, ask first). Check with a partner: is anything missing?
6. **Close (5 min).** Healthy, safe, and strong — you know what your body needs, how to keep
   yourself and others safe, and how to honor every person's boundaries. That is the whole unit,
   carried into how you live.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a partially filled review page and picture prompts; work in a small group
  with the facilitator.
- **Accessibility:** A "no" or "yes" may be shown without words — a head shake or nod, a raised
  stop hand, a card, or a sign — and the ask–listen–respect flow can be practiced seated. Reading
  body cues is itself non-verbal, so a step back, frown, or head shake is honored as a full "no."
- **Extension:** Write a short reflection on why respecting a "no" is not just kind but
  necessary, and connect consent to the dignity of every body — and give one example from real
  life.

## Assessment

- **Formative/summative (performance/portfolio):** Can the learner demonstrate ask–listen–respect
  (including accepting a "no" as full) and complete the review page covering nutrients, too
  little/too much over time, emergency response, and consent?
- **Peer-check:** Partners confirm each other's review pages are complete and that every "no" in
  role-play was met with "Okay!"
- **Self-check:** The learner asks, "Can I name the six nutrients, the four emergency steps, and
  the consent rule — and did I respect every 'no' I heard?"

## Home connection

At home, practice asking before touching or borrowing, and share your unit review page — one
thing about nutrients, one about emergencies, and one about consent — with your family.

## Resources

- Respecting others' boundaries — asking before touching, reading verbal and non-verbal cues, and
  accepting a "no" as a full answer — is standard in child-safety and social-emotional learning
  and supports the dignity of every person (see UNICEF child-protection guidance and the UN
  Convention on the Rights of the Child, Art. 19). The nutrient review draws on the World Health
  Organization "Healthy diet" fact sheet and micronutrient guidance cited in Lessons 1–4. Consent
  content here remains age-appropriate and non-sexual.
