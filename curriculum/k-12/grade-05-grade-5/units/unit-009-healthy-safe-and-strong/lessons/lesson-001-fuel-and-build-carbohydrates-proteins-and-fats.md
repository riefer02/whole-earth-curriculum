# Lesson 1 — Fuel and Build: Carbohydrates, Proteins, and Fats

## Summary

Learners meet the three nutrients that **fuel** and **build** the body: **carbohydrates**
(give fuel for energy), **proteins** (build and repair muscle, skin, and blood), and **fats**
(store energy, build cells, and protect organs). They sort everyday foods from many regions
into these three jobs and connect each nutrient to what it does. Every way of eating is
honored; no food or body is judged.

## Objectives

- Explain how nutrients such as carbohydrates, proteins, and fats support different parts of
  the body. _(D03.S3.05.01)_

## Connection

Think of a cook fire and a builder. A cook fire needs **fuel** — wood or charcoal — to burn
and make heat. A builder needs **bricks** to raise a wall. Your body needs both, every day.
**Carbohydrates** are the body's main **fuel**: they break down into energy that lets you
run, think, and breathe. **Proteins** are the **building blocks**: they build and repair your
muscles, skin, hair, and blood. **Fats** do a third job: they **store energy** for later, help
build your brain and every cell, and cushion your organs. Whether your plate holds rice,
cassava, or bread; beans, fish, or eggs; nuts, avocado, or oil — the same three nutrients are
at work.

## Materials

- Fuel and build poster
- Nutrient sort page
- Pencils

## Preparation

- Print or draw the fuel and build poster (a body with three labeled arrows: carbohydrates →
  fuel, proteins → build, fats → store and build).
- Copy a nutrient sort page for each learner.
- Recall Grade 4: learners read simple nutrition labels and described how families eat
  different healthy foods; today they name the nutrients themselves.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land is that **different nutrients
do different jobs**, not that any food or body is "good" or "bad." Carbohydrates, proteins, and
fats are all needed; there is no "bad" nutrient. Use food examples from many regions and ways
of eating — rice, maize, cassava, plantain, bread, beans, lentils, chickpeas, tofu, fish, eggs,
milk, nuts, seeds, avocado, olive and palm and coconut oils — so no learner's home food is left
out or made to seem wrong. Sort by **job**, never by "healthy vs unhealthy." Model the poster
once (a worked example) before independent sorting. See `docs/facilitation.md` and
`docs/contexts.md`.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Remember Grade 4, when you read nutrition labels and saw that families
   everywhere eat different healthy foods. Today you learn **what is inside** those foods:
   **nutrients** — the parts of food that fuel and build your body.
2. **Meet the three nutrients (10 min).** Look at the poster. Here are the three big ones.
   **Carbohydrates** give **fuel**: your body breaks them into energy, like wood burning on a
   fire — for your legs, your arms, and your thinking brain. **Proteins** **build and repair**:
   they are the bricks for your muscles, skin, hair, and blood — like bricks for a wall.
   **Fats** **store energy and build cells**: they keep a spare supply of fuel, help build your
   brain and every cell, and cushion your organs. Three nutrients, three jobs.
3. **Sort together (10 min).** Say a food out loud and name its main job. *Rice, maize, bread,
   cassava, plantain* — mostly **fuel**. *Beans, lentils, fish, eggs, tofu, chickpeas, milk* —
   mostly **build**. *Nuts, seeds, avocado, oils* — mostly **store and build**. Notice: many
   foods give more than one nutrient. Beans give build *and* a little fuel. That is normal and
   good.
4. **Sort on your page (15 min).** On your page, place each food into its column — fuel, build,
   or store and build. Then write one sentence: name one **fuel** food and one **build** food
   and say what each does for your body.
5. **Close (5 min).** Every day your body burns fuel, builds new parts, and stores a little for
   later — and the nutrients in your food make it all happen. Next time: the helpers that
   protect and regulate your body.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a picture-word bank of the foods and pre-drawn columns; scribe the
  closing sentence.
- **Extension:** Explain that one food can give several nutrients at once — give an example
  (beans, fish, or an egg) and name two nutrients it provides.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name a fuel nutrient, a build nutrient, and a
  store-and-build nutrient, and give one food for each with its job?
- **Self-check:** The learner reads their sentence back and checks that each nutrient is
  matched to its real job (fuel, build, or store).

## Home connection

At your next meal, name one food that gives fuel and one that gives building blocks — and share
the three jobs with someone at home.

## Resources

- Carbohydrates are the body's main energy source, proteins build and repair tissues, and fats
  store energy, build cell membranes, and aid brain and cell function — this is established
  nutrition science. See the World Health Organization "Healthy diet" fact sheet
  (<https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/healthy-diet>) and the Food and
  Agriculture Organization's food-based dietary guidelines for how cultures worldwide build
  meals from these nutrients (<https://www.fao.org/nutrition/education/food-dietary-guidelines/en/>).
  These are evidence about what nutrients do — never a judgment about any body or way of eating.
