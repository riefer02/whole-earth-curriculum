# Lesson 3 — How Different Families Show Feelings

## Summary

Learners discover that the **same feeling can be shown in many different ways** from one
family to the next. One family greets a joy with a loud group hug; another shows it with
quiet smiles; a third breaks into song. Learners compare three families' ways of showing
one feeling, then draw or tell how their *own* family shows joy and sadness — always
holding that **no family's way is better than another's**.

## Objectives

- Compare how feelings are expressed in different families, cultures, and situations.
  _(D01.S1.05.02)_

## Connection

When a good thing happens, one family cheers and hugs; another family smiles quietly and
says a kind word; another sings. All three feel the *same* joy — they just show it
differently. The same is true for sadness, pride, and love. Whether your family is big
or small, here or far away, its way of showing feeling is real and worth honoring —
and so is everyone else's.

## Materials

- Family expression cards
- Blank "my family shows it this way" page
- Pencils and crayons

## Preparation

- Print or draw the family expression cards (three families, one shared feeling, three
  ways of showing it).
- Copy a "my family shows it this way" page for each learner.
- Arrange a circle for sharing; keep the tone warm and non-judging.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The one idea to land is **non-ranking
comparison**: differences in how families show feelings are real and *equal*, never
"better" or "worse," "right" or "wrong." Guard against any ranking language ("too loud,"
"too quiet," "weird"). Learners come from many family shapes — two parents, one parent,
grandparents, adoptive or chosen family, foster care, and more. Frame "family" as
"the people who care for you." Never ask a learner to justify or explain a family
difference they do not wish to share. See `docs/facilitation.md` on honoring every
family's shape.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** You have met three shapers of feeling: thoughts, memories, and
   your body. Today a new question: when you *have* a feeling, how do you show it? Here
   is the surprise — people show the same feeling in very different ways.
2. **Meet three families (10 min).** Look at the cards. Each shows a family with a big
   happy event — a new baby, a harvest, a good exam result. Family one: a loud group
   hug. Family two: quiet smiles and a gentle nod. Family three: a song and a dance.
   Same feeling — **joy** — shown three different ways. Which way feels most like *your*
   family? There is no wrong answer.
3. **Compare (10 min).** With a partner, look at one more feeling — **sadness**. Talk:
   how might each of these three families show sadness? Some cry, some go quiet, some
   gather close and cook together. Notice: all of them are *feeling* sadness, just
   showing it differently. No way is better.
4. **Your family's way (15 min).** On your page, choose one feeling — joy or sadness.
   Draw or write how *your* family shows it. If you have more than one family or
   caregiver, show both. Then write one sentence: "My family shows joy by…"
5. **Close (5 min).** Feelings are shared by everyone; the *ways we show them* are our
   own. Learning to see and honor different ways is a kindness you can carry everywhere.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer a set of three illustrated "ways to show it" (hug, quiet smile,
   song) to choose from rather than drawing from scratch; scribe the sentence.
- **Extension:** Compare the same family across two situations — a celebration and a
  goodbye — and describe how the expression changes while the feeling stays real.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name one feeling and describe two
  different, equally valid ways families might show it?
- **Self-check:** The learner checks that they described their own family's way without
  calling any other family's way "wrong."

## Home connection

Ask a family member how *their* family showed joy or sadness when they were young — and
notice how it is alike or different from how you show it now.

## Resources
