# Lesson 6 — Living by a Value I Care About

## Summary

Learners move from **naming** values to **living** them. They study a worked example of a
value turned into a real action, then write or draw a real (or imagined) moment when they
lived by fairness, honesty, or kindness — and set a small **"next time I will"**
commitment. The central idea: a value is proven by what a person *does*, not just what
they say.

## Objectives

- Name values they care about, such as fairness, honesty, and kindness, and give an
  example of living by one. _(D01.S2.05.01)_

## Connection

A value you only say is like a seed you never plant. A value you **live by** is the seed
growing — the fair share you give, the true word you speak, the help you offer. Whether
you return a dropped coin purse, tell the truth about a broken cup, or sit with a lonely
newcomer, the small deed is the value made real. All over the world, a person's values
are read not from their words but from their **choices and actions**.

## Materials

- Living-by-a-value poster
- Blank "living by a value" page
- Pencils and crayons

## Preparation

- Print or draw the living-by-a-value poster (a value paired with a real scene of acting
  on it).
- Copy a "living by a value" page for each learner.
- Arrange a circle and space for paired role-play.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land is that **a value is
shown in action**, and that even a *small* deed counts — this protects learners from the
perfectionism of "I must do something big to matter." Model the worked example first
(Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006): value → choice → action. Let learners write an
*imagined* example if a real one is hard to recall — the skill is connecting value to
deed, not confessing a deed. Never grade whose example is "more virtuous"; celebrate each
value lived, however small. See `docs/philosophy.md` §8 on mastery, not ranking.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Last time you named the values you care about. Today the harder,
   truer question: what does it look like to **live by** one?
2. **Watch a value turn into action (10 min).** Look at the poster. Here is a worked
   example. *Value:* honesty. *Choice:* a child finds a dropped coin purse on the path.
   *Action:* the child runs it back to the person who dropped it, saying, "This is
   yours." The value was never said out loud — it was **done**. That is living by a
   value.
3. **Practice matching (10 min).** In pairs, act out a tiny scene of living by one value
   — fairness, honesty, or kindness. Let your partner guess which value you showed.
   Notice: the value shows up in the *doing*.
4. **Your own example (15 min).** On your page, write or draw the value you chose, and
   one real (or imagined) moment you lived by it. Then finish this line: **"Next time I
   will…"** — one small, doable way to live that value again soon.
5. **Close (5 min).** A value is not a poster on a wall; it is a choice you make again and
   again. Each small deed is you, becoming the person you mean to be.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer three illustrated "value in action" scenes to choose from and label;
   use a sentence starter for the commitment ("Next time I will ___").
- **Extension:** Write two examples — one easy and one hard — and name what made the hard
  one hard, and what helped you still do it.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner connect a value to a concrete example of
  acting on it, and state one small next step?
- **Self-check:** The learner checks that their example is a real *action*, not only a
  word, and that their "next time" step is small enough to actually do.

## Home connection

Choose one value and do one small, real deed today — then tell someone at home what you
did and which value it lived.
