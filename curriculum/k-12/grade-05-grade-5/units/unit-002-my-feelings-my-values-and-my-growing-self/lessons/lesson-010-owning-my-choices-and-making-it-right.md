# Lesson 10 — Owning My Choices and Making It Right

## Summary

In this **capstone** lesson, learners learn that when a choice affects someone else, they
can take responsibility **without shame**: name the choice, notice its effect, and make a
**repair plus a better next step**. They work through a **choice–effect–repair** frame,
rehearse a repair in role-play, and connect the skill back to the thoughts (Lesson 1) and
values (Lessons 5–6) they have studied all unit — closing the unit with agency and hope.

## Objectives

- Take responsibility for a choice and its effects on others, and name a better next
  step. _(D01.S3.05.02)_
- Explain how thoughts, memories, and body sensations can shape a feeling.
  _(D01.S1.05.01)_
- Name values they care about, such as fairness, honesty, and kindness, and give an
  example of living by one. _(D01.S2.05.01)_

## Connection

Everyone makes a choice that hurts someone, sooner or later — a sharp word, a broken
promise, a spill that ruins a friend's work. The question is not "Am I a bad person?" but
"**What can I do now?**" You can own it — "I did that" — notice the effect — "and it hurt
you" — and repair it — "let me make it right." A person who can do that is not weak; they
are strong, and trustworthy. This is true in a village, a city, or anywhere people live
together.

## Materials

- Choice–effect–repair poster
- Blank choice–effect–repair page
- Pencils

## Preparation

- Print or draw the choice–effect–repair poster (three stepping stones: choice, effect,
  repair + next step).
- Copy a choice–effect–repair page for each learner.
- Arrange a circle and space for paired role-play.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land is **accountability plus
repair, never shame** — the learner is never a "bad kid"; a choice can be changed and a
harm can be repaired. Use a **non-shaming frame** throughout: name the *behavior*, not the
*person* ("that choice hurt someone," never "you are bad"). Model repair as strength, not
weakness, and let the worked example show the *feeling behind* the choice (a thought
shaping a feeling, from Lesson 1) so learners see the full picture, not a villain. Some
learners live where "responsibility" has meant punishment; gently distinguish
**accountability** (facing the truth and repairing) from **shame** (being told you are
wrong as a person). See `docs/facilitation.md` on assessing without ranking and holding
difficult topics safely.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** This is your capstone. All unit you have learned how feelings are
   shaped, how they are shown, what you value, and what shapes you. Today, the grown-up
   skill that ties it together: **owning a choice and making it right**.
2. **Meet the three stones (10 min).** Look at the poster: three stepping stones.
   **Choice** — what you did. **Effect** — how it touched someone else. **Repair** — how
   you make it right, plus a **better next step**. Worked example: a child bumps the table
   and spills a friend's bowl. *Choice:* I bumped the table. *Effect:* my friend's food
   spilled, and she felt upset — maybe the thought "why did you do that?" was shaping her
   feeling (remember Lesson 1). *Repair:* I say, "I did that. I'm sorry. Let me help you
   clean it and share some of mine." *Better next step:* I will carry bowls with two hands
   and watch where I walk. Notice: the child is not "bad" — the choice is fixed, not the
   child.
3. **Rehearse a repair (15 min).** With a partner, act out a small, safe mistake and its
   repair. Use the honest words: "I did it. I'm sorry. How can I make it right?" Take
   turns being the one who repairs and the one who hears it. Notice how a real repair
   feels different from only a quick "sorry."
4. **Write your own (15 min).** On your page, fill the three boxes for a small, real (or
   imagined) choice of your own. Connect it to a value: which of your values — honesty,
   kindness, fairness — does the repair live by? Write the **better next step** you will
   actually take.
5. **Close (5 min).** Mistakes are not the end of the story; they are the beginning of a
   repair. You can own a choice, care for the effect, and choose better next time. That is
   how a growing person grows.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide three picture cards (a spill, a sharp word, a broken promise) to
  choose from and sentence starters ("I did ___, it made you feel ___, I will ___");
  scribe as needed.
- **Extension:** Write two repairs — one small and one bigger — and name what a *real*
  repair needs that a quick "sorry" alone does not.

## Assessment

- **Formative (performance):** In role-play, can the learner name the choice, its effect,
  and a concrete repair plus a better next step, without shaming language?
- **Self-check:** The learner rereads their page and checks that their next step is real
  and doable, and that they framed the *behavior* (not themselves) as the thing to change.

## Home connection

If a small thing goes wrong at home this week, practice the three stones — choice, effect,
repair — and notice how the repair changes the day.
