# Lesson 5 — The Values I Care About

## Summary

Learners learn what a **value** is — something a person cares about that guides their
choices — and meet three core values: **fairness**, **honesty**, and **kindness**. They
sort illustrated value cards, match short stories to the value each one shows, name the
values they care most about, and begin a **"my values" page** they will finish next
lesson with a real example of living by one.

## Objectives

- Name values they care about, such as fairness, honesty, and kindness, and give an
  example of living by one. _(D01.S2.05.01)_

## Connection

When you split the last piece of bread fairly, tell the truth even when it is hard, or
help someone who dropped their things, you are living by a **value** — something you care
about that guides your choices. Values are like an inner compass: they help you decide
what to do when no one is watching. Every person's compass points a little differently,
and that is worth understanding — in yourself and in others, in a village or a city, in
any corner of the world.

## Materials

- Value cards
- Blank "my values" page
- Pencils and crayons

## Preparation

- Print or draw the value cards (fairness, honesty, kindness, and several more such as
  courage, respect, care, responsibility).
- Copy a "my values" page for each learner.
- Arrange a circle; prepare one short story per value for the matching activity.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land is that a **value is
something you care about that guides choices** — and that values differ from person to
person without one being "better." Avoid ranking values ("honesty is the best value");
instead honor each learner's genuine values. Distinguish **evidence from values**
(`docs/philosophy.md` §5): *what values exist* is a fact we can observe; *which values to
hold* is a choice each person and community makes. This is a place for inquiry, not
creed — invite, never impose. See `docs/facilitation.md` on inquiry over creed.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** You have been learning how feelings are shaped and shown. Now a
   new question: what do you **care about**? The answer points to your **values**.
2. **Meet three values (10 min).** Look at the cards. **Fairness** — everyone gets what
   they need, and rules are the same for all. **Honesty** — telling the truth and being
   real, even when it is hard. **Kindness** — caring for others and helping without being
   asked. These are values many people hold. A value is not just a nice word — it is
   something that **guides what you do**.
3. **Match story to value (10 min).** Listen to each short story and name the value it
   shows. *Story:* Amara finds a wallet on the path and returns it to the person who
   dropped it. Which value is that? (Honesty.) *Story:* Jun insists the game follow the
   same rules for everyone, even his best friend. (Fairness.) *Story:* Priya sits with the
   new child who is eating alone. (Kindness.)
4. **Name your values (15 min).** Look through all the value cards. Which two or three do
   *you* care about most? On your "my values" page, draw or write each one and one
   sentence about why it matters to you. There is no right answer — your compass is your
   own.
5. **Close (5 min).** Your values are part of who you are. Next time, you will think of a
   real moment when you **lived by** one of them.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer picture-supported value cards and a sentence starter ("I care about
   ___ because ___") to complete; scribe as needed.
- **Extension:** Sort the cards from "most like me" to "least like me" and write why the
  top value matters most right now.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name at least one value they care about
  and match a value to a simple story?
- **Self-check:** The learner reads their page and checks that each value they named is
  something they *truly* care about, not just a word they were told.

## Home connection

Ask someone at home what value they care about most and one reason why — then share
yours.
