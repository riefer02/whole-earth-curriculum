# Lesson 2 — Memories and My Body Shape a Feeling

## Summary

Learners meet two more **shapers** of feeling: **memory** and **body sensation**. A
memory can bring a feeling back even when nothing is happening right now, and a body
sensation — a warm chest, a tight stomach, loose shoulders — can signal and shape what
we feel. Learners complete a **memory–body–feeling** frame for a real experience and
notice where feelings live in their own bodies.

## Objectives

- Explain how thoughts, memories, and body sensations can shape a feeling.
  _(D01.S1.05.01)_

## Connection

Have you ever smelled a certain food and suddenly felt **happy** or **homesick** — even
before you remembered why? That is a memory shaping a feeling. Or have you felt your
shoulders tighten and only then realized you were **nervous**? That is your body shaping
a feeling. Whether the memory is of a harvest festival, a market street, or a
grandmother's kitchen, and whether your body speaks in a flutter or a warmth, these
signals are part of how feelings come to be — everywhere in the world.

## Materials

- Memory–body–feeling poster
- Blank memory–body–feeling page
- Pencils and crayons

## Preparation

- Print or draw the memory–body–feeling poster (a memory and a body sensation, each
  feeding into a feeling).
- Copy a blank memory–body–feeling page for each learner.
- Arrange a circle and a quiet space; prepare a shared memory object if using one.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land is that memory and body
are **two more shapers** of feeling, alongside the thoughts met in Lesson 1. Invite a
range of memories — joyful and tender — but let each learner choose a *safe* one to
share; memories can touch loss, and a learner's right to keep a memory private must be
protected. Model your own example first, and keep it ordinary. Watch for learners who
reveal a heavy memory; honor it calmly and move gently, never pressing for detail. See
`docs/facilitation.md` on holding difficult topics safely.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Last time you met the first shaper of feeling: **thoughts**. An
   event plus a thought makes a feeling. Today you meet two more shapers: **memory** and
   **body sensation**.
2. **Meet the two shapers (10 min).** Look at the poster. Two arrows point into one
   feeling. First, a **memory**: a remembered day can bring a feeling back right now,
   even though nothing is happening. Second, a **body sensation**: your body speaks a
   feeling before you have the words — a warm chest, a tight stomach, loose shoulders.
   Worked example: the smell of bread from a grandmother's kitchen (**memory**) plus a
   warm, soft feeling in the chest (**body**) together shape the feeling **safe and
   loved**.
3. **Scan your body (10 min).** Close your eyes or soften your gaze. Slowly scan from
   head to toe. Where is there a sensation — warm, tight, fluttery, heavy, light? Name
   it without judging it. Share one with a partner: "I notice a flutter in my stomach."
4. **Build your frame (15 min).** On your page, draw or write one safe **memory** of
   your own. Beside it, draw or write the **body sensation** it brings. Then name the
   **feeling** the two of them shape together. Color the body spot where it lives.
5. **Close (5 min).** Your thoughts, your memories, and your body all shape what you
   feel. Noticing all three — together — is a powerful way to know yourself.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a memory word bank and a simple body outline to color the
  sensation spot instead of writing; scribe the feeling name.
- **Extension:** Trace two memories — one that warms and one that tightens — and
  compare how the body signal differs for each feeling.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name a memory, a body sensation, and the
  feeling they shape together?
- **Self-check:** The learner points to the body spot and checks, "Is this truly where
  and how that feeling shows up for me?"

## Home connection

With someone at home, name one memory and the body sensation it brings — and ask them
for one of theirs.
