# Lesson 7 — What Shapes Me: Family, Culture, Place, and History

## Summary

Learners learn that **identity is not one thing** but is shaped by four forces: **family,
culture, place, and history**. Using a **four-petal diagram**, they fill in what each
shaper contributes to who they are, then explain in a sentence how one of them has shaped
them. Throughout, they hold each learner's family shape, place, and history — including
adoption, loss, and moving — as equally worthy of honor.

## Objectives

- Explain how a person's identity is shaped by family, culture, place, and history.
  _(D01.S2.05.02)_

## Connection

Who are you? Not one answer, but many threads woven together: the **family** that raised
you (in whatever shape it takes), the **culture** you share (its foods, words, music,
ways of belonging), the **place** you live or come from (a city block, a river valley, a
coast), and the **history** behind you (stories, moves, and people who came before). Pull
any one thread and you change the cloth. Knowing your threads — and honoring that every
person's cloth is different — is how you understand yourself and make room for others.

## Materials

- Four-shapers-of-me poster
- Blank four-petal "what shapes me" page
- Pencils and crayons

## Preparation

- Print or draw the four-shapers-of-me poster (center "me," four petals: family, culture,
  place, history).
- Copy a four-petal page for each learner.
- Arrange a circle and a quiet space for individual work.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land is that **identity has
four shapers — family, culture, place, history** — and that *every* form of each is
worthy. Handle family with care: family means "the people who care for you" and may
include two parents, one parent, grandparents, adoptive or foster parents, siblings, or a
chosen community. Handle history gently: some histories include adoption, loss, or
leaving a home. **Never** require a learner to share a painful detail; let them fill a
petal with what feels safe and leave what does not. Celebrate the many shapes in the
room as a strength. See `docs/facilitation.md` on holding difficult topics safely.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** You know how feelings are shaped — by thoughts, memories, and
   your body. Now a bigger question: what shapes **you**? The answer has four parts.
2. **Meet the four shapers (10 min).** Look at the poster: a center **me** with four
   petals. **Family** — the people who care for you, whatever shape that takes.
   **Culture** — shared foods, words, music, and ways of belonging. **Place** — where you
   live or come from. **History** — the stories and people and moves that came before you.
   Worked example: a child named Kofi says — family: my aunt and cousin; culture: the
   songs and food of our home; place: a city by the sea; history: we moved here two years
   ago. Four threads, one self.
3. **Fill your petals (20 min).** On your page, fill each petal with a drawing or a few
   words — as much as feels safe to share. There is no "right" family shape, no "better"
   place. Your threads are yours.
4. **Explain one shaper (10 min).** Choose one petal and write one sentence: "___ has
   shaped me because ___." Share your sentence with a partner if you wish.
5. **Close (5 min).** You are many threads woven together — and every thread, and every
   person's cloth, deserves honor. Next time, you will draw your full identity map.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer picture prompts for each petal (a house, a bowl of food, a map, an
  old photo) and a sentence starter; scribe as needed.
- **Extension:** Add a fifth petal (language, faith, ability, or a chosen community) and
  write why it belongs on your flower.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name all four shapers and give at least
  one real detail for each?
- **Portfolio:** The four-petal page, kept as an artifact of the learner's growing
  identity.

## Home connection

Ask a family member or caregiver to help you add one detail to a petal — a family story,
a food, a place name, or a person who came before you.
