# Lesson 7 — Choreograph a Group Dance or Scene Cooperatively

## Summary

Learners choreograph their first group dance or scene: they choose a **clear idea**, map a
**beginning** (a shared starting shape), a **middle** (travel, pathways, and levels), and
an **end** (a final group shape), and rehearse it **cooperatively**. The emphasis is on
listening, taking turns leading, and shaping the piece together so everyone belongs in it.

## Objectives

- Choreograph a short group dance or scene with a clear idea, working cooperatively to
  shape and perform it. _(D07.S3.05.01)_

## Connection

Bodies in space already make dance without a name for it: a flock of birds turns as one, a
market crowd weaves around stalls, a game of tag scatters and regroups. A dance is that
same thing made **on purpose** — people deciding, together, where to begin, where to
travel, and how to finish. The secret is not fancy steps; it is **cooperation**: listening
to each other, so that many bodies become one piece. Today you and your group become
choreographers.

## Materials

- Movement map page
- Clear idea cards
- Open space and optional music

## Preparation

- Copy a movement map page for each group and prepare the idea cards.
- Clear a safe space to move; agree on a quiet "freeze" signal.
- Recall Grade 3: learners performed movement with high and low levels, speeds, and shapes,
  and retold a feeling with movement alone.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land is **cooperation as the
craft**: the group shapes the piece together, and every body belongs. Model the
beginning–middle–end structure in movement before groups begin (a worked example). Watch
for one person dominating or others drifting out — name the norm gently: "in this piece,
everyone has a part, and the lead passes around." Honor every body: some learners move
differently or use chairs or wheels; a dance includes them fully, and their movement is
the dance, not an exception. No ranking of "better dancers." See `docs/facilitation.md`
and `docs/contexts.md` on cooperative group work and including all bodies.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Watch a friend cross a room — they already move with shape, speed,
   and level. Today you turn that into a **dance**: a group of bodies moving together with
   a clear idea and a beginning, middle, and end.
2. **Meet the map (10 min).** Look at the movement map. Here is a worked example. *Idea:*
   a seed growing. *Beginning:* everyone starts low, curled in a tight cluster — the seed.
   *Middle:* travel outward, rising to different **levels** along curved **pathways** —
   the shoot reaching for sun. *End:* come together in a tall, open group shape — the
   tree in full leaf. One clear idea, three parts.
3. **Choose your idea (5 min).** Pick one from the cards — a storm, a river, a journey, a
   market morning, a day at work — or invent your own. Say it in one sentence.
4. **Map it together (15 min).** On your movement map, sketch your **start shape**, your
   **middle travel** (who goes where, at what level, along what pathway), and your **end
   shape**. Let different people propose each part, and decide together. Everyone has a
   place in every part.
5. **Rehearse (10 min).** Practice slowly, then up to a simple beat. Fix the spacing, the
   timing, and the shared shape. Ask: does the idea read clearly? Does everyone belong?
6. **Close (5 min).** You made a dance the way people have always made dances — together.
   Next lesson you will see how dances around the world carry the place and history of the
   communities that made them.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide the idea and the start/end shapes pre-sketched, and ask the group
  to create only the middle travel; keep one shared, simple pathway.
- **Extension:** Add a change of speed or level in the middle and explain how it deepens
  the idea for an audience.

## Assessment

- **Performance (peer/observation):** Does the group perform a dance or scene with a clear
  idea and an audible/visible beginning, middle, and end, shaped cooperatively with every
  member included?
- **Self-check:** The group asks, "Did we shape it together, does every person belong in
  every part, and does our idea read clearly to someone watching?"

## Home connection

Watch any group moving together — birds, a sports team, a family at work — and notice the
beginning, middle, and end of their movement, and how they cooperate.

## Resources

- The elements of choreography (levels, pathways, shapes) are standard in elementary dance
  education; cooperative ensemble work is a core practice. For accessible framing, see the
  Kennedy Center's arts education resources on dance: <https://www.kennedy-center.org/education/>
