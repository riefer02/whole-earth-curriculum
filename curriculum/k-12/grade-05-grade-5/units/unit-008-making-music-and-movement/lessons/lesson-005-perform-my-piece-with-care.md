# Lesson 5 — Perform My Piece with Care

## Summary

Learners take the piece they composed last lesson and **perform** it for an audience.
They rehearse with a performance-care checklist, warm up their voice and body, perform
with a steady tempo and a clear beginning–middle–end, and receive **specific, kind
feedback**. Performing is framed as sharing a gift, and listening as a skill of respect.

## Objectives

- Perform the composed piece with a clear beginning, middle, and end, and with care for
  the audience. _(D07.S2.05.01)_

## Connection

A story shared is worth more than a story kept inside. Singers, drummers, and storytellers
in every place on Earth do the same thing: they **prepare**, they **breathe**, and they
give their work to an audience. Performing is not showing off — it is offering what you
made so others can feel it too. And listening well is its own kindness: the audience
receives the gift with attention and respect. Today you become both the giver and the
receiver.

## Materials

- Learner's composition from Lesson 4
- Performance care checklist
- A performance space and audience

## Preparation

- Have each group's composition and notation page ready.
- Clear a performance space; arrange the audience to face it.
- Recall Grade 3: learners already performed rhythm patterns and described how tempo and
  dynamics change a song's feeling.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land is **performance as gift,
listening as respect**. Keep the tone warm and safe: performing in front of others makes
grade-5 learners vulnerable, so protect every performer — applause first, then one
specific kind comment, never a ranking. Model the listener's job: "I noticed your steady
beat" or "I could hear three clear parts," not "yours was best." Feedback is the strongest
lever in learning when it is specific and kind (Hattie, *Visible Learning*). For learners
who cannot perform in front of the whole group, honor a smaller audience of one or two.
See `docs/facilitation.md` and `docs/contexts.md`.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Last lesson you composed a piece with a beginning, a middle, and
   an end. Today you **perform** it — you share it with an audience so they can feel what
   you made.
2. **Rehearse (10 min).** With your group, practice your piece twice. Read the
   performance-care checklist: *ready to start together, steady tempo, clear
   beginning–middle–end, strong finish, look at your audience.* Fix anything that wobbles.
3. **Warm up (5 min).** Shake out your hands, take three slow breaths, and hum a gentle
   note together (or, if you do not voice sound, take one slow, deep breath). A warm,
   calm body makes a clearer sound.
4. **Perform (15 min).** Each group takes the space and performs. Begin together, keep
   your tempo steady, let the middle contrast, and finish clean. Look at your audience —
   you are sharing, not hiding.
5. **Listen and give feedback (10 min).** When you are in the audience, your job is to
   watch quietly and then offer **one specific, kind comment** to each group: "I noticed
   your steady beat" or "I could hear where the middle changed." No rankings, only
   noticing.
6. **Close (5 min).** You made a piece, and you gave it away — and others gave theirs to
   you. That exchange is one of the oldest human gifts there is.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Let anxious learners perform to a partner or two first, or join a larger
  group where they carry one part; allow performing with eyes on the floor at first.
- **Support (voice-free and motor-free roles):** A learner who does not sing or speak can
  still perform — cue the group's start with a raised hand, conduct the steady tempo, hold
  or turn the notation page as each part arrives, or play a single percussion part. The
  piece needs everyone's part, not every voice.
- **Extension:** Add dynamics (loud/soft) or expression to the middle and explain how it
  changes the feeling of the piece.

## Assessment

- **Performance (peer/self):** Does the group perform with a steady tempo and an audible
  beginning, middle, and end, and does the audience's feedback reflect respectful,
  specific noticing?
- **Self-check:** The performer asks, "Did I share my piece with care, and did I listen to
  others with the same respect I wanted for myself?"

## Home connection

Perform something small for someone at home — a song, a rhyme, a story — and notice what
it feels like to share it and be received.

## Resources

- Feedback is among the strongest influences on learning and works best when it is
  specific, timely, and kind (Hattie, *Visible Learning*). For classroom-friendly framing
  of performing and listening with care, see Smithsonian Folkways' "Tools for Teaching":
  <https://folkways.si.edu/tools-for-teaching>
