# Lesson 1 — Plan an Artwork with a Clear Idea

## Summary

Learners begin the unit by learning to **plan an artwork the way a builder plans a
house**: they settle on a **clear idea**, then choose **materials** and a **technique**
that fit it. A worked example shows the three-part plan, learners write their own, and
a partner checks that the plan is clear enough to follow next lesson.

## Objectives

- Plan an artwork with a clear idea, choosing materials and techniques to fit the
  idea. _(D07.S1.05.01)_

## Connection

A builder draws plans before the first brick is laid. A cook reads a recipe before the
first vegetable is chopped. A gardener decides what to plant where before the first seed
goes in. An artwork is the same: it begins not with the first stroke but with a **clear
idea** — what you want to show — and then a choice of **materials** and a **technique**
that fit that idea. A stormy sea calls for dark blues and smudged chalk; a bright market
calls for warm colors and cut paper. Planning first is what turns a lucky accident into
something you meant to make.

## Materials

- My Art Plan page
- Sample artworks
- Pencils and colored pencils

## Preparation

- Copy a "My Art Plan" page for each learner.
- Gather two or three sample artworks whose idea, materials, and technique are easy to
  name.
- Recall Grade 4: learners already showed depth and space and chose a subject and
  purpose; today they add a formal **plan**.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land is **plan before you
make**: a clear idea, then materials and a technique chosen *to fit* the idea. Model the
worked example aloud and in writing before learners begin (explicit instruction for a
novice skill — Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006). Watch for the common slip of choosing
materials *first* ("I want to use glitter") and then forcing an idea onto them; gently
redirect to "what do you want to show, and what will best show it?" The plan is a promise
to the learner's future self; next lesson they carry it through. See
`docs/facilitation.md` on working through plans and `docs/contexts.md` on peer checking.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of something you have made before — a drawing, a fort, a
   kite, a meal you helped cook. Did you plan it first, or just start? Today you learn to
   **plan an artwork** the way a builder plans a house: idea first, then materials, then
   technique.
2. **Meet the plan (10 min).** Here is a worked example. *Idea:* "a stormy sea at night."
   *Materials:* dark blue and grey paper, chalk. *Technique:* smudge the chalk for the
   waves, tear the paper for the jagged rocks. *Because:* smudged chalk looks like moving
   water, and torn edges look like rough rock. Notice the order: the **idea** came first,
   and the materials and technique were chosen to **fit** it.
3. **Choose your idea (5 min).** Pick one clear idea. It can be a place, a person, a
   feeling, a story moment — something you can say in one sentence and sketch small.
4. **Write your plan (15 min).** Fill your "My Art Plan" page: write or sketch your
   **idea** in one sentence; list the **materials** you will use; name the **technique**
   you will try; finish with "because" — why these fit your idea.
5. **Partner check (10 min).** Swap pages. Read your partner's plan. Could you picture
   the artwork from it? Ask one clarifying question ("What will the background be?").
   Tell them one thing about the plan that is already clear. This helps before, not
   after, the making.
6. **Close (5 min).** A clear plan is a gift to yourself: tomorrow you will not wonder
   what to do — you will begin. Keep your plan safe; next lesson you make it real.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer a bank of idea starters and material/technique cards to choose from;
  scribe the "because" sentence while the learner dictates.
- **Extension:** Plan two different material–technique pairs for the same idea and write
  which one fits better and why.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation/peer):** Can the learner state a clear idea and name materials
  and a technique that fit it, with a "because"?
- **Self-check:** The learner asks, "Could someone else picture my artwork from my plan,
  and did I choose materials and technique to fit the idea — not the other way around?"

## Home connection

Plan one small thing at home before you make it — a snack, a drawing, a tidy-up — and
tell someone your idea, your "materials," and why they fit.
