# Lesson 4 — Compose a Short Piece or Song: Beginning, Middle, and End

## Summary

Learners compose their first short piece or song by learning the shape every story and
song shares: a **beginning** that introduces the idea, a **middle** that contrasts, and an
**end** that returns home. A worked example builds an A–B–A piece with body percussion,
then small groups compose and notate their own three-part piece, ready to perform next
lesson.

## Objectives

- Compose a short piece or song with a clear beginning, middle, and end. _(D07.S2.05.01)_

## Connection

Every good story has a beginning, a middle, and an end. A song is a story told in sound:
the beginning introduces the idea, the middle takes you somewhere new, and the end brings
you home. You already know this shape — a lullaby starts softly, rises to a fuller middle,
and settles back to quiet; a work song sets a rhythm to match the task, and returns to its
opening line. Today you become the one who shapes it: you **compose** — you put sounds
together on purpose.

## Materials

- Beginning–middle–end form card
- Simple notation page
- Found-sound makers and body percussion

## Preparation

- Copy a form card and a notation page for each group.
- Recall Grade 4: learners read and played simple melodies and rhythms from basic
  notation; today they compose their own.
- Arrange the room into small groups with room to make sound.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land is **form**: a piece has
a beginning (the idea), a middle (contrast), and an end (return). Model the worked example
step by step before groups compose (explicit instruction for a novice skill — Kirschner,
Sweller & Clark, 2006). Keep the vocabulary concrete: "beginning, middle, end" before
"A–B–A." Grade 4 gave basic notation, so simple shape/letter/body-mark notation is
enough — do not demand staff notation. Celebrate every completed three-part piece; form is
the goal, not virtuosity. Next lesson adds performance. See `docs/facilitation.md` and
`docs/contexts.md` on cooperative group work.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of a story you love. It has a beginning, a middle, and an
   end. Music works the same way. Today you **compose** a short piece with those three
   parts.
2. **Meet the shape (10 min).** Look at the form card. Here is a worked example you will
   try together. *Beginning:* clap a slow, steady beat four times — "clap, clap, clap,
   clap." That is the idea introduced. *Middle:* change it — tap the desk fast eight
   times, or hum a rising sound. Something **new** happens. *End:* clap the slow beat
   four times again — you have **returned** to the start. That is A (beginning), B
   (middle), A (end) — a piece with a shape.
3. **Choose your sounds (5 min).** With your group, pick your sound makers — voices,
   claps, taps, shakers, or a pitched instrument. Decide: what is the *idea* of your
   piece? Is it a calm morning, a busy market, a rolling river, a walk home?
4. **Compose the three parts (15 min).** Build your piece: a **beginning** that
   introduces the idea (one short phrase you can repeat), a **middle** that contrasts —
   faster or slower, higher or lower, louder or softer, or new words — and an **end**
   that returns to the beginning. Record each part on your notation page with shapes,
   lines, letter names, or body marks.
5. **Check your shape (10 min).** Perform your piece once for yourselves. Ask: can we
   hear three parts? Does the middle really **contrast**? Does the end **return**? Adjust
   anything that blurs the shape.
6. **Close (5 min).** You have just done what every composer on Earth does: shaped sound
   into a beginning, a middle, and an end. Keep your piece safe — next lesson you share
   it with an audience.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a pre-made beginning and ending and ask learners to compose only
  the contrasting middle; use one repeated body-percussion phrase.
- **Extension:** Label the form (A–B–A or A–B–C) and write one sentence explaining how
  the middle creates contrast and how the end resolves it.

## Assessment

- **Formative (performance/peer):** Can the group perform a piece with an audible
  beginning, a contrasting middle, and an ending that returns, and can they point to where
  each part happens?
- **Self-check:** The group asks, "Can we hear three clear parts, and does our middle
  really feel different from our beginning and end?"

## Home connection

Listen to a song you like and try to find its beginning, middle, and end — and whether
the ending returns to something from the start.
