# Lesson 2 — Complete My Planned Artwork

## Summary

Learners carry last lesson's plan through to a **finished artwork**. They re-read their
plan, gather materials, make during a long hands-on block, pause at a **midpoint
checklist** to check they are still following their idea, then **finish, title, and
reflect**. This lesson lands the second half of the standard: not just planning but
*completing* an artwork with a clear idea.

## Objectives

- Complete the artwork planned last lesson, carrying a clear idea through to a finished
  piece. _(D07.S1.05.01)_

## Connection

Many starts are easy; **finishing** is the skill. A seed must be tended before it fruits.
A cake must be baked after it is mixed. A story must reach its ending after its first
page. Finishing asks you to keep the idea in your mind, adjust when something doesn't
work, and keep going until the work is whole. Today you do that: you turn the plan you
wrote into something you can hold up and say "I made this."

## Materials

- Learner's "My Art Plan" page from Lesson 1
- Art materials (per plan)
- In-progress checklist

## Preparation

- Have each learner's Lesson 1 plan ready to hand back.
- Lay out the art materials plans call for; keep a "found objects" box for unplanned
  needs.
- Post the in-progress checklist where all can see it.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land is **follow-through**:
completion is part of craft, not an afterthought. Keep the block mostly *making* — your
job is to keep learners moving and unblock them, not to direct every stroke. The midpoint
checklist is the key structure: it is a self-check that turns "am I done?" into "have I
kept my idea and am I almost there?" Honor every finish, however modest; a completed
artwork is a real achievement, never to be ranked against another's. Some learners will
finish at very different times — have an extension ready (add a border, a title card, a
second smaller piece). See `docs/facilitation.md` on mastery and
`docs/development.md` on the grade-5 need for independence with adult safety nearby.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Last lesson you wrote a plan. Today you keep your promise to
   yourself and **complete** the artwork. Read your plan once, slowly. What is your idea?
   What materials and technique did you choose?
2. **Set up (5 min).** Gather your materials. Lay them out so everything you need is
   within reach. If your plan needs something you didn't list, you may swap one material —
   but keep the **idea** the same.
3. **Make (25 min).** Work on your artwork. Follow your plan, but let your hands make
   small discoveries. If a material isn't working, adjust the *technique*, not the idea.
   Work with care, as if this piece will be seen by people you respect.
4. **Midpoint check (5 min).** Pause. Read the checklist: *Did I keep my idea? Are my
   materials doing what I planned? Is anything missing? Am I almost done?* Answer
   honestly, then return to work knowing what to do next.
5. **Finish and title (10 min).** Complete the last parts — a background, a detail, a
   clean edge. Give your artwork a **title** that fits the idea. Write the title on a
   small card or on the back.
6. **Share and reflect (5 min).** Hold up your work. Say your idea in one sentence and
   one choice you are proud of. Listen as others do the same. A finished artwork is
   worth a moment of honor.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a pre-cut or simplified version of a tricky step; partner the
  learner with someone who can read the plan aloud; accept a smaller finished piece.
- **Extension:** Write a two-sentence reflection comparing the finished work to the plan
  and naming one change you made along the way and why.

## Assessment

- **Portfolio (observation/self):** Is there a finished artwork that matches the stated
  idea, with materials and technique chosen to fit it? Does the learner's reflection name
  the idea and at least one choice?
- **Self-check:** The learner asks, "Did I complete my artwork, did it keep my idea, and
  can I name one material or technique choice that helped?"

## Home connection

Finish something at home that you started and left unfinished — a drawing, a model, a
chore — and notice how it feels to complete it.
