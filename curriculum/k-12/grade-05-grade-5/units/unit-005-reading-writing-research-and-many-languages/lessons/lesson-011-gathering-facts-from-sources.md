# Lesson 11 — Gathering Facts from Several Sources

## Summary

Learners learn to **gather information from several sources** and **note key facts**. They meet
different kinds of sources (a book, a person, a chart, a place), practice turning what they
find into short notes that name the source, and begin the research that will become a short
report in the next lesson.

## Objectives

- Gather information from several sources and note key facts. _(D04.S3.05.02)_

## Connection

How do you know whether a plant is safe to eat, or what time the market opens, or how a tool
works? Not from one guess — from **several sources**: you ask someone who knows, you watch it
yourself, you check a book or a sign. A careful person doesn't trust a single source; they
**gather** from a few and write down the **key facts**. That is what research is: collecting the
best available truth, from more than one place, and keeping track of where each bit came from.

## Materials

- Sources poster
- Note-card page
- A small research topic menu

## Preparation

- Print or draw the sources poster (book, person, chart, place → note card).
- Copy several note-card pages per learner.
- Prepare a menu of simple, local, checkable topics.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The ideas to land are that research means
**gathering from several sources** and that a good note records **both the fact and its source**.
Make "sources" wide — a person, a place, a chart, an object — so the skill is not limited to
books, which many learners may not have. Model taking a note from two sources on one topic,
then let learners choose topics they can actually investigate in their own lives. This is the
first half of the research arc; the next lesson turns the notes into a report in the learner's
own words. See `docs/contexts.md` on low-resource research.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of something you'd like to know for certain — about a plant, an
   animal, a food, a place, a job. Today you become a **researcher**: you gather facts from
   **more than one source** and write them down.
2. **Meet sources (10 min).** Look at the poster. A source is anywhere a fact comes from: a
   **book**, a **person** who knows, a **chart**, a **place you visited**, an **object you
   examined**. Good research uses **several** sources, not just one — because one source can be
   wrong, and several that agree are stronger.
3. **Learn to note a key fact (10 min).** Look at a note card. It has two parts: the **source**
   (where the fact came from) and the **key facts** (the important bits, in short words). A key
   fact is not every word — it is the heart. Practice: from a fact your facilitator says aloud,
   write one key-fact note with the source named.
4. **Gather from your sources (15 min).** Choose a topic from the menu (or your own). Find **two
   or three sources** — ask a person, watch something, check a book or sign. For each, fill a
   note card: source on top, key facts below.
5. **Compare your sources (10 min).** Look at your cards. Which facts appear in more than one
   source? A fact two sources agree on is one you can trust more. Circle those.
6. **Close (5 min).** You now have the raw material of research — key facts, with their sources
   kept safe. Next lesson, you will turn these notes into a short report in your **own words**.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a pre-filled source line and a sentence frame ("From ___, I learned
  that ___."); allow one source to start.
- **Extension:** Gather from three or more sources, and note a place where two sources disagree
  and what you would check next.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation/portfolio):** Does each note card record a key fact and name its
  source, drawn from more than one source?
- **Self-check:** The learner asks, "For every fact I wrote, could I point to which source it
  came from — and did I use more than one source?"

## Home connection

Ask two different people at home the same question — and compare their answers, noticing which
facts they agree on.
