# Lesson 15 — Present My Report

## Summary

Learners deliver the **capstone** of the unit: they present the short report they researched
and wrote, using the skills of the whole unit — a clear **main idea**, **supporting facts**, a
**conclusion**, their **own words**, and **credit to sources**. The audience practices
**paraphrasing a point back** and **asking a clarifying question**, and learners celebrate the
many languages and writing systems honored along the way.

## Objectives

- Paraphrase a speaker's points accurately and ask clarifying questions when unsure.
  _(D04.S1.05.01)_
- Give a short presentation with a clear main idea, supporting reasons, and a conclusion.
  _(D04.S1.05.02)_
- Explain how an author uses structure, such as cause and effect or problem and solution.
  _(D04.S2.05.01)_
- Distinguish fact from opinion and identify an author's purpose and point of view.
  _(D04.S2.05.02)_
- Write for different purposes and audiences, choosing an appropriate style and structure.
  _(D04.S3.05.01)_
- Gather information from several sources, note key facts, and write a short report in their own
  words. _(D04.S3.05.02)_
- Recognize that many writing systems exist and identify a few. _(D04.S4.05.01)_
- Describe how language is tied to culture, such as words for family, food, or respect.
  _(D04.S4.05.02)_

## Connection

You have spent this unit becoming a listener who paraphrases and asks, a reader who sees
structure and purpose, a writer who matches words to a reader, and a researcher who honors
sources and languages. Today all of it comes together in one act as old as human speech:
**sharing what you know with others — standing or seated** — and listening while they share
back. A report is not finished until it reaches someone else's ears.

## Materials

- Presentation check poster
- The learner's report from Lesson 12
- A listening-and-question guide

## Preparation

- Print or draw the presentation check poster (main idea, facts, conclusion, own words, credit).
- Have learners bring their short report from Lesson 12.
- Copy the listening-and-question guide.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). This is the unit's **summative performance** —
the moment learners show what they can do, not to be ranked but to be seen and celebrated.
Keep audiences small and warm so every voice is heard; rotate a peer questioner. Listeners
practice the unit's listening skills (paraphrase + clarifying question), so *everyone* is doing
the work, not just the presenter. Honor home languages: presenting in a home language is
welcome, and a learner may tell how their report relates to their language and its writing
system. Assess for growth and mastery, never against one another (`docs/philosophy.md` §8).
See `docs/facilitation.md` on assessing without ranking.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Look back over this unit: you paraphrased and asked questions, built a
   presentation, saw how authors structure and persuade, wrote for real readers, researched
   several sources, wrote in your own words, and honored many languages and scripts. Today you
   **present your report**.
2. **Check your presentation (10 min).** Use the poster. Does your presentation have a clear
   **main idea**, **supporting facts** from several sources, a **conclusion**, your **own
   words**, and **credit** to your sources? Rehearse it once, softly, to yourself.
3. **Present (20 min).** In a small group, take turns presenting your report. Speak so your
   audience can hear and follow. If your report relates to your home language or script, tell
   them that too.
4. **Listen well (15 min).** When a classmate presents, your job is the unit's first skill:
   **paraphrase one point back** in your own words, and **ask one clarifying question**. This
   is how a listener honors a speaker.
5. **Close and celebrate (10 min).** Notice what you can do now that you could not do before
   this unit. Thank one presenter whose words you understood better because you listened well.
   Your voice, your words, and your respect for every language are part of one skill: being a
   person who communicates with care.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Present one fact plus a credit line; allow the learner to point to their report
  instead of speaking — via pointing, signing, or a device — and to present while seated; a
  partner may voice or present alongside them if invited.
- **Extension:** Field follow-up questions that compare sources, structure, and word choice, and
  explain the choices you made as a writer.

## Assessment

- **Summative (performance/portfolio):** Does the presentation show a clear main idea,
  supporting facts from several sources, a conclusion, the learner's own words, and credit —
  and does the learner paraphrase and ask a clarifying question as a listener?
- **Self-check:** The learner asks, "Did my audience get my main idea, and did I help a
  classmate by saying their point back and asking one good question?"

## Home connection

Present your report to someone at home, and ask them to tell you one thing they'll remember —
then say it back to check you heard them.
