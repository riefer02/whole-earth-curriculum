# Lesson 4 — Rights Belong to Every Child

## Summary

Learners explain **why rights apply to every child, everywhere**. They see that the
needs rights protect — play, learn, be safe, be heard — belong to every child, no
matter who or where, so the promise that protects them must belong to every child too.
They test this against scenarios that vary one feature at a time, and separate the
*promise* (rights apply to all) from the *fact* (not everywhere honors them yet).

## Objectives

- Explain why rights apply to all children, everywhere. _(D09.S2.05.01)_

## Connection

A child in a tall city building, a child in a fishing village, a child who uses a
wheelchair, a child who speaks a language few others know — different lives, same
needs. Each one needs to play, learn, be safe, and be heard. Because the *need* belongs
to every child, the *right* that protects it belongs to every child too. Your rights do
not depend on who you are or where you live.

## Materials

- Rights-for-every-child poster
- Every-child scenario cards
- Every-child promise page

## Preparation

- Print or draw the rights-for-every-child poster (diverse children, same rights card).
- Copy scenario cards for each pair and a promise page for each learner.
- Arrange a circle for the standing version if going low-tech.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land is **universality**:
rights apply to every child because the needs they protect are shared by every child.
Two careful distinctions keep the lesson honest. First, **evidence vs. values**
(`docs/philosophy.md` §5): *that* the UNCRC says rights apply to every child is a fact;
*that they should* is a value held by the countries that signed — teach it as a shared
promise, not a law of nature. Second, and crucially for emotional safety: **"applies to
all" is not the same as "is honored everywhere."** A right can be real and still be
broken somewhere; that is exactly why people keep working. Name this gently and attach
it to hope and agency, never to a child's shame. See `docs/facilitation.md`.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Last time you learned what a right is. Try to recall it in your
   own words. (Pause.) Now a new question: *who* does a right belong to?
2. **Meet universality (10 min).** Look at the poster. Six children, all different, all
   holding the *same* card: **rights**. Why the same card? Because the needs a right
   protects — to **play, learn, be safe, and be heard** — belong to *every* child. So the
   promise that protects those needs belongs to every child too. That is what
   **universal** means: *for every one*.
3. **Test it: does the right still apply? (15 min).** Take your scenario cards. Each
   names a child by one feature — where they live, how their body works, what language
   they speak, who their family is. Ask: *does the right to learn (or play, or be safe)
   still apply to this child?* If you say yes, say why: *because they are a child, and
   the need belongs to every child.* Notice which features change **nothing** about the
   right.
4. **Two different things (10 min).** Here is a careful idea. "Rights **apply to all**"
   is a *promise* — the countries that signed the UNCRC agreed it. "Rights are **honored
   everywhere**" is a *fact* about the world — and it is not fully true yet. A right can
   be real even where it is still being broken. That gap is not a reason to give up; it
   is the reason people keep working.
5. **Write your promise page (5 min).** Finish the sentence: "Rights belong to every
   child because ___." Then list one right that belongs to each child in your scenarios.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide the sentence starter and three right-word choices (play, learn, be
  safe); answer scenarios aloud before writing.
- **Extension:** Find a real example where a right is not yet honored somewhere, and say
  what "still applies" means in that case.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner say why a right applies to a child who
  differs from them, using the "shared need" reason?
- **Self-check:** The learner asks, "Did I confuse 'applies to all' with 'honored
  everywhere'? Can I tell them apart?"

## Home connection

Talk with someone at home about one right every child has, and one place where it is
still not honored — and one thing people do to help close that gap.
