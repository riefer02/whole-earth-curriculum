# Lesson 5 — Fairness: Honored or Denied?

## Summary

Learners compare **how fairness was or was not honored** in story and historical
examples, and **who benefited**. They learn two analysis questions — *was fairness
honored or denied? who benefited, and who was left out?* — apply them to a worked
example, then analyze example cards in pairs and record their own comparisons.

## Objectives

- Compare how fairness was or was not honored in a story or historical example, and who
  benefited. _(D09.S2.05.02)_

## Connection

Three children, three bowls of food. Fairness honored: each gets a bowl — all benefit.
Fairness denied: one child holds all three bowls while two hold empty hands — one gains,
others are left out. The same two questions work everywhere, in a school yard or a whole
society: *was fairness honored or denied? and who benefited?* Asking them is how we see
clearly.

## Materials

- Fairness-two-panels poster
- Fairness example cards
- Fairness analysis page

## Preparation

- Print or draw the fairness-two-panels poster (honored vs. denied, who benefited).
- Copy example cards for each pair and an analysis page for each learner.
- Prepare one historical example with a clear 'who benefited' answer for the whole group.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The skill to land is the **two-question
fairness analysis**: *was fairness honored or denied? who benefited, and who was left
out?* Model it once as a worked example before independent practice (Kirschner, Sweller
& Clark, 2006). Choose historical examples that are **true, age-appropriate, and
non-shaming** — the point is to see the pattern, not to make any group or learner feel
blamed. Distinguish **evidence from values**: *what happened and who benefited* are facts
we can investigate; *that fairness should be honored* is a value held by this curriculum
and by the children it serves. Keep both questions open, and always attach the "denied"
examples to hope — fairness can be repaired, and people do repair it. See
`docs/facilitation.md`.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** You have learned what rights are and that they belong to every
   child. Today a closely linked idea: **fairness** — and how to tell when it was honored
   and when it was denied.
2. **Meet the two questions (10 min).** Look at the poster. Two panels. In one, fairness
   is **honored**: everyone gets a fair turn — all benefit. In the other, fairness is
   **denied**: one person gains, others are left out. Whenever you look at a story or a
   piece of history, ask just two questions: *Was fairness honored or denied?* and *Who
   benefited — and who was left out?*
3. **Worked example (10 min).** Build one together. *Story:* "In a village, one well is
   for everyone. A new rule says only one family may use it." Ask the two questions
   together. Was fairness honored or denied? (Denied.) Who benefited? (That one family.)
   Who was left out? (Everyone else who needs water.) Say it in one sentence.
4. **Practice with a partner (10 min).** Take an example card. It might be a story, or a
   true piece of history — a school door shut to some children, or a harvest shared
   fairly. Ask the two questions, then state your answer: "Fairness was ___ because ___;
   the people who benefited were ___, and those left out were ___."
5. **Record your own (10 min).** On your page, write or draw two examples — one where
   fairness was honored, one where it was denied — and for each, who benefited and who
   was left out.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a card with the two answers partly filled in and a sentence
  starter; work one example aloud first.
- **Extension:** Compare two "denied" examples and explain which denied fairness more
  deeply, and how you can tell.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner state, for a given example, whether
  fairness was honored or denied, and who benefited and who was left out?
- **Peer check:** Partners check each other: "Did you name *who benefited* and *who was
  left out*, not only 'fair or unfair'?"

## Home connection

Think of one moment at home or nearby where fairness was honored — and one where it was
not — and ask who benefited in each.
