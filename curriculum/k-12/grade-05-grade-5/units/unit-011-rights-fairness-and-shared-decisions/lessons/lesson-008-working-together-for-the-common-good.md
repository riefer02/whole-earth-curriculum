# Lesson 8 — Working Together for the Common Good

## Summary

Learners **plan and carry out a small collective action for the common good** — a
cleanup, a kindness campaign, or another small act of shared care — and **reflect** on
what made it work. They follow a **plan-do-reflect** cycle, assign roles, act together,
and record what they learned, producing the unit's **collective-action plan and
reflection**.

## Objectives

- Plan and carry out a small collective action for the common good, and reflect on what
  made it work. _(D09.S3.05.02)_

## Connection

A littered path no single person wants to clean alone. Ten people, ten minutes — done.
**Collective action** is people working together on something that helps *everyone* — the
**common good**. One person's small act is a drop; many drops together are a river. Today
you will plan one, do one, and think about what made it work.

## Materials

- Plan-do-reflect poster
- Collective-action plan page
- Reflection page

## Preparation

- Print or draw the plan-do-reflect poster.
- Copy a plan page for each team and a reflection page for each learner.
- Prepare one or two ready, no-cost action options (a space tidy, a kindness round, a
  litter walk, helping a younger group) so teams can choose quickly.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The skill to land is the **plan-do-reflect
cycle applied to a real, small, doable collective action for the common good**. Keep the
action genuinely small and completable within the session or one short follow-up window —
the reflection matters as much as the deed. This is a *skill* (planning, dividing roles,
following through), so model one worked plan briefly before teams plan their own
(Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006). The **common good** framing is a value of this
curriculum (that acting for everyone's benefit is worth doing) — name it as a value, not
a fact. Make reflection honest and non-judging: *what worked* and *what we would change*
are information, never blame. Carry the **technology & environment lens** honestly: people
organize and communicate to act together in different ways — face to face, on a shared
board, or through digital tools — and each way shapes who can take part and how far the
action reaches; the shared environment is itself part of the common good (a tidied space,
watered plants, a cleared path). Name tools as helpers of people, never replacements for
them. See `docs/facilitation.md`.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** You have learned how decisions get made. Today you *make*
   something good happen — together. Try to recall: what is one thing a group can do
   that helps everyone, not just one person? (Pause.)
2. **Meet the cycle (10 min).** Look at the poster. **Plan** — choose a small action and
   decide who does what. **Do** — carry it out together. **Reflect** — ask what worked
   and what you would change. The goal is the **common good**: something that helps
   everyone, not just the doer. People organize to act in different ways — talking in a
   circle, writing on a shared board, or using digital tools to reach more people — and
   the common good often includes our shared environment: a tidied space, watered plants,
   a cleared path.
3. **Worked example (5 min).** Build a tiny plan together: *action* — tidy the shared
   reading corner. *Roles* — one person gathers books, one dusts, one checks the floor.
   *Steps* — gather, sort, sweep, return. Watch how a plan turns a big job into small
   parts.
4. **Plan and do (20 min).** In your team, choose your small action for the common good.
   Fill your plan page: the action, the goal, the roles, the steps. Then **carry it
   out**. Work together; if a step changes, adjust the plan — that is part of doing.
5. **Reflect (5 min).** Sit together and fill your reflection page: *What worked? What
   surprised us? What would we change next time?* Say, sign, write, or draw one thing to
   share with the group.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer a pre-planned action with roles already listed; the learner does one
  step and completes a simple two-line reflection.
- **Extension:** Add a "what could go wrong and how we will handle it" step to the plan
  before acting.
- **Seated & non-speaking participation:** Choose a seated, no-walking action (a
  kindness-notes round, a thank-you poster, a picture story read to a younger group) and
  assign seated, non-verbal roles such as scribe, illustrator, sorter, or greeter (wave
  or hold up a card). A learner may plan and reflect entirely by writing, drawing, or
  pointing to picture cards — no speech or standing is required to take part.

## Assessment

- **Summative (performance/portfolio):** Did the team plan an action for the common good,
  carry it out, and reflect — naming at least one thing that worked and one thing to
  change? The plan page and reflection page are kept as artifacts.
- **Self-check:** The learner asks, "Did our action help everyone, not just us? Did I do
  my part? Can I name what worked and what I would change?"

## Home connection

With someone at home, do one small thing for the shared space or neighborhood — then
talk about what worked and what you would change.
