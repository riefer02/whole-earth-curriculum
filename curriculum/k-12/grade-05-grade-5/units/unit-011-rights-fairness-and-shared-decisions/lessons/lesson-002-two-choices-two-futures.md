# Lesson 2 — Two Choices, Two Futures

## Summary

Learners practice **comparing two possible choices and their consequences for others
before deciding**. They meet a simple tool — the **decision fork** — analyze a worked
example, then work through choice scenarios in pairs and record their own two-futures
comparison.

## Objectives

- Compare two possible choices and their consequences for others before deciding.
  _(D09.S1.05.02)_

## Connection

You find a wallet on the path. Choice A: return it. Choice B: keep it. Before you
decide, you can look down each road and ask: *what happens to the other person?* The
owner gets their money and papers back — or loses what they need. Comparing the
consequences **for others**, not just for yourself, is how careful decisions are made —
at a market stall, on a shared trail, or anywhere your choice touches someone else.

## Materials

- Decision-fork poster
- Choice scenarios
- Two-futures record page

## Preparation

- Print or draw the decision-fork poster (one situation, two branches, consequences for
  others under each).
- Copy choice scenarios for each pair and a two-futures record page for each learner.
- Prepare one spoken scenario for the opening if going low-tech.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The skill to land is **comparing two
choices by their consequences for others before deciding** — a reasoning routine, so
model it as a worked example before independent practice (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark,
2006). Keep the emphasis on **consequences for others**, not just self-interest; that
egalitarian move — *who else is affected?* — is the heart of the standard. Do not mark
any learner's genuine choice as "wrong"; instead ask whether they *considered* the
consequences for others before deciding. Distinguish **evidence from values**: *what the
consequences are* is something we can observe and compare; *which consequence matters
most* is a value judgment. Hold it open. See `docs/facilitation.md`.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Last time you saw that values shape choices. Today you practice
   the next step: **before** you decide, compare two choices by what they do **to other
   people**.
2. **Meet the decision fork (10 min).** Look at the poster. A situation sits at the top.
   Two branches split down from it — Choice A and Choice B. Under each branch, a bubble
   names the **consequences for others**. That fork is a thinking tool: you walk each
   branch in your mind before you pick one.
3. **Worked example (10 min).** Build one together. *Situation:* "Sam borrowed Maya's
   tool and broke it." Choice A: tell Maya and offer to fix it. Choice B: hide it and say
   nothing. Walk each branch: what happens **for Maya** in each? For the friendship? Then
   decide together, and say why. Notice: you compared consequences *for another person*
   before deciding.
4. **Practice with a partner (10 min).** Take a scenario. Draw or say the fork: two
   choices, and under each, the consequences **for others**. Then decide together and
   explain your choice in one sentence: "We chose ___ because for the other person it
   means ___."
5. **Your own fork (10 min).** On your page, write a real or invented situation, draw two
   branches, and fill in the consequences for others under each. Then mark the choice you
   would make and why.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a pre-drawn fork with one branch filled in; the learner completes
  the second branch and the consequences-for-others bubbles.
- **Extension:** Add a third choice to the fork, or weigh *which* consequence matters most
  and defend that judgment.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name two choices and describe a consequence
  *for another person* for each, before deciding?
- **Peer check:** Partners check each other's fork: "Did you name a consequence for
  someone *else*, not only for yourself?"

## Home connection

Next time a choice at home affects someone else, stop and name two options and one
consequence for the other person for each — then decide.
