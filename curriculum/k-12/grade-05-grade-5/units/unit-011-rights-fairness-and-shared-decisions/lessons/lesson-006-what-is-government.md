# Lesson 6 — What Is Government?

## Summary

Learners explain **what government is** — the people and groups who make and carry out
decisions and rules for a whole community or country — and **what it does**: make rules
and laws, provide services, settle disputes, and protect people and rights. They sort
everyday examples, meet government at several scales (class, village, town, country),
and notice that governments take many different forms around the world.

## Objectives

- Explain what government is and what it does. _(D09.S3.05.01)_

## Connection

Who makes sure the road is repaired, the school runs, and clean water reaches the tap?
Not one person, but a group whose job is to make and carry out decisions for *everyone*
in a place — a **government**. It is at work all around you, every day, in quiet ways:
a rule about traffic, a shared water supply, a place to learn. Understanding it is
understanding how a whole community lives together.

## Materials

- What-government-does poster
- Who-does-what cards
- Levels of government page

## Preparation

- Print or draw the what-government-does poster (center + four branches).
- Copy card sets for each group and a levels page for each learner.
- Prepare one local example (a road, a school, a water supply) to open the discussion.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The ideas to land: **government is the
people and groups who make and carry out decisions/rules for a whole community or
country**, and it **does four kinds of things** — make rules and laws, provide services,
settle disputes, and protect people and rights. Teach this **descriptively, not as one
model**: governments around the world take many forms, and none is assumed to be "the"
right one (this is the unit's no-single-model commitment). Distinguish **evidence from
values**: *what governments do and what forms they take* are facts we can observe and
compare; *what they should do* — including this curriculum's egalitarian view that they
should serve everyone — is a value. Hold it open. See `docs/facilitation.md`.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** You have learned how to see fairness. Now a bigger question: who
   looks after the *whole* community — the rules, the roads, the schools? That is the
   idea of **government**. Try to recall: what do you already think it is? (Pause.)
2. **Meet the idea (10 min).** A **government** is the people and groups who make and
   carry out **decisions and rules for a whole community or country**. It is not one
   person, and it is not the same everywhere — but every large group of people needs
   some way to decide together, and that way is called government.
3. **What it does (15 min).** Look at the poster. Government does four kinds of things:
   **make rules and laws** (so people know how to live together safely); **provide
   services** (roads, schools, clean water, health care, safety); **settle disputes**
   (help people resolve disagreements fairly); and **protect people and rights** (so
   every person is safe and treated with worth). Sort your cards: which of these four
   does each example belong to?
4. **Many levels, many forms (10 min).** Government is not only national. It works at
   many scales: your **class** makes rules together; a **village or town** decides about
   a shared well or path; a **country** makes laws for everyone. And around the world,
   governments take **many different forms** — some are led by councils, some by
   assemblies, some by a single leader. Next lesson you will compare a few.
5. **Record (5 min).** On your page, name one thing each level of government helps
   decide or do — class, village, town, country.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a word bank (rule, service, dispute, protect) and picture prompts
  for the four branches.
- **Extension:** Name one need a government should help meet, and one thing it should
  *not* decide, with a reason for each.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner say what government is in their own words
  and name at least two things it does?
- **Self-check:** The learner asks, "Can I name the four things government does, and give
  a real example of each?"

## Home connection

On the way home or nearby, notice one thing a government helps provide (a road, a tap,
a school) and one rule it makes — and tell someone what you found.
