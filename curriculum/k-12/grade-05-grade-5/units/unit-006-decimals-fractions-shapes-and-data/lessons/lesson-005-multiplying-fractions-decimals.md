# Lesson 5 — Multiplying Fractions and Decimals in Real Life

## Summary

Learners **multiply fractions and decimals** in real contexts. They find a **fraction of a
group** (1/4 of 12 = 3), **double a recipe** amount (1 1/2 × 2 = 3), multiply a **fraction by
a fraction** using an **area model** (1/2 × 1/3 = 1/6), and multiply a **decimal by a whole
number** with money (0.5 × 6 = 3).

## Objectives

- Multiply fractions and decimals in real contexts such as sharing, cooking, and money, using
  models and reasoning. _(D05.S1.05.02)_

## Connection

A recipe serves 4, but you are cooking for 8, so you **double** it: every 1/2 cup becomes 1
cup. Or a farmer keeps **1/4** of 12 eggs and sells the rest — that is 3 eggs kept. Or you buy
**6** half-kilo bags of rice: 6 × 0.5 = **3 kilos**. Multiplying fractions and decimals is the
math of **scaling up and finding parts**, done in kitchens, fields, and markets around the
world.

## Materials

- Area-model grids
- Real-life multiplication cards
- Multiplication recording page

## Preparation

- Copy area-model grids for pairs and multiplication cards for groups.
- Copy the recording page.
- Have worked examples ready: 1/4 of 12; 1/2 × 1/3; 0.5 × 6.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The ideas to land: (1) "a fraction of a group"
means multiply — 1/4 of 12 = 12 ÷ 4 = 3; (2) **fraction × fraction** uses an **area model**
where the product is the overlapping region (1/2 × 1/3 = 1/6); and (3) **decimal × whole
number** works like whole-number multiplication, then place the decimal point in the product.
Model each as a **worked example**, then guide a second together (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark,
2006). Common slips: adding instead of multiplying, and misplacing the decimal point (0.5 × 6 =
30 instead of 3). Retrieval: ask learners to recall the last two lessons' fraction and decimal
skills and reuse them to check answers. Multiplication here is evidence; the habit of scaling
fairly (so a doubled recipe feeds everyone equally) is a fairness value (`docs/philosophy.md`
§4).

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Recall: what is 1/2 + 1/4? Today you multiply fractions and decimals —
   the math of doubling recipes and finding parts.
2. **Fraction of a group (10 min).** "A farmer keeps **1/4 of 12** eggs." This means multiply:
   divide the 12 eggs into 4 equal groups (3 each) and take 1 group: **1/4 of 12 = 3**. Draw 12
   dots in 4 rows to see it.
3. **Fraction × fraction with area (10 min).** What is **1/2 × 1/3**? Draw a rectangle, split
   it into 3 columns (thirds) and 2 rows (halves) — 6 cells. Shade 1/3 (one column). Then shade
   1/2 of that (one of the two rows). The overlap is **1 cell out of 6**, so **1/2 × 1/3 =
   1/6**.
4. **Decimal × whole number (10 min).** You buy **6** half-kilo bags of rice. Multiply 0.5 × 6
   = 3.0, or **3 kilos**. Think: six halves make three wholes. Multiply as whole numbers (5 × 6
   = 30), then place the decimal point: **3.0**.
5. **Practice with a partner (10 min).** Take your multiplication cards. For each, draw the
   model (dots, area, or groups), compute, and check each other: did you multiply, not add? Is
   the decimal point placed correctly?
6. **Close (5 min).** Multiplying fractions and decimals always answers "how much is part of a
   part, or several parts?" Draw the model first, and the answer will make sense.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use only fraction-of-a-group and decimal-times-whole problems with easy numbers;
  keep models pre-drawn. Describe each step aloud and use tactile grids so learners with low
  vision can follow by touch.
- **Extension:** Multiply mixed numbers (1 1/2 × 2 1/4) and decimals to hundredths, estimating
  the product before computing.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation/self):** Can the learner find a fraction of a group, multiply a
  fraction by a fraction with an area model, and multiply a decimal by a whole number
  correctly?
- **Self-check:** The learner asks, "Did I multiply (not add)? Can I show 1/2 × 1/3 = 1/6 with
  an area model, and is my decimal point in the right place?"

## Home connection

While cooking, find a recipe amount and double or halve it; while shopping, find the price of
several items that cost a fraction or decimal each and multiply.

## Resources

- Fraction and decimal multiplication models (area model, fraction of a group) are standard in
  elementary mathematics; see John A. Van de Walle, *Elementary and Middle School Mathematics*
  (10th ed., 2019). The fairness value of scaling so everyone gets an equal share is a
  commitment (`docs/philosophy.md` §4).
