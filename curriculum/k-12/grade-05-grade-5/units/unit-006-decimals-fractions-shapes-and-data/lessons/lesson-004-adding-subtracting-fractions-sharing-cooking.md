# Lesson 4 — Adding and Subtracting Fractions in Sharing and Cooking

## Summary

Learners **add** and **subtract fractions** in real sharing and cooking contexts. With **like
denominators** they combine or take away parts directly (1/4 + 2/4 = 3/4). With **unlike
denominators** they first rename using **equivalent fractions** (1/2 = 2/4, so 1/2 + 1/4 =
3/4). Bar models and paper folding make the renaming visible.

## Objectives

- Add and subtract fractions in real sharing and cooking contexts, using like denominators
  and simple equivalent-fraction renaming. _(D05.S1.05.02)_

## Connection

You are cooking with a grown-up. The recipe calls for **1/2 cup** of rice and **1/4 cup** of
lentils. How much grain is that altogether? Or you share a flatbread: you eat **3/4** of it
and give **1/4** to a friend — how much did you keep? Adding and subtracting fractions is the
math of **sharing and cooking**, done the same way in a home in Cairo, a farm kitchen in the
Andes, or a soup kitchen serving a neighborhood.

## Materials

- Fraction bar strips
- Recipe fraction cards
- Fraction recording page

## Preparation

- Copy fraction bar strips for pairs and recipe cards for groups.
- Copy the recording page.
- Have worked examples ready: 1/2 + 1/4 = 3/4; 3/4 − 1/4 = 1/2.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The ideas to land: (1) with **like
denominators**, add or subtract the numerators and keep the denominator (the size of the
pieces does not change); (2) with **unlike denominators**, rename to a common denominator using
equivalent fractions before combining. Model each as a **worked example with bar models**
first, then guide a second together (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006). The most common slip
is adding denominators too (1/2 + 1/4 = 2/6); the bar model shows why that is wrong — the
pieces are not the same size. Retrieval: ask learners to recall decimal–fraction equivalence
(0.5 = 1/2) and reuse it to check that 1/2 + 1/4 = 3/4 = 0.75. Sharing food fairly is a
fairness value woven through the unit (`docs/philosophy.md` §4); the arithmetic itself is
evidence.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Recall: what fraction is the same as 0.5? Today you add and subtract
   fractions — the math of sharing and cooking.
2. **The rule (5 min).** To add or subtract fractions, the pieces must be the **same size** —
   same denominator. Then add or subtract the **numerators** and keep the denominator.
3. **Worked example — like denominators (10 min).** You eat **3/4** of a flatbread and give
   **1/4** to a friend. How much did you keep? Same denominator (fourths): 3/4 − 1/4 = 2/4,
   which is the same as **1/2**. Model it with bars: shade 3 fourths, erase 1, see 2 fourths.
4. **Worked example — unlike denominators (10 min).** A recipe needs **1/2 cup** rice plus
   **1/4 cup** lentils. The pieces differ, so rename: 1/2 = **2/4**. Now 2/4 + 1/4 = **3/4**.
   Build it with bars: a half bar equals two quarter bars.
5. **Practice with a partner (15 min).** Take your recipe cards. For each, draw or build the
   bar model, rename if needed, and find the sum or difference. Check each other: are the
   pieces the same size before you combine?
6. **Close (5 min).** Fractions are about **equal parts**. Add or subtract them only when the
   parts are the same size — rename first if they are not. That one idea unlocks all of
   sharing and cooking math.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use only halves and fourths; provide pre-cut bars and keep denominators the
  same. Describe each step aloud and use raised/tactile bars so learners with low vision can
  follow by touch.
- **Extension:** Add and subtract mixed numbers (1 1/2 + 3/4) and explain each renaming step.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation/self):** Can the learner add and subtract fractions with like
  denominators, and rename to add unlike denominators like 1/2 and 1/4, using a model?
- **Self-check:** The learner asks, "Did I keep the pieces the same size? Can I show 1/2 + 1/4
  = 3/4 with bars?"

## Home connection

While helping cook or share food, find a fraction (half a cup, a quarter of a loaf) and add or
subtract it with another to name the total.
