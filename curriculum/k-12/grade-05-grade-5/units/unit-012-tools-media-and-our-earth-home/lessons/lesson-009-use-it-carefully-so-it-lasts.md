# Lesson 9 — Use It Carefully So It Lasts

## Summary

Learners explain what it means to use a **resource carefully so it lasts** and give one example
from their own life. A worked example contrasts careless water use (tap left running) with
careful use (tap off, a bucket, leaks fixed), landing the rule: **use less than comes back, and
it lasts**. Learners then choose water, food, paper, electricity, or fuel/wood and write one
careful use from their own life, connecting it to **fairness** for people today and for people
not born yet.

## Objectives

- Explain what it means to use a resource carefully so it lasts, and give one example from
  their own life. _(D10.S4.05.02)_

## Connection

If you eat the last of the rice today, there is none for tomorrow — unless more is growing. A
resource lasts when we **use less than comes back**: we take what we need, we do not waste, and
we leave some for later. The water you turn off, the paper you reuse, the food you do not throw
away — each is a small choice that keeps a resource going for the next person, and the next
generation. Today you name the choice you can make in your own life.

## Materials

- Careful-use poster
- Resource cards
- My-resource page
- Pencils

## Preparation

- Print or draw the careful-use poster.
- Copy resource cards for each pair and a my-resource page for each learner.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land is the **"use less than comes
back" rule** and its fairness reason: careful use is fair to people today and to future people.
Model the water example fully as a worked example, then have learners choose their own
resource and their own-life example. This builds on Grade 3 (renewable vs. non-renewable
resources) and on this unit's cycle and stewardship lessons. Distinguish evidence from values:
*that resources can be used up or wasted* is observable; *that we ought to use them fairly,
across today and tomorrow* is a value we hold. Honor that careful use looks different in
different lives — a learner without a tap cannot "turn off the tap," so keep examples broad
(water, food, paper, fuel, electricity) and let each learner pick what fits their life. See
`docs/facilitation.md`.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Recall the water cycle: water keeps going around. But what about the
   water *you* use — and food, paper, fuel, and electricity? Some can be used up if we are not
   careful. Today you learn what "careful" means.
2. **Meet careful use (12 min).** Look at the poster. **Careless use:** the tap runs, water
   spills, less is left for later. **Careful use:** the tap is off, a bucket catches what we
   need, leaks are fixed — the same water serves today and tomorrow. The rule: **use less than
   comes back, and it lasts.** This is fairness too: careful use leaves something for the next
   person, and the next generation.
3. **Choose your own resource (18 min).** Pick a resource you use every day — **water, food,
   paper, electricity, or fuel/wood**. On your my-resource page, write or draw one **careless
   use** and one **careful use** from your own life, and one sentence on why it matters. (If
   you have no tap, choose food or paper or fuel instead.)
4. **Share with a partner (10 min).** Tell your partner: "I use ___; a careful way I can use it
   is ___, so it lasts for ___." Ask: is the careful use real, and could you actually do it
   starting today?
5. **Close (5 min).** Careful use is not about doing without — it is about leaving enough for
   everyone, now and later. One small careful choice, made again and again, is stewardship.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a resource card with the two uses partly filled in and a sentence
   starter; work one resource aloud first.
- **Extension:** Compare two resources and explain which one needs more careful use, and how
   your own choice changes that.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner state the "use less than comes back" idea and
   give one real, own-life example of careful use?
- **Portfolio artifact:** The my-resource page, kept for the year-end celebration.

## Home connection

Choose one resource at home and make one careful-use choice each day this week — turn off a
light, finish your food, reuse a bag. Tell someone why it matters for the future.

## Resources

- The idea that a resource lasts when use stays below what renews or returns is the core of
  sustainable use, taught in age-appropriate terms by UNESCO (Education for Sustainable
  Development) and UNEP. The renewable/non-renewable distinction (Grade 3) and the
  reduce-reuse-recycle ladder (Grade 4) lead into this lesson. The fairness-to-future-generations
  framing follows `docs/philosophy.md` §4 and §12.
