# Lesson 10 — Our Earth Home: A Year-End Celebration

## Summary

The capstone closes the unit — and the grade-5 year — in a celebration of the **whole
person**, the **caring community**, and the **living Earth**. Learners assemble their eight
portfolio artifacts, walk a gallery, and connect each to the big question, then name one thing
they will do as **stewards**. The celebration is retrieval and synthesis, not re-reading, and
it ends on joy and agency: **looking ahead to Grade 6**.

## Objectives

- Revisit and connect the unit's eight threads — tools changing daily life, two places using a
  tool differently, checking claims, fair reporting, the water cycle, interdependence,
  pollution and action, and careful resource use — into one caring picture. _(D10.S1.05.01,
  D10.S1.05.02, D10.S2.05.01, D10.S2.05.02, D10.S3.05.01, D10.S3.05.02, D10.S4.05.01,
  D10.S4.05.02)_

## Connection

A home is not one room but many rooms under one roof. All year, and all through this unit, you
have been building rooms of your own — a tool traced from before to after, two places compared
with respect, a claim checked, a message made fair, a water cycle drawn, a ripple traced, a
problem named with a fix, and a resource used carefully. Today you walk through your whole
home, admire every room, and see how it all fits together — because you are not just a guest
in this world; you are someone who helps care for it.

## Materials

- Portfolio artifacts (the learner's own pages)
- Earth-home celebration web poster
- Capstone card
- Pencils and art supplies

## Preparation

- Have each learner gather their artifacts from this unit (before–after chain, two-places
  comparison, claim-check page, fair-reporting page, water-cycle diagram, ripple page,
  pollution-and-action page, my-resource page).
- Print or draw the Earth-home celebration web poster.
- Copy a capstone card for each learner, and arrange a gallery wall or story path.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). This is the unit's **capstone and summative
assessment**, and the **year-end celebration** — a moment of retrieval and synthesis, not
re-reading. Keep the tone **joyful, collective, and hopeful**: success is shared, mastery is
individual, and no one is ranked. Land on **agency and celebration** — the learner is a whole
person in a caring community on a living Earth, and a steward who can act. If a learner's
artifacts are incomplete, let them finish one now rather than be judged "done" or "not done."
This lesson completes the unit's portfolio and looks ahead to Grade 6. See
`docs/facilitation.md` on assessing without ranking.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Look back with a partner: **what did we learn this unit?** Take turns
   recalling one thing each — a tool's before and after, two places using one tool, a claim
   checked, a fair message, water's cycle, a ripple, a problem with a fix, a careful use. This
   is retrieval: pulling it out of memory, not re-reading it.
2. **Assemble your rooms (15 min).** Lay out your eight artifacts in order: your before–after
   chain, two-places comparison, claim-check page, fair-reporting page, water-cycle diagram,
   ripple page, pollution-and-action page, and my-resource page. Check each is complete; finish
   any that need it.
3. **Walk the gallery (20 min).** Display the artifacts and walk the gallery. At each page,
   notice what it shows about our Earth home. Then, on your capstone card, answer three
   prompts: **Choose one artifact** you are proud of. **Say how it connects** to the big
   question — how tools, media, and the living Earth come together in one caring home. **Name
   one thing you will do** as a steward.
4. **Close the circle (15 min).** Gather. Each learner shares their chosen artifact and their
   one steward action — one sentence is enough. Listen to how many rooms make up this one home.
5. **Look ahead (5 min).** The year is not the end of the story. In Grade 6, you will go
   deeper into all of it — who designs technology, how to read a flood of information, and how
   living systems recover. You are not only learning about the world; you are learning to care
   for it.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Choose one artifact with the learner and scribe their capstone card; celebrate
   completion of a single page rather than all eight.
- **Extension:** Write a one-paragraph synthesis linking two or more threads (tools, media,
   cycles, interdependence, care) with an example from the unit and one from your own life.

## Assessment

- **Summative (portfolio):** The learner's assembled artifacts — the before–after chain, the
  two-places comparison, the claim-check page, the fair-reporting page, the water-cycle
  diagram, the ripple page, the pollution-and-action page, and the my-resource page — together
  show mastery of the unit's eight standards.
- **Peer/self:** In the circle, learners listen and each names one connection they heard and one
  steward action they will take.
- **Self-check:** The learner asks, "Did I connect my artifact to the big question, and did I
  name a real thing I will do to care for people and the Earth?"

## Home connection

Share your whole portfolio with someone at home — walk them through your rooms like a gallery —
and tell them the one steward action you chose for the year ahead.

## Resources

- This capstone synthesizes the unit's documented content: technology's before-and-after effects
  (World Bank, ITU); mobile money and the many uses of bicycles and water pumps (CGAP, UN
  Water); claim-checking and fair reporting (UNESCO Media and Information Literacy; Common
  Sense Media); the water cycle (USGS, NASA); ecosystem interdependence and ripples (BBC
  Bitesize, NOAA, UNESCO); plastic and air pollution and habitat loss with actions (UNEP, WHO,
  FAO); and careful resource use (UNESCO ESD). Each is cited in its own lesson. The closing
  commitment to the whole person, caring community, and living Earth — and to acting as a
  steward — follows `docs/philosophy.md` §12 and `docs/vision.md`.
