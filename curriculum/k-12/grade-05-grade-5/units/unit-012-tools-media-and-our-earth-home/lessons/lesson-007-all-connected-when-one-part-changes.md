# Lesson 7 — All Connected: When One Part Changes

## Summary

Learners explain how living things in an **ecosystem** depend on one another and what happens
when **one part changes**. A worked example traces a **pond** chain — water plants → small
animals → fish and frogs → birds — and the ripple when the plants die. Learners then trace a
change through a **coral reef, mangrove, savanna, or forest**, learning that a change ripples
outward: change one part, and the rest feel it — and care and restoration can ripple helpfully
too.

## Objectives

- Explain how living things in an ecosystem depend on one another and what can happen when one
  part changes. _(D10.S3.05.02)_

## Connection

Pull one thread in a woven cloth and the whole cloth can pucker. An ecosystem is like that
cloth. In a pond, the plants feed the snails, the snails and insects feed the fish, the fish
feed the frogs and birds — each thread holds the next. When one thread is pulled hard enough,
the rest feel it. Today you learn to trace that ripple, and to see that care can ripple the
other way too.

## Materials

- Ripple-chain poster
- Ecosystem ripple cards
- Ripple page
- Pencils

## Preparation

- Print or draw the ripple-chain poster.
- Copy ecosystem ripple cards for each pair and a ripple page for each learner.
- If using the string activity, have one length of string or yarn ready.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The skill to land is the **ripple trace**: name
the chain of dependence, then follow one change through it ("if ___ changes, then ___, then
___"). Model the pond chain fully as a worked example. This builds on Grade 4 (producers,
consumers, decomposers) and on this unit's food-web and cycle work. Keep it hopeful and
agency-focused: interdependence means harm can ripple, *and so can care and restoration* —
never teach a "doomed ecosystem" without also teaching what can help. Distinguish evidence
from values: *how living things depend on one another* is observable science; *that we should
care for ecosystems* is a value this curriculum holds. See `docs/facilitation.md` and
`docs/philosophy.md` §4–5.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Recall the food web you built earlier this year: energy flows from what
   is eaten to what eats it. Today, a bigger idea — what happens when **one part** of that web
   changes.
2. **Meet the pond ripple (12 min).** Look at the poster. The **water plants** feed the
   **small animals** (snails, insects); the small animals feed the **fish and frogs**; the fish
   and frogs feed the **birds**. Now ask *what if*: if the water plants die, the small animals
   lose food, then the fish and frogs decline, then the birds have less to eat. One change —
   and the whole chain feels it. That is a **ripple**.
3. **Trace your own ripple (18 min).** Choose an ecosystem card — a **coral reef** (if the
   coral bleaches, the fish that live in it decline, then the bigger fish and the fishers who
   depend on them feel it), a **mangrove** (if the mangroves are cut, young fish lose their
   nursery, then the larger fish and the birds decline, and the coast loses its storm shield),
   a **savanna** (if too many grazers are removed, the grass thickens, then fires and insects
   change, then other animals shift), or a **forest** (if the big trees fall, the shade and
   homes for many animals vanish, then the small animals and birds decline). On your ripple
   page, trace the chain and the change.
4. **Check with a partner (10 min).** Say your ripple in one breath: "if ___ changes, then
   ___, then ___." Then ask together: what could help it recover — what care would ripple the
   *good* way?
5. **Close (5 min).** Living things hold each other up. Change one, and the rest feel it —
   and that means your care can ripple too.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a ripple page with the first two boxes filled in, and a sentence
   starter; work one chain aloud first.
- **Extension:** Trace a change that *helps* (a plant restored, a predator returned) as well as
   one that harms, and compare how the two ripples differ.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name a chain of dependence and trace one change
   through it, stating at least two "then" steps?
- **Peer check:** Partners ask each other, "Did you trace the change all the way through — and
   did you name something that could help?"

## Home connection

Pick a small ecosystem near home — a garden, a patch of grass, a pond, a tree. Name who
depends on whom, and ask what would happen if one part changed.
