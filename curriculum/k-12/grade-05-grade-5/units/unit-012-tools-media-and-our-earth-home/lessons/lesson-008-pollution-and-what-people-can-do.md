# Lesson 8 — Pollution, and What People Can Do

## Summary

Learners describe a form of **pollution** or **habitat loss** and name one thing people can
do to reduce it. A worked example traces **plastic** from a dropped bag or bottle through a
river to the ocean, where an animal mistakes it for food or gets tangled — then turns to what
people can do: **refuse, reuse, recycle, and join a clean-up**. Learners then choose **air
pollution** or **habitat loss**, trace its pathway, and name a real action, holding both truths
at once: *the problem is real, and so are the fixes*.

## Objectives

- Describe a form of pollution or habitat loss and one thing people can do to reduce it.
  _(D10.S4.05.01)_

## Connection

You have probably seen a wrapper or bottle lying where it should not be — by a path, in a
drain, near a river. That one piece has a journey: into the water, out toward the sea, and into
the life of an animal that mistakes it for food. The good news is that the journey can be
stopped before it starts — by people who refuse, reuse, recycle, and clean up. Today you learn
to trace the problem *and* the fix.

## Materials

- Pollution-pathway poster
- Pollution-and-action cards
- Action page
- Pencils

## Preparation

- Print or draw the pollution-pathway poster.
- Copy pollution-and-action cards for each pair and an action page for each learner.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The skill to land is the **pathway-and-action
move**: name the form of pollution or habitat loss, trace its path to the harm, then name a
real action people can take. Model the plastic example fully as a worked example. Keep the
tone **hopeful and agency-focused**, never doom: always pair the problem with a real, doable
fix and with the fact that people everywhere are already acting (clean-ups, reforestation,
cleaner stoves, recycling). Distinguish evidence from values: *what pollution does and how it
travels* is observable science; *that we should reduce harm to other beings and future people*
is a value we hold. Avoid shaming any learner or region for waste they did not choose. See
`docs/facilitation.md` and `docs/philosophy.md` §12.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Recall from earlier grades: reduce, reuse, recycle. Today you add the
   *why* — what pollution and habitat loss actually do — and the *what* — one real thing people
   can do.
2. **Meet the plastic pathway (12 min).** Look at the poster. A **plastic bag or bottle** is
   dropped or blown away → it washes **into the river** → it flows **out to the ocean** → an
   **animal mistakes it for food or gets tangled**. That is the harm. Now the fix — **what
   people can do:** refuse single-use plastic, reuse bags and bottles, recycle what you can,
   and join a clean-up. One action stops the journey before it starts.
3. **Practice with a partner (18 min).** Take a card. **Air pollution** (from burning trash or
   fuels): the smoke harms lungs and skies; people can burn less, use cleaner stoves, and plant
   trees. **Habitat loss** (from clearing forests): animals lose their homes; people can plant
   native trees, use less paper, and protect reserves. On your action page, write or draw the
   problem, its pathway, and one real action.
4. **Check with a partner (8 min).** Say it in one breath: "the problem is ___, because ___;
   one thing people can do is ___." Ask: is the action real, and does it actually reduce the
   harm?
5. **Close (5 min).** The problem is real — and so are the fixes. You are not just learning
   about harm; you are learning what to do about it.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a card with the pathway partly filled in and a sentence starter for the
   action; work one problem aloud first.
- **Extension:** Compare two actions for the same problem and explain which reduces more harm,
   and how you can tell.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name a form of pollution or habitat loss, trace
   its pathway, and give one real action people can take to reduce it?
- **Self-check:** The learner asks, "Did I name both the problem and one real fix — and could I
   actually do the fix myself?"

## Home connection

Notice one form of waste or habitat change near home. Name its pathway and one action your
family could take — then try that action once this week.

## Resources

- Plastic pollution's path from land to rivers to oceans and its harm to wildlife is documented
  by UNEP and the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA) in age-appropriate
  terms; air pollution from open burning and fuels and deforestation-driven habitat loss are
  documented by the World Health Organization and the UN Food and Agriculture Organization
  (FAO). The "refuse, reuse, recycle, clean up" actions are the standard reduce-waste ladder
  (UNEP). The stewardship-and-hope framing follows `docs/facilitation.md`.
