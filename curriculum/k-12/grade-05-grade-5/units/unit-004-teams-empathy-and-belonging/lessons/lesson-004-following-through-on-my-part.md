# Lesson 4 — Following Through on My Part

## Summary

Learners complete the teamwork arc by learning to **follow through** on their part. They
meet a **three-step path** — make the promise, do the part, check in — then actually carry
out the part they divided in Lesson 3, **helping a teammate who is stuck** and asking for
help when they need it. Reliability is framed as care, not duty alone.

## Objectives

- Cooperate in a team by following through on my part, checking in, and helping or asking
  for help when needed. _(D02.S1.05.02)_

## Connection

In a relay race, everyone runs their leg — if one runner stops, the whole team stops. In a
garden everyone waters their row; in a kitchen everyone cooks their dish. **Following
through is how a team trusts each other.** When you say "I'll do my part" and then do it,
people learn they can count on you — and when you're stuck, a good teammate asks for help
instead of quietly disappearing.

## Materials

- Follow-through poster
- Commitment card
- The team task from Lesson 3

## Preparation

- Print or draw the follow-through poster (promise → do → check in).
- Copy a commitment card for each learner.
- Have the Lesson 3 team task and its job list ready so learners can carry out their part.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land is **reliability as care**:
a team runs on people keeping their promises, and "stuck" is a normal, shareable state —
never a shame. Model the three-step path explicitly, then let learners *do* their real part
(this is guided practice becoming real practice). Praise follow-through and honest
check-ins equally; do not shame forgetfulness — investigate what got in the way and name a
strategy for next time. Contribution and responsibility are protective for children
(Search Institute's developmental assets). See `docs/facilitation.md`.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Last time you divided a team's work fairly. Today you learn the
   other half of teamwork: **following through** — keeping the promise you made.
2. **Meet the path (10 min).** Look at the poster. Three steps: **make the promise** ("My
   part is ___. I'll finish by ___."), **do the part**, and **check in** ("I finished —
   are you stuck? Can I help?"). A reliable teammate does all three — and when *they* are
   stuck, they **ask for help** instead of going quiet.
3. **Write your commitment (5 min).** On your commitment card, fill in: *My part is
   ___ . I will finish it by ___ . If I am stuck I will ___ .* Say it to your team so
   everyone knows the promise.
4. **Do your part (15 min).** Carry out the part you divided in Lesson 3. As you work, if
   a teammate is stuck, offer help. If *you* are stuck, ask. When you finish, **check in**
   with your team: "I finished my part — how is yours?"
5. **Close (5 min).** A team is only as strong as its promises. Today you kept one — and
   you helped keep the whole team moving. That is what it means to be someone others can
   count on.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Give learners a very small, finishable part and a check-in partner; scribe
  the commitment card if needed.
- **Extension:** Reflect on what makes follow-through hard (forgetting, fear, too much) and
  write one strategy that helps you keep promises — a reminder, a buddy, a smaller first
  step.

## Assessment

- **Formative (performance/self):** Did the learner do the part they promised, check in
  with their team, and help or ask for help when stuck?
- **Self-check:** The learner asks, "Did I keep my promise? Did I ask for help or offer it
  when someone was stuck?"

## Home connection

Make one small promise at home tonight — a chore, a call, a note — and keep it, then tell
someone how it went.
