# Lesson 10 — Sharing Fairly So Everyone Has Enough

## Summary

In this **capstone** lesson, learners practice fairness by **dividing limited resources so
everyone has enough**. They meet two ways to divide — **equal shares** and **need-based
shares** — role-play dividing a real limited resource in teams, record their division and
its reasons, and revisit the unit's skills: **kind feedback (S1)** and **checking feelings
(S2)** as they negotiate, producing the unit's **fair-division record**.

## Objectives

- Practice fairness by dividing resources so everyone has enough, in a role-play or real
  task. _(D02.S4.05.02)_
- Use kind feedback (D02.S1.05.01) and check rather than assume about feelings
  (D02.S2.05.02) while negotiating a fair division.

## Connection

Six people, one jug of water, and different thirsts. One person just ran, another sat in
the shade. "Same for each" might leave the runner still thirsty; "to each by need" gives
the runner a little more and still leaves enough for all. **Fair does not always mean
equal** — sometimes it means **each gets what they need so everyone has enough**. In a
home, a classroom, or a village sharing a harvest, that is the question we solve together.

## Materials

- Fair-division poster
- Resource cards
- Fair-division record page

## Preparation

- Print or draw the fair-division poster (equal vs. need-based shares).
- Copy a set of resource cards and a fair-division record page for each team.
- Set up one division scenario per team (10 food tokens, 8 pencils, 6 blankets, one jug of
  water) with a few "characters" who need more than others.

## Facilitator note

This is the unit **capstone**, written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land is that
**fairness is a practice with two tools** — equal shares and need-based shares — and the
measure is **everyone has enough**, not "everyone is identical." Model one worked example
first (this is a skill; Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006), then let teams negotiate a real
division. Watch for the two traps: "equal at all costs" (ignoring real need) and "loudest
gets most" (ignoring fairness). Invite the unit's skills back in: when a teammate proposes
a share, respond with **kind feedback** (Lesson 1); when a teammate seems unhappy, **check
instead of assume** (Lesson 6). This lesson produces the unit's **fair-division record**.
Keep it a game of care, never a ranking. See `docs/facilitation.md`.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** For ten lessons you have practiced giving kind feedback, following
   through, seeing others' lives, checking your guesses, and helping one another. Today you
   put it all together: **divide limited resources so everyone has enough**.
2. **Meet two ways to divide (10 min).** Look at the poster. **Equal shares:** same for
   each. **Need-based shares:** more for greater need, less for less. Both can be fair —
   the goal is that **everyone has enough**. Think of the water jug: same for each, or a
   little more to the one who just ran?
3. **Worked example (5 min).** Together, divide the jug of water among six characters:
   a runner (very thirsty), a baby (small sips), four others (ordinary thirst). Discuss:
   equal, or by need? Decide, and check: does everyone have enough?
4. **Role-play your division (15 min).** In your team, take your resource cards and your
   scenario. Read who needs what. Negotiate the division. Use your unit skills: give
   **kind feedback** on a proposal ("that's fair, but could the one who needs more get a
   little more?"), and if a teammate seems unhappy, **check instead of assume** ("are you
   okay with your share, or do you need more?"). Agree on a split, then record it: how you
   divided, why, and whether everyone had enough.
5. **Close (5 min).** Fairness is not a rule you memorize — it is a skill you practice,
   with others, out of care. Today you made sure **everyone had enough**, and you did it
   kindly, together. That is the whole unit, in one act.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a pre-set scenario with two clear needs and a simple equal-vs-need
  choice, and scribe the record.
- **Extension:** Debate a hard case — what counts as "enough" when needs differ — and
  propose a rule the whole group could accept and defend.

## Assessment

- **Summative (performance/portfolio):** Does the team divide the resource so everyone has
  enough, can they explain why (equal or need-based), and do they use kind feedback and
  checking during the negotiation?
- **Self-check:** Each learner asks, "Did everyone end with enough? Could I explain our
  rule? Was I kind, and did I check instead of assume?"

## Home connection

Next time something limited is shared at home — food, space, a turn — help divide it so
everyone has enough, and say out loud which rule you used: equal, or by need.
