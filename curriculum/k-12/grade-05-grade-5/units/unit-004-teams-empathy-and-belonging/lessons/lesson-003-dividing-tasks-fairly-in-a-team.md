# Lesson 3 — Dividing Tasks Fairly in a Team

## Summary

Learners learn how a team divides its work **fairly**. They meet a worked example of a
job list in which **every job is named and matched to a person by skill and interest**,
with nobody left out and nobody overloaded. In teams of three or four they list all the
jobs of a real small task, match jobs to people, and check that **everyone has a real
part**.

## Objectives

- Cooperate in a team by dividing tasks fairly, matching jobs to people so everyone has a
  real part. _(D02.S1.05.02)_

## Connection

A family makes a meal: one washes the greens, one chops, one watches the pot, one sets the
table. Nobody does everything, and nobody stands idle. That is fair task division — list
the jobs, share them so each person carries a real part, and the work gets done without
one person carrying it all. In a sports team, a garden, or a shop, the same trick holds:
**fair teams name every job and give everyone a part that matters**.

## Materials

- Task-division poster
- Blank job list page
- A real team task

## Preparation

- Print or draw the task-division poster (a job list with every job matched to a person).
- Copy a blank job list page for each team.
- Choose or set up a real small team task (a class display, a snack plan, a tidy-up).

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land is **fair division of
work**: list all the jobs, match them to people by skill and interest, and make sure
nobody is left out and nobody is overloaded. Fairness here is a practice, not a slogan —
model one worked example before teams work independently (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark,
2006; Slavin's cooperative-learning structures). Watch for the two failures: one child
does everything, or one child gets nothing. Name them gently and redirect to "everyone
has a real part." Cooperative learning is a strong lever for achievement and belonging
(Bloom, 1984; Hattie's relationships finding). See `docs/contexts.md`.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of a team that worked — and one where one person did
   everything or someone was left with nothing. Today you learn to divide a team's work
   so it is **fair**.
2. **Meet fair division (10 min).** Look at the poster. A team's job list: *draw the
   title, cut the pieces, write the steps, check the work.* Every job has a name beside
   it. Jobs are matched to people by **skill** (who can do it) and **interest** (who wants
   to) — and everyone has a real part, no one is left out, no one is overloaded.
3. **List the jobs (10 min).** In your team, take your real task. First, **list every
   job** — write down every single thing that must be done, even small ones. Don't assign
   yet. Just name them all.
4. **Divide fairly (15 min).** Now match jobs to people. Ask: who can do each job? who
   wants to? Does everyone have a real part? Is anyone carrying too much? Adjust until the
   split feels fair to everyone — then write each person's name beside their job.
5. **Close (5 min).** A fair team is not one where everyone does the same thing, but one
   where **every job is named and every person has a part that matters**. That is how work
   becomes lighter and belonging grows.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a pre-written job list to sort rather than generate, and pair
  learners so a confident teammate scribes the names.
- **Extension:** Discuss fairness when jobs are unequal — heavy vs. light, fun vs. dull —
  and propose a fair way to balance them (rotate, trade, pair up).

## Assessment

- **Formative (peer/observation):** Did the team name all the jobs and match them so
  everyone has a real part, with no one left out or overloaded?
- **Self-check:** Each learner asks, "Do I have a real part, does everyone else, and does
  the split feel fair to all of us?"

## Home connection

Help divide one household job fairly tonight — list the parts of the job and give every
helper a real part.
