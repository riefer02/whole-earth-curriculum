# Lesson 2 — Using Feedback to Improve Shared Work

## Summary

Learners take the next step from Lesson 1: **using kind feedback to actually improve
shared work**. They meet a **before-and-after** example, follow a **three-step loop**
(hear the feedback, choose one change, make it), and improve a real shared thing with
their partner, recording the before and after.

## Objectives

- Use kind feedback to improve shared work by choosing and making one real change.
  _(D02.S1.05.01)_

## Connection

A cook tastes the soup and a helper says, "It needs a little salt." The cook doesn't
argue — she adds a pinch, tastes again, and the soup is better. Feedback is only half the
skill; the other half is **doing something with it**. When someone offers you a kind tip
about a shared thing — a sign, a plan, a game, a meal — the magic happens when you pick
one change and make it.

## Materials

- Before–after poster
- Shared-work page
- Pencils

## Preparation

- Print or draw the before–after poster (one shared sign improved by one piece of
  feedback).
- Copy a shared-work page for each pair (a two-step instruction page, a mini-map, or a
  recipe strip).
- Arrange pairs with room to work side by side.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land is that **feedback becomes
learning only when it is used** — hear it, choose one change, make it. Model the
before-and-after loop explicitly; this is a skill, so worked examples and guided practice
come before independent work (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006). Guard against the trap
of "I heard you" without a real change: the change is the evidence. Praise the *change*,
not just the politeness. Keep the shared thing small and safe so learners can risk
changing it. See `docs/facilitation.md`.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Last time you learned to give and receive feedback kindly. Today
   you learn the other half: **using** that feedback to make your shared work better.
2. **Meet the loop (10 min).** Look at the before–after poster. *Before:* a sign reads
   "water plants" with no order. A helper says, "Could you number the steps?" *After:* the
   sign now reads "1. Fill can. 2. Water each plant. 3. Put can away." That is the loop:
   **hear the feedback → choose one change → make it.**
3. **Practice together (5 min).** With the whole group, run the loop once aloud on a
   shared thing: someone names one kind, specific tip; the group chooses one change and
   says exactly what it will be.
4. **Improve your shared thing (15 min).** Take your pair's shared-work page. Partner A
   gives one kind, specific comment (from Lesson 1's starter). Partner B **chooses one
   change and makes it** on the page. Then swap. Record the before and after — draw or
   write what changed and why it is better.
5. **Close (5 min).** A kind tip only becomes a real improvement when you act on it.
   Today you turned someone's help into a better shared thing. That loop — hear, choose,
   change — is how teams make everything better together.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a shared thing that needs one obvious change (a missing label, an
  out-of-order step), and scribe the before–after sentence.
- **Extension:** Use two pieces of feedback, or explain how the change helps a *third*
  person who will use the shared thing.

## Assessment

- **Formative (performance/peer):** Can the learner take a kind, specific piece of
  feedback, choose one real change, make it, and say how the work improved?
- **Self-check:** The learner asks, "Did I actually change something, and is the shared
  thing clearer or better because of it?"

## Home connection

Use one piece of feedback from someone at home to improve a shared thing — a chore chart,
a recipe, a note — and show them the before and after.

## Resources

- Feedback drives learning only when learners act on it — mastery learning's

  the learner *using* the feedback to close the gap. For classroom SEL framing, see
  CASEL's relationship-skills competency: <https://casel.org/fundamentals-of-sel/>
