# Lesson 10 — My Body at Work

## Summary

In this **capstone** lesson, learners bring the whole unit together in a **"my body at work"**
note: one **mind–body** example, one **growth-at-my-pace** idea, one **practiced skill with
feedback**, and one **movement benefit**. They assemble the four-part note, share it with a
peer, and close the unit with agency, dignity, and joy. The note becomes a **portfolio
artifact**.

## Objectives

- Explain the mind–body connection with a simple example, such as how worry can make a
  stomach feel tight. _(D03.S1.05.01)_
- Describe how bodies grow and change at different rates and that every pace is normal.
  _(D03.S1.05.02)_
- Refine a specific motor skill, such as throwing accurately or balancing, through repeated
  practice and give a peer helpful feedback. _(D03.S2.05.01)_
- Explain how regular movement benefits the heart, muscles, and mood. _(D03.S2.05.02)_

## Connection

Throughout this unit you have been getting to know your body — how it talks with your mind, how it grows
at its own pace, how it learns through practice, and how movement keeps it strong and joyful.
Now you put it together, because these are not four separate facts; they are **one body, at
work, every day**. Knowing all four is how you care for yourself — and how you honor everyone
else's body too.

## Materials

- My body at work poster
- My body at work note page
- Pencils and crayons

## Preparation

- Print or draw the "my body at work" poster (four sections: mind–body connection, growth at
  my pace, practice and feedback, movement benefits).
- Copy a "my body at work" note page for each learner.
- Arrange a circle and a space for paired sharing.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). This capstone is a **celebration of mastery, not
a ranking** — each learner assembles what *they* learned about *their own* body, and every note
is complete and valuable as it is. Review the four sections with the poster, then let learners
work independently and share in pairs. Honor the wide range of bodies: some learners will write
a seated or adapted movement, a different growth pace, or a signal their own body sends — all
are correct. Keep the tone warm and proud. See `docs/facilitation.md` on assessing without
ranking.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** This is your **capstone** — the lesson that ties the whole unit
   together. You have learned four big things about your body. Today you show them all, in one
   **"my body at work"** note.
2. **Review the four sections (10 min).** Look at the poster. Section one: **mind and body are
   connected** — a worry can make a stomach tight (Lesson 1). Section two: **growth at my pace**
   — bodies grow at different rates, and every pace is normal (Lessons 3–4). Section three:
   **practice and feedback** — repeated practice builds skill, and kind, specific feedback helps
   (Lessons 5–6). Section four: **movement benefits** — movement strengthens the heart and
   muscles and lifts mood (Lessons 7–8). Four ideas, one body.
3. **Build your note (15 min).** On your page, write or draw one example for each section from
   **your own** body and week. One mind–body signal you noticed. One way you are growing at your
   own pace. One skill you practiced and one piece of feedback you gave or received. One way
   movement helped your heart, muscles, or mood.
4. **Share and listen (10 min).** With a partner, share your note. Listen to theirs with
   respect. Notice: your bodies are different, and your notes are different — and both are
   complete.
5. **Close (5 min).** You know your body now with new precision — its signals, its pace, its
   skills, its strength. Carry that knowledge like a good friend, for yourself and for every
   body you meet.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a four-section frame with sentence starters ("I noticed my body sends
  the signal ___ when I feel ___") and a word bank; scribe as needed.
- **Extension:** Add one sentence connecting two sections — for example, how practice (section
  three) and movement benefits (section four) work together in your week.

## Assessment

- **Summative (portfolio):** Does the learner's note include one accurate example in each of
  the four sections, grounded in their own body and week, without shaming language about any
  body?
- **Self-check:** The learner reads their note and checks each section is real and complete,
  then shares it with a peer who confirms they understood it.

## Home connection

Share your "my body at work" note with someone at home, and tell them one thing you learned
about your own body that you will keep practicing.
