# Lesson 9 — My Practice Plan

## Summary

Learners turn the unit's learning into action: they build a simple **three-day practice plan**
that **interleaves** the motor skill they refined (throwing or balancing) with a heart-pumping
movement, each day framed by a **warm-up** and **cool-down**. They set one small **goal** and
connect the plan to the heart, muscle, and mood benefits of movement. This is a **portfolio
artifact**.

## Objectives

- Refine a specific motor skill, such as throwing accurately or balancing, through repeated
  practice and give a peer helpful feedback. _(D03.S2.05.01)_
- Explain how regular movement benefits the heart, muscles, and mood. _(D03.S2.05.02)_

## Connection

Wanting to get better is one thing; a **plan** is how "someday" becomes "today." A farmer
plans which days to plant; a musician plans which days to practice. Your plan does not need to
be big — just three days, a skill to practice, a movement that makes your heart work, and a
gentle warm-up and cool-down to care for your body. A small plan, kept, beats a big wish,
forgotten.

## Materials

- Practice plan poster
- Blank practice plan page
- Pencils

## Preparation

- Print or draw the practice plan poster (a three-day card: Day 1 throwing, Day 2 balancing,
  Day 3 a heart-pumping walk, each with a warm-up and cool-down).
- Copy a blank practice plan page for each learner.
- Arrange a circle and a quiet space for writing.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land is that a **simple plan turns
learning into action**, and that the plan should be **small, flexible, and kind to every body**.
Interleaving — mixing the skill with heart-pumping movement — helps learning stick (spacing and
interleaving). Keep goals **small and personal**, never competitive. If learners mention
step-counters or fitness apps, frame them as **optional tools** — useful, but the body's own
signals always come first, and no one needs a device to move well. Honor every body's pace and
ability; a seated movement is a full movement. See `docs/contexts.md`.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** You have learned so much: the mind–body connection, how bodies grow,
   how practice builds skill, how movement helps your heart, muscles, and mood. Today you make
   a **plan** to keep all of it alive.
2. **Meet the plan (10 min).** Look at the poster — a **worked example**. A three-day plan.
   *Day 1:* warm up (march in place), practice **throwing** at a target, cool down (slow
   breathing). *Day 2:* warm up, practice **balancing** on a line, cool down. *Day 3:*
   warm up, a **heart-pumping walk** or dance, cool down. Notice it **mixes** the skill you
   practiced (throwing or balancing) with heart-pumping movement — mixing helps learning stick
   and gives the heart, muscles, and mood their gifts.
3. **Set a small goal (10 min).** Choose one **small, doable goal** for your plan — "hit the
   target five times," "balance for ten slow steps." Write it on your page. A small goal you
   can keep is better than a big one you drop.
4. **Build your three days (15 min).** Fill your plan frame: for each day, a warm-up, a skill
   or movement, and a cool-down. Include at least one day for your **motor skill** and one for
   a **heart-pumping movement**. Share one day with a partner and listen to theirs.
5. **Close (5 min).** A plan is a promise you make to yourself — small, kind, and doable. Keep
   it for three days and watch what your body and mood tell you.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a partially filled plan with pictures and a word bank of warm-ups,
  skills, and cool-downs; scribe the goal.
- **Extension:** Interleave two skills across the days and write one sentence about why mixing
  skills — rather than cramming one — helps learning stick.

## Assessment

- **Formative (portfolio):** Does the plan include a warm-up, a skill or movement, and a
  cool-down for each of three days, with a small, doable goal and a heart-pumping day?
- **Self-check:** The learner asks, "Is my goal small enough to keep, and does my plan care
  for my body with a warm-up and cool-down?"

## Home connection

Share your three-day plan with someone at home and invite them to try one day of it with you.

## Resources

- Spacing and interleaving — mixing and revisiting skills across days rather than cramming —
  support long-term learning (see the learning-science literature; Kirschner, Sweller & Clark,
  2006). Optional tracking tools can help, but movement requires no device; the body's own
  signals are the primary guide. For activity guidance, see WHO:
  <https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/physical-activity>
