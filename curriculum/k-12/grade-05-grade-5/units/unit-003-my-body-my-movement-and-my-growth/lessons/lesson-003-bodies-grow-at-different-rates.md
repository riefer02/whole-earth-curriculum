# Lesson 3 — Bodies Grow at Different Rates

## Summary

Learners discover that **bodies grow and change at different rates**, and that **every pace
is normal**. They meet a simple growth-line picture showing two children of the same age at
different heights, learn that growth comes in **spurts** rather than a steady straight line,
and draw their own growth line, marking where they are now. The wide range of human bodies
is honored with dignity, never shame.

## Objectives

- Describe how bodies grow and change at different rates and that every pace is normal.
  _(D03.S1.05.02)_

## Connection

Plant two seeds of the same kind at the same time. One sprouts tall and fast; the other
comes up later, smaller, and then catches up — or stays smaller, and that is fine too. Human
bodies are like that. Two children the same age can be very different heights, and both are
**normal**. Whether the difference comes from family, food, place, or just the timing that
each body has, no pace is better than another — every pace is a real pace.

## Materials

- Growth rates poster
- Blank growth line page
- Pencils and crayons

## Preparation

- Print or draw the growth rates poster (two same-age children at different heights, both
  smiling, each with a rising growth line labeled "same age, different heights — both
  normal").
- Copy a blank growth line page for each learner.
- If using a height line, prepare a wall, string, or stick for marking heights.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land is that **growth varies and
every pace is normal** — never that a smaller or larger body is "wrong," "slow," or "behind."
Height and build are shaped by **genes, nutrition, and environment**, and growth comes in
**spurts**, so comparing bodies as a race is both scientifically false and unkind. Guard
against teasing about height or size; frame all bodies with dignity. Some learners may feel
self-conscious about their own size — keep the tone warm and factual, and never single
anyone out to be measured publicly. See `docs/facilitation.md` on assessing without ranking.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** You have learned how your mind and body are connected. Now another
   part of knowing your body: **growth**. And the first truth about growth is that it does
   not happen at the same rate for everyone.
2. **Meet the growth line (10 min).** Look at the poster. Two children, the **same age** —
   one shorter, one taller. Both are smiling; both are **normal**. Beneath each, a growth
   line rises, but not the same way: growth comes in **spurts** — fast for a while, then
   slower — like a staircase more than a smooth slide. No one grows "on time"; each body has
   its own clock.
3. **Notice a range (10 min).** In a group or by looking around, notice the wide range of
   heights, hands, and shoe sizes among people your age. Is there one "right" size? No — a
   wide **range** is exactly what healthy looks like. Share one thing you notice with a
   partner.
4. **Draw your own line (15 min).** On your page, draw a rising growth line. Mark **"me
   now"** somewhere on it, and **"me soon"** a little further up. Under it, write one reason
   heights can differ — family, food, place, or timing. There is no right place on the line;
   your spot is a real spot.
5. **Close (5 min).** Your body is growing on its own schedule, and so is everyone else's.
   Different rates, every pace normal — that is how humans actually grow.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a pre-drawn growth line to mark "me now" and "me soon" and a word
  bank of reasons heights differ; scribe the learner's sentence.
- **Extension:** Describe what a growth **spurt** might feel like (clothes fitting
  differently, shoes too small, feeling tired) and why that is normal, not a problem.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner state that bodies grow at different rates and
  that every pace is normal, and give one reason heights can differ?
- **Self-check:** The learner reads their growth line and sentence back and checks that they
  framed their own pace as normal, never "behind."

## Home connection

Ask someone at home about when *they* had a growth spurt — and share that every body grows
on its own schedule.

## Resources

- Human growth is measured against standard growth references that show a **range** of
  normal, not a single number. The World Health Organization publishes child growth
  standards that treat a wide range of heights and weights as normal:
  <https://www.who.int/toolkits/child-growth-standards>. Growth is shaped by genes,
  nutrition, and environment — evidence, not a value judgment about any body.
