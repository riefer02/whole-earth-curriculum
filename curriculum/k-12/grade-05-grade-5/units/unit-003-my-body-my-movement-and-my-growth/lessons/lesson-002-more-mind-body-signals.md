# Lesson 2 — More Mind–Body Signals

## Summary

Learners build on Lesson 1 by meeting a small set of common **body signals** — butterflies,
heavy eyes, a warm chest, quick breath — and learning to match each to a **feeling** and a
**helpful response**. They then build a personal **signal chart** for three real signals of
their own, strengthening the skill of reading the body's messages with care.

## Objectives

- Explain the mind–body connection with a simple example, such as how worry can make a
  stomach feel tight. _(D03.S1.05.01)_

## Connection

Your body is always sending you messages — a **flutter of butterflies** before a
performance, **heavy eyes** when you are tired, a **warm chest** when you feel safe, a
**quick breath** when you are excited. These signals are the same in a busy market, a quiet
village, or a high mountain school: the body speaks, and the mind can learn to listen.
Noticing the signal — and what it is asking for — is a skill anyone can grow.

## Materials

- Body signals poster
- Blank signal chart page
- Pencils

## Preparation

- Print or draw the body signals poster (four signal cards, each with a signal, a feeling,
  and a helpful response).
- Copy a blank signal chart page for each learner.
- Arrange a circle and space for a signal guessing game.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land is that **body signals carry
meaning, and each signal points to a helpful response**. Present the responses as *choices
the learner can make*, never as rules — "you *could* rest" rather than "you *must* rest."
Some learners live with bodies that signal differently (pain, fatigue, sensory differences);
honor that every body speaks its own language. Keep examples ordinary and let each learner
choose safe, real signals to share. See `docs/facilitation.md` on holding difficult topics
safely.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Last time you learned that your mind and body are connected — a
   worry can show up as a tight stomach. Today you meet more of the body's language, and you
   learn to read it: **signal → feeling → helpful response**.
2. **Meet the signals (10 min).** Look at the poster. Four **worked examples**. *Signal:*
   butterflies in the stomach. *Feeling:* excited or nervous. *Helpful response:* take a slow
   breath and name what is coming. *Signal:* heavy eyes. *Feeling:* tired. *Response:* rest.
   *Signal:* warm chest. *Feeling:* happy or safe. *Response:* enjoy it. *Signal:* quick
   breath. *Feeling:* excited. *Response:* move your body or take a breath. The body tells,
   and you can choose what to do.
3. **Play a guessing game (10 min).** With a partner, act out one signal silently — flutter,
   droop, warm smile, quick breath. Let your partner guess the feeling and suggest one helpful
   response. Trade roles.
4. **Build your own chart (15 min).** On your page, write three **real** signals your body
   has sent you this week. For each, name the feeling it carried and one helpful response you
   could choose. Share one with a partner: does the same signal ever mean different things
   for different people?
5. **Close (5 min).** Your body speaks to you every day. The more you listen — signal,
   feeling, response — the better you can care for yourself and understand others.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer signal picture cards and a word bank to arrange into signal–feeling–
  response rows; scribe the learner's responses.
- **Extension:** Find two feelings that share one signal (butterflies for excited *and*
  nervous) and name the clue that tells them apart — the thought, the situation, or another
  body cue.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner match a signal to a feeling and name one
  helpful response, in the game and on their chart?
- **Self-check:** The learner rereads their chart and asks, "Is this really the signal my
  body sent, and is my response one I can actually do?"

## Home connection

Notice one body signal at home tonight — hunger, tiredness, excitement — and share the
signal, the feeling, and one helpful response with someone there.
