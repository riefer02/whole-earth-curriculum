# Lesson 4 — Every Pace Is Normal

## Summary

Learners take the truth from Lesson 3 — bodies grow at different rates — one step further and
learn to **honor** the wide range of human bodies and abilities with dignity and joy. They
meet a picture of diverse bodies — different heights and builds, a wheelchair user, a hearing
aid, a prosthetic — all playing together, and complete an **"every body belongs"** drawing and
an **honor statement** of their own. Shame has no place here; belonging does.

## Objectives

- Describe how bodies grow and change at different rates and that every pace is normal.
  _(D03.S1.05.02)_

## Connection

In a playground, a classroom, or a family, every body is different — some grow tall early,
some later; some walk, some roll in a wheelchair; some hear with the help of a device, some
move with a prosthetic. None of these is "less"; each is a real, complete way to be a person.
A group that makes room for **every body** is not just kinder — it is *stronger and more
interesting*. Honoring every pace and every ability is something you can practice every
single day.

## Materials

- Diverse bodies poster
- Blank "every body" page
- Pencils and crayons

## Preparation

- Print or draw the diverse bodies poster (children of different heights and builds, a
  wheelchair user, a hearing aid, a prosthetic, all playing joyfully).
- Copy a blank "every body" page for each learner.
- Arrange a circle and a space for drawing.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land is **dignity for every body**:
different sizes, paces, and abilities are all normal, and every body belongs. Model
**person-first, non-shaming** language — "a child who uses a wheelchair," never labels that
reduce a person to a condition. Guard against any teasing about bodies; interrupt it gently
and turn it toward honor. Some learners in the room may have bodies that are different or
disabilities; never make them the "example," and never put them on the spot. See
`docs/facilitation.md` on assessing without ranking and holding difficult topics safely.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Last time you learned that bodies grow at different rates, and every
   pace is normal. Today you learn the *heart* of that truth: how to **honor** every body —
   your own and everyone else's.
2. **Meet diverse bodies (10 min).** Look at the poster. Different heights. Different builds.
   A child who rolls in a wheelchair. A child who hears with a hearing aid. A child who moves
   with a prosthetic leg. All of them are playing **together, joyfully**. None is missing
   something the others have and is "less"; each is a full person. A wheelchair, a hearing
   aid, a prosthetic are **tools that help a body do what it wants** — just like glasses help
   eyes see.
3. **Talk honor (10 min).** With a partner, finish this sentence in two ways: "Every body is
   honored when we ___." (Ideas: wait for a slower runner, make room for a wheelchair, speak
   so a friend with a hearing aid can see your face, never tease about size.) Share one
   aloud.
4. **Draw belonging (15 min).** On your page, draw a scene where **every body belongs** — a
   game, a meal, a walk, a classroom — with many kinds of bodies in it. Under it, write your
   honor statement: "Every body is honored when we ___."
5. **Close (5 min).** Different is not less. A group that honors every body honors every
   person. You can build that group wherever you are.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a pre-drawn scene to add diverse figures into, and a sentence starter
  to complete; scribe as needed.
- **Extension:** Design one concrete change to a game or space — a ramp, a wider gate, a
  slower pace, a spoken signal — so a specific body can fully join.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner describe at least two different bodies or
  abilities as normal and give one concrete way every body can be honored?
- **Self-check:** The learner reads their honor statement and checks that it names a real
  action, not just a feeling.

## Home connection

With someone at home, name one way your home or neighborhood could make room for a body
different from your own — and one way you already do.

## Resources

- Treating every body and ability with dignity reflects the social model of disability, which
  asks what a community can *change* to include everyone rather than what a person "lacks."
  For accessible classroom framing, see the United Nations Convention on the Rights of
  Persons with Disabilities (CRPD): <https://www.un.org/development/desa/disabilities/convention-on-the-rights-of-persons-with-disabilities.html>
