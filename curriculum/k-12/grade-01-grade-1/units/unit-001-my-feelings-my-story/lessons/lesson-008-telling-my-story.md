# Lesson 8 — Telling My Story

## Summary

Learners gather the pieces they have built this unit — their facts, their feelings, a
proud moment, and a family story or tradition — and tell them as one story. They
discover that stories can be told aloud, drawn, or written, and that every person's
story is worth hearing.

## Objectives

- Name a family story or tradition and tell what it means to them. _(D01.S2.01.02)_

## Connection

A story is how we hold the pieces of ourselves together and share them with others.
Long before books, people told their stories out loud around fires, and families
passed them down. Today you can tell your story with your voice, with pictures, or
with words — but the story itself is the treasure. Your story has never been told by
anyone else, and it is worth hearing.

## Materials

- "My Story" page or booklet template
- Crayons and pencils
- A listening partner guide (two or three gentle questions)

## Preparation

- Prepare the "My Story" page or booklet (self-portrait, facts, family story).
- Set out drawing supplies.
- Arrange small sharing circles.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Prepare the page or booklet, then
support learners as they assemble and tell. Storytelling is the oldest technology of
all — celebrate that stories live in voices and pictures, not only in books or on
screens. Keep listening gentle and unrushed; every story, however small, is honored.
A learner who does not want to speak may show their page instead.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think back over this unit: your facts, your feelings, your
   proud moment, your family story. Today you weave them into one story — *your*
   story.
2. **Assemble the pieces (15 min).** On your page or in your booklet, put together:
   a self-portrait, two or three facts about you, one proud moment, and one family
   story or tradition.
3. **Practice telling it (10 min).** Tell your story to a partner, using the page as
   your guide. The partner listens and asks one gentle question ("What does that
   tradition mean to you?").
4. **Share in a small circle (10 min).** In a group of three or four, take turns
   telling your story. Listen like a treasure-keeper: quietly, kindly, fully.
5. **Close (5 min).** Your story is yours — no one else has it. Telling it is how
   people come to know you, and how you come to know yourself.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use the page as a picture script; the learner points while an older
  peer or facilitator voices the words.
- **Extension:** Add a "next" page — one hope for your story going forward.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner tell their story, including a family
  story or tradition and what it means?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The "My Story" page or booklet, kept in the portfolio.

## Home connection

Tell your story to someone at home tonight — the same story you told today — and ask
them to tell you a piece of their own.
