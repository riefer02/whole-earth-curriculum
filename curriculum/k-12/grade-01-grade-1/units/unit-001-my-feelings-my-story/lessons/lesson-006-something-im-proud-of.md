# Lesson 6 — Something I'm Proud Of

## Summary

Learners choose one thing they are proud of and tell why it matters — usually the
effort or practice behind it. They celebrate pride as a feeling about *your own*
growth, not about being better than anyone else.

## Objectives

- Describe themselves with several facts such as age, likes, abilities, and family,
  and tell one thing they are proud of. _(D01.S2.01.01)_

## Connection

Being proud feels warm and tall — like you grew a little. You might feel proud when
you learn to tie your shoes, help someone, or try something hard many times. Pride is
not about being the best; it is about seeing how far *you* have come. Knowing what
you are proud of helps you know yourself.

## Materials

- "Proud" star template
- Crayons and pencils
- A small basket or box (optional)

## Preparation

- Prepare the star templates (or plain paper for hand-drawn stars).
- Set out crayons and pencils.
- Arrange a sharing circle.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Keep pride personal: each learner's
proud moment is *their* moment, and none is "bigger" than another. Model naming the
effort behind your own proud moment ("I am proud of finishing my garden because I
watered it every day"). Watch for learners who struggle to find something — offer
small, real examples (shared a toy, tried a new food, climbed higher).

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of a time you felt proud — not because you beat someone,
   but because you did something that mattered to you.
2. **The effort inside (10 min).** Pride usually has effort behind it. Ask yourself:
   what did I practice, try, or work at? ("I am proud of reading a whole book because
   I practiced every day.")
3. **Make your star (15 min).** Draw or write your proud moment inside the star. You
   can draw the moment, the effort, or both.
4. **Share (10 min).** Show your star and tell the group your proud moment and the
   effort behind it. After each one, everyone gives a gentle round of applause. A
   learner who prefers not to speak aloud may hold up their star, let a partner voice
   the words, or simply add the star to the collection — sharing is a choice, not a
   requirement.
5. **Close (5 min).** Pride is a feeling about your own growth. Every star in the
   room is worth celebrating — yours included.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Give a starter: "I am proud of ___ because ___." Offer picture choices
  if needed.
- **Extension:** Write a second sentence about what you want to be proud of next.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name one thing they are proud of and a
  reason (effort) behind it?
- **Performance:** The learner shares their proud moment with the group.

## Home connection

Tell someone at home one thing you are proud of, and ask them one thing *they* are
proud of.
