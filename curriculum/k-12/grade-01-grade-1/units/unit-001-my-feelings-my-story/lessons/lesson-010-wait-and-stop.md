# Lesson 10 — Wait and Stop

## Summary

Learners practice two first steps of self-regulation — waiting for a turn and
stopping a behavior when asked, with gentle support. The lesson doubles as a light
capstone: learners gather what they have learned this unit and share one feeling
word, one fact about themselves, one family story, and one calming strategy.

## Objectives

- Wait for a turn and stop a behavior when asked, with support. _(D01.S3.01.02)_
- Use one calming strategy when upset, with a gentle reminder. _(D01.S3.01.01)_
- Name and show a growing set of feelings. _(D01.S1.01.01)_
- Connect a feeling to its cause using "I felt ___ when ___." _(D01.S1.01.02)_
- Describe themselves with several facts and tell one thing they are proud of.
  _(D01.S2.01.01)_
- Name a family story or tradition and tell what it means to them. _(D01.S2.01.02)_

## Connection

Waiting and stopping are hard — even for grown-ups. When many people share one space,
we take turns so everyone gets a fair chance, and we stop when a safe grown-up asks
so that everyone stays safe and respected. These are not punishments; they are how we
care for each other. Practicing them, with a gentle reminder, is how we learn to
choose our actions instead of just reacting.

## Materials

- A talking object (stone, shell, or ball)
- Stop-and-wait cue cards ("wait," "stop")
- A simple group game (pass the ball, or a circle chant)

## Preparation

- Prepare the talking object and the wait/stop cue cards.
- Choose a simple turn-taking game.
- Arrange a circle.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Keep the "stop" cue calm and kind —
an open palm and a soft word, never a shout or a threat. Wait and stop are skills to
be built with support and patience, not obedience drills. The capstone is a
celebration, not a test: every learner shares what they can, and every share is
honored. Offer the frame and let learners choose which piece to share.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think: is it easy or hard to wait for your turn? Is it easy or
   hard to stop when someone says stop? Today you practice both — with help.
2. **The talking object (10 min).** Only the person holding the object speaks. When
   it is not your turn, you **wait** — hands quiet, ears open. Pass the object around
   and let each person say their name and one favorite thing.
3. **Stop, with kindness (10 min).** Learn the stop cue: an open palm and a calm
   voice. Practice a freeze game — move, then **stop** on the signal, then wait for
   "go." Stopping keeps everyone safe; it is a kind thing, not a mean one.
4. **Turn-taking game (10 min).** Play your group game, taking turns and practicing
   wait and stop as you go. Notice how it feels to be included when everyone waits
   fairly.
5. **Capstone circle (10 min).** Take the talking object one more time. Share one
   thing you learned this unit: a feeling word, a fact about you, a family story, or
   your calming strategy.
6. **Close (5 min).** You can name your feelings, tell your story, calm yourself, and
   wait and stop. Those are real powers — and they grow with practice.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use only the talking object and the stop cue; skip the full capstone
  and let the learner share just one piece.
- **Extension:** Reflect on the capstone by drawing all four things — a feeling, a
  fact, a family story, a calming strategy — on one page.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner wait for a turn and stop a behavior
  when asked, with a gentle reminder?
- **Performance:** The learner shares one piece from the unit in the capstone circle.

## Home connection

Play a turn-taking game at home tonight, and practice the stop cue together — an open
palm and a calm voice.
