# Lesson 2 — Showing Feelings

## Summary

Learners discover that a feeling is not just a word — it shows up in three places:
the face, the body, and the voice. They practice reading those clues in themselves
and in others, and they learn that noticing how someone feels is the first step to
being kind.

## Objectives

- Name and show a growing set of feelings, such as proud, lonely, and excited, in
  themselves and others. _(D01.S1.01.01)_

## Connection

Feelings do not stay hidden inside — they show on your face, in how you stand, and in
how your voice sounds. When a friend is quiet with drooping shoulders, you can guess
they might be sad or lonely, even before they say a word. Reading these clues helps
you understand the people around you and know when to offer a hand.

## Materials

- Feeling face cards (happy, sad, angry, scared, proud, lonely, excited)
- Hand mirrors (optional)
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Set out the feeling face cards and mirrors.
- Clear a space where learners can move freely for "feeling freeze."
- Arrange partners.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Prepare the cards, mirrors, and open
space, then lead the statue game and the closing discussion. Remind learners that
clues are guesses, not certainties — a quiet person might be tired, not sad. Honor
that people from different families and places may show the same feeling in
different ways; treat no single face or gesture as the "right" one. Model curiosity
("I wonder if...") rather than certainty.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of a time you felt proud, lonely, or excited. Where did
   that feeling show? On your face? In your body? In your voice?
2. **Three clue spots (10 min).** A feeling shows in three places: your **face** (a
   smile, a frown, wide eyes), your **body** (tall, curled up, bouncing), and your
   **voice** (loud, quiet, fast). Look at each card and name the clues in all three
   spots.
3. **Mirror check (10 min).** With a partner or a mirror, make a feeling. Watch your
   face change. Try the same feeling with a different body — how does proud look when
   you stand tall versus slouched?
4. **Feeling freeze (15 min).** The group calls a feeling. Everyone freezes into a
   statue of it using face and body. Look around: what clues do you see in others?
   Take turns describing what you notice.
5. **Close (5 min).** Reading feelings is like reading clues. When you notice someone
   else's feeling, you can be kind.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Focus on one clue spot at a time (faces first, then bodies).
- **Extension:** Draw a friend's face showing a feeling and label the clues you used
  to guess.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name at least one clue in the face,
  body, or voice for a given feeling?
- **Peer:** Learners describe a clue they saw in a partner's statue.

## Home connection

Play "guess the feeling" at home: make a face, and have someone guess what you feel
from your face, body, and voice.
