# Lesson 4 — What Made Me Feel That Way?

## Summary

Learners practice the second half of the feeling sentence: finding the situation that
caused a feeling. They match situations to feelings, then state the link using "I
felt ___ when ___," and notice that a feeling always has a reason behind it.

## Objectives

- Connect a feeling to the situation that caused it, using the frame "I felt ___
  when ___." _(D01.S1.01.02)_

## Connection

Sometimes the feeling is easy to see but the reason is a puzzle. You feel angry — but
why? Was it a toy that was taken, or a plan that changed? Finding the *when* is like
being a detective of your own heart. Once you know the cause, you can understand your
feeling and decide what to do about it.

## Materials

- Scenario picture cards (a gift, a lost toy, a new friend, a storm, a finished
  drawing)
- Feeling face cards (happy, sad, angry, scared, proud, lonely, excited)
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Prepare the scenario picture cards (draw them if printed ones are unavailable).
- Set out the feeling cards.
- Clear space for acting out situations.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Model "working backward": hold up a
scenario, wonder aloud about the feeling, and say the sentence. Accept that one
situation can cause different feelings in different learners — that is real, not an
error. Keep the focus on the link between situation and feeling, not on a single
"correct" answer.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Remember the frame: "I felt ___ when ___." Today you become
   the detective: start with the feeling, then hunt for the *when*.
2. **Situation cards (15 min).** Look at each scenario picture. What feeling might it
   cause? Say the full sentence. (A gift → "I felt excited when I got a gift." A lost
   toy → "I felt sad when I lost my toy.")
3. **Act it out (10 min).** Choose a scenario card and act out the situation, then
   freeze in the feeling. A partner names the feeling and the cause, using the frame.
4. **Draw the link (10 min).** Draw a picture of a situation from your own life, and
   write or say the sentence beneath it: "I felt ___ when ___."
5. **Close (5 min).** Every feeling has a reason. Finding the reason is the first step
   to understanding yourself.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Give a choice of two feeling cards for each scenario; the learner
  picks and says the sentence.
- **Extension:** Explain how the same situation could make two different people feel
  two different things.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner connect a given situation to a
  plausible feeling and state it with the frame?

## Home connection

Be a detective at home: notice a feeling you have today, then name the situation that
caused it, using "I felt ___ when ___."
