# Lesson 9 — Calm Down

## Summary

Learners learn and practice a calming strategy — a deep breath, counting, or finding
a safe space — to use when they feel upset. Calming is framed positively: it is a
tool that helps, never a punishment, and everyone gets upset sometimes.

## Objectives

- Use one calming strategy, such as a deep breath, counting, or finding a safe space,
  when upset, with a gentle reminder. _(D01.S3.01.01)_

## Connection

Everyone gets upset sometimes — angry, scared, or so excited it feels like too much.
When that happens, your body might go tight and fast. A calming strategy helps your
body slow down so your heart and mind can catch up. It is not a punishment; it is a
tool you can carry with you anywhere, like a deep breath in your pocket.

## Materials

- A calm corner or safe-space marker (pillow, mat, or quiet spot)
- Picture cards of calming strategies (deep breath, count to ten, safe space, hug a
  pillow, drink water)
- A soft object to hold (stone, shell, or soft toy)

## Preparation

- Set up the calm corner or safe space and show learners where it is.
- Prepare the strategy picture cards.
- Arrange a quiet space for breathing practice.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Frame calming as help, never as
discipline — never send a learner to the calm corner as a punishment. Model your own
calming ("I am going to take a deep breath now"). With a gentle reminder, guide a
learner who is upset to the strategy, but let them choose. Some learners calm better
with movement than stillness; honor that.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of a time you felt upset. What did your body do? Fast
   heart, tight shoulders, hot face. Today you learn a tool to help your body slow
   down.
2. **Meet the strategies (10 min).** Look at the strategy cards: a **deep breath**, a
   **count to ten**, a **safe space**, a hug, a drink of water, and ways to **move** —
   rocking, swaying, stretching, or shaking out the tight feeling. Each one helps your
   body feel safe and slow. Some bodies calm with stillness; others calm with
   movement. Both are good.
3. **Practice the deep breath (10 min).** Hold your soft object. Breathe in slowly
   through your nose, like smelling a flower. Breathe out slowly through your mouth,
   like blowing on hot soup. Do it three times. Feel your body relax.
4. **Find your safe space (10 min).** Visit the calm corner. Notice what makes it
   calm — soft, quiet, still. Practice walking there, taking one breath, and
   returning when you feel ready.
5. **Close (5 min).** Choose your favorite strategy and give it a name, like "flower
   breath" or "my quiet spot" — or just point to its picture. You can use it any time
   you need it.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer one strategy only (the deep breath) and practice it daily as a
  routine before it is needed.
- **Extension:** Practice choosing a strategy *without* a reminder and naming when
  you would use it.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner use one calming strategy (deep breath,
  counting, or safe space) when prompted gently?

## Home connection

Teach your calming strategy to someone at home, and try it together once before bed.
