# Lesson 1 — Feelings Grow Bigger

## Summary

Learners review the four feeling names they learned in Kindergarten and grow their
vocabulary with three new ones — proud, lonely, and excited. They act out each
feeling, notice where it lives in the body, and match it to a real moment.

## Objectives

- Name and show a growing set of feelings, such as proud, lonely, and excited, in
  themselves and others. _(D01.S1.01.01)_

## Connection

Last year you learned four feeling names — happy, sad, angry, and scared. This year
your feelings can be named more exactly. When you finish something hard, you feel
proud; when no one is around to play, you might feel lonely; when something wonderful
is about to happen, you feel excited. More feeling words mean you can tell people
exactly what is going on inside you.

## Materials

- Feeling face cards (happy, sad, angry, scared, proud, lonely, excited)
- Chart paper and markers
- A soft ball or beanbag (optional)

## Preparation

- Print or draw the seven feeling face cards.
- Write the three new words — proud, lonely, excited — on chart paper.
- Arrange a circle for gathering and the match game.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Prepare the cards, chart, and circle, then guide the match
game and the closing reflection. Feelings are personal — no answer is "wrong"; if a
learner names a different feeling, honor it and wonder together about why.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Sit in a circle. Quickly name the four feelings you already
   know: happy, sad, angry, scared. Make each face together.
2. **Meet three new feelings (15 min).** For each new word, listen to a short story,
   then make the face and describe the body: **proud** (chest lifted, big smile, "I
   did it!"), **lonely** (shoulders droop, quiet, "nobody to play with"), **excited**
   (bouncy, eyes wide, "I can't wait!").
3. **Body check (10 min).** Choose one new feeling and act it out with your whole
   body. Where do you feel it? Proud might be warm and tall; lonely might be heavy;
   excited might be buzzy all over.
4. **Match game (10 min).** Pass the ball or point: when you hear a moment, show the
   card it matches. ("Your team wins the game" — proud. "You are the only one at the
   park" — lonely. "Tomorrow is your birthday" — excited.)
5. **Close (5 min).** Feelings grow bigger as you grow. More names mean you can share
   more. That is a good thing.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Introduce one new feeling at a time; use only pictures, no words.
- **Extension:** Draw two new feelings and label each with its name.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name or match proud, lonely, or
  excited to a described moment?

## Home connection

Teach one new feeling word — proud, lonely, or excited — to someone at home, and
share a time you felt it.
