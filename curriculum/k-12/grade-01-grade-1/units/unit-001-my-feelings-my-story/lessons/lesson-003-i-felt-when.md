# Lesson 3 — "I Felt ___ When ___"

## Summary

Learners meet a simple sentence frame — "I felt ___ when ___" — that joins a feeling
to the situation that caused it. They practice the frame aloud, with their bodies,
and in writing or drawing.

## Objectives

- Connect a feeling to the situation that caused it, using the frame "I felt ___
  when ___." _(D01.S1.01.02)_

## Connection

Every feeling comes from somewhere. You do not just feel excited — you feel excited
*when* your birthday comes, or *when* you see a friend. Putting the feeling and the
situation into one sentence — "I felt excited when my aunt arrived" — helps you
understand yourself and helps other people understand you, too.

## Materials

- A large "I felt ___ when ___" frame on chart paper
- Feeling face cards (happy, sad, angry, scared, proud, lonely, excited)
- Paper and pencils

## Preparation

- Write the frame "I felt ___ when ___" large enough for all to see.
- Set out the feeling cards as reminders.
- Prepare a few example sentences to model the frame.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Model the frame several times before
inviting learners to try it. Accept any feeling, even a "negative" one — the goal is
accurate naming, not "good" answers. Keep the frame concrete: a real situation the
learner experienced, not an abstract one.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Listen to two example sentences: "I felt excited when I saw my
   cousin" and "I felt lonely when my friend went home." What two parts does each
   sentence have? A feeling, and a when.
2. **Learn the frame (10 min).** Say the frame together: "I felt ___ when ___." The
   first blank is a feeling; the second blank is the situation that caused it. Clap
   the rhythm as you say it.
3. **Practice with the cards (10 min).** Choose a feeling card, then add a real
   "when." Say the whole sentence to a partner. ("I felt proud when I learned to
   swim.")
4. **Write or draw it (15 min).** Write the frame and fill in your own blanks, or
   draw a picture of the situation with the sentence below it.
5. **Close (5 min).** Feelings and situations go together like a lock and a key. The
   frame helps you put them together.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer the frame with the feeling word already filled in; the learner
  only supplies the "when."
- **Extension:** Add a "because" — "I felt proud when I learned to swim because I
  tried many times."

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner complete the frame with a real feeling
  and a real situation that match?

## Home connection

At home tonight, tell someone one thing that happened and how it made you feel,
using the frame "I felt ___ when ___."
