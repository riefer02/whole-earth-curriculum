# Lesson 5 — Making a Class Rule With a Reason

## Summary

Learners propose a rule that helps everyone feel safe and treated well, give one
reason for their idea, and take part in a shared choice. The lesson practices making
decisions together, with every voice heard.

## Objectives

- Take part in making a class rule or choice and give one reason for their idea.
  _(D09.S3.01.01)_

## Connection

Think of a game you play with friends. To play together, you have to agree on how it
goes — who goes first, what counts as a turn. A **rule** is a shared idea that helps
everyone live, work, and play together safely and fairly. People everywhere make
rules together — a village deciding when to draw water, a family deciding how to
share chores. Today you will help make a rule for your own class, and give a reason
for your idea.

## Materials

- A class rule chart (large sheet or poster)
- A soft object as a turn marker
- Choice tokens (a pebble, seed, or mark per learner)

## Preparation

- Prepare the class rule chart and a place to record ideas and reasons.
- Have the turn marker and choice tokens ready; arrange a circle.

## Facilitator note

Written to the learner ("you"). This lesson teaches **making decisions together**, not
obeying a teacher's rule. Rules here are questions about how we live well together,
not commands to obey — invite learners to wonder what the class needs, then propose a
rule and a reason. If you use a passing object so each person is heard, call it a
**turn marker** — a small cloth, stone, or ball. People everywhere find ways to
make sure one voice is heard at a time; a passing object is one simple, respectful
way to do that. The marker reminds everyone to listen while one person speaks —
it is not a decoration. Every voice matters — not just the loudest or fastest; invite quieter
voices and show that a good idea can come from anyone. A learner who does not speak
aloud proposes their rule by pointing to a picture, signing, or having a peer voice
it; a learner who is blind or low-vision names their rule and reason aloud and hears
the choices read back; a learner with a motor difference shows their choice by
directing a partner or using eye gaze, and their idea is recorded. Choice can be
shown many ways — a raised hand, a token, a step into a circle, a nod. Never require
anyone to speak in front of the group; a learner may always pass. The goal is a rule
the class *chose together* with reasons, not a rule handed down.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of a game. What rule makes it work? Why does that rule
   help everyone?
2. **Propose a rule (15 min).** Pass the turn marker. When you hold it, suggest a
   rule that helps everyone feel safe and treated well — or point to a picture, sign,
   or have a peer voice your idea. For each idea, give one **reason**: "This rule
   helps because…" Record each rule and reason on the chart.
3. **Choose together (15 min).** Look at the ideas. Show your choice — a token, a
   raised hand, a step into a circle, or a nod. Count together. The choice everyone
   helped make becomes the class rule.
4. **Say it in our own words (5 min).** Together, say the rule and its reason. Make
   sure it is a rule the whole class helped build.
5. **Close (5 min).** You made a rule together, with reasons. A rule made by
   everyone is a rule everyone can keep.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer three ready-made rule ideas with picture reasons to choose from;
  a non-speaking learner points or nods to choose. A learner with limited movement
  directs a partner to place their token.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to wonder what might happen if a rule were made by
  only one person and no one else had a voice — and why everyone's voice matters.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner take part in making a class rule and
  give one reason for their idea?
- **Performance:** The learner's proposal and its reason, recorded on the class chart.

## Home connection

At home, suggest one small family rule with a reason ("we wash hands before eating
because…"). Hear what others propose and why.
