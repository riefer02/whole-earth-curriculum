# Lesson 4 — What Everyone Needs to Feel Safe and Treated Well

## Summary

Learners name one thing every person in the class needs to feel safe and treated
well, and build a shared class list. The lesson holds one firm idea: these needs
belong to *every* person — no one is left out.

## Objectives

- Name one thing every person in the class needs to feel safe and treated well.
  _(D09.S2.01.02)_

## Connection

Think of how you know you are safe and treated well somewhere — someone says your
name kindly, there is room for your body, people listen when you speak, no one
hurts you. Every person needs these things, not just some people. In every
classroom, family, and village on Earth, people build a place where everyone can
feel safe and treated well. Today you will name what your class needs and write it
down together.

## Materials

- A large shared sheet or poster
- Markers or crayons
- A soft object as a turn marker

## Preparation

- Prepare a large shared sheet or clear space to build the class list.
- Have the turn marker ready; arrange a circle so every voice can be heard.

## Facilitator note

Written to the learner ("you"). This lesson is about **every person**, not "most
people" — the heart of egalitarianism is that needs are universal. Keep it concrete
for age ~6: a kind word, being listened to, space for one's body, a name said
kindly, no one hurting you, a turn to speak. If you use a passing object so each
person is heard, call it a **turn marker** — a small cloth, stone, or ball. People
everywhere in the world find ways to make sure one voice is heard at a time; a
passing object is one simple, respectful way to do that. The marker is a reminder
to listen while someone else speaks, not a decoration. The
marker also serves accessibility: a learner who does not speak aloud holds the
marker and points, signs, or gestures their idea; a learner who is blind or
low-vision feels the marker pass into their hands and names their need aloud; a
learner with a motor difference can have a partner place the marker for them while
they show their idea. Every voice matters — not just the loudest or fastest. A
learner may always pass. This is not a test of right answers; every need offered is
a real contribution.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of a place where you feel safe and treated well. What
   makes it feel that way?
2. **Wonder: what does everyone need? (10 min).** Name one thing every person in the
   class needs — a kind word, being listened to, space for their body, a name said
   kindly, no one hurting you. Why does *every* person need it, not just some?
3. **Pass the turn marker (15 min).** In the circle, pass the turn marker. When you
   hold it, name (or show, point, or sign) one thing everyone needs. Everyone else
   listens. Add each idea to the shared list.
4. **Make our list (10 min).** Draw or write one need on the class sheet. Look at
   all the needs together — this is what your class needs to feel safe and treated
   well.
5. **Close (5 min).** These needs belong to everyone, everywhere. Your class now has
   its own words for taking care of each other.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer picture cards of needs to point to; model one idea ("everyone
  needs a kind word"). A non-speaking learner points to a picture; a learner with
  limited movement directs a partner to write for them.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to wonder whether these needs are the same at home,
  at the market, or in a village far away — and to share what they think.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name one thing every person in the
  class needs to feel safe and treated well?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The class "safe and treated well" list, kept on the wall
  and copied into learners' portfolios.

## Home connection

At home, ask: what does every person in our family need to feel safe and treated
well? Share your class list and hear theirs.
