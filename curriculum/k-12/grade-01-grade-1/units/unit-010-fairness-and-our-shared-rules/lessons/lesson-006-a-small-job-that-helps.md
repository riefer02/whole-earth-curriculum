# Lesson 6 — A Small Job That Helps

## Summary

Learners do a small job that helps the classroom or school and tell how it helped.
Helping is framed as a contribution everyone can make, not a reward to win or a way
to rank children.

## Objectives

- Do a small job that helps the classroom or school and tell how it helped.
  _(D09.S3.01.02)_

## Connection

Think of the small things that make your home or classroom a good place to be —
plants watered, the floor clear, pencils ready, a friend helped to carry something.
When you do one of these, you are **contributing**: you are helping the place and the
people in it. Everywhere in the world, people do small jobs that help everyone — a
child sweeping the family's courtyard, a neighbor watching a pot of soup, someone
carrying water for an elder. Today you will do one small job and tell how it helped.

## Materials

- A class job chart (water the plants, tidy the shelf, help carry, sharpen pencils,
  care for a class animal, if there is one)
- Job picture cards

## Preparation

- Choose a few safe, real small jobs learners can actually do in your space.
- Prepare the job chart and picture cards, and gather any objects the jobs need.

## Facilitator note

Written to the learner ("you"). The central commitment is **contributions, not
rewards, and no ranking**: a job is something everyone can do to help, not a prize
for the "best" child or a way to sort children into helpers and non-helpers. Every
job matters equally — watering a plant helps as much as carrying a book; do not
rank jobs as "big" or "small" in worth. Rotate jobs so everyone takes a turn, and
celebrate the *helping*, never compare children. Keep jobs safe and concrete for age
~6. A learner who does not speak aloud chooses a job by pointing to a card and tells
how it helped by pointing, signing, or gesturing; a learner who is blind or
low-vision does a job by touch and names how it helped aloud; a learner with a motor
difference can do a job by directing a partner or by choosing a job that suits how
they move, and their help counts fully. A learner may always pass. The aim is that
every learner can say, in their own way, "I helped, and here is how."

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of a time you helped at home or in class. How did your
   help make things better?
2. **Meet the jobs (10 min).** Look at the job chart and picture cards. Name each
   job and wonder: who does it help, and how?
3. **Do a small job (15 min).** Choose one small job — point to its card, or pick up
   the real object. Do the job carefully. A learner who needs a partner can do it
   together.
4. **Tell how it helped (10 min).** Tell a partner (or show, sign, or point): "I
   helped by… and it helped because…" Listen to how their job helped too.
5. **Close (5 min).** Every job helped, and every helper's contribution matters.
   Your class is better because you helped.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer two jobs to choose from; model the sentence "I helped by… and it
  helped because…" A non-speaking learner points to the job and to a picture of who
  it helped.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to notice another job the class or school needs and
  propose it for the chart, with a reason.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner do a small job and tell how it helped?
- **Performance:** The job done and the learner's own words about how it helped.

## Home connection

At home, do one small job that helps your family and tell a grown-up how it helped.
