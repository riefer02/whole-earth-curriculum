# Lesson 8 — Listening to a Friend's Feelings

## Summary

Learners practice the listening half of solving a conflict: facing the speaker,
waiting their turn, and repeating back the feeling they heard. They discover that
both sides in a conflict deserve to be heard.

## Objectives

- Use words to say how they feel during a conflict and listen when a friend says how
  they feel. _(D09.S4.01.01 — listening)_

## Connection

Think of a time someone really listened to you — they looked at you, waited, and you
could tell they heard you. Being **heard** feels like being cared for. In a conflict,
saying how you feel is only half of it; the other half is **listening** when your
friend says how they feel. People everywhere, in every language, show care by
listening. Today you will practice being the listener.

## Materials

- A listening picture strip (look/face toward the speaker, wait your turn, hear and
  repeat back their feeling)
- Two puppets or soft toys
- A soft object as a turn marker

## Preparation

- Prepare the listening picture strip and have the turn marker ready.
- Arrange pairs or small groups where learners can sit face to face.

## Facilitator note

Written to the learner ("you"). This lesson teaches that **both sides get heard** — in
a conflict, no one side is the only one with feelings. Listening is active, not
passive: face toward the speaker (in whatever way a learner can — a learner who is
blind or low-vision turns an ear toward the voice and shows listening by stillness
and a response; a learner who is deaf or hard of hearing watches the speaker's face
and signs "I hear you" or nods), wait for their turn without interrupting, and repeat
back the feeling. If you use a passing object to mark whose turn it is, call it a
**turn marker**. People everywhere find ways to make sure one voice is heard at a
time; a passing object is one simple, respectful way. It is a reminder to listen
while one person speaks, not a decoration. The turn marker also supports accessibility: a
learner who does not speak aloud holds it and points, signs, or gestures their
feeling while the listener hears it; a learner with a motor difference can have a
partner place the marker. "Repeating back" can be spoken, signed, or shown by
pointing to the feeling on a board. Keep the practice gentle and free of blame; never
require anyone to share a real, private feeling — invented or acted feelings are
welcome, and a learner may always pass. Listening is a gift everyone can give,
whatever their body can do.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of a time someone listened to you. How did it feel to
   be heard?
2. **Meet the listening steps (10 min).** Learn to listen: **face toward** the
   speaker (or turn an ear, or watch their hands), **wait** for their turn without
   interrupting, and **hear and repeat back** their feeling.
3. **Practice with a partner (15 min).** Sit face to face. One of you holds the turn
   marker and says, signs, or points to a feeling. The other listens and repeats it
   back: "I hear that you feel…" Then trade — everyone gets to speak and to listen.
4. **Listen in a small conflict (10 min).** With the puppets, show a small conflict.
   Practice listening to both sides, one at a time, repeating back each feeling.
5. **Close (5 min).** In a conflict, both sides have feelings, and both deserve to
   be heard. Listening is how we show we care.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Practice only "wait for your turn" first, using the turn marker; model
  repeating back one feeling. A non-speaking learner points to the feeling they
  heard; a learner with limited movement directs a partner.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to practice listening to two different feelings in a
  row and repeating back both, noticing what changes.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner listen when a friend says how they
  feel and repeat back the feeling?
- **Performance:** The practiced listening exchange with a partner, reflected on
  aloud.

## Home connection

At home, practice being the listener: let someone tell you about their day and repeat
back one feeling you heard.
