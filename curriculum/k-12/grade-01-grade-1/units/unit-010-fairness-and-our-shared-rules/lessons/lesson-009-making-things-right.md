# Lesson 9 — Making Things Right

## Summary

Learners practice suggesting a way to make things right after a small hurt — return
what was taken, fix what was broken, ask what would help — and learn that repair,
not punishment, heals the hurt so everyone can be well again.

## Objectives

- Suggest one way to make things right after someone's feelings or something was
  hurt. _(D09.S4.01.02)_

## Connection

Think of a time something small was broken or someone's feelings were hurt — a tower
knocked over, a toy grabbed, a mean word said. The hurt can be fixed. **Making it
right** means repairing the hurt: returning what was taken, fixing what was broken,
saying sorry in a way that means it, and checking the person is okay. People
everywhere repair what is broken — mending a torn net, replanting a broken branch,
saying sorry to a friend. Today you will practice making things right.

## Materials

- A "make it right" picture strip (say what happened, name how the other felt, ask
  what would help, do the repair, check back)
- Two puppets or soft toys
- Crayons and paper

## Preparation

- Prepare the "make it right" picture strip.
- Set up a small space for acting out a gentle hurt and its repair.

## Facilitator note

Written to the learner ("you"). This lesson teaches **repair, not punishment**. After
a small hurt, the goal is making things right — returning what was taken, fixing what
was broken, a sorry that means it, checking the friend is okay — never shaming,
excluding, or "getting in trouble." Name the *act* ("that word hurt"), never the
child; there are no "bad children," only hurts to repair. An apology is real when it
means something: the person names what happened, notices how the other felt, and
tries to fix it. A learner may suggest any honest repair — a kind word, a returned
toy, help rebuilding — there is no single correct answer. Keep hurts small and
gentle (a knocked-over block tower, a grabbed turn, a hurtful word), never heavy or
frightening, and never ask a learner to confess a real private hurt. A learner who
does not speak aloud suggests a repair by pointing to a step or a picture, signing,
or having a peer voice it; a learner who is blind or low-vision names the repair
aloud and touches the repaired object; a learner with a motor difference can direct
a partner to do the repair while they choose it. A learner may always pass. The aim
is that every learner can say, in their own way, "I can help make it right."

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of a small hurt you have seen fixed — something
   returned, something rebuilt, a sorry that felt real. How did it feel when things
   were made right?
2. **Meet the steps (10 min).** Learn to make it right: say what happened, name how
   the other person felt, ask what would help, do the repair, and check back. A
   **sorry that matters** is one where you mean it and try to fix the harm.
3. **Act it out (15 min).** With the puppets, show a small hurt — one puppet knocks
   over the other's block tower. Practice making it right: "I knocked your tower
   over. You look sad. Can I help you build it again?" Then help rebuild. A learner
   who does not move the puppets can point to a step or direct a partner.
4. **Suggest a repair (10 min).** Think of another small hurt. Suggest one way to
   make it right (say, sign, point, or draw it). Wonder together: does it fix the
   hurt and help everyone feel better?
5. **Close (5 min).** Hurts can be repaired. Making things right heals the hurt so
   everyone can be well again.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Practice one step at a time — first "name how the other felt," then
  "do one small fix." A non-speaking learner points to the repair on the strip; a
  learner with limited movement directs a partner.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to compare two repairs for the same hurt and wonder
  which heals better and why.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner suggest one way to make things right
  after feelings or something was hurt?
- **Performance:** The acted-out repair, shared with the group.

## Home connection

At home, if a small hurt happens, try making it right together — return, fix, say a
sorry that matters, and check everyone is okay.
