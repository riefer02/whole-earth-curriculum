# Lesson 6 — Letter Groups

## Summary

Learners discover that marks often travel together as groups — letter groups in
alphabetic systems, or syllable and word parts in character systems — and that
these groups stay together as one chunk of a word. They practice spotting and
saying groups inside simple words.

## Objectives

- Decode simple words by sounding out letters and letter groups in their home
  writing system. _(D04.S2.01.01)_

## Connection

Some friends always walk to school together, side by side. Marks in writing do
this too — certain marks appear together so often that we read them as one part.
Reading groups as one chunk makes reading faster, the way a cook who knows a
recipe stirs several steps at once.

## Materials

- A chart of one or two common letter groups (or syllable blocks)
- Word cards built from those groups

## Preparation

- Choose 1–2 letter groups (or syllable blocks) from the learners' home writing
  system.
- Prepare word cards that contain each group.
- Plan to model finding the group inside a word.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Choose groups that are real in the learners' **home writing
system** — in an alphabetic system, a letter group such as a common vowel pair; in
a syllabic or character system, a syllable block or a two-mark combination.
Remain script-agnostic; do not import English-specific groups into a different
system. Explicitly model, then guide practice (philosophy §13). For a learner who
is blind or has low vision, make the group **tactile** (raised string, sand-glue,
or clay) so it can be felt and found by touch; a learner who does not use a
speaking voice spots and says the group by pointing, tapping, or using a device.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Look at the group on the chart. Say it together. It is a
   team of marks that almost always stays together.
2. **Meet the group (10 min).** Listen as the group is said slowly, then fast.
   Notice it is one part, one chunk — clap once for the whole group, not once for
   each mark.
3. **Find it in words (15 min).** Look at a word card. Slide your finger until you
   spot the group hiding inside. Underline it. Say the whole word, keeping the
   group together as one part.
4. **Build with groups (10 min).** Take the group and add marks before or after it
   to make a real word. Read your new word aloud. Does it sound right?
5. **Close (5 min).** Reading in groups makes words come faster. The marks of your
   language work as a team, and now you can read them as one.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Practice only one group, finding it in two words and saying it many
  times.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Use tactile group shapes and find them by touch.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Point to or tap the group and blend with a gesture or
  a device instead of a spoken voice.
- **Extension:** Find the same group in a short sentence and read the sentence
  aloud.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner spot a letter group inside a word
  and say the word with the group kept together as one part?

## Home connection

Show someone at home the letter group you practiced, and find it together inside
one written word.
