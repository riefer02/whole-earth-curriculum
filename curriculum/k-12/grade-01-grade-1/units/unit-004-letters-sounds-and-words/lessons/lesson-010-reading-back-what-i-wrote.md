# Lesson 10 — Reading Back What I Wrote

## Summary

Learners write a simple word or two, then read them back, checking that the marks
say what they meant. Reading back closes the loop between writing and reading and
builds confidence that their marks carry meaning.

## Objectives

- Write letters and simple words to express an idea, and read back what they
  wrote. _(D04.S3.01.01)_

## Connection

A word you write is a message you send — to a reader, or to your future self. But
a message only works if you can read it back and see your idea still there.
Reading back is like checking a wrapped gift before giving it: you look to be sure
what is inside is what you meant to give.

## Materials

- The learner's own written words (from a previous day, or written fresh today)
- A pointer or finger

## Preparation

- Gather the learners' previously written words, or plan time to write fresh ones.
- Have a pointer or finger ready for tracking.
- Plan to model reading back one word.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Model reading back a word while pointing to each mark, then
guide practice. Celebrate the connection between writing and reading — a learner
reading their own marks is a milestone. Do not grade handwriting or spelling here;
the goal is that the writer can read their own writing and confirm its meaning. A learner may have composed their word
by dictating to a scribe, typing, or choosing marks — reading back that word is
still reading their own writing, and it counts equally.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Look at a word you wrote before. Can you still tell what it
   says? Today you will read your own writing back.
2. **Watch a read-back (5 min).** Watch a word being read back: a finger points
   under each mark, and the voice says the sounds or syllables in order, then the
   whole word.
3. **Write fresh (10 min).** Write one or two words you know — a thing you like, a
   place, a person. Write slowly and carefully.
4. **Read it back (15 min).** Point under each mark of your word and read it back
   aloud, just like the model. Then say what the word means. Check: do the marks
   say what you meant? If a mark is missing or mixed up, fix it and read again.
5. **Read to a partner (5 min).** Read your word to a partner. Let them check too:
   does it match what you say it means?
6. **Close (5 min).** You wrote it, and you read it — the marks you made carry your
   idea. That is reading and writing, working together.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Read back a single word together, pointing for the learner, then let
  them try alone.
- **Extension:** Read back two words in a row and say them as a little sentence.

## Assessment

- **Formative (self):** Can the learner read back their own written word and
  confirm its meaning, fixing it if needed?

## Home connection

Write a word, leave it somewhere a family member will find it, and read it to them
when they do.
