# Lesson 14 — Greetings in Another Language

## Summary

Learners learn a greeting and a courtesy word — such as please or thank you — in
another language, and practice using them with a partner. They discover that a
greeting in someone's language is a gift of respect.

## Objectives

- Learn and use a greeting and a courtesy word, such as please or thank you, in
  another language. _(D04.S4.01.02)_

## Connection

When you greet someone in their own language, you are saying, "I see you, and I
want to meet you where you are." Even one word — hello, please, thank you — opens a
door between two people. Learning a greeting in another language is a small act
with big kindness, and it is how strangers become neighbors.

## Materials

- A chart of a greeting and a courtesy word in one or more languages
- Greeting cards for practice

## Preparation

- Choose one or more languages to teach, ideally one spoken by a learner or
  community member.
- Prepare the chart with the greeting, the courtesy word, and their meanings.
- Plan to have learners practice greeting each other.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Where possible, learn from a fluent speaker so the sounds
are right; if no speaker is available, use a careful recording. There is **no
"correct" accent** — the goal is a respectful attempt, not perfection. Honor the
language and its speakers; never reduce a language to a gimmick. Let learners who
already know the language be the experts and teachers. Signed languages carry
greetings and courtesy words too — a learner may greet in a sign language, or with
a gesture, wave, or device; these are greetings, not lesser ones.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of how you greet a friend. Now imagine greeting
   someone whose home language is different from yours. Wouldn't it be kind to say
   hello in their words?
2. **Learn the greeting (10 min).** Listen to the greeting. Say it slowly together,
   then at a normal speed. Notice how it feels in your mouth. Repeat it until it
   feels comfortable.
3. **Learn the courtesy word (10 min).** Now learn a courtesy word — "please" or
   "thank you." Say it slowly, then naturally. Learn what it means and when to use
   it.
4. **Practice together (15 min).** Walk around the room. Greet each classmate with
   the new greeting, and use the courtesy word — thank someone, or say please when
   you ask. Trade greetings with as many people as you can.
5. **Close (5 min).** One greeting, one kind word — and you have reached across
   the space between languages. Every attempt at another's language is a gift of
   respect.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Learn only the greeting today, and use it with a smile and a wave.
- **Support (deaf/non-speaking):** Greet in a sign language or with a gesture,
  wave, or device — the respectful reaching-out is the greeting.
- **Extension:** Learn one more courtesy word, or teach the greeting to someone at
  home and report back.

## Assessment

- **Formative (performance):** Can the learner say the greeting and the courtesy
  word in another language and use them with a partner?

## Home connection

Greet someone at home with the new greeting tonight, and teach them the courtesy
word.
