# Lesson 13 — My Home Language

## Summary

Learners name their home language or languages and share a few words in each with
pride. Together they build a class "word treasure" that treats every language as
wealth and no language as better than another.

## Objectives

- Name their home language or languages and share a few words in each with pride.
  _(D04.S4.01.01)_

## Connection

The first words you ever heard were probably in your home language — the language
of your family's lullabies, greetings, and stories. Your home language is a
treasure you carry with you, wherever you go. Sharing its words with others is
sharing a piece of your home, and every language is a treasure to its people.

## Materials

- A "language treasure" chart or wall
- Word cards for home-language words

## Preparation

- Prepare the language treasure chart or wall.
- Prepare word cards (or plan for drawing).
- Think of how you will welcome every language, including signed languages and
  languages that are mostly spoken, not written.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. This is a **values-critical** lesson: honor each learner's
home language first, and treat multilingualism as wealth, never a deficit. Do not
rank languages or scripts, and remember that oral and signed traditions are full
languages too — a learner's language may not have a written form, and that is
fine. Celebrate every word with genuine warmth, and let the home language lead.
Sharing is by invitation, never requirement: a learner may pass, draw a word
instead, or share only with a trusted partner — no one is ever made to perform
their language. A learner may also share by signing, pointing to a picture, or
using a device; their home language may be a signed or spoken (not written) one,
and that is a full language to celebrate.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think: what language or languages does your family speak at
   home? You might speak one, or more than one. Hold the name of yours in your
   mind.
2. **Name your language (10 min).** Say the name of your home language aloud to
   the group. If you speak more than one, name them all. Each name is a treasure.
3. **Share your words (15 min).** Choose two or three words from your home
   language to share — words for love, food, family, or something you love. Say
   each word, then tell what it means. Everyone listens and repeats it with care.
4. **Build the word treasure (10 min).** Write or draw your words on a card and add
   them to the class treasure chart. Look at all the different words gathered
   there — many languages, all valuable.
5. **Close (5 min).** Your home language is part of who you are. Sharing it makes
   everyone richer, and no language is better than another — every one is a
   treasure.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Share just one word; the facilitator or a peer helps say it slowly
  and write it.
- **Extension:** Teach the group a short phrase or a counting rhyme from your home
  language.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name their home language and share a
  few of its words with pride?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The learner's word card in the class word treasure.

## Home connection

Ask someone at home for one more special word from your home language and bring it
to share tomorrow.
