# Lesson 15 — Letters, Sounds & Words Festival

## Summary

The unit's capstone: a "language festival" where learners bring together everything
they have practiced — listening and asking questions, speaking in complete
sentences, sounding out and reading aloud with a beginning, middle, and end,
writing a complete sentence, and sharing their home language and a greeting in
another. It is a celebration of every language as wealth.

## Objectives

- Listen to a speaker or story and ask or answer a simple question about it.
  _(D04.S1.01.01)_
- Speak in complete sentences to share an idea or experience. _(D04.S1.01.02)_
- Decode simple words by sounding out letters and letter groups in their home
  writing system. _(D04.S2.01.01)_
- Read a simple text aloud and retell its beginning, middle, and end.
  _(D04.S2.01.02)_
- Write letters and simple words and read back what they wrote. _(D04.S3.01.01)_
- Compose a complete sentence with a capital letter and an end mark.
  _(D04.S3.01.02)_
- Name their home language and share a few of its words with pride.
  _(D04.S4.01.01)_
- Learn and use a greeting and a courtesy word in another language.
  _(D04.S4.01.02)_

## Connection

Over these weeks you have learned to listen, speak, read, write, and greet in more
than one language — the same powers people everywhere use to share their inner
worlds and build shared ones. Today you put them all together, the way a cook
gathers every ingredient into one feast. Language is how we reach each other, and
you are now a traveler with many ways to do it.

## Materials

- A short story or text in the home writing system
- Writing surfaces and tools
- A "language festival" display space

## Preparation

- Set up four festival stations: Listen & Ask, Read & Retell, Write, Share
  Languages.
- Prepare the story or text, writing surfaces, and display space.
- Plan how learners rotate through stations, and who guides each one.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. This is a **summative celebration**, not a ranking — assess
what each learner can *do* and celebrate it. Keep every commitment alive: honor
home language first, treat multilingualism as wealth, remain script-agnostic, and
never rank languages, scripts, or accents. Where possible, invite families or
community members to share in the festival. Oral and signed traditions are
welcomed as full equals. Every station honors every way of communicating: a
learner may read by signing or by pointing while a partner or device voices, ask
and answer by signing or pointing, write by dictating or typing, and greet by
signing or gesturing — each is a full success at the festival.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Welcome to the language festival! You have four things to do
   today, one at each station.
2. **Listen & Ask (10 min).** Hear a short story. Listen with your whole body, then
   ask one question about it — or answer a friend's question in a complete
   sentence.
3. **Read & Retell (10 min).** Read a simple text aloud, sounding out tricky words
   as you go. Then retell its beginning, middle, and end in your own words.
4. **Write (10 min).** Write one complete sentence about the festival or the
   story — capital letter, full thought, end mark. Then read it back and add it to
   the display.
5. **Share Languages (10 min).** Say the name of your home language and share a
   word or two with pride. Greet a classmate in another language and use a
   courtesy word — please or thank you.
6. **Close (5 min).** Look at all you can do now. You listen, speak, read, write,
   and reach across languages. Every one of them is a treasure, and every language
   — including yours — makes the world richer.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Travel with a partner or guide who reads the prompts aloud; complete
  one task per station at your own pace.
- **Extension:** Add a second sentence at the writing station, or teach the group a
  phrase in your home language.

## Assessment

- **Summative (performance/portfolio):** Across the four stations, can the learner
  ask or answer a question, read and retell a text, compose a complete sentence,
  and share a home-language word and a greeting in another language? Collect each
  learner's written sentence and word card as portfolio artifacts.

## Home connection

Invite your family to hear you read, see your sentence, and teach them the
greeting you learned — then celebrate together.
