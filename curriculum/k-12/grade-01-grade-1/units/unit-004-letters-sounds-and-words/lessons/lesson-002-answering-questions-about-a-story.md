# Lesson 2 — Answering Questions About a Story

## Summary

Learners listen to a short story and practice answering simple questions about it —
who was there, what happened, and where it took place. They build from short
answers toward full sentences, so others can understand them.

## Objectives

- Listen to a speaker or story and answer a simple question about it.
  _(D04.S1.01.01)_

## Connection

After someone tells a story, people often ask, "Who was it about? What happened?
Where?" Answering well shows that you listened and lets you share the story with
someone else. Every culture keeps and passes on its stories this way — by telling
and answering.

## Materials

- A short story to tell or read aloud
- Question cards (who, what, where)

## Preparation

- Choose a story with a clear who, what, and where — a few sentences is enough.
- Prepare the three question cards.
- Arrange a circle for listening and answering.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Prepare the story and cards, then guide the answering
practice. Answers can be short at first; gently stretch learners toward full
sentences ("A little bird" → "A little bird was looking for water"). Honor every
way of telling — there is no single correct accent or way of speaking. An answer
can be spoken, signed, pointed to on a picture, or said through a device — all are
answers. For a learner who is Deaf or hard of hearing, use a picture book or a
signed or written telling of the story, and accept a signed or pointed answer as
fully as a spoken one.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Sit comfortably. Get ready to listen with your whole body,
   like you practiced before.
2. **Listen to a story (5 min).** Hear the story once, all the way through. Do not
   talk yet — just listen.
3. **Learn the three questions (5 min).** Meet three questions that fit almost any
   story: **Who** was there? **What** happened? **Where** did it happen? Hold up
   one, two, or three fingers to match each.
4. **Answer together (15 min).** Hear the story again. Now answer each question
   aloud. First try a short answer — "a bird." Then try it as a full sentence —
   "A bird was thirsty." Say both ways and hear the difference.
5. **Partner practice (10 min).** With a partner, one of you asks a who, what, or
   where question, and the other answers in a full sentence. Then switch.
6. **Close (5 min).** Listening plus answering means the story travels from one
   person to another. You just kept a story alive.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Ask only "who" questions at first; accept a pointing or one-word
  answer, then model the full sentence.
- **Support (deaf/Hard of Hearing):** Use a picture book or signed/written story;
  accept a signed or pointed answer.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Answer by signing, pointing, choosing a picture, or
  using a device, then have a partner voice the full sentence.
- **Extension:** Answer a "why" question — why do you think it happened — and give a
  guess with a reason.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner answer a who, what, or where
  question about the story, ideally in a full sentence?

## Home connection

Retell one story you know to someone at home, then ask them a "what happened"
question and listen to their answer.
