# Lesson 12 — Writing My Own Sentence

## Summary

Learners compose their own complete sentence about a topic or picture, using a
capital letter and an end mark, and share what they wrote. This is where all the
unit's writing skills come together.

## Objectives

- Compose one or more complete sentences about a topic or picture, using a capital
  letter and an end mark. _(D04.S3.01.02)_

## Connection

You have ideas every day — what you see on the way to school, what you love to eat,
who you play with. Writing lets you catch one idea and pin it to the page as a
complete sentence, with a capital to open it and an end mark to close it. Today
you will be the author of your own sentence.

## Materials

- A picture or topic to write about
- A writing surface and tool

## Preparation

- Choose a picture or topic (or a real scene) learners can describe.
- Set out writing surfaces.
- Plan to model composing one sentence aloud, then in writing.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Model "say it, write it, check it" explicitly, then guide
practice (philosophy §13). The convention of a capital letter and end mark belongs
to the learner's writing system; honor other systems' ways of marking sentences and
never rank them. Celebrate every attempt — composing is the goal; inventing
spelling is a normal and healthy part of early writing and should not be
discouraged here. A learner may compose by dictating to a scribe, typing on any
device, or arranging word cards — the sentence, not the handwriting, is the goal.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Look at the picture (or topic). Think of one thing it makes
   you want to say.
2. **Say it first (5 min).** Say your idea aloud as a complete sentence, with a who
   and a what happened. Say it a second time so it is firm in your mind.
3. **Write it (15 min).** Write your sentence. Open with a capital letter, and
   finish with an end mark — a period, question mark, or exclamation mark. Write
   slowly and carefully.
4. **Check it (10 min).** Read your sentence back. Does it have a capital at the
   start? An end mark at the finish? A full thought in the middle? Fix anything
   missing.
5. **Share it (5 min).** Read your sentence to a partner or the group. Listen to
   theirs. Each one is an idea caught on the page.
6. **Close (5 min).** You composed a sentence — your own words, your own idea. That
   is what writers do.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use a sentence frame ("I see ___.") and write it together, with the
  capital and end mark pointed out.
- **Support (motor-limited):** Compose by dictating to a scribe, typing, or
  arranging word cards, then check the capital and end mark by pointing.
- **Extension:** Write two sentences about the same topic, each with a capital and
  an end mark.

## Assessment

- **Formative (portfolio):** Can the learner compose a complete sentence about the
  topic or picture with a capital letter and an end mark? Keep the sentence as a
  portfolio artifact.

## Home connection

Write one sentence about something at home and read it to your family at a meal.
