# Lesson 8 — Beginning, Middle, End

## Summary

Learners read a simple text and learn to retell it in order — beginning, middle,
and end. They use a three-part frame to hold the story's shape, so a retelling
keeps its sense.

## Objectives

- Read a simple text aloud and retell its beginning, middle, and end.
  _(D04.S2.01.02)_

## Connection

A story is like a path with three parts: where it starts, what happens along the
way, and where it lands. If you skip the middle, the story makes no sense — like
telling someone you baked a cake but forgetting the mixing and the oven. Retelling
the beginning, middle, and end keeps the whole story whole.

## Materials

- A simple text with a clear beginning, middle, and end
- A three-part story frame (beginning, middle, end)

## Preparation

- Choose a story with three clear parts.
- Prepare the three-part frame (three boxes labeled beginning, middle, end).
- Plan to model retelling once.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Model a retelling in three clear moves, then guide
practice. Retelling is about order and understanding, not exact words — honor a
learner's own wording. Oral traditions carry stories the same way; celebrate
retelling as an ancient and universal skill. A retelling can be spoken, signed,
pointed to in pictures, or acted out — holding the order is what matters.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Listen to or read the story once, all the way through.
2. **Meet the three parts (5 min).** Every story has a **beginning** (where it
   starts), a **middle** (what happens), and an **end** (where it lands). Say the
   three words and hold up one, two, three fingers.
3. **Find the parts (10 min).** Think about the story. What happened first? That is
   the beginning. What happened next, in the middle? What happened last, at the
   end?
4. **Fill the frame (15 min).** In the three boxes, draw or write one picture for
   each part. Then point to each box and retell that part aloud in order:
   beginning, middle, end.
5. **Retell to a partner (5 min).** Tell the whole story to a partner in three
   moves. Listen as your partner retells it back.
6. **Close (5 min).** Order is what makes a story make sense. Beginning, middle,
   end — that is the shape of every tale, in every language.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Retell with just two parts at first (beginning, end), then add the
  middle; use pictures instead of words.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Retell by pointing to three pictures in order,
  signing, or acting out each part — the order is the retelling.
- **Extension:** Retell a second story and notice what the middle adds that the
  beginning and end alone cannot tell.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner retell the story's beginning,
  middle, and end in order, in their own words?

## Home connection

Retell a favorite story to someone at home in three parts — beginning, middle, and
end — and ask them to guess which part was the middle.
