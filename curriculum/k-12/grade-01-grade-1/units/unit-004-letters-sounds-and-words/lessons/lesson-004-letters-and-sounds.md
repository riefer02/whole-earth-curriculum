# Lesson 4 — Letters and Sounds

## Summary

Learners meet the marks of their own writing system — letters or characters — and
discover that marks stand for sounds, syllables, or whole words. They match a few
marks to what they say, honoring the writing system of their home language.

## Objectives

- Decode simple words by sounding out letters and letter groups in their home
  writing system. _(D04.S2.01.01)_

## Connection

Your language is written with marks. In some writing systems a mark stands for one
sound; in others a mark stands for a whole syllable or even a whole word. Every
system works, and no writing system is better than another — they are just
different ways humans record their words. Today you will look closely at the marks
of your own language.

## Materials

- A chart of a few marks from the learners' home writing system
- Picture cards of familiar words

## Preparation

- Choose 3–5 marks (letters or characters) from the learners' home writing system.
- For each mark, pick a familiar word and picture that use it.
- Prepare the chart or plan to write marks large.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. The key commitment is **script-agnosticism**: do not assume
the Latin alphabet or that "one mark = one sound." A learner's system may be
Arabic, Devanagari, Chinese characters, Cyrillic, Ethiopic, Hangul, or another —
honor it. Work in the writing system(s) actually present in your classroom or home,
and never rank scripts. If learners use different systems, celebrate each one
side by side. For a learner who is blind or has low vision, make the marks
**tactile** — raised lines of string, sand-glue, clay, or found objects — so the
shape can be felt with the hands; the learner points or touches to match a mark to
its sound. A learner who does not use a speaking voice matches by pointing,
touching, or gesturing instead of saying the sound aloud.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of a word you say every day. How would you write it
   down? Every language has its own way — its own **writing system**.
2. **Meet your marks (10 min).** Look at each mark on the chart. Say its sound (or
   its syllable or word) out loud together. Notice its shape — where it is tall,
   where it curves, where it connects to its neighbors.
3. **Match mark to word (15 min).** For each mark, find the picture whose word uses
   it. Say the word slowly and listen for the mark inside it. In a system where a
   mark is a whole syllable or word, point to the picture whose word that mark
   already tells.
4. **Write it big (10 min).** With your finger or a stick, trace each mark large in
   the air or on the ground while saying its sound. Feel its shape with your whole
   arm.
5. **Close (5 min).** The marks of your writing system carry your language's
   sounds. Learning them is the first step to reading. Every language's marks are a
   treasure.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Introduce just two marks; match each to one picture and repeat the
  sound many times.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Use raised or textured marks so the shape can be
  felt; match by touch, not sight.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Match mark to sound by pointing or touching rather
  than saying it aloud.
- **Extension:** Find another word that uses the same mark and say it, hearing the
  shared sound.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner match a mark to the sound, syllable,
  or word it stands for, and say it back?

## Home connection

Ask someone at home to show you one mark of your home writing system and tell you
its sound. Bring what you learned to share.
