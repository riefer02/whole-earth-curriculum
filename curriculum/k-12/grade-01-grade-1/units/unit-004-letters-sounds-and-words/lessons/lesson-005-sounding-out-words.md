# Lesson 5 — Sounding Out Words

## Summary

Learners learn to decode — to sound out a word mark by mark and blend the parts
into a whole word. In alphabetic systems they blend sounds; in character systems
they combine syllables or word-marks. They practice with explicit modeling and
guided practice in their own writing system.

## Objectives

- Decode simple words by sounding out letters and letter groups in their home
  writing system. _(D04.S2.01.01)_

## Connection

Reading is like putting beads on a string: you say each part, one after another,
then slide them together into one word. A cook follows steps in order to make a
dish, and a reader follows marks in order to make a word. Today you will learn to
sound out and blend — the heart of decoding.

## Materials

- A short list of simple words in the home writing system
- A sliding pointer or finger

## Preparation

- Write 3–5 simple words made of marks the learners know.
- Plan to model "sound out, then blend" for each word.
- Have pictures for the words when possible, so learners can check their blend.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Use **explicit instruction, worked examples, and guided
practice** (philosophy §13). Stay **script-agnostic**: in an alphabetic system,
sound out letter by letter and blend; in a syllabic or character system, combine
the syllables or word-marks the same way. Never assume one mark = one sound. Honor
the learner's actual writing system, and celebrate every way words are built. For
a learner who is blind or has low vision, offer **tactile marks** (raised string,
sand-glue, clay, or textured objects) so each part can be felt and blended by
touch. A learner who does not use a speaking voice sounds out by pointing, tapping,
or touching each mark in order and then blending with a gesture or a device.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Watch your teacher (or a helper) read one word. Notice they
   do not guess the whole word at once — they build it piece by piece.
2. **Watch and listen (10 min).** For one word, point under the first mark, say its
   sound, then the next, then the next. Now slide your finger under the whole word
   and say it fast, blended together. That is **sounding out** and **blending**.
3. **Do it together (10 min).** Pick a word. Point under each mark together and say
   each sound or syllable. Then blend it in one voice. Check: does it match the
   picture?
4. **Try it yourself (15 min).** Sound out the next words on your own or with a
   partner — point, say each part, then blend. If the blend sounds strange, go
   slower and try again.
5. **Close (5 min).** Decoding means you do not need anyone to tell you the word —
   you can sound it out yourself. That power belongs to every reader.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use two-mark words only; blend the word for the learner and have
  them echo it, then try together.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Use tactile marks so blending can happen by touch,
  tapping each part in order.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Point, tap, or touch each mark in order and blend
  with a gesture or a device rather than a spoken voice.
- **Extension:** Sound out a longer word and read it in a full sentence.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner point to each mark, say its sound or
  syllable, and blend it into the correct word?

## Home connection

Find one word in your home writing system and sound it out for someone at home,
mark by mark.
