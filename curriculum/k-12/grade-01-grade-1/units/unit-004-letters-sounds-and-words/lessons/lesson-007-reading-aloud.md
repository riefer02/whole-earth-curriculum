# Lesson 7 — Reading Aloud

## Summary

Learners read a simple text aloud in their home writing system, practicing a clear
voice, careful tracking, and reading for a real listener. Reading aloud turns a
silent text into something shared.

## Objectives

- Read a simple text aloud and retell its beginning, middle, and end.
  _(D04.S2.01.02)_

## Connection

When you read silently, the story lives only inside your head. When you read
aloud, it reaches everyone in the room — like passing a bowl of food around a
table so all can taste. Reading aloud with a clear, lively voice is how stories
have been shared around fires, tables, and classrooms for thousands of years.

## Materials

- A very simple text in the home writing system
- A "reading voice" pointer (optional)

## Preparation

- Choose a text of a few short sentences the learners can mostly decode.
- Write it large enough for shared reading, or prepare copies.
- Plan to model reading aloud once.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Model reading aloud with a clear voice and expression, then
guide practice. Honor every voice and accent — there is no single "correct" way to
read aloud, only clarity and care. Choose texts in the learners' own writing
system, and let oral traditions be honored alongside written ones. A learner who
does not use a speaking voice "reads aloud" by signing each word, pointing to each
word as a partner or a device voices it, or communicating with an AAC device —
that is reading aloud too, honored just as fully.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Listen as the text is read aloud once. Notice the voice is
   clear and not too fast — you can follow every word.
2. **Warm up your voice (or your hands) (5 min).** Say a silly phrase together,
   first very quietly, then in a big clear voice — or sign it small, then big. Find
   the "reading voice" others can follow.
3. **Read together (10 min).** Read the text aloud together in one voice, pointing
   under each word as you go. Slow down at the end of each sentence.
4. **Read in turns (15 min).** With a partner, take turns reading one sentence
   each. Listen for a clear voice. If a word is tricky, sound it out like you
   practiced, then keep going.
5. **Read for a listener (5 min).** Read the whole text aloud once more for your
   partner or group, as if you are telling them the story.
6. **Close (5 min).** A clear voice carries words from the page to the people.
   Reading aloud is sharing.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Echo-read one sentence at a time — the learner repeats after a
  model — before reading independently.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Read by signing each word, pointing to each word
  while a partner or device voices it, or communicating with an AAC device —
  tracking words still counts.
- **Extension:** Read with expression, changing your voice for different parts, and
  try a slightly longer text.

## Assessment

- **Formative (performance):** Can the learner read the simple text aloud —
  spoken, signed, or communicated — clearly enough for a listener to follow,
  tracking words and sounding out tricky ones?

## Home connection

Read one short page or sentence aloud to someone at home, using your clearest
reading voice.
