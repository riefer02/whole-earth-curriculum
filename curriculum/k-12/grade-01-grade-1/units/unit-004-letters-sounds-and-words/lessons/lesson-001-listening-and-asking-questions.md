# Lesson 1 — Listening and Asking Questions

## Summary

Learners practice listening with their whole body, then ask one simple question
about a story or speaker using the starter words who, what, where, when, and why.
They close by wondering together about what they still want to know.

## Objectives

- Listen to a speaker or story and ask a simple question about it.
  _(D04.S1.01.01)_

## Connection

Every day someone tells you something — a story at bedtime, a direction in the
kitchen, a friend's news on the walk to school. When you listen with your whole
body and ask one question, you understand more and the speaker feels heard.
Asking a question is how everyone, everywhere, learns new things.

## Materials

- A short story or a willing speaker (about one minute)
- Question starter cards (who, what, where, when, why)
- A wonder box (optional)

## Preparation

- Choose or tell a short story — about one minute, with one clear happening.
- Prepare the five question starter cards.
- Arrange a circle so everyone can see and hear the speaker.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Prepare the story, cards, and circle, then guide the
question practice — the learner follows the procedure directly. No question is
"silly"; every honest question is welcome, and the wonder box lets quieter
learners ask without speaking up. There is no one "correct" accent or way of
speaking; every way of telling is honored. For a learner who is Deaf or hard of
hearing, tell the story with pictures, a written summary, or a fluent signer —
watching faces and hands *is* listening, and a question can be signed, pointed
to, or drawn. A learner who does not use a speaking voice asks by signing,
pointing to a starter card, showing a picture, or using a communication device —
all of these are asking.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Sit where you can see the speaker. Notice what "listening"
   feels like in your body: your full attention on the speaker — with your eyes,
   your ears, or both. If you listen by watching faces and hands in sign, that is
   listening too; if a picture or written story helps you follow, that is listening
   too.
2. **Meet the question starters (5 min).** Look at the five starter words: **who**,
   **what**, **where**, **when**, **why**. Say each one. Each word begins a
   different kind of question.
3. **Listen to a story (5 min).** Listen once all the way through without talking.
   Listen with your whole body.
4. **Listen again, then ask (15 min).** Hear the story a second time. Now think of
   one question it makes you wonder. Start it with a starter word: "Who…?"
   "What…?" "Where…?" "When…?" "Why…?" Take turns asking your question aloud.
5. **Wonder together (10 min).** Pick one question the group wants to explore.
   Wonder about it together — you do not need the answer today. If you have a
   wonder box, drop in a question you are still carrying.
6. **Close (5 min).** Remember: a good listener gives their full attention — with
   eyes, ears, or hands — and a good learner asks questions.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer just two starters (who, what); model one question and have the
  learner echo it.
- **Support (deaf/Hard of Hearing):** Use a visual or signed story; a question can
  be signed, pointed to, or drawn.
- **Support (non-speaking):** A question can be signed, pointed to on a card, shown
  with a picture, or spoken through a device — asking in any form counts.
- **Extension:** Ask a second question that begins with "why," and wonder what kind
  of answer it might have.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner ask one question about the story
  that uses a starter word and fits what they heard?

## Home connection

Ask someone at home to tell you one small thing about their day, then ask them one
"who," "what," or "why" question about it.
