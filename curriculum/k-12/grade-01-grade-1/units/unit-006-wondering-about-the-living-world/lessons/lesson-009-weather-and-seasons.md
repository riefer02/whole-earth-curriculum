# Lesson 9 — Weather and Seasons

## Summary

Learners observe and describe the **weather** where they live and discover that the
world has many kinds of weather and many kinds of **seasons**. Some places have wet
and dry times, some are warm all year, some are cold — and there is no "correct"
season, only the one that is home.

## Objectives

- Observe and describe weather and seasons where they live and in other places.
  _(D06.S4.01.02)_

## Connection

Step outside and the world tells you its weather: sun on your face, rain on your skin,
wind in your hair. The weather where you live is *your* weather — and it is not the
same everywhere. While it rains here, the sun may blaze in a faraway desert, or snow
may fall on a distant mountain. Weather and seasons are different in every place, and
every place's weather is a real and good one.

## Materials

- Weather picture cards (sun, rain, wind, snow, storm clouds, dry ground, green
  leaves, fallen leaves)
- A weather chart
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Prepare weather picture cards and a simple weather chart (today / tomorrow).
- Gather one or two images or stories of weather in very different places (a snowy
  place, a desert, a rainforest, a coast).

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). **No four-season default:** do not
assume spring–summer–autumn–winter. Many places have two seasons (wet and dry), some
are warm all year near the equator, some are cold most of the year near the poles,
and the monsoon shapes life across South and Southeast Asia. Say plainly: there is no
correct season, only the one that is home — and all of them are real. Name seasons in
the learners' local language and calendar. This is a good place to be *global*: a
child who has never seen snow can still wonder at it, and a child who has never seen
a dry season can wonder at that. Honor every body: describe weather with skin and
ears (warmth, wind sound) for a learner who cannot see; act it out for a learner who
does not speak. Avoid frightening content: name storms matter-of-factly and keep the
tone of wonder, never fear.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** What is the weather like right now? Go to the window or door,
   or remember. Use your senses: what do you see, feel, and hear?
2. **Describe today (10 min).** Use the weather cards to name what you observed — sun,
   rain, wind, heat, cold, clouds. Record today's weather on the chart. Then predict:
   what might tomorrow be like? (A **guess** you can check by watching.)
3. **Seasons here and elsewhere (15 min).** What is your weather like at this time of
   year? Some places have wet times and dry times; some are warm all year; some are
   cold for much of the year. Look at pictures of two very different places and
   wonder: what would the air feel like there? What would you wear, what would grow
   there? There is no correct season — only the one that is home.
4. **Act out the weather (10 min).** Be the weather: rain (fingers tapping), wind
   (swaying side to side), sun (arms wide and warm), snow (soft and still). Move from
   one kind of weather to another.
5. **Close (5 min).** Weather changes day to day, and seasons change through the year
   — here and everywhere. All of them are the world's weather, and all are worth
   noticing.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Describe just today's weather with two words (hot/cold, wet/dry) and act
  it out.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Describe weather by feel and sound — warmth, cool
  air, wind, rain tapping.
- **Support (Deaf/HH):** Name the weather by what you see and feel — sun, clouds, wind
  on the skin — and act it out with your body.
- **Extension:** Draw two places with very different weather and label one difference,
  or keep a weather chart for a full week.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner describe today's weather with a sense
  word and name one way weather or seasons differ in another place?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The weather chart entry or a "two places' weather" drawing.

## Home connection

At home, watch the weather tomorrow morning and record it — sunny, rainy, windy, hot,
cold. Ask a grown-up what the weather is like right now in another place your family
knows.
