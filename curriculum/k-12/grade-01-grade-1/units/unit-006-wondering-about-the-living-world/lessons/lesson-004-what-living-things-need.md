# Lesson 4 — What Living Things Need

## Summary

Learners discover the four things every living thing needs to stay alive and healthy:
**food**, **water**, **air**, and **shelter**. They match each need to a plant, an
animal, and a person, and learn that caring for living things means making sure they
have what they need.

## Objectives

- Observe and describe what living things need to stay alive (food, water, air, and
  shelter). _(D06.S2.01.01)_

## Connection

You get thirsty and you drink water. You get hungry and you eat. You breathe air all
day without thinking about it, and you sleep somewhere warm and safe. Every living
thing — a plant on a windowsill, a fish in a river, a camel in the desert, a person
anywhere on Earth — needs the same four things to stay alive: **food, water, air, and
shelter**.

## Materials

- Picture cards of living things and their needs (a plant, a puppy/dog, a chick/hen, a
  fish, and a person — each with food, water, air, and shelter)
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Prepare cards: a few living things, and cards showing food, water, air, and shelter
  for each.
- Plan a simple way to act out each need (drinking, breathing, finding shelter).

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Keep it gentle and concrete. Use the
word "alive and healthy," and let learners lead with their own lives. Be sensitive: a
learner may live somewhere food, water, or a safe home is not always certain. Hold
this without shame — say that everyone needs these four things, and that people
everywhere work hard so that all living things can have them; that is why caring for
each other and the Earth matters. Different living things meet the four needs
differently (a fish "breathes" water's air; a cactus finds water in dry places) — let
that be wonder, not a quiz. Honor every body: act, point, or draw rather than speak
if that is easier.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of a plant or an animal you know — a pet, a bird you
   have seen, a fish, a farm animal, or a wild creature near your home. What does it
   need every day so it can stay alive and healthy?
2. **The four needs (10 min).** There are four things every living thing needs:
   **food** to grow, **water** to drink, **air** to breathe, and **shelter** — a safe
   place to rest and be protected. Take a big breath in. Feel the air. That is a need,
   right now.
3. **Match needs to living things (15 min).** For each living thing — the plant, the
   puppy, the chick, the fish, the bird, and you — point to its food, its water, its
   air, and its shelter. A plant's food comes from light and soil; a fish's shelter is the
   water itself; a bird's shelter is a nest. Act out one need for one living thing.
4. **Draw one living thing and its needs (10 min).** Draw one living thing and the
   four things it needs around it.
5. **Close (5 min).** Food, water, air, and shelter — every living thing, everywhere.
   When we care for living things, we make sure they have them.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Focus on one living thing (the plant or the puppy) and match its four
  needs one at a time.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Point to the need cards and to the living thing to show
  the match.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Use real objects and touch or spoken description
  instead of picture cards — feel a leaf (a plant), water, air on the skin, and a soft
  "shelter" cloth, and match each need by touch and voice.
- **Extension:** Compare two living things and name how their shelter (or food) is
  different.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name (or point to) the four needs and
  match at least two of them to a living thing?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The "living thing and its needs" drawing.

## Home connection

At home, help care for one living thing — a plant, a pet, or even a seed — by giving
it one thing it needs (water, food, or a safe spot). Tell a grown-up which need you
gave it.
