# Lesson 10 — The Wonder Fair

## Summary

A capstone celebration: learners gather everything they observed, asked, guessed, and
found across the unit, share it with one another, and name the wonders they will carry
forward. This is a chance to remember, to teach each other, and to celebrate how much
they have grown as scientists — together, with no ranking.

## Objectives

- Review and show learning across all four strands of the unit. _(D06.S1.01.01,
  D06.S1.01.02, D06.S2.01.01, D06.S2.01.02, D06.S3.01.01, D06.S3.01.02,
  D06.S4.01.01, D06.S4.01.02)_

## Connection

Think back over these weeks: you met your five senses, looked closely, asked
questions and made guesses, found out what living things need, sorted living from
nonliving, felt the properties of materials, pushed and pulled things, watched the
sky, and noticed the weather. You have become a scientist — someone who wonders,
observes, and finds out. Today you celebrate that, together.

## Materials

- Learners' own work from the unit (sense drawings, wonder pages, needs drawings,
  sorts, property and movement drawings, day/night and weather pages)
- A display space (table, mat, or wall)
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Help learners gather their work from the unit.
- Set up a display space and decide how sharing will flow (gallery walk, circle share,
  or wonder walk).

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). This is a **celebration of mastery, not
a competition** — there are no winners, no rankings, no "best" work. Every learner
shares; every share is welcomed exactly as it is. Keep praise about effort and
wonder, not comparison ("you looked so closely," not "yours is the best"). If a
learner has gaps or "not yet" moments, this is not the time to surface them — the
fair is about what they *did* learn and what they *still wonder*. Retrieval is built
in: recalling and explaining to a peer is the deepest kind of remembering (spaced,
interleaved across the whole unit). Honor every body: sharing may be by speaking,
pointing, signing, acting, or simply displaying work; a learner who prefers not to
speak can point to their work while a peer or adult reads a label. End by gathering
the unit's questions — "what do you still wonder?" — and honor that some questions we
cannot answer yet, and that is wonderful.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** You are a scientist now. What is one thing you remember from
   this unit — something you saw, asked, or found out?
2. **Set up your share (10 min).** Gather your work from the unit and choose the piece
   you are most proud of, or the one that holds your favorite wonder. Put it in the
   display space.
3. **The Wonder Fair (20 min).** Visit each other's work. When it is your turn to
   share, tell (or point to or show) two things: one thing you *observed or found
   out*, and one thing you *still wonder about*. Listen to your friends with kind
   ears. Ask each other questions.
4. **One more wonder (7 min).** On fresh paper, draw or write your biggest question to
   carry into the rest of the year: "I still wonder…" Add it to the wonder wall or
   wonder book if you have one.
5. **Close (3 min).** Look at all we did together. You asked, you looked, you guessed,
   you checked. Science is wondering — and you are good at it. Keep wondering.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Choose one piece and share one sentence ("I saw…" or "I wonder…") with a
  partner first.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Display work and point to it; a peer or adult reads the
  label aloud.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Share by telling and listening; a peer or adult
  describes each displayed piece aloud as you visit the fair.
- **Extension:** Write or draw three "I wonder" questions and star the one you most
  want to explore next.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name one thing they learned and one
  thing they still wonder, across the unit?
- **Self / peer:** Learners tell one another one thing they liked about each other's
  sharing.
- **Portfolio:** The gathered unit work plus the final "I still wonder…" page, as a
  record of growth.

## Home connection

Bring your "I still wonder…" question home and ask a grown-up what *they* wonder
about. Wonder together — and keep watching the world.
