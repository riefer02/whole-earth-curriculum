# Lesson 5 — Living and Nonliving

## Summary

Learners sort real things into **living** and **nonliving** and discover three clues
that something is alive: living things **grow**, **move**, and **reproduce** (make
more of themselves). They practice gentle care for the living things they meet.

## Objectives

- Sort things into living and nonliving, and tell what living things do (grow, move,
  and reproduce). _(D06.S2.01.02)_

## Connection

A rock and a seed sit side by side. They might look alike when they are small and
still. But the seed can grow into a plant, and the rock mostly stays the same. The
seed is **living**; the rock is **nonliving**. Knowing the difference helps us care
for the living world around us.

## Materials

- A mixed collection to sort (a seed or plant, a picture of an animal, a rock, a
  pencil, a toy, water, a leaf)
- Two sorting mats or hoops (one "living," one "nonliving")
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Gather a mixed pile of living and nonliving things learners can touch safely.
- Label two mats or hoops "living" and "nonliving."

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The three clues — **grow, move,
reproduce** — are a first map, not a strict rule; a seed does not look like it moves,
and a cloud "moves" but is not living. Hold the clues loosely and welcome wonder:
"living things do these three things — what about this one?" Do not frighten: there
is no death or harm content here. Model **gentle care**: if a learner finds a live
creature (an insect, a worm), watch without touching, and return it gently where it
came from; never hurt a living thing. "Reproduce" is best said to this age as "make
more of themselves" or "have babies" — with the seed–plant and chick–hen pairs kept
gentle. Honor every body: sort by pointing, touching, or acting; speaking is one way,
not the only way. Many cultures name and care for living things differently — honor
local names and ways.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Look at a rock and a seed. Which one will grow? Which will stay
   the same? One is living, one is not.
2. **Three clues (10 min).** Living things do three things. They **grow** (a puppy
   becomes a dog). They **move** (a bird flies, a plant turns toward the light). They
   **reproduce** — they make more of themselves (a seed makes more seeds; a hen lays
   an egg that can become a chick). Act out growing: curl small, then stretch tall.
3. **Sort the pile (15 min).** Sort each thing into "living" or "nonliving." For each
   one ask: does it grow? does it move on its own? does it make more of itself? A
   leaf is living (it grew on a plant); a pencil is not. Water is a trick question —
   it is not alive, but living things need it. There are no silly answers; explain
   your thinking.
4. **Draw the sort (10 min).** Draw one living thing and one nonliving thing and the
   clue that told you apart (a seed growing vs a rock staying the same).
5. **Close (5 min).** Living things grow, move, and make more of themselves. The rest
   of the world is nonliving — and living things need it. Both matter.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Sort with just one clue ("does it grow?") and a small pile.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Place each object on the mat by pointing or moving it.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Sort by touch and spoken description — feel a leaf
  beside a rock and tell (or act out) which clue told them apart.
- **Extension:** Find three living and three nonliving things in the room or outside,
  and tell the rule for each.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner sort several things into living and
  nonliving and give at least one living clue (grow, move, or reproduce)?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The living/nonliving drawing.

## Home connection

At home, walk through one room or the yard and point to two living things and two
nonliving things. Tell a grown-up how you knew which was which.
