# Lesson 10 — Our People, Places & Long Ago Story

## Summary

The capstone: learners gather the whole unit into one "my story" page — a day in
order, a then-and-now pair, a tradition, a home place, and a kind act. It becomes
the unit's portfolio artifact and a first telling of their own place in the long
human story.

## Objectives

- Sequence three or four events from their own day or week in order, using words
  like before, after, and next. _(D08.S1.01.01)_
- Compare one thing from long ago with one thing today, such as a tool or a toy, and
  tell what changed and what stayed the same. _(D08.S1.01.02)_
- Describe a family or community tradition and what it means to the people who
  practice it. _(D08.S2.01.01)_
- Compare how two families or communities celebrate or mark an event, such as a
  birthday, harvest, or new year, and notice what is different and what is
  alike. _(D08.S2.01.02)_
- Describe the place where they live, including land, water, weather, and living
  things, and one way it shapes how people live there. _(D08.S3.01.01)_
- Use a simple map or globe to find where they live and one other place, and tell one
  way the two places are different. _(D08.S3.01.02)_
- Tell when a character in a story is treated unfairly and describe one way someone
  could help or stand up for them. _(D08.S4.01.01)_
- Describe a kind act where someone helped another person or animal and why it
  mattered. _(D08.S4.01.02)_

## Connection

All this time you have been gathering pieces of a bigger picture: how time unfolds,
how things change and stay the same, how people keep traditions and celebrate, what
your place is like, and how noticing unfairness and doing kindness can help. Today
you will put the pieces together into one story — *your* story of people, places,
and long ago. Every learner's story is different, and every one belongs.

## Materials

- A "my story" page divided into sections (my day, then-and-now, a tradition, my
  place, a kind act)
- Crayons, pencils, paste

## Preparation

- Prepare the "my story" page template, or plan to divide a sheet of paper into five
  labeled sections.
- Gather learners' earlier work from the unit (day cards, then-and-now drawings,
  tradition drawings, place charts, kind-act drawings) to revisit.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. **This is the unit's portfolio artifact and summative
moment — but it is a celebration, not a test.** Retrieval practice across the whole
unit is the engine here: learners are recalling and *using* everything they learned,
spaced across the unit (this is the science-of-learning payoff — Dunlosky et al.,
2013). The page has five
sections, one per idea; a learner may complete all five, or tell some sections
orally, by acting, or with a scribe. Honor every body and every family shape: no one
must disclose their own tradition, a moving story, or a private unfairness — the
sections can be filled with *any* tradition, place, or kind act they have met this
unit. "No correct family," "no correct place," "no child is ever the problem." Gather
the pages into a class mural so the whole becomes a shared, many-voiced story.

## Procedure

1. **Gather and remember (10 min).** You have learned five things. Say them together:
   - My day has an **order** — before, after, next.
   - Things **change and stay the same** from long ago to today.
   - Families and communities keep **traditions** that mean something to them.
   - My **place** has land, water, weather, and living things, and it shapes how we
     live.
   - I can **notice unfairness** and do a **kind act** to help.
2. **Make your story page (25 min).** Fill each section — drawing, or telling an
   older learner who scribes for you:
   - **My day in order:** three or four things you did, in order.
   - **Then and now:** one thing from long ago and one today, with one "same" and one
     "different."
   - **A tradition:** a tradition you have met this unit, and what it means.
   - **My place:** land, water, weather, and living things, and one way it shapes
     life here.
   - **A kind act:** a kind act you have met, and why it mattered.
3. **Share your story (15 min).** Tell your story to a partner or the group — point
   to each section as you go. You may speak, sign, act, or show your page.
4. **Gather the mural (10 min).** Add your page to the class mural of people,
   places, and long ago. Stand back and see how many different stories now live side
   by side — each one true, each one worthy.
5. **Close (5 min).** Your story is part of the long human story — told from many
   perspectives, never just one. You are the author of your own page in it.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Complete two sections with heavy support, and tell the rest orally.
- **Non-speaking:** Build the story with picture cards, objects, or acting, with a
  peer or older learner scribing captions.
- **Blind/low-vision:** Tell the story aloud by section while an older learner or the
  facilitator records it; use textured markers to place each part.
- **Deaf/HH:** Build the story with pictures, objects, and a signed telling — the
  page itself is visual; sign each section as you point to it.
- **Motor difference:** Tell each section aloud or by pointing while a partner or
  older learner scribes or assembles the page; use larger sections and easy-grip
  crayons.
- **Extension:** Add a sixth section — "two celebrations, alike and different" — and
  tell one thing alike and one thing different.

## Assessment

- **Summative (portfolio):** Does the "my story" page (or its told/scribed version)
  show evidence across the four strands — an ordered day, a then-and-now comparison,
  a tradition, a place, and a kind or standing-up act?
- **Performance (oral):** Can the learner tell their story, moving through the
  sections, using the unit's ideas in their own words?

## Home connection

Take your story page home and share it with someone. Ask them: what is one thing
they would add to their own people, places, and long ago story?
