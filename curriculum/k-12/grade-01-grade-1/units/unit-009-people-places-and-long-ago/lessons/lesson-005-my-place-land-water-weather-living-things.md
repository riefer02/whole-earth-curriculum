# Lesson 5 — My Place: Land, Water, Weather, Living Things

## Summary

Learners describe the place where they live — its land, water, weather, and living
things — and name one way that place shapes how people live there. There is no
"correct" place; there is only the place that is home.

## Objectives

- Describe the place where they live, including land, water, weather, and living
  things, and one way it shapes how people live there. _(D08.S3.01.01)_

## Connection

Every home sits in a particular place: flat and wide, or hilly, or by the sea, or in
the mountains. Some places are hot and dry, some cold and snowy, some warm and
rainy. The place you live shapes little everyday things — what people wear, how they
build their homes, what they grow or catch to eat, where their water comes from.
Looking closely at your own place is how geography begins.

## Materials

- A "my place" chart divided into land, water, weather, and living things
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Choose a way to observe the local place: a window, a short step outside, or
  remembered and shared descriptions.
- Prepare the four-part chart for each learner.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. **No travel is needed** — the place learners already live is
the whole subject. **Never rank places:** a desert is not "worse" than a forest, a
city is not "better" than a farm. Say "different places, different ways of living,"
and mean it. Honor every home shape: some learners have moved, some live in two
homes, some live in a new place. No one must tell a moving story; the place they
live *now* is enough, and "home" may be more than one place. For a learner who is
blind or has low vision, use touch and sound — a stone, a leaf, a cup of water, the
feel of wind — and describe the place aloud together. The four parts (land, water,
weather, living things) are a concrete anchor, not a test.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of your home. What is the ground like there — flat,
   hilly, sandy, rocky, grassy?
2. **Observe your place (10 min).** Look out, step outside, or remember. Find one
   thing for each of the four parts:
   - **Land** — is it flat, hilly, or mountainous? What is on it?
   - **Water** — is there a river, a sea, a pond, or rain? Where does water come from?
   - **Weather** — is it hot, cold, rainy, dry, or does it change a lot?
   - **Living things** — what plants and animals live here with you?
3. **Fill your chart (15 min).** Draw or place one thing in each of the four parts.
   You may draw, or touch a real stone, leaf, or cup of water and describe it.
4. **How the place shapes life (10 min).** Ask: because the place is like this, what
   do people do? Do they build homes for heat or for cool? Do they grow certain
   foods or fish? Do they carry water or catch rain? Name or show one way.
5. **Close (5 min).** There is no correct place — only the one that is home. Your
   place has shaped how you and your neighbors live, just as every place shapes its
   people.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Fill two of the four parts together, and offer one "how it shapes
  life" idea to choose from.
- **Blind/low-vision:** Build the place's story from touch and sound — hold a stone,
  feel the wind, hear the birds — and describe each part aloud.
- **Non-speaking:** Point to pictures, place found objects in the four squares, or
  act out the weather, instead of speaking.
- **Deaf/HH:** Observe the place with your eyes — colors, shapes, moving clouds and
  leaves — and name or sign each of the four parts from what you see.
- **Motor difference:** Use larger found objects that are easy to hold, or have a
  partner place each object in its square while the learner directs and describes.
- **Extension:** Describe the same place in another season and how life there
  changes with it.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name or show land, water, weather,
  and living things for their place?
- **Formative (oral/performance):** Can the learner name one way the place shapes
  how people live there?

## Home connection

With a grown-up, step outside or look out a window. Name one thing you see for land,
water, weather, and living things. How does your place shape something your family
does — what you wear, grow, or eat?
