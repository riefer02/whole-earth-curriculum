# Lesson 7 — A Character Treated Unfairly

## Summary

Learners hear a gentle, blame-free story in which a character is treated unfairly.
They name the unfair moment and describe one way someone could help or stand up for
the character. The story ends in repair and hope, never shame.

## Objectives

- Tell when a character in a story is treated unfairly and describe one way someone
  could help or stand up for them. _(D08.S4.01.01)_

## Connection

Unfair means someone is treated differently for no good reason — left out of a game,
skipped for a turn, or not listened to. Fair means everyone gets a turn and no one
is left out for no reason. You notice fair and unfair every day, at home and at
play. When you notice someone being treated unfairly, there are kind things anyone
can do to help.

## Materials

- A gentle story about a character treated unfairly (with a kind, repair-filled
  ending)
- "Help cards" showing ways to help (say something kind, invite them in, sit with
  them, tell a grown-up)

## Preparation

- Choose or write a short, gentle story with one clear unfair moment, ending in
  repair without blame. Example: a new child is told "you can't play, there's no
  room" while others play on; a friend notices and invites them in.
- Prepare the "help cards" or plan to act out ways to help.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. **Keep the story gentle and blame-free.** The goal is
noticing and naming unfairness, then turning toward repair — never shaming a
character. Avoid heavy or traumatic unfairness at this age: no frightening or
violent content. **No child is ever the problem** — the unfairness is in the
treatment, not in the child. End with someone helping or standing up, so learners
leave with agency and hope, not fear. Honor every body: a learner who does not use a
speaking voice points to the unfair moment or acts it out; a learner who is blind or
has low vision hears the story told with clear, gentle language and describes what
happened aloud. Never require a learner to share a real unfairness from their own
life.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of a game where everyone gets a turn. How does that
   feel? Now think of a time someone was left out. How might that feel?
2. **Hear the story (15 min).** Listen to a story about a character who is treated
   unfairly — maybe a new child told "there's no room" while everyone else plays.
   Listen for the exact moment it happens.
3. **Name the unfair moment (10 min).** Point to the "unfair" spot. When was the
   character treated unfairly? What happened? How did the character feel?
4. **Find a way to help (10 min).** Look at the help cards. Choose or act out one way
   someone could help: say something kind, invite them in, sit with them, or tell a
   grown-up. Which one do you think would help most?
5. **Hear the repair (5 min).** Finish the story: someone does help, and the
   character is included and cared for. How does that feel now?
6. **Close (5 min).** Noticing unfairness, kindly, is the first step. Standing up and
   helping is the next — and anyone can do it.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Tell only the unfair moment and ask one question: "How did the
  character feel?" Then show the repair.
- **Non-speaking:** A learner points to the unfair moment, acts it out, or orders
  picture cards, instead of speaking.
- **Blind/low-vision:** Tell the story with clear, warm language; the learner
  describes the unfair moment and the help aloud.
- **Deaf/HH:** Tell the story with a picture sequence, in sign, or acted out so it
  is seen; the learner points to the unfair moment and the helping moment on the
  cards.
- **Motor difference:** The learner directs a partner or puppet to act out the
  unfair and helping moments, or points/eye-gazes to the matching picture card.
- **Extension:** Make up a tiny new ending where someone stands up, and act it out.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner point to or say the moment the
  character was treated unfairly?
- **Formative (oral/performance):** Can the learner describe one way someone could
  help or stand up for the character?

## Home connection

With a grown-up, think of a story you both know where someone was treated unfairly.
What happened, and what could someone do to help? (Only share a real-life one if you
want to.)
