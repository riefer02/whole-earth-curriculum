# Lesson 8 — A Kind Act

## Summary

Learners meet a gentle kind act where someone helped a person or animal. They
describe what happened and say why the kindness mattered — for the one helped, and
for the world around them.

## Objectives

- Describe a kind act where someone helped another person or animal and why it
  mattered. _(D08.S4.01.02)_

## Connection

Think of the last time someone was kind to you — they shared, they helped you up,
they sat with you when you were sad. A kind act is small, but it can change a whole
day. Kindness matters not only because it helps the one who receives it, but because
it grows — one kind act often leads to another, and it tells the other person: you
are not alone.

## Materials

- A gentle story or picture sequence of a kind act
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Choose or write a short, gentle kind-act story: someone sharing food, helping a
  friend who fell, or giving water to a thirsty animal.
- Prepare the "because it mattered" holding object if using one.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. **Keep it gentle and concrete.** The kind act should be
something a six-year-old can picture and one they could do themselves — sharing,
helping, comforting, caring for an animal. The key thinking is the *why*: kindness
matters because it meets a need, eases a hurt, or tells someone they are not alone.
Welcome every body: a learner who does not use a speaking voice acts the kindness
out or points to a picture; a learner who is blind or has low vision hears the story
with clear, warm language and tells why it mattered aloud. No one is asked to
disclose a private hurt. End on warmth and agency — learners are the ones who can do
the next kind act.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of one kind thing someone did for you. How did it make
   you feel? (You do not have to share it.)
2. **Hear the kind act (15 min).** Listen to a story of a kind act — a child shares
   their lunch with someone who has none, or gives water to a thirsty dog on a hot
   day, or helps a friend who fell. Listen for what happened, step by step.
3. **Describe it (10 min).** Tell the story back: who helped whom, and what did they
   do? Point to the pictures, act it out, or say it.
4. **Find why it mattered (10 min).** Ask: why did this kindness matter? The one
   helped felt cared for, safe, full, or less alone. The helper felt warm inside.
   And the world felt a little kinder. Name or show one reason.
5. **Draw the why (5 min).** Draw the kind act and mark or caption why it mattered.
6. **Close (5 min).** Kindness is small and mighty. It helps one person or animal
   now, and it grows — because kindness teaches kindness.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Focus on one question — "how did the one helped feel after?" — and
  offer two feeling words to choose from.
- **Non-speaking:** A learner acts the kindness out, points to a picture, or orders a
  picture sequence, instead of speaking.
- **Blind/low-vision:** Describe the story's sounds and warmth richly; the learner
  tells why it mattered aloud.
- **Deaf/HH:** Tell the kind act with a picture sequence or in sign so it is seen;
  the learner shows what happened and why it mattered by pointing or signing.
- **Motor difference:** The learner tells the "why" while a partner scribes or
  draws; use picture cards that can be pointed to rather than drawn.
- **Extension:** Tell one more kind act you have seen or done, and why it mattered.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner describe a kind act — who helped whom
  and what they did?
- **Formative (oral/performance):** Can the learner say or show why the kindness
  mattered?

## Home connection

With a grown-up, think of a kind act you saw this week — for a person or an animal.
What happened, and why did it matter?
