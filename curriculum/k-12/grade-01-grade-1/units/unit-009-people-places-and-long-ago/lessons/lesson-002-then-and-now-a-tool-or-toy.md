# Lesson 2 — Then and Now: A Tool or Toy

## Summary

Learners compare one thing from long ago with one thing today — a tool or a toy —
and say what changed and what stayed the same. Neither is called better; both are
real ways people live, and many are still used today.

## Objectives

- Compare one thing from long ago with one thing today, such as a tool or a toy, and
  tell what changed and what stayed the same. _(D08.S1.01.02)_

## Connection

Long ago, people lit the dark with a candle or an oil lamp. Today many homes light
the dark with an electric bulb — and many homes still use a candle or a lamp too.
Both give light; the flame and the bulb just do it differently. A tool or toy from
long ago is not "worse" and today's is not "better" — they are different ways humans
have solved the same problem.

## Materials

- Then-and-now object or picture pairs (candle/lamp, clay pot/jug, hand-carved
  top/store toy)
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Gather or draw at least one then-and-now pair for the worked example.
- Set up two labeled spots — "long ago" and "today" — plus a "both" spot where they
  overlap.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. **Comparing is a foundational skill, so model it once before
independent work** (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006): hold up a candle and a lamp,
and think aloud — "Both give light. That is the same. The candle burns with a flame;
the lamp uses a bulb. That changed." Then guide the same two moves for a second pair.
**Never rank:** do not say old things were harder, slower, or worse. A candle-lantern
and a pot carried on the head are equally valid living ways — still today, in many
homes, these are the everyday tools. The point is noticing change and sameness, never
progress. Honor every body: a learner who is blind or has low vision feels the
objects; a learner who does not use a speaking voice points to "same" and "different"
spots.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of one thing you use every day — a light, a cup, a toy.
   Did people long ago use something for the very same job?
2. **Meet "long ago" and "today" (10 min).** Look at the pair: a candle and a lamp.
   Both help you see in the dark. The candle uses a flame; the lamp uses a bulb.
   Say it together: "The same: both give light. Different: flame and bulb."
3. **Guided practice (10 min).** Take a second pair — a clay pot and a jug, or a
   carved top and a store toy. Together, put the older one on the "long ago" spot,
   the newer one on the "today" spot, and the thing they both do on the "both" spot.
4. **Compare your own pair (15 min).** Choose one pair and hold or look at both.
   Find one thing that **stayed the same** (they both hold water; they both roll;
   they both give light). Find one thing that **changed** (what it is made of, or how
   it works). Draw both and mark your two findings.
5. **Share (5 min).** Tell a partner: "It is the same because... It is different
   because..." Point, sign, or draw if you like.
6. **Close (5 min).** Many people today still use the older tool, and many use the
   newer one. Both are real, both work, and both are part of how humans have lived.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use just one pair; ask only two questions — "what is the same?" and
  "what is different?" — with clear gestures toward the "both" spot.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Compare by touch — a smooth bowl and a ribbed
  bottle — and describe what feels the same and different aloud.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Point to the "same" spot, the "different" spot, and
  the "both" spot to show each finding, or draw the pair and mark the two findings.
- **Support (Deaf/HH):** The objects and picture pairs are already visual — compare
  by looking and pointing, and sign "same" and "different" as you place each item on
  its spot.
- **Support (motor-difference):** Choose objects that are easy to hold or use larger
  cards; a partner moves each object to its spot while the learner directs with a
  point, a word, or eye-gaze.
- **Extension:** Choose a second pair and find two things that changed and two that
  stayed the same.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Does the learner name at least one thing that changed
  and one thing that stayed the same for a pair?
- **Formative (oral/performance):** Does the learner say or show "same" and
  "different" without ranking one as better?

## Home connection

Ask someone at home about a tool or toy they used when they were little. What was it
for? What is the same about what you use today, and what is different?
