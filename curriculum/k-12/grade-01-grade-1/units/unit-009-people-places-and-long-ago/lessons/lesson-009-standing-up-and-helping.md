# Lesson 9 — Standing Up and Helping

## Summary

Learners bring the two strands together: noticing unfairness and doing a kind act.
They follow a gentle story where a character is treated unfairly and someone both
stands up and helps, and they retell the two-step arc — notice, then help.

## Objectives

- Tell when a character in a story is treated unfairly and describe one way someone
  could help or stand up for them. _(D08.S4.01.01)_
- Describe a kind act where someone helped another person or animal and why it
  mattered. _(D08.S4.01.02)_

## Connection

Standing up and helping are two parts of one brave act. First you notice that
someone is being treated unfairly — left out, skipped, not listened to. Then you do
something kind to help — you say it's not fair, you invite them in, you sit with
them. You do not need to be big or strong to do either; noticing and being kind are
things every person, even a child, can do.

## Materials

- A gentle story that pairs unfairness with a kind act (ending in repair)
- "Notice → Help" cards showing the two steps

## Preparation

- Choose or write a short, gentle story with one clear unfair moment followed by a
  clear standing-up-and-helping moment, ending in repair and warmth.
- Prepare the "Notice → Help" cards or plan to act out the two steps.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. **This is the synthesis lesson: the two steps are the whole
point.** Keep the story gentle and blame-free; the unfairness is mild and repairable
(a child left out of a game, a smaller child skipped for a turn). The helper does two
things — *notices* ("that's not fair") and *helps* (invites them in, sits with them,
gets a grown-up). No child is ever the problem, and no learner must disclose a real
experience. End in repair and warmth so learners leave with agency. Honor every
body: non-speaking learners act the two steps or order picture cards; blind or
low-vision learners hear the story told clearly and describe the two steps aloud.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Last two days you met two ideas: *unfairness* and *a kind
   act*. Today you will see how they fit together.
2. **Hear the story (15 min).** Listen to a story where a character is treated
   unfairly — a smaller child is skipped for a turn, or a new child is told there is
   no room. Then someone does something about it. Listen for two moments.
3. **Step one — notice (10 min).** First, point to the moment the character was
   treated unfairly. What did the helper notice? ("That's not fair. They were left
   out.")
4. **Step two — help (10 min).** Next, what kind act did the helper do? Did they say
   something kind, invite the person in, sit with them, or tell a grown-up? Point to
   the "help" card or act it out.
5. **Retell the arc (5 min).** Tell the two steps in order: "First someone noticed
   it was unfair. Then someone helped." Say it, act it, or show it with the cards.
6. **Close (5 min).** Noticing and helping are two parts of one brave act. You can do
   both — and that is how a child makes the world fairer and kinder.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Name the two steps together and ask only "what did the helper notice?"
  and "what did the helper do?"
- **Non-speaking:** A learner orders the "Notice → Help" cards or acts out the two
  steps, instead of speaking.
- **Blind/low-vision:** Tell the story with clear, warm language; the learner
  describes the notice step and the help step aloud.
- **Deaf/HH:** Tell the story with a picture sequence or in sign so the two moments
  are seen; the learner orders the "Notice → Help" cards and signs each step.
- **Motor difference:** The learner directs a partner or puppet to act the two
  steps, or orders large, easy-to-grip cards by pointing or eye-gaze.
- **Extension:** Invent a new situation — a classmate left out at snack — and plan
  both steps for it.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner identify both the unfair moment and
  the helping moment in the story?
- **Formative (oral/performance):** Can the learner retell the two-step arc — notice
  unfairness, then help — in order?

## Home connection

With a grown-up, make up a tiny story together where someone is treated unfairly and
someone else notices and helps. What are the two steps?
