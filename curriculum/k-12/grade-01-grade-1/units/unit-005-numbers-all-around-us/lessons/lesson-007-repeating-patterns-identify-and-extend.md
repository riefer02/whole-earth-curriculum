# Lesson 7 — Repeating Patterns: Identify and Extend

## Summary

Learners move beyond Kindergarten's "copy the pattern" to *finding* the repeating
part — the core — of patterns made of objects, sounds, and colors, and continuing
the pattern on their own. They learn that every repeating pattern has a part that
comes again and again.

## Objectives

- Identify, extend, and create repeating patterns using objects, sounds, or colors,
  and tell the rule of the pattern. _(D05.S2.01.01)_

## Connection

Patterns are everywhere people live and make things. A weaver passes the shuttle
over and under, over and under; a beadworker threads red, blue, red, blue; a
drummer taps boom-boom-clap, boom-boom-clap; a tiler lays a mosaic of light stone,
dark stone, light stone, dark stone. From one end of the Earth to the other, people
find the same secret: something repeats. When you can spot the repeating part, you
can keep the pattern going as far as you like.

## Materials

- Collections of objects in two or three kinds (seeds, shells, buttons, leaves)
- Paper or slate and a drawing tool

## Preparation

- Prepare collections with two or three clearly different kinds or colors.
- Build one starter pattern to model finding the core.
- Clear space for body-and-voice pattern games.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It spirals up from Kindergarten's
"copy a pattern" to **identifying the core and extending it** — the new skill. Teach
it with a **worked example** (philosophy §13): say the core aloud, mark where it
ends, and show it repeating. Patterns can live in objects, sounds, colors, or
movement — use all of them so every body can join. There is no "wrong" place to find
a pattern; if a learner's core is different from yours but truly repeats, honor it.
For learners who are Deaf or hard of hearing, use visible patterns (objects, colors,
movement) and sign or clap with a partner; for learners who are blind or low-vision,
use sound and touch patterns — beats, and rows of textured objects. A learner who
does not speak continues a pattern by pointing to or placing the next object.
Pattern-making is a joy, not a race.

## Procedure

1. **Gather — recall shapes of things (5 min).** Last year you copied patterns —
  red, blue, red, blue. Today we learn the secret inside a pattern: the part that
  repeats, called the **core**.
2. **Worked example — find the core (10 min).** Watch and help: here is a pattern —
  stone, leaf, stone, leaf, stone, leaf. What comes again and again? It is
  "stone, leaf." That is the **core**. Say it: "stone, leaf — stone, leaf." The core
  repeats over and over.
3. **Extend the pattern (10 min).** Keep the pattern going. After "stone, leaf,
  stone, leaf," what comes next? A stone! Then a leaf. Say the core as you place
  each thing, and the pattern will carry you forward.
4. **Patterns in sound and body (10 min).** Clap a pattern: clap-clap-pat,
  clap-clap-pat. What is the core? "clap-clap-pat." Continue it together. Now make a
  pattern with your body — step-step-hop, step-step-hop — and keep it going.
5. **Your turn — build and extend (7 min).** Build your own repeating pattern with
  your objects. Find its core, say it, and extend it until it is long. A friend can
  check by finding the core in your pattern.
6. **Close (3 min).** Every repeating pattern has a core — a part that comes again.
  Finding the core is how you see the pattern, and continuing it is how you make the
  pattern your own.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use a two-part core (AB) only; say the core together and place the
  next item hand-over-hand if needed.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Use sound and touch patterns — two claps and one
  pat, or a row of two round pebbles and one flat leaf — felt and heard.
- **Support (motor / seated):** Make patterns that need no large movement — tap a
  table or your lap, blink, or direct a partner to place objects or step for you;
  the pattern is still the learner's own, and "step-step-hop" becomes a tap-tap-pause.
- **Extension:** Build a three-part core (ABC) or a pattern with two attributes
  (big red, small blue), and find the core of a friend's long pattern.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner find the repeating core of a pattern
  and extend it correctly with objects, sounds, or colors?
- **Formative (oral):** Can the learner say the core of a pattern aloud or in sign?

## Home connection

At home, find a repeating pattern on cloth, a wall, or a song, and tell a grown-up
what the repeating part is — then continue it together.
