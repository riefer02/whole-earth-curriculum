# Lesson 6 — What Add and Subtract Mean

## Summary

Learners practice telling a **joining** story from a **taking-away** story, act each
one out with real objects, and explain in their own words what adding and
subtracting mean. They record each story as a number sentence. The focus is on the
*meaning* of the operation, not just finding answers.

## Objectives

- Add and subtract within 20 using objects, drawings, or counting strategies, and
  explain what the operation means. _(D05.S1.01.02)_

## Connection

Every day is full of joining and taking-away stories. In the morning you put on
socks — you *join* each sock to your feet. At a meal you share food out — some
*takes away* from the pot and lands on plates. A flock of birds lands in a tree
(joining), then some fly off (taking away). When you can tell these two stories
apart, you understand the very heart of numbers: things grow by joining and shrink
by taking away.

## Materials

- Small objects for acting out stories (up to 20)
- Paper or slate and a drawing tool

## Preparation

- Prepare a few simple joining and taking-away stories about real life.
- Give each learner or pair objects and a drawing surface.
- Plan to model deciding "join or take away?" aloud before learners try.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It is a *meaning* lesson: the goal is
that a learner can hear a story and say whether it is joining (add) or taking away
(subtract), then act it out and explain it in their own words. Use **worked
examples** and guided practice (philosophy §13). Model your own thinking aloud: "The
birds came *to* the tree — that is joining, so I add." Draw the story before writing
the sentence, so the picture carries the meaning. Keep numbers within 20. The
learner's own words are the measure of understanding, and there is no single
"correct" way to explain — every clear explanation is honored. For learners who are
Deaf or hard of hearing, tell the story in sign or pictures and let the learner act
it out with objects; for learners who are blind or low-vision, tell the story aloud
and act it out with real objects by touch. A learner who does not speak explains by
pointing, signing, drawing, or moving objects — all of these are explaining. Never
rank explanations; each learner's words are their own.

## Procedure

1. **Gather — recall the two moves (5 min).** You have met two math moves: joining
   (adding) and taking away (subtracting). Today we practice telling them apart and
   saying what each one means.
2. **Worked example — hear and decide (10 min).** Listen to a story: "Three ducks
   swam in the pond. Two more ducks landed." Is this joining or taking away? It is
   **joining** — the ducks came *together*. Act it out with objects: 3 and 2 make 5.
   Write 3 + 2 = 5. Now listen: "Five ducks swam in the pond. Three flew away." This
   is **taking away** — some *left*. Act it out: 5 − 3 = 2.
3. **Listen and act (12 min).** Hear more stories, one at a time. For each, first
   say "join" or "take away," then act it out with your objects, then write the
   number sentence. Does the story match your sentence?
4. **Explain it in your own words (10 min).** Tell a partner (or draw): what does
   *adding* mean? What does *subtracting* mean? Use your own words — "adding is
   putting together," "subtracting is taking some away" — any clear way is right.
5. **Check together (5 min).** Trade one story with a friend. Act out their story
   and write the sentence. Do you both agree on join or take away? If not, act it
   out again together and talk it through.
6. **Close (3 min).** Numbers grow by joining and shrink by taking away. When you
   can explain that in your own words, you truly understand it — and that matters
   more than being fast.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use only two very clear stories (one join, one take away) and act
  them out with the learner; accept a drawing as the explanation.
- **Support (non-speaking):** The learner explains by moving objects or drawing;
  acting out the story is the explanation.
- **Extension:** Write a joining story *and* a taking-away story that use the same
  numbers (for example, 6 and 3), and show how the stories are opposites.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner hear a story, identify it as joining
  or taking away, act it out, and record a matching number sentence?
- **Formative (oral/drawn):** Can the learner explain in their own words what adding
  and subtracting mean?

## Home connection

At home, tell a grown-up one "joining" story and one "taking-away" story from your
day, and act each one out with small objects together.
