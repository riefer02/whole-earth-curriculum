# Lesson 14 — Telling and Writing Time

## Summary

Learners read a clock to the **hour** and the **half hour** and write the time. They
meet the hour hand and the minute hand, set times on a clock face, and connect clock
time to the rhythm of their own day — waking, eating, and resting — while honoring
that people have kept time in many ways, from the sun to the clock.

## Objectives

- Tell and write time and identify the value of coins, using them in simple real
  situations such as buying a snack. _(D05.S4.01.02)_

## Connection

Every day has a rhythm: you wake, you eat, you play, you sleep. For a long time,
people read that rhythm in the sun — high at noon, low in the evening. Then humans
built clocks to mark time more evenly: water clocks, sand glasses, and the clock
with hands that you will read today. A clock is a tool that helps people meet one
another, catch a bus, and know when to gather. Reading it is reading the day's
rhythm made precise.

## Materials

- A clock face (a real clock, a paper clock with movable hands, or a drawn circle)
- Paper or slate and a writing tool

## Preparation

- Prepare a clock face for each learner or pair, with a long and a short hand.
- Plan to model reading "3 o'clock" and "half past 3."
- Have a few times from the learners' own day ready (wake-up, meal, rest).

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). For grade 1, the standard is reading
time to the **hour** and **half hour** — not minutes yet. Teach with a **worked
example** (philosophy §13): set the clock to 3 o'clock, note the short hand on the 3
and the long hand on the 12; then move the long hand to the 6 for "half past." The
new skill is reading the *position* of the two hands, so always work on a real,
movable clock face. Honor that timekeeping has many traditions — sundials, water
clocks, bells, and calls to prayer — and that the clock is one tool, not the only
truth about time. For learners who are blind or low-vision, use a tactile clock with
raised numbers and hands the fingers can feel; for learners who are Deaf or hard of
hearing, set and read times in sign and with the hands. A learner who does not speak
sets the hands or points to the written time. Reading a clock is a skill of the
hands and eyes together, and everyone can build it at their own pace.

## Procedure

1. **Gather — the day's rhythm (5 min).** Think of your day: when do you wake? When
   do you eat? People have always wanted to mark these times. Today we read the tool
   that does it — the **clock**.
2. **Worked example — meet the hands (10 min).** Watch and help: the clock has two
   hands. The **short hand** tells the **hour**; the **long hand** tells the
   **minutes**. When the long hand points straight up at the 12, it is "o'clock."
   Set the short hand to 3 and the long hand to 12: it is **3 o'clock**. Write it:
   **3:00**.
3. **Half past (10 min).** Now move the long hand down to the 6 — it has gone halfway
   around. That is **half past**. With the short hand between 3 and 4, it is **half
   past 3**. Write it: **3:30**. The 30 means half of the 60 minutes in an hour.
4. **Set and read times (12 min).** With your own clock, set these times: 2 o'clock,
   6 o'clock, half past 8. Read each one aloud (or write it). Then set the times of
   your own day — when you wake, when you eat — and write them.
5. **Check with a partner (5 min).** Set a time and ask a friend to read it. Then
   read the time your friend sets. Do you agree? If not, look at the hands again
   together.
6. **Close (3 min).** A clock turns the day's rhythm into numbers you can read and
   write. Now you can tell the hour and the half hour — the first step to keeping
   time with the whole world.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Read only "o'clock" times today; introduce "half past" another day,
  one example at a time.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Use a tactile clock with raised numbers and movable
  hands, and read the position of the hands by feel.
- **Extension:** Write the time for a few everyday events, and predict where the
  hands will be one hour later.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner read a clock to the hour and half
  hour and write the time?

## Home connection

At home, look at a clock (or a phone) at three moments — waking, a meal, bedtime —
and read the time to a grown-up, then write it down.
