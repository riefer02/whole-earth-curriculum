# Lesson 10 — My Body, My Senses

## Summary

The capstone lesson gathers everything from the unit: learners name their body parts
and one use for each, name the signals their bodies send, perform a movement sequence
(skip, gallop, hop, jump, balance), and complete a careful-hands task (cut, thread, or
button). They close by celebrating all the things their one body can do.

## Objectives

- Identify and name basic body parts and tell a simple use for each. _(D03.S1.01.01)_
- Notice and name body signals such as hunger, thirst, and tiredness. _(D03.S1.01.02)_
- Perform basic movements such as skipping, galloping, hopping, and jumping with
  balance and control. _(D03.S2.01.01)_
- Use small hand movements such as cutting, threading, and buttoning with growing
  accuracy. _(D03.S2.01.02)_

## Connection

You have spent this whole unit getting to know the body that is yours — its names,
its messages, its movements, and its careful hands. Today you put it all together.
Every body is different, and every body can do wonderful things in its own way. This
is a day to notice and celebrate yours.

## Materials

- Body-parts and signal cards
- A beat source (clapping or drum)
- Safety scissors, beads, string, and a button board
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Set up four stations: **Name** (cards and labels), **Signal** (signal cards),
  **Move** (a clear space and a beat), **Careful Hands** (scissors, beads, button
  board).
- Prepare paper and crayons for the closing celebration drawing.
- Plan the rotation so every learner visits every station.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Set up the four stations, run the rotation, then lead the
closing celebration. The capstone is a celebration of growth, not a test: observe
what each learner can now do, and note it for the portfolio without ranking anyone.
Honor every body in its own way — adapted movement and adapted hand tasks count fully.
Keep hunger, thirst, and tiredness responses gentle and non-shaming, as in the earlier
lessons.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Look back over the unit. What did you learn about your body?
   Name one thing you are proud of.
2. **Name station (10 min).** Point to head, arms, legs, hands, and feet, and say one
   job each one does. Label a quick drawing of yourself with the five names.
3. **Signal station (10 min).** Look at the signal cards. Name hunger, thirst, and
   tiredness, and say where each one lives in your body. What is your body telling you
   right now?
4. **Move station (10 min).** To the beat, do your movement sequence: skip, gallop,
   hop, jump — then balance on one foot, steady as a tree. Do it in your own body's
   way, with wheels or seated springs if that is you.
5. **Careful-hands station (10 min).** Choose one careful job — cut along a line,
   thread some beads, or button a button — and do it slowly and well.
6. **Celebrate (10 min).** Draw or show one thing your body can do and name one
   signal it sends. Share it with the group. Your body is yours, it talks to you, and
   it can do wonderful things.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Shorten the rotation to two stations (name and move), with an adult or
  partner helper at each.
- **Extension:** Ask learners to combine everything into a short "all about my body"
  performance — naming, signaling, moving, and careful work in one flow.

## Assessment

- **Summative (performance/observation):** Can the learner name body parts and uses,
  name body signals, perform skip/gallop/hop/jump with control, and complete a
  careful-hands task?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The labeled self-portrait and the "my body celebration"
  drawing, kept in the learner's portfolio.

## Home connection

Show someone at home your "my body celebration" drawing and tell them three things
your body can do and one signal it sends you.
