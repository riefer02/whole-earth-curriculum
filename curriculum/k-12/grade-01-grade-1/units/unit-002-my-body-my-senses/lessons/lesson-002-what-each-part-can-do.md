# Lesson 2 — What Each Part Can Do

## Summary

Learners match each body part they named in Lesson 1 to a simple job it can do — the
head to thinking and looking, arms to reaching and hugging, legs to walking, hands to
holding, feet to standing. They act the jobs out and notice that different bodies do
the same jobs in different ways.

## Objectives

- Tell a simple use for basic body parts such as head, arms, legs, hands, and feet.
  _(D03.S1.01.01)_

## Connection

Every part of your body is busy with a job, all day long. Your hands hold your spoon,
your chopsticks, or your pencil; your legs walk you — or your wheels roll you — to
where you are going; your arms can reach a cup or wrap around someone in a hug. When
you know what each part does, you can thank your body for all the work it does for
you.

## Materials

- Body-parts chart or cards (head, arms, legs, hands, feet)
- A soft ball or beanbag (optional)

## Preparation

- Set out the body-parts cards from Lesson 1.
- Clear space to act out each part's job.
- Place the ball or beanbag nearby for the "what can it do?" game.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Prepare the cards and space, then guide the acting-out game.
The heart of this lesson is that the same job can be done many ways: a body that uses
a wheelchair still reaches and goes; a hand, a mouth, a tool, or a friend's help can
all hold and carry. Never hold up one way of moving as the "right" way. Celebrate
each learner's own way.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Look at your five body-part cards. Quick — point to your head,
   arms, legs, hands, and feet as you say each name.
2. **Match a job to each part (10 min).** For each part, say one job it can do:
   **head** — think, look, listen; **arms** — reach, lift, hug; **legs** — walk, run,
   kick; **hands** — hold, build, touch; **feet** — stand, walk, balance. Act out each
   job as you say it.
3. **"What can it do?" game (15 min).** Pass the ball or point to a card. When you
   get a part, act out its job and name it: "Legs — I use them to run!" Others guess
   the job.
4. **Different bodies, same jobs (10 min).** Think: can a person reach without arms
   or walk without legs? Yes — people reach with a mouth, a tool, or a chair that
   moves, and go with wheels or a friend's help. The job is the same; the body just
   does it its own way.
5. **Close (5 min).** Your body is full of helpers. Name one part and thank it for
   the job it does for you today.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Match one part to one job at a time; model the action and let the
  learner copy.
- **Extension:** Ask learners to name a second job for each part (feet can tip-toe;
  hands can wave).

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name a simple use for head, arms,
  legs, hands, and feet, and act it out?

## Home connection

Watch someone at home do a job (stirring, sweeping, carrying) and name the body part
doing the work.
