# Lesson 3 — My Body's Signals

## Summary

Learners learn that their body talks to them through signals. They meet three big
ones — hunger, thirst, and tiredness — feel where each one shows up, and practice
noticing and naming the signal their own body is sending right now.

## Objectives

- Notice and name body signals such as hunger, thirst, and tiredness. _(D03.S1.01.02)_

## Connection

Your body is always sending you little messages, like a bell that rings to get your
attention. A growling tummy is your body saying "I need food"; a dry mouth says "I
need water"; heavy, sleepy eyes say "I need rest." When you learn to notice these
messages, you can take good care of yourself.

## Materials

- Signal picture cards (hunger, thirst, tiredness)
- A bell or shaker (optional)

## Preparation

- Print or draw three signal cards: hunger, thirst, tiredness.
- Have the bell or shaker ready to show what a "signal" is.
- Arrange a circle for gathering and the guessing game.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Prepare the cards and bell, then guide the body check and
guessing game. Handle hunger and thirst with care: never assume what a learner has or
has not eaten or drunk today, and never make a learner feel that noticing hunger or
thirst is a problem. A body signal is always okay to have. If a learner names a
signal, respond warmly and help them think about what to do next; if food or water is
not available, help them name a trusted adult to tell.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Sit comfortably and get very still. Put a hand on your tummy.
   Listen. Does your body feel quiet and settled, or is it trying to tell you
   something?
2. **Meet three signals (15 min).** A signal is a message your body sends. Meet three:
   **hunger** — a growling, empty tummy; **thirst** — a dry mouth and throat;
   **tiredness** — heavy eyelids, yawning, a slow, heavy body. Act out each signal
   with your face and body, and say where you feel it.
3. **Guessing game (10 min).** When you hear a body message, point to the card it
   matches: "Your tummy growls" — hunger. "Your mouth feels dry" — thirst. "Your eyes
   want to close" — tiredness.
4. **Body check now (10 min).** Close your eyes for a quiet moment. Which signal, if
   any, is your body sending right now? Name it to yourself, then share with a partner
   if you want. There is no right answer — noticing is the skill.
5. **Close (5 min).** Your body talks to you all day. Listening to it is a way of
   being kind to yourself.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Introduce one signal at a time and act it out together before moving
  on.
- **Extension:** Ask learners to name where in the body each signal lives (tummy,
  mouth, eyes) and draw it.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner match hunger, thirst, or tiredness to
  its body feeling and name one signal their own body is sending?
