# Lesson 8 — Balance and Control

## Summary

Learners practice staying steady and in control: balancing on two feet and then one,
walking a line heel-to-toe, and carrying a soft object while balanced. They close with
a freeze-statue game that turns balance into joy. Seated and supported versions let
every body join in.

## Objectives

- Perform balancing movements with balance and control. _(D03.S2.01.01)_

## Connection

A bird standing on one leg, a person crossing a narrow bridge, a child carrying a
full cup without spilling — all of these are balance. Balance is your body's way of
staying steady and in control, whether you are still or moving. It helps you walk,
carry, and play without toppling.

## Materials

- A rope or chalk line
- Beanbags or small soft objects to balance

## Preparation

- Lay a rope or draw a line for a "balance beam."
- Set out beanbags or soft objects for balancing.
- Clear space so learners can move and freeze without bumping.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Prepare the line and beanbags, then model each balance slowly.
Balance is about steadiness and control, never about "how long" — celebrate any
successful steady moment. Honor every body: seated balance (a beanbag on the head or
hand, a straight still back) is real balance too, and a learner who uses a wheelchair
can practice keeping steady while gliding or holding a pose. Always offer a wall or a
friend's hand as support; no one should feel they might fall. For the freeze game, use
a visible signal too — a raised hand, a flag, or a flicked light — alongside the
spoken "freeze," so learners who are deaf or hard of hearing join in fully. Invite
learners with breathing differences (such as asthma) to rest and rejoin whenever they
need, moving at their own pace; a calm, steady breath matters more than finishing the
sequence.

## Procedure

1. **Warm up (5 min).** Stand tall and still like a tree. Sway gently side to side
   and find your center. Feel your feet press the ground.
2. **Balance on two, then one (10 min).** Stand on two feet, steady as a tree. Now
   slowly lift one foot and balance on the other. Hold your arms out like branches.
   Switch feet. Use a wall or a friend's hand if you want.
3. **Walk the line (10 min).** Walk along the line slowly, one foot in front of the
   other. Keep your eyes ahead and your body steady. If you use wheels, glide the
   line slowly and smoothly; if you are seated, sit tall and still, balancing a
   beanbag on your head or hand.
4. **Carry and balance (10 min).** Put a beanbag on your open hand or your head.
   Walk the line without dropping it. Move slowly — careful is strong.
5. **Freeze-statue game (10 min).** Move around the space. When you hear "freeze,"
   stop and hold a steady shape — a tree, a flamingo, a bridge. Stay still and
   balanced until the word "go."

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Balance only on two feet at first, then try lifting one foot with
  support; walk the line with a partner's hand.
- **Extension:** Ask learners to walk the line backwards, or balance the beanbag on a
  different body part (shoulder, knee).

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner hold a steady balance on one foot (or
  a seated steady pose) and walk a line with control?

## Home connection

Practice standing like a flamingo on one foot — or sitting tall and still — with
someone at home, and stay steady together through one slow breath.
