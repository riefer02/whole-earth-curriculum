# Lesson 1 — Meet My Body Parts

## Summary

Learners name the basic body parts — head, arms, legs, hands, and feet — find each
on their own body, and play a touch-and-name game to lock the names in. They close by
drawing and labeling a simple self-portrait.

## Objectives

- Identify and name basic body parts such as head, arms, legs, hands, and feet.
  _(D03.S1.01.01)_

## Connection

You have had your body your whole life, but do you know the name of every part? When
you learn the names — head, arms, legs, hands, feet — you can tell a grown-up exactly
where something tickles, hurts, or feels good. Knowing your body's names is the first
step to caring for it.

## Materials

- Body-parts chart or cards (head, arms, legs, hands, feet)
- Paper and crayons
- Chalk or tape (optional)

## Preparation

- Print or draw a body-parts chart or card set, one card for each part.
- Clear a circle for gathering and the touch-and-name game.
- Set out paper and crayons for the self-portrait.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Prepare the chart or cards, clear the circle, then lead the
touch-and-name game and guide the self-portrait. Every body is different, and every
body is good: some learners may use a wheelchair, a hearing aid, or a friend to help
point. Honor each learner's own body; never correct a learner for naming a part in
their own way, and never compare bodies. In the touch-and-name game, hold up or point
to the matching card as you say each part, so learners who are deaf or hard of hearing
can follow along. The game is already hands-on: touching and feeling each part is a
tactile path for learners with low vision. For the self-portrait, drawing around one's
own hand or foot (touching counts) or telling/pointing to a partner who labels are
equal ways to make the artifact.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Sit comfortably. Touch your own head, arms, legs, hands, and
   feet. Which ones do you already know by name?
2. **Meet the parts (10 min).** Look at the chart or cards. Name each part together:
   head, arms, legs, hands, feet. Point to each part on your own body as you say its
   name. If a friend helps you point, that is great too.
3. **Touch-and-name game (15 min).** Listen for a part and touch it: "Touch your
   hands!" "Touch your feet!" Then take a turn calling a part for others to touch.
   Everyone's body may do this a little differently, and that is fine.
4. **Draw your body (10 min).** Draw a picture of yourself. Label the five parts —
   head, arms, legs, hands, feet. You can draw around your own hand or foot to get it
   just right.
5. **Close (5 min).** Your body has names you know now. Say them once more: head,
   arms, legs, hands, feet. That is your body, and it is yours.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Introduce two parts at a time; point to a card and have the learner
  echo the name and touch their own matching part.
- **Extension:** Ask learners to name one more body part they know (elbow, knee,
  belly) and point to it.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name head, arms, legs, hands, and feet
  and point to each on their own body?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The labeled self-portrait, kept in the learner's
  portfolio.

## Home connection

Teach the five body-part names to someone at home and point to each part together.
