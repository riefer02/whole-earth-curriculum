# Lesson 9 — Cutting, Threading, and Buttoning

## Summary

Learners practice three small hand movements — cutting along a line, threading beads
onto a string, and buttoning — at three stations. Each job builds the careful finger
control needed for dressing, making, and writing, with larger pieces and adapted
versions for every hand.

## Objectives

- Use small hand movements such as cutting, threading, and buttoning with growing
  accuracy. _(D03.S2.01.02)_

## Connection

Think of buttoning a shirt in the morning, threading a needle, or cutting paper to
make a card. These little jobs use your fingers in careful, exact ways — the same
finger control you use to write and draw. Practice makes your hands stronger, surer,
and more skillful.

## Materials

- Safety scissors
- Paper strips with drawn lines
- Beads or pasta with holes, and a string or shoelace
- A button board or a shirt with large buttons

## Preparation

- Set up three stations: cutting (scissors and lined paper), threading (beads and
  string), buttoning (button board or shirt).
- Tie a knot in one end of each string so beads stay on.
- Choose buttons and beads in a range of sizes so each learner can pick what fits
  their hands.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Set up the three stations and rotate learners through them.
Small hand movements take time and repetition; praise effort and growing control, not
speed or perfection. Honor every hand: some learners need larger beads and buttons,
thicker string, or pre-cut paper; some do the job with a mouth, a tool, or a friend's
help. Offer adapted versions as real choices, never as "less." Supervise scissors
closely.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Wiggle your fingers. Look at your hands — they are your small,
   careful tools. Today they will practice three jobs.
2. **Cutting station (10 min).** Hold the scissors with your thumb in one hole and
   fingers in the other. Cut slowly along the drawn line, turning the paper, not the
   scissors. If cutting is hard, tear along a folded line instead — it builds the same
   control.
3. **Threading station (10 min).** Pick a bead and push the string through its hole.
   Slide it down, then thread the next. Choose beads that fit your hands — bigger
   beads are a fine place to start.
4. **Buttoning station (10 min).** Push a button through its hole, then pull it
   through the other side. Try it on the board, then on a shirt or coat. Buttons take
   patience — slow fingers are careful fingers.
5. **Close (5 min).** Look at what your hands did today. Your fingers are getting
   stronger and more careful with every try.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer pre-cut shapes, extra-large beads and buttons, and hand-over-hand
  help with the first cut or button.
- **Extension:** Ask learners to cut along a curved or zig-zag line, or thread a
  pattern of bead colors (two red, one blue).

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner cut along a line, thread several
  beads, and button a large button with growing accuracy?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** A cut-and-threaded strand or a buttoned sample, kept or
  photographed for the portfolio.

## Home connection

Practice one careful hand job at home — buttoning your own shirt, threading a bead,
or folding paper — and show someone what your hands can do.
