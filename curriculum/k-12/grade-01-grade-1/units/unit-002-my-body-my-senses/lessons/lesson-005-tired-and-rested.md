# Lesson 5 — Tired and Rested

## Summary

Learners notice what tiredness feels like in the body — heavy eyelids, yawning, a
slow, heavy feeling — and learn that rest and sleep help energy come back. They
practice a short rest-and-breathe pause and sort tired from rested.

## Objectives

- Notice and name the body signal of tiredness, and say how rest helps.
  _(D03.S1.01.02)_

## Connection

Everyone gets tired, every day — after running, after working, or when a day has been
long. A tired body sends messages: your eyes want to close, your body feels slow and
heavy. Rest is not being lazy; rest is how your body fills its energy back up so you
can play and learn again.

## Materials

- Signal picture cards (tiredness, rested)
- A quiet mat or blanket (if available)

## Preparation

- Set out the tiredness card and a new "rested" card.
- Lay out mats or blankets for the rest-and-breathe pause.
- Clear a calm corner for quiet rest.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Prepare the cards and a calm resting space, then lead the
rest-and-breathe pause. Be gentle about tiredness: never blame a learner for being
tired or sleepy, and never assume how much rest a learner got last night — homes
differ. Rest here is framed as kind care, not as a punishment or a fix for
misbehavior. A short quiet pause is enough; it can be done sitting, lying, or with
eyes closed or open.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** How does your body feel right now — full of energy, or a
   little slow and heavy? Look at the tiredness card. Name what a tired body does:
   yawns, heavy eyelids, slow heavy limbs.
2. **What is rest? (10 min).** When your body is tired, rest helps energy come back.
   Sleep is a long rest; a quiet pause is a short one. Rest is kind care, not being
   lazy. Look at the rested card — bright eyes, light body, ready to play.
3. **Rest-and-breathe pause (10 min).** Find a comfy spot on your mat or in your
   seat. Close your eyes or look softly down. Take three slow breaths in and out.
   Let your body be still and heavy for one quiet minute. Feel your body settle.
4. **Tired or rested? sort (10 min).** When you hear a clue, point to the card:
   "yawning and heavy eyelids" — tired; "bright and ready to run" — rested; "you just
   woke up from a good sleep" — rested.
5. **Close (5 min).** Your body tells you when it needs rest, and rest fills your
   energy back up. Name one way you can rest today.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Practice only the rest-and-breathe pause together, with the
  facilitator modeling slow breaths.
- **Extension:** Ask learners to draw "my tired body" and "my rested body" and tell
  the difference.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name tiredness signals (yawning,
  heavy eyes, slow body) and say that rest or sleep helps?
