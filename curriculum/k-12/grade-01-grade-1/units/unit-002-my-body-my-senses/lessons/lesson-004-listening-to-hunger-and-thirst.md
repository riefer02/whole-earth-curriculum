# Lesson 4 — Listening to Hunger and Thirst

## Summary

Learners take a closer look at two body signals — hunger and thirst — and learn
simple, kind responses to each: drink water when thirsty, eat when hungry if food is
near, and tell a trusted adult. They practice the thirsty response with a real drink
of water and draw what their own body is telling them.

## Objectives

- Notice and name the body signals of hunger and thirst, and say a simple, kind
  response to each. _(D03.S1.01.02)_

## Connection

When your mouth feels dry or your tummy rumbles, your body is asking for something.
Drinking water when you are thirsty helps your body work well, and eating when you
are hungry gives it energy to play and learn. Knowing the message — and what to do —
helps you take care of the one body that is yours.

## Materials

- Signal picture cards (hunger, thirst)
- A cup and a safe jug or bottle of water
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Set out the hunger and thirst cards from Lesson 3.
- Fill a clean jug or bottle with drinking water and set out a cup.
- Prepare paper and crayons for the drawing.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Prepare the cards and a safe water station, then guide the
sorting and the water break. Be especially gentle here: never assume a learner has
food or water available, and never single anyone out. If a learner says they are
hungry or thirsty and nothing is available, respond warmly and help them name a
trusted adult to tell. Eating and drinking customs vary across families and cultures,
and every body's needs are different — there is no "right" amount or time to eat.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Look at your two cards: hunger and thirst. Where do you feel
   each one? Hunger is an empty, growling tummy; thirst is a dry mouth and throat.
2. **What can I do? (10 min).** Match a kind response to each signal: when you are
   **thirsty**, drink some water; when you are **hungry**, eat if food is near. If
   there is no food or water right now, that is not your fault — tell a grown-up you
   trust. Your body's message is always right, even when the answer has to wait.
3. **Water break (10 min).** Practice the thirsty response. If you feel thirsty, pour
   a little water and drink it. If you are not thirsty, that is fine too — you can
   watch and learn.
4. **Hungry or thirsty? sort (10 min).** When you hear a clue, point to the card:
   "tummy growling" — hunger; "mouth feels dry" — thirst; "you just drank a big cup"
   — neither. There is no wrong answer; wonder together.
5. **Draw it (10 min).** Draw a picture of your body telling you something today —
   hunger, thirst, or neither — and what you can do. Close by naming your body's
   message and its answer aloud.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Focus on thirst only, with a real water break; name the dry-mouth
  feeling together.
- **Extension:** Ask learners to draw a second body signal and write or tell one
  response.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name hunger and thirst, tell where
  each is felt, and say one kind response (drink water, eat if food is near, tell an
  adult)?
