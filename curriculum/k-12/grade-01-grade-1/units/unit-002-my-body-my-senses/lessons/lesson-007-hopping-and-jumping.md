# Lesson 7 — Hopping and Jumping

## Summary

Learners learn two springing movements — hopping on one foot and jumping with two
feet — and practice landing softly with bent knees so they stay balanced and in
control. Seated and adapted versions are offered so every body can spring and land in
its own way.

## Objectives

- Perform hopping and jumping with balance and control. _(D03.S2.01.01)_

## Connection

Think of a frog pushing off the ground, or a child leaping over a puddle. Hopping on
one foot and jumping with two are ways your body springs and lands. Learning to land
softly, with bent knees, keeps you safe and in control — so you can jump again and
again.

## Materials

- A rope, chalk line, or flat ribbon to make a "stream"
- Floor markers or chalk dots for landing spots

## Preparation

- Lay a rope or draw a line to make a "stream" to jump over.
- Mark landing spots with chalk dots or floor markers.
- Clear space so no one bumps into anyone while hopping and jumping.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Prepare the line and landing spots, then model hop, jump, and
the soft landing before learners try. Emphasize control and a soft landing, never
height or distance. Honor every body: a learner who uses a wheelchair can do seated
bounces and arm springs; a learner who needs support can hold a friend's hand or a
wall. Offer adapted versions as real choices so every learner is moving, not
watching. A visual landing cue (a raised hand or a partner's tap) lets learners who
are deaf or hard of hearing time their springs. Learners with breathing differences
may rest and rejoin as often as they need; control and a soft landing matter more than
repetitions.

## Procedure

1. **Warm up (5 min).** Bounce gently on the spot with both feet. Shake out your arms
   and legs. Feel your knees ready to bend.
2. **Learn to hop (10 min).** A hop is a spring on one foot. Try a small hop on one
   foot, then the other. Hold your arms out to balance. Land softly with a bent knee.
3. **Learn to jump (10 min).** A jump is a spring with two feet. Bend your knees,
   swing your arms, and jump up — then land softly, knees bent. Now jump forward over
   the "stream," landing on the marked spot.
4. **Spring and land game (15 min).** Take turns: hop along the line on one foot,
   then jump over the stream with two feet. Land softly every time. If you use
   wheels, do a push-and-glide "jump" and a seated bounce; if you are seated, spring
   with your arms and bounce in your seat to the beat.
5. **Close (5 min).** Slow down and breathe. You can hop and jump and land softly.
   Your knees and your balance keep you safe.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Practice the soft landing first (bend knees) before adding the
  spring; hop in place with a hand held for balance.
- **Extension:** Ask learners to combine movements — hop twice, then jump over the
  stream — keeping control the whole way.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner hop on one foot and jump with two
  feet, landing softly with balance and control?

## Home connection

Find a safe line at home (a crack, a rope) and practice hopping along it and jumping
over it with a soft landing.
