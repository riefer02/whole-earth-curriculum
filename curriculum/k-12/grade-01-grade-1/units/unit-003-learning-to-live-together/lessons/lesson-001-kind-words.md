# Lesson 1 — Kind Words

## Summary

Learners discover that kind words are a way to show care, practice "please," "thank
you," and "may I," and learn that every family and language has its own kind words.
They build a class "kind words wall."

## Objectives

- Use kind words such as please, thank you, and may I when speaking with classmates
  and adults. _(D02.S1.01.01)_

## Connection

In every part of the world, people use kind words to show that they see and care for
one another. When you say "please" or "thank you," you tell someone their help
matters. Every family and every language has its own kind words — and yours do too.

## Materials

- Chart paper and markers
- A soft talking piece (ball, stone, or beanbag)

## Preparation

- Arrange a circle for gathering.
- Hang a blank "kind words wall" chart where everyone can reach it.

## Facilitator note

The standard names "please, thank you, and may I," but kind words are not one
language's property. Invite — never require — learners to share how they speak
kindly at home. Honor every version as a real kind word. Some learners may not feel
safe sharing a home language; accepting "I say it quietly at home" is fine. Read the
procedure aloud; guide the sharing and the wall.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Sit in a circle. Think of a time someone was kind to you.
   What did they say?
2. **Meet the kind words (10 min).** Say "please," "thank you," and "may I" together.
   Act each one out: open hands for "please," a nod for "thank you," a raised hand
   for "may I." Ask yourself: what kind words do we say in my family, in my language?
   Share one if you want to.
3. **Kind word practice (15 min).** With a partner, practice three small scenes: ask
   to borrow a crayon, thank someone who helped you, and ask to join a game. Use your
   kind words in each one.
4. **Kind words wall (10 min).** Draw or write one kind word — one from class and, if
   you like, one from home. Add it to the wall.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: kind words are not all the same, and that is the
   beautiful part. Kind words come in every language.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Model one kind word with a gesture; offer a sentence starter
  ("May I...?").
- **Extension:** Invite learners to teach the class one kind word in a home language
  and add it to the wall with a drawing of when they would use it.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Does the learner use a kind word in a practice scene
  or everyday interaction?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The learner's kind-word drawing on the wall.
