# Lesson 6 — My Communities

## Summary

Learners name several communities they belong to and draw them in circles or a web.
They discover that "family" and "community" come in many shapes — and that every
shape is a real family, a real community.

## Objectives

- Name several communities they belong to, such as family, class, and neighborhood.
  _(D02.S3.01.01)_

## Connection

You are never just one person alone — you belong to groups. The people you live
with, your class, the place where you live, a team or a place of worship: each is a
community you are part of. Belonging to many groups at once is what makes your world
rich.

## Materials

- Large paper circles or a community web
- Crayons and yarn

## Preparation

- Arrange a space for drawing (tables or floor).
- Prepare one large circle per learner, or one big yarn web for the whole class.

## Facilitator note

Never define "family" as one shape (two parents, or any single model). Honor every
shape — a grandparent raising a child, an aunt, siblings, foster or chosen family, a
classroom that feels like family. Some learners may not want to share private family
details; drawing and sharing are always optional. Say "the people who care for you"
as one open way to name family. Read the procedure aloud.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Sit in a circle. Think: what groups are you part of? You belong
   to more than one!
2. **Meet the word "community" (10 min).** A community is a group of people who belong
   together. Name some: your family — the people who care for you — your class, your
   neighborhood, a team, a place you go with others. Share one you belong to.
3. **Draw your communities (15 min).** Draw a circle for each community you belong to.
   Inside each circle, draw or write who is in it. How many circles do you have?
4. **Weave a web (10 min).** As a class, hold a piece of yarn. Name a community and
   pass the yarn. Watch the web grow — many communities, one connected class.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: families and communities come in many shapes. Every
   one of them counts, and you belong to all of yours.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide three pre-drawn circles labeled with picture prompts (home,
  school, neighborhood).
- **Extension:** Invite learners to add one more community (a team, a place of
  worship, a group of friends) and share what makes it special.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name at least two or three communities
  they belong to?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The community circles drawing, kept in the learner's
  portfolio.
