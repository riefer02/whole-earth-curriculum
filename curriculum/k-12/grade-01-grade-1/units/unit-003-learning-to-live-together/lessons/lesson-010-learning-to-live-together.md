# Lesson 10 — Learning to Live Together

## Summary

This capstone lesson gathers everything from the unit into one caring act. Learners
plan and carry out a small act of care for their class community — cleaning a shared
space, making a welcome card, or helping a younger group — using kind words,
listening, reading feelings, guessing needs, and sharing along the way.

## Objectives

- Use kind words such as please, thank you, and may I when speaking with classmates
  and adults. _(D02.S1.01.01)_
- Listen without interrupting while a classmate is speaking. _(D02.S1.01.02)_
- Guess how a classmate feels by reading their face, voice, or body.
  _(D02.S2.01.01)_
- Tell what another person might want or need in a familiar situation.
  _(D02.S2.01.02)_
- Name several communities they belong to, such as family, class, and neighborhood.
  _(D02.S3.01.01)_
- Describe one thing each community does together and one way they help out.
  _(D02.S3.01.02)_
- Offer help to a classmate who is struggling, without being asked. _(D02.S4.01.01)_
- Share classroom resources and take care of shared materials. _(D02.S4.01.02)_

## Connection

All the skills in this unit fit together like tools in one box: kind words, listening,
reading feelings, guessing needs, knowing your community, helping, and sharing. Today
you use the whole box at once to make your class a kinder place — the same way people
everywhere build caring communities with many small acts.

## Materials

- A "caring community" plan sheet
- Caring-act supplies (cloth, paper, a card, a plant to water — whatever the act needs)

## Preparation

- Prepare a short menu of caring acts learners can choose from (tidy a shared space,
  make a welcome card, help another class, water the plants).
- Set out supplies and a plan sheet for each small group.

## Facilitator note

This is a performance of the whole unit, not a test. Keep it joyful and low-stakes.
Let learners choose their caring act; your job is to notice and name the skills as
they appear ("I heard kind words," "I saw you notice she needed help"). Celebrate
effort, not perfection. No learner must speak in front of the group; sharing can be
by drawing, action, or a partner speaking on their behalf. Every culture builds
community with small acts of care — name that as you close. Read the procedure aloud.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Sit in a circle. Think: what is one small thing that would make
   our class kinder today?
2. **Pick a caring act (10 min).** With your group, choose one caring act from the
   menu. Talk it over: what will we do together, and how will each of us help out?
   Use kind words and take turns speaking.
3. **Do it together (20 min).** Carry out your caring act. While you work: notice how
   friends feel, guess what they might need, and offer help before being asked. Share
   materials so everyone has enough.
4. **Share back (10 min).** Gather in a circle. Each group shares what it did — with
   words, with pictures, or by acting it out — and the class listens without
   interrupting. Name one skill you used today.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: living together is a skill we practice every day, with
   kind words, listening, care, and help. Today you did it all at once.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer a two-option menu and a picture-based plan sheet; assign a
  "helper buddy" to each learner.
- **Extension:** Invite groups to plan a *second* caring act for a community beyond
  the classroom (a sibling group, a neighbor) and describe how the same skills apply.

## Assessment

- **Performance (observation):** Does the learner use kind words, listen, and offer
  help during the caring act?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The group's plan sheet and a drawing of the caring act,
  kept as the unit's culminating artifact.
