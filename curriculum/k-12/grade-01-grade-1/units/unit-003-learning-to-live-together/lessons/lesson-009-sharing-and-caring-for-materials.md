# Lesson 9 — Sharing and Caring for Materials

## Summary

Learners share a limited set of supplies so everyone has enough, and practice a
"materials doctor" routine for caring for shared things — using them gently, cleaning
up, and putting them away. They see that sharing is fair, and care makes things last.

## Objectives

- Share classroom resources and take care of shared materials. _(D02.S4.01.02)_

## Connection

Shared things — crayons, books, toys, water, the playground — work for everyone only
when everyone uses them fairly and gently. When you share, everyone gets a turn; when
you care, things last longer and the Earth is spared waste. Caring for shared things
is caring for each other.

## Materials

- A shared supply set (crayons, scissors, books, or blocks)
- "Materials doctor" check cards

## Preparation

- Divide supplies into shared sets, one per small group — make some sets slightly
  short on purpose so sharing is real.
- Arrange small-group work spaces and a tidy-up spot.

## Facilitator note

Sharing is taught here as fairness and mutual aid, not sacrifice: when one child
shares, everyone — including them — is better off. Make the shortage gentle, not
punishing, so no one is left empty. Connect caring for shared materials to caring for
the Earth (reuse, less waste) naturally. Read the procedure aloud; join the "materials
doctor" check.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Sit in a circle. Think: what happens to crayons or toys when no
   one takes care of them?
2. **Share so everyone has enough (15 min).** Work in a small group with one shared
   supply set. Some sets are short. Talk together: how can everyone get a turn? Pass,
   take turns, and use kind words to ask. Notice: sharing makes the little bit enough
   for all.
3. **Be a materials doctor (15 min).** Use the check card to care for your supplies:
   wipe them, check they are whole, count them, and put them away where they belong.
   Treat the things gently, the way a doctor treats a patient.
4. **Close (5 min).** Remember: shared things are everyone's things. Share fairly,
   care gently, put away. When we care for our things, we care for each other and for
   the Earth.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Give a smaller supply set and model one turn-taking exchange at a time.
  Pair a learner with limited movement with a "materials buddy" who does the lifting
  while the learner checks and counts — caring is shared, not a test of hands.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to propose one new class rule or routine for caring
  for a shared material, and try it for a week.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Does the learner take a turn, use kind words to ask,
  and handle materials gently?
- **Performance (routine):** Did the learner complete a "materials doctor" check and
  put materials away correctly?
