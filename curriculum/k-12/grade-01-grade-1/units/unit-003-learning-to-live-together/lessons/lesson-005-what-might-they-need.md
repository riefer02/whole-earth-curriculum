# Lesson 5 — What Might They Need?

## Summary

Learners look at familiar situations, read the clues, and guess what the person
might want or need. They practice turning a guess into a kind offer and learn that
needs are not always the same as wants.

## Objectives

- Tell what another person might want or need in a familiar situation.
  _(D02.S2.01.02)_

## Connection

When you notice someone's clues, you can guess what might help them: a friend who
spilled their water might need a cloth; a friend sitting alone at play might want
company. Guessing what another person needs is how caring moves from noticing to
doing.

## Materials

- Scene picture cards (spilled drink, dropped pencil, a friend alone, a heavy bag)
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Arrange a circle for gathering.
- Set out the scene picture cards where all can see them.

## Facilitator note

Guide learners from clue to need with a simple chain: "I see... so they might
need..." Keep guesses open — there is no single right answer, and that is the point.
Name gently that a need (water, rest, a hand) is not the same as a want (a toy,
first turn). Read the procedure aloud and model the chain sentence.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Sit in a circle. Think of a time you needed something. How did
   you feel before someone helped you?
2. **Clue to need (10 min).** Look at the scene cards. For each one, say the chain: "I
   see ___ (the clue), so they might need ___ (the need)." Say your guess out loud — or
   point to the clue and the need on the cards, or act it out.
3. **Guess kindly (15 min).** Practice saying your guess as a kind offer: "You look
   like you might need help. Can I help you?" — or offer it with a gesture, a sign, or
   a picture card. Remember a guess can be wrong — ask, don't assume.
4. **Draw a need (10 min).** Draw one scene and the need you guessed. Show what the
   person might need and how you could help.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: needs and wants are different. When you guess what
   someone needs and ask kindly, you turn caring into help.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer a two-choice guess ("might they need a cloth or a toy?") to make
  the chain easier.
- **Extension:** Ask learners to name two different guesses for the same scene and
  explain why both could be right.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner state a want or need that fits the
  scene's clues?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The "need and help" drawing, kept in the learner's
  portfolio.
