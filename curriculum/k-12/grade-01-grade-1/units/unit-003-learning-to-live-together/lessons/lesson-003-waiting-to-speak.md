# Lesson 3 — Waiting to Speak

## Summary

Learners practice how to take a turn in group talk: raise a hand, wait without
grabbing, and use kind words to ask for a turn. They play turn-taking games so that
waiting feels fair and doable.

## Objectives

- Listen without interrupting while a classmate is speaking. _(D02.S1.01.02)_
- Use kind words such as please, thank you, and may I when speaking with classmates
  and adults. _(D02.S1.01.01)_

## Connection

In a group, everyone has something to say, but only one voice can be heard at a
time. Waiting your turn is a way of sharing the talking time so that everyone gets a
fair chance to be heard — not just the loudest or the fastest.

## Materials

- A talking stick
- Hand-raising picture cards

## Preparation

- Arrange a circle for gathering.
- Agree on a quiet signal for "I want to speak" (a raised hand).

## Facilitator note

This lesson turns waiting from a rule into a fair-sharing practice. The aim is not
obedience but equity: every voice gets a turn. Use the talking stick so the turn is
visible and concrete. Praise waiting ("you waited and your idea still got heard")
more than you praise speed. The "raised hand" signal can be any clear signal a learner
can make — a raised hand, a raised card, a look, a tap, a small sound, or a partner
who raises on their behalf; honor each learner's way of asking for a turn. A learner
who does not speak with their mouth can ask for a turn with sign, a picture card, or a
device — that is a real "May I have a turn, please?" Read the procedure aloud; play
the games with the learners.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Sit in a circle. Think: what happens when everyone talks at
   once? Is it easy to hear anyone?
2. **Meet the talking stick (10 min).** The person holding the stick speaks; everyone
   else listens. Practice: pass the stick around and have each person share their name
   and one favorite thing — with their voice, with sign, or by pointing to a picture.
   Only the stick-holder talks.
3. **Raise a hand, wait (10 min).** Learn the signal: show your signal to ask for a
   turn — a raised hand, a raised card, a look, a tap, or a partner who raises for you.
   Practice signaling and waiting — not calling out — until the stick comes to you.
   Notice how it feels to wait.
4. **Ask with kind words (15 min).** When you want a turn, you can say "May I have a
   turn, please?" — or sign it, or point to a "my turn, please" card. Play a game where
   you ask with kind words, wait for the stick, then speak.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: waiting your turn is fair to everyone. Every voice
   matters, including yours.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use a very short turn ("one word about today") so waiting stays short;
  offer a physical token to hold while waiting. Offer a non-hand signal (a card, a
  look, a tap) for learners who cannot raise a hand, and a picture "my turn, please"
  card for learners who do not speak.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to run the talking-stick circle themselves, choosing
  who speaks next with kind words.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Does the learner show their turn signal (a raised hand,
  a card, a look, or a partner raising for them) and wait for the talking stick instead
  of calling out?
- **Self (reflection):** Ask: "How did it feel to wait? Was it hard or easy today?"
