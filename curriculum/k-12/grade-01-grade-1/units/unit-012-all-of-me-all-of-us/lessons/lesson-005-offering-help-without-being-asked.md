# Lesson 5 — Offering Help, Without Being Asked

## Summary

Learners practice noticing when a classmate is struggling — with a zipper, a hard
word, a spill — and offering help *without being asked*. They learn that help is
**mutual aid**, not pity: everyone needs help sometimes and everyone can give it, and
"no thank you" is always an okay answer.

## Objectives

- Offer help to a classmate who is struggling, without being asked. _(D02.S4.01.01)_

## Connection

Everyone needs help sometimes, and everyone can give help. When you see a classmate
struggling — a stuck zipper, a hard word, a dropped stack of pencils — you can notice
and offer to help, even before they ask. Help is something people give to each other,
back and forth: today you give it, another day you receive it. It is not something one
"better" person gives to a "lesser" one. And a person can always say "no thank you,"
and that is completely okay — it just means they want to try alone.

## Materials

- Picture cards of helping moments
- A soft ball or beanbag (optional)

## Preparation

- Prepare or gather picture cards of simple struggles (a stuck zipper, a hard word, a
  spill, dropped pencils).
- Arrange pairs for practice.
- Plan a live example from the room to use as the first "notice."

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Its commitments: help is **mutual aid,
not pity** — no one is only a helper or only the helped — and **consent matters**:
"no thank you" is always okay and must be honored calmly. Model the offer with warm,
plain words ("Would you like help?" or "Can I help you with that?") and the calm
reply when the answer is no. For a learner who does not use a speaking voice, an offer
can be a point, a sign, a gesture, or a gentle touch to get attention — practice these
as real offers, not lesser ones. Keep this about noticing and caring, never about
being the "best" helper.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of a time you struggled with something. Did anyone help
   you? How did it feel? Now think of a time you helped someone. How did *that* feel?
2. **Notice a struggle (10 min).** Look at the picture cards (or around the room).
   What clues tell you someone is struggling? A frown at a page, a stuck zipper, a
   spill, dropped pencils. Practice noticing without being told.
3. **Offer help (10 min).** Offer with kind words: "Would you like help?" or "Can I
   help you with that?" Then wait for their answer. Practice the offer with a partner.
4. **"No thank you" is okay (10 min).** Sometimes a person wants to try alone. If they
   say "no thank you," say "okay" and let them be — that is respect. If they say "yes
   please," then help, side by side, not taking over.
5. **We all help and are helped (10 min).** Help is mutual. Today you give it; another
   day you receive it. No one is only ever the helper or only ever the helped. Name
   one way you gave help this year and one way you received it.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Practice one scripted offer with a partner; use pictures of helping
  moments to choose from.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Offer help by pointing, signing, or a gentle gesture;
  practice the calm "no thank you" pause.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Describe the struggle aloud and practice the spoken
  offer; use a partner to role-play.
- **Extension:** Notice a help need for the whole class (a mess, a broken thing, a
  shared chore) and offer to help the group.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner notice a classmate struggling and
  offer help without being asked, and accept "no thank you" calmly?
- **Performance:** The learner practices a real offer during the lesson.

## Home connection

At home, watch for one time someone needs help and offer it without being asked — and
notice how it feels.
