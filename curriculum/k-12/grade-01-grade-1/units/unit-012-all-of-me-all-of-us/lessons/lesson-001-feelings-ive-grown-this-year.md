# Lesson 1 — Feelings I've Grown This Year

## Summary

Learners return to the feeling words they gathered across the year — beginning with
the four Kindergarten feelings (happy, sad, angry, scared) and the three new ones
(proud, lonely, excited) from the opening unit, then reaching into the fuller word
bank they built all year. They practice naming and showing each feeling in
themselves and reading it in others, celebrating that no feeling is ever "wrong."

## Objectives

- Name and show a growing set of feelings, such as proud, lonely, and excited, in
  themselves and others. _(D01.S1.01.01)_

## Connection

At the very start of this year you learned three new feeling words — proud, lonely,
and excited — to go with the four you knew from Kindergarten. All year long you kept
adding more. Now, near the end of the year, you can say exactly how you feel, and you
can read a feeling on a friend's face, in their voice, or in their body. That is what
it means to grow your feelings: more names, more knowing.

## Materials

- Feeling face cards (happy, sad, angry, scared, proud, lonely, excited)
- Chart paper and markers
- A small mirror (optional)

## Preparation

- Gather or draw the seven feeling face cards, carried forward from the year's
  opening unit.
- Set out chart paper to record the feeling words the class can now name.
- Arrange a circle and, if used, small mirrors for pairs.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. It is a **spiral-back**: it revisits the opening unit's
feelings rather than introducing them fresh. The deepest commitment here is that **a
feeling is never wrong** — only a guess about someone else's feeling can miss. Honor
every feeling a learner names, even one outside the list, and wonder together about
it. Feeling words live differently in every language and culture: invite a learner to
share a feeling word from their home language, even one English has no single word
for — every language carries feelings in its own way. For a learner who is blind or
has low vision, name the body-sense of each feeling
aloud (proud = warm and tall; lonely = heavy and quiet; excited = buzzy and bright)
so the feeling can be *felt* rather than seen. A learner who does not use a speaking
voice shows the feeling with their face or body and a partner names it. A learner who
is Deaf or hard of hearing shows and reads feelings through the face, body, and sign —
no spoken voice is needed to name or read a feeling.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Remember the start of the year. You knew four feelings from
   Kindergarten — happy, sad, angry, scared — and you met three new ones: **proud**,
   **lonely**, and **excited**. How many feeling words can you name now? Count them
   together and write them on the chart.
2. **Show each feeling (15 min).** For each feeling card, show it three ways: on your
   face, in your voice, and in your body. Proud is tall and warm; lonely is quiet and
   heavy; excited is bouncy and bright. Then show a feeling and let a friend read
   which one it is.
3. **Read it in others (10 min).** A partner shows a feeling without using words. You
   guess which one it is, then swap. Ask yourself: is a feeling ever "wrong"? No — a
   feeling is never wrong; only our *guess* about it can miss. If you guessed
   differently, ask: "Did I read it right?"
4. **Name your year (10 min).** Think of one feeling you felt this year — a big one or
   a small one. Name it, then draw it or act it out. What did you learn to do with
   that feeling this year?
5. **Close (5 min).** Your feeling word bank grew all year. More names mean you can
   share more about yourself and understand your friends more. That is something to
   be proud of.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Show just two feelings at a time; use faces and bodies, not written
  words.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Point to a card or make the face; a partner names the
  feeling aloud.
- **Support (Deaf/hard-of-hearing):** Show and read feelings through faces, bodies,
  and signs; pair each spoken word with a visual or sign cue.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Describe the body-sense of each feeling aloud and
  let a partner name it, or name it and let the partner show it.
- **Extension:** Name a feeling beyond the seven words — such as frustrated, worried,
  or joyful — from the year's growing word bank.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name proud, lonely, or excited, show
  it in themselves, and read it in another person?

## Home connection

Teach someone at home one feeling word you learned this year, and ask them to show
you a feeling so you can guess it.
