# Lesson 2 — Who I Am: Facts and One Thing I'm Proud Of

## Summary

Learners gather the facts of who they are — age, likes, abilities, and family — and
choose one thing they are proud of, connecting it to the effort or practice behind
it. They revisit the year's opening "All About Me" work and see how much of their own
story they can now tell, with pride held as personal growth, not comparison.

## Objectives

- Describe themselves with several facts such as age, likes, abilities, and family,
  and tell one thing they are proud of. _(D01.S2.01.01)_

## Connection

You are a whole person made of many facts — how old you are, what you like, what you
can do, and who is in your family. And you also carry a feeling called **pride**:
the warm, tall feeling of seeing how far *you* have come. Pride is not about beating
anyone. It is about your own growth — something you practiced, tried, or worked at.
Today you gather both: the facts of you, and one thing you are proud of.

## Materials

- A "Who I Am" page folded into four boxes (age, likes, abilities, family) plus a
  proud-moment box
- Crayons and pencils

## Preparation

- Fold or prepare the "Who I Am" page with its boxes.
- Set out crayons and pencils.
- Arrange partners for sharing.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It **spirals back** to the opening
unit's "All About Me" and "Something I'm Proud Of" lessons and deepens them: facts
are joined to pride, and pride is firmly tied to *growth, not competition*. Watch for
any learner drifting toward "I am proud I won" — gently re-anchor to effort: "What
did you practice or work at?" Remember that "family" means many things — a parent, a
   grandparent, a guardian, a foster parent, siblings, chosen kin; there is no correct
   family to draw. No two pages should look alike, and that is the point. For a
   learner who does not use a
speaking voice, accept pointing, drawing, acting, or a partner voicing the words.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** At the start of the year you collected four kinds of facts
   about yourself: your **age**, your **likes**, your **abilities**, and your
   **family**. Do you still remember them? Today you will tell them again — and add
   one thing you are proud of.
2. **My facts today (10 min).** Say or draw one fact for each box. Age: a number (and
   your birthday, if you know it). Likes: a favorite game, food, or place. Abilities:
   one thing you can do *now* that you could not do at the start of the year. Family:
   the people you live with or love.
3. **One thing I'm proud of (15 min).** Pride is a feeling about your own growth. Pick
   one thing you are proud of — something you practiced, tried, or worked at. Finish
   this sentence: "I am proud of ___ because ___." Draw it in the proud-moment box.
4. **Share with a partner (10 min).** Tell your partner your facts and your proud
   thing. Listen to theirs. Notice what is the same about you and what is different —
   both are good.
5. **Close (5 min).** Your facts and your pride are yours alone, and no one's page is
   better than another's. Each page is a true story, and yours is one of a kind.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer a sentence starter — "I am proud of ___ because ___" — and
  picture choices for facts.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Point to pictures for each fact; show the proud thing
  by acting it or pointing; a partner voices the words.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Tell facts aloud and have a partner scribe; use
  raised or textured marks to build the page tactually.
- **Extension:** Name a second thing you are proud of, or one thing you are still
  learning and want to grow at next year.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner state several facts about themselves
  (age, a like, an ability, family) and name one thing they are proud of with a
  reason behind it?

## Home connection

Tell someone at home one fact about you and one thing you are proud of — then ask
them one fact about themselves and one thing *they* are proud of.
