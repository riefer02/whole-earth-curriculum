# Lesson 4 — My Communities

## Summary

Learners name the circles they belong to — family, class, and neighborhood, plus
others such as a faith community, a sports team, a market, or a language group. They
build a "community web" with themselves at the center and celebrate that communities
exist everywhere on Earth, each with its own way, and none is "correct" or better
than another.

## Objectives

- Name several communities they belong to, such as family, class, and neighborhood.
  _(D02.S3.01.01)_

## Connection

You do not live alone — you belong to circles of people. Your **family** is one
community. Your **class** is another. Your **neighborhood** is another. So are a
faith community, a sports team, a market you visit, or a group of people who speak
your language. A community is a group of people who share something and do things
together. Communities look different all over the world — some gather around a well,
a market, a festival, or a game — and no community is better than another. They are
just different ways people belong.

## Materials

- Drawing paper and crayons
- Chart paper and markers

## Preparation

- Set out drawing supplies.
- Prepare chart paper for listing communities.
- Plan a simple example of a community web to model.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Its commitment is that **no community
is "correct" and none is better** — the goal is belonging, not ranking. Honor every
community a learner names, including ones outside the usual list (a language group, a
market, a place of worship, a team, a care circle). Keep the global view visible:
communities vary worldwide, and each has its own way. For a learner who is blind or
has low vision, build the web tactually — place an object for each community around a
center object that stands for the learner, and name each one aloud. A learner who
does not use a speaking voice names communities by pointing to pictures or objects.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of all the circles you belong to. How many can you name?
   Start with three everyone shares in some form: your **family**, your **class**, and
   your **neighborhood**.
2. **Name my communities (15 min).** Name several — family, class, neighborhood, and
   any others: a faith community, a team, a market you visit, a language group. Draw
   yourself in the middle of your page, then draw a circle for each community and a
   line from you to it. This is your **community web**.
3. **What we do together (10 min).** Pick one community and name one thing people do
   together there. (In my family we eat together; in my class we read together; in my
   neighborhood we greet each other.)
4. **Communities everywhere (10 min).** Communities look different all over the
   world — some gather around a well, a market, a festival, or a game. Notice: every
   community has its own way, and none is "correct" and none is better. They are all
   real homes of belonging.
5. **Close (5 min).** You belong to many circles, and every circle is a place where
   you are known. All of them together are part of who you are.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Name just two communities (family and class) and draw them.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Point to pictures of communities and circle the ones
  you belong to.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Build a tactile web with objects; name each
  community as it is placed.
- **Extension:** Name one thing each community does together, or add a community you
  would like to belong to someday and say one thing you would do there.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name several communities they belong
  to (family, class, neighborhood, plus at least one more)?

## Home connection

Ask someone at home what communities *they* belong to, and add one new circle to your
web.
