# Lesson 6 — Making My "All About Me" Page

## Summary

Learners pull the year together onto a single "All About Me" page: a feeling word
that matters to them, a fact about who they are, one thing they are proud of, a
family story or tradition, a community they belong to, and a kind act they did. This
page is the portfolio artifact for the year-end celebration, and no page is better
than another's — each is a whole person's story.

## Objectives

- Name and show a growing set of feelings in themselves and others. _(D01.S1.01.01)_
- Describe themselves with several facts and tell one thing they are proud of.
  _(D01.S2.01.01)_
- Name a family story or tradition and tell what it means to them. _(D01.S2.01.02)_
- Name several communities they belong to. _(D02.S3.01.01)_
- Offer help to a classmate who is struggling, without being asked. _(D02.S4.01.01)_

## Connection

All year you have been collecting pieces of who you are — your feelings, your facts,
your proud moments, your family's stories, your communities, and your kind acts.
Today you gather the most important ones onto one page. A **portfolio** is a
collection of your own work and story, kept so you can see how far you have come. Like
the opening unit's "All About Me" page — but fuller, because you have grown a whole
year since then.

## Materials

- A large "All About Me" page or poster with boxes for each piece
- Crayons, pencils, glue
- Work from the year (writing, number work, made things)

## Preparation

- Prepare or fold the "All About Me" page with spaces for: a feeling, a fact, a proud
  thing, a family tradition, a community, and a kind act.
- Gather each learner's year of work so they can choose pieces to include.
- Set out crayons, pencils, and glue.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It is the **portfolio-building** step:
every child assembles their own page, and every page is kept — none is ranked or
compared. Remind learners that a page can be made many ways: writing, drawing,
pasting, pointing, acting, signing, or dictating — all are real pages. For a learner
who is blind or has low vision, build the page tactually (raised shapes, textured
marks, a spoken or recorded description) so it can be "read" by hand and ear. For a
learner with motor differences, allow pasting pre-made pieces or dictating while a
partner places them. The point is the whole self, not a perfect page.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of all the things you have learned and made this year.
   Today you gather the most important ones onto one page: your "All About Me" page.
2. **Choose your pieces (10 min).** Pick one of each, from this year's work: a
   feeling word that matters to you; a fact about you; one thing you are proud of; a
   family story or tradition; a community you belong to; and a kind act you did.
3. **Make your page (25 min).** Put each piece in its own box — draw it, write it, or
   paste in work you already made. Add your name and a self-portrait. You may point,
   draw, act, or sign instead of writing; the page can be made many ways.
4. **Check your page (5 min).** Look at your page. Does it show the whole you? Is
   there anything you want to add? You are the author of this story.
5. **Close (5 min).** Every page in the room is a whole person's story. No page is
   better than another's. Keep your page safe — tomorrow you will share it, if you
   choose.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use pre-folded boxes and picture choices for each piece; scribe words
  the learner says.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Build the page tactually — raised shapes, textured
  marks, or a recorded spoken description of each box.
- **Support (motor differences):** Paste pre-made pieces or dictate while a partner
  places them.
- **Extension:** Add a forward-looking box: "In Grade 2, I want to…"

## Assessment

- **Portfolio:** The completed "All About Me" page, kept in the learner's portfolio.
- **Formative (observation):** Does the page include a feeling, a fact, a proud
  thing, a family/community piece, and a kind act?

## Home connection

Show your page to someone at home and tell them one thing on it you are most proud
of.
