# Lesson 2 — Looking at an Artwork

## Summary

Learners look closely at an artwork and practice two moves: **noticing** something
they see and naming a **feeling** it gives them. There is no right answer to what a
picture makes you feel — everyone's looking is welcome.

## Objectives

- Look at an artwork and name one thing they notice and one feeling it gives them.
  _(D07.S1.01.02)_

## Connection

Have you ever stopped to look at a picture on a wall, a pattern on a cloth, or a
mural painted on a building? Looking is a way of listening with your eyes. Art is
made by people everywhere to share an idea, a story, or a feeling — and when you look
carefully, you receive it. Today you will look at one artwork and share what you see
and how it makes you feel.

## Materials

- An artwork to look at (a printed picture or a real object)
- A simple "notice" frame (a cut-out paper frame to look through)

## Preparation

- Choose one artwork to look at together — a picture or a real object from any
  culture, presented respectfully as a maker's work.
- Make or gather the "notice" frames so learners can focus on one part.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Choose an artwork that is safe and warm for young learners —
something they can notice and feel without distress. Honor the maker and the culture
it comes from as a living people's work, not a museum piece; do not tokenize sacred
objects. Remember there is no "correct" notice or feeling — every answer is welcome,
and "I notice the color" is just as true as "I notice the shape." A learner who is
blind or has low vision receives the artwork by **touch or description**: a woven
cloth to feel, a carved surface to trace, or a careful spoken description. A learner
who does not speak notices by pointing or gesturing, and shows a feeling with their
face or hands.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Sit where you can see the artwork. Let your eyes rest on it.
   Look at the whole thing first — what is it a picture of, or what is it?
2. **Look close (10 min).** Use your "notice" frame (or your hands) to focus on one
   small part. Look at the lines, the shapes, the colors. What is one thing you
   **notice**? Say it, or point to it. Everyone notices something different, and that
   is good.
3. **Feel it (10 min).** Sit quietly for a moment. How does the artwork make you
   **feel** — happy, calm, curious, excited, a little sad? Show the feeling with your
   face or hands, and try to say its name. There is no wrong feeling.
4. **Share (15 min).** Take turns telling one thing you notice and one feeling it
   gives you. Listen to a friend's notice — is it different from yours? Both are
   true.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: looking carefully is a gift you give the artwork, and
   the artwork gives you a feeling back. Every maker hoped someone would look.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer a sentence starter ("I notice the ___" and "It makes me feel
  ___") and accept pointing or showing as a full answer.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Offer the artwork by touch or spoken description;
  notice through the hands, not the eyes.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Notice by pointing and show the feeling with face or
  gesture.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to find a second thing they notice and guess what the
  maker might have wanted to share.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Does the learner name (or point to) one thing they
  notice and name or show one feeling the artwork gives them?

## Home connection

Find something made by hand at home or nearby — a cloth, a pot, a picture, a carving.
Look at it closely and tell a grown-up one thing you notice and one feeling it gives
you.
