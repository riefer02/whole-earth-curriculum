# Lesson 5 — Moving to Show a Feeling

## Summary

Learners discover that the body can speak feelings without words. To music or a
story, they move to show **happy**, **angry**, **tired**, and feelings they choose.
There is no wrong way to move — every body tells its own true story.

## Objectives

- Move their body to show a feeling such as happy, angry, or tired in response to
  music or a story. _(D07.S3.01.01)_

## Connection

Think how your body already tells your feelings. When you are happy, you might bounce
or spin; when you are tired, your arms and head grow heavy and slow; when you are
angry, your body gets tight and small and fast. Everywhere in the world, people turn
feelings into movement — in dance, in play, in greeting. Today you will let your body
show a feeling on purpose, to music or a story.

## Materials

- Music or a short story to move to
- Open floor space

## Preparation

- Choose a short piece of music (or a told story) that suggests different moods.
- Clear a space big enough to move freely, and have a seated option for everyone.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Name the feelings clearly and move along with the learners —
your body is a model, not a rule. There is no correct way to show a feeling; each
learner's movement is their own and every movement belongs. Never single out a
learner to perform alone. A learner who uses a wheelchair or moves with support shows
the feeling from where they are — a "tired" body can be a heavy head and slow hands;
a "happy" body can be a bright face and open arms. A learner who is blind or has low
vision follows a spoken prompt ("let your arms get heavy and slow") or holds a
partner's elbow to feel the movement. A learner who is Deaf or hard of hearing
follows your modeled movement and a visible beat — tap a drum or the floor so the
feeling's rhythm can be felt, and name feelings with sign or a gesture. A learner who
does not speak shows the feeling with the body itself and "tells" how it felt by
pointing or gesturing. Watching and copying is full participation.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of a feeling — happy, angry, tired. How does your body
   feel inside when you have that feeling? Feelings live in the body, not only the
   head.
2. **Warm up your body (5 min).** Shake your hands, roll your shoulders, stretch tall.
   Your body is about to become a language.
3. **Move one feeling (10 min).** Hear the music (or the story). What feeling does it
   suggest? Move your whole body to show it — happy might be bouncy and open; angry
   tight and fast; tired slow and heavy. There is no wrong way.
4. **Move more feelings (15 min).** Change the music or the story, and move a new
   feeling. Try happy, angry, tired, and one more you choose — calm, excited, sad,
   brave. You can stay in one spot or travel through the space.
5. **Show and guess (10 min).** Move a feeling while a friend watches. Can they guess
   which one? Then tell them how it felt in your body.
6. **Close (5 min).** Remember: your body can say what words cannot. Every feeling is
   welcome, and every movement you make belongs.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Give one feeling and one clear prompt ("let your arms get slow and
  heavy like you are tired"); let the learner copy.
- **Support (seated/adapted):** Show the whole feeling from a chair — a happy face
  and open arms, tired shoulders, tight fists; moving just the face, hands, and
  shoulders is full participation.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Follow spoken movement prompts or hold a partner's
  elbow to feel the movement.
- **Support (Deaf/HH):** Follow the modeled movement and a felt beat on a drum or the
  floor; name feelings with sign or gesture.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Show the feeling with the body; "tell" how it felt by
  pointing or gesturing.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to move a feeling that changes — start happy and
  slowly become tired — and name the change.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Does the learner move their body to show a feeling in
  response to music or a story?
- **Performance:** The learner's movement, observed live (or in a seated/adapted
  form).

## Home connection

At home, pick a feeling and show it with your body while a grown-up guesses. Then ask
them to show a feeling for you to guess.
