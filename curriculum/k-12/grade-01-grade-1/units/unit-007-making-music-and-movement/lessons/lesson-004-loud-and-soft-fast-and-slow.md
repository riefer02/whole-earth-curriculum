# Lesson 4 — Loud and Soft, Fast and Slow

## Summary

Learners explore two ways sounds differ: **loud** or **soft**, and **fast** or
**slow**. They make sounds with their bodies and found objects, then listen and sort
what they hear. Sound has a size and a speed, and every culture plays with both.

## Objectives

- Tell the difference between loud and soft, and fast and slow, sounds they hear.
  _(D07.S2.01.02)_

## Connection

Listen for a moment. Some sounds are **loud** — a drum, a shout, thunder — and some
are **soft** — a whisper, a leaf falling, rain far away. Some are **fast** — running
feet, a bird's quick call — and some are **slow** — a slow wave, a long, low bell.
Everywhere, music and the world itself are made of these two pairs. Today you will
listen and sort sounds by loud or soft, and fast or slow.

## Materials

- Hands, voices, and feet
- A small set of sound makers: a pot, a shaker, a cup, a leaf

## Preparation

- Gather a few sound makers that clearly contrast (something loud, something soft).
- Arrange the group so everyone can hear and see the sound being made.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Keep contrasts big and obvious at first ("a loud clap" vs "a
soft tap"), then make them finer. There is no wrong answer, but you can always ask,
"what made you say soft?" — that is the thinking we want. For a learner who is Deaf
or hard of hearing, teach the pairs as **felt size and speed**: a loud sound makes a
bigger, stronger **vibration** and a soft one a smaller one; a fast beat moves quickly
under the hand and a slow one moves gently. They sort by feeling the drum or floor,
not only by hearing. A learner who does not speak answers with signals — arms wide
for loud and together for soft, hands moving fast or slow. A learner who is blind or
has low vision sorts by listening and by touch.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Close your eyes (or look down) and listen. What sound do you
   hear? Is it loud or soft? Is it fast or slow? These are two ways to describe any
   sound.
2. **Loud and soft (10 min).** Clap loudly. Now tap your finger softly. Which is
   loud? Which is soft? Take turns making a loud sound and a soft sound, and let
   friends name which is which. If you can, feel the difference — a loud clap makes a
   bigger shake than a soft tap.
3. **Fast and slow (10 min).** Pat your knees fast, then slow. Which is fast? Which
   is slow? Make a fast sound and a slow sound and let friends name them.
4. **Listen and sort (15 min).** Play a sound — a pot, a shaker, a leaf, a voice, a
   stomp. Is it loud or soft? Is it fast or slow? Show your answer with your hands
   (wide for loud, together for soft; fast or slow with your hands). There is no
   wrong way to feel a sound.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: every sound has a size (loud or soft) and a speed
   (fast or slow). Your ears — and your hands — can tell them apart.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Sort one pair at a time (loud/soft first); give very clear contrasts
  and let the learner echo each sound.
- **Support (Deaf/HH):** Sort by feeling vibration on a drum or the floor — bigger
  shake is loud, smaller is soft; quick beats are fast, gentle ones slow.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Answer with hand signals (wide/together, fast/slow).
- **Extension:** Invite learners to make a sound that is loud AND slow, then one that
  is soft AND fast, and name both parts.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Does the learner tell loud from soft and fast from
  slow in sounds they hear or make?

## Home connection

At home or outside, listen for three sounds. For each one, tell a grown-up: is it
loud or soft, and fast or slow?
