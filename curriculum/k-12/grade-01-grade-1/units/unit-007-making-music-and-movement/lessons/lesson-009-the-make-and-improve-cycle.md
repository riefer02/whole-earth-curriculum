# Lesson 9 — The Make-and-Improve Cycle

## Summary

Learners put the last two lessons together into one **cycle**: imagine, plan, make,
test, improve — and then travel the loop again. They discover that making is not one
try but a circle, and that every maker everywhere moves through these same steps.

## Objectives

- Build something with a simple plan in mind and tell what it is for.
  _(D07.S4.01.01)_
- Test what they made and change one thing to make it work better.
  _(D07.S4.01.02)_

## Connection

Think of a potter shaping a bowl, a boat-builder fixing a leak, a cook tasting a stew
and adding salt. Each one is doing the same circle: **imagine** what to make,
**plan** how, **make** it, **test** it, and **improve** it — then around again. The
cycle has no "wrong" stop on it; every stop teaches you something. Today you will
travel the whole circle on one build.

## Materials

- A full set of building materials (blocks, boxes, cups, tape, string, cloth, found
  parts)
- A simple cycle chart: imagine → plan → make → test → improve

## Preparation

- Set out a full set of materials so learners can change and re-change their builds.
- Display the cycle chart (or plan to draw it) where everyone can see the five steps.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. The cycle is a **skill structure**, so model it explicitly
once — you imagine aloud, plan aloud, build, test, improve — before learners run it
themselves (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006). Keep the five steps as pictures or
spoken words; reading and writing are not required. Let learners travel the loop at
their own speed — some will go around once, some several times; both are complete.
Treat "improve" as a step that always exists, not a sign of earlier failure. A
learner with limited fine motor narrates the cycle and directs a partner who builds.
A learner who is blind or has low vision travels the cycle by feel and spoken steps;
the cycle chart can be made tactile — a string circle with a stone marking each step.
Honor every journey around the circle.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Look at the cycle chart. These five steps are a circle:
   **imagine**, **plan**, **make**, **test**, **improve**. Makers everywhere travel
   this circle.
2. **Imagine and plan (10 min).** Think of something to build and what it is for.
   Say or sketch your plan. What will hold it up? What is its job?
3. **Make (10 min).** Build it. Work from your plan, and let the plan change as you
   learn.
4. **Test and improve (10 min).** Try the job and watch. Change one thing to make it
   work better, then test again. You have now traveled the whole circle once.
5. **Travel again (10 min).** Go around the circle again — improve one more thing,
   or start a new imagine-and-plan for a better build. The circle never has to stop;
   it is how things keep getting better.
6. **Close (5 min).** Remember: making is a circle, not a single try. Every stop on
   the circle taught you something, and your build is yours.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Guide one full cycle step by step, saying each step's name as the
  learner does it ("now we test — what happened?").
- **Support (fine motor):** The learner narrates and decides while a partner builds.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Point to each step on the chart as the learner reaches
  it, and to the part changed.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Travel the cycle by feel and spoken steps; use a
  tactile chart (string circle and stones) to mark each step.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to run the cycle on a harder job (a bridge that holds
  a heavier stone) and tell which step taught them the most.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Does the learner travel the cycle — planning a build,
  telling its purpose, testing it, and changing one thing to improve it?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** A note, drawing, or photo of the build and the change
  made, kept in the portfolio.

## Home connection

At home, choose a small making job and travel the circle with a grown-up: imagine,
plan, make, test, improve. Tell them which step was your favorite.
