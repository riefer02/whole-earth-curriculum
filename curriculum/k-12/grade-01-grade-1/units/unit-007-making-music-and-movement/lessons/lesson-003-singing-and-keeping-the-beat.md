# Lesson 3 — Singing and Keeping the Beat

## Summary

Learners learn a simple song by ear and keep a **steady beat** by clapping or tapping
along. They discover that a steady beat is the heartbeat of the song — the same
everywhere people make music.

## Objectives

- Sing a simple song and keep a steady beat by clapping or tapping along.
  _(D07.S2.01.01)_

## Connection

Think of a song someone sings to you — a lullaby, a work song, a song for a
celebration. In every part of the world, people sing together and keep time with
their hands, their feet, or an instrument. The steady beat is the part everyone can
join, whether or not they sing. Today you will sing a simple song and keep its beat
steady.

## Materials

- A simple song everyone can learn
- Hands and voices

## Preparation

- Choose a short, simple song the learners can learn by ear (in their home language
  is best). Have the words ready to teach one short line at a time.
- Arrange the group in a circle so everyone can see and hear.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Singing and keeping a beat are **skills**, so teach them with
explicit modeling and guided practice before independent trying (Kirschner, Sweller &
Clark, 2006): you sing one short line, they echo; you clap one slow beat, they join.
Keep the beat slow and visible. There is no "correct" voice — closeness to the beat
grows with practice, and "not yet" is never "wrong." For a learner who is Deaf or
hard of hearing, make the beat **visible and felt**: clap and pat where everyone can
see, and invite them to feel the beat as **vibration** by resting a hand on a drum, a
tabletop, or the floor while others tap — sound is vibration the body can feel. A
learner who does not sing keeps the beat by clapping, tapping, or swaying, which is
full participation. Never push a shy learner to sing alone; singing together is
enough.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Sit or stand in a circle. Pat your knees slowly and evenly —
   one, two, three, four. That even, steady pulse is a **beat**.
2. **Learn the song (10 min).** Listen to one short line of the song, then sing it
   back like an echo. Learn the next line the same way, then put them together. There
   is no wrong voice — sing softly or loudly, or hum along.
3. **Keep the beat (10 min).** Now sing the song while you **clap** or **tap** the
   steady beat. Keep it even, like a heartbeat — not faster, not slower. If you can,
   feel the beat as a vibration through a drum, the table, or the floor.
4. **Try leading (10 min).** Take a turn leading the beat for the group, if you want
   to — clap the steady pulse while everyone sings. Trading the lead means everyone
   gets to make the music.
5. **Close (5 min).** Notice how it felt to sing and keep the beat together. The
   steady beat is the song's heartbeat — and it belongs to everyone.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Echo one short line at a time, very slowly; keep the beat with one
  single clap per beat, and let the learner lead with a single beat when ready.
- **Support (Deaf/HH):** Make the beat visible (clap and pat where everyone can see)
  and felt as vibration through a drum, the floor, or a tabletop; keeping the beat by
  touch is full participation.
- **Support (non-speaking/non-singing):** Keep the beat by clapping, tapping, or
  swaying; humming or moving along is singing enough.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to keep the beat with two different body sounds
  (pat–clap–pat–clap) while singing.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Does the learner join the song and keep a steady beat
  by clapping or tapping along?
- **Performance:** The learner's singing and beat-keeping, observed live.

## Home connection

Teach someone at home the song you learned and clap the steady beat together. Ask
them if they know another song with the same kind of heartbeat.
