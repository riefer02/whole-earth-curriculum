# Lesson 10 — Making, Music & Movement

## Summary

This capstone gathers everything the unit explored. Learners lay out their work like
a gallery and share one thing they drew, sang, moved, or built. They see, all at
once, that drawing, music, movement, and building are all **making** — and that every
piece has value and a story.

## Objectives

- Create a drawing or painting using lines, shapes, and colors and tell what they
  made. _(D07.S1.01.01)_
- Look at an artwork and name one thing they notice and one feeling it gives them.
  _(D07.S1.01.02)_
- Sing a simple song and keep a steady beat by clapping or tapping along.
  _(D07.S2.01.01)_
- Tell the difference between loud and soft, and fast and slow, sounds they hear.
  _(D07.S2.01.02)_
- Move their body to show a feeling such as happy, angry, or tired in response to
  music or a story. _(D07.S3.01.01)_
- Pretend to be a character in a simple story and use their voice, face, and body to
  show it. _(D07.S3.01.02)_
- Build something with a simple plan in mind and tell what it is for.
  _(D07.S4.01.01)_
- Test what they made and change one thing to make it work better.
  _(D07.S4.01.02)_

## Connection

All through this unit, you have made things with your hands, your voice, your body,
and your ideas — a picture of lines and shapes, a song with a steady beat, a movement
that shows a feeling, a build with a purpose. Today you get to look at it all
together, the way a family or a village shows its work at a fair or a festival.
Making is how you share who you are with the world — and every piece you made has a
story worth hearing.

## Materials

- Each learner's favorite piece from the unit
- A simple display space (table, wall, or cloth)

## Preparation

- Help learners choose one favorite piece (a drawing, a rhythm or song, a movement, a
  build).
- Arrange a display space and a circle for sharing.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Make this a true celebration, not a contest. There are no
"best" pieces — the point is that every child shares and every child is heard. Let
learners share in whatever form suits them: talk, point, sing, move, or show. Sharing
is a choice, never a duty — a learner may pass, and watching and clapping for others
is full participation. Never single out a shy learner to share alone. Honor every
culture's arts as living heritage, and every learner's work as belonging. Close by
naming the unit's big idea together: making is a voice, and every voice matters.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Bring your favorite thing from this unit. Look around at what
   everyone brought. It is a gallery of making.
2. **Set up the gallery (10 min).** Lay out the drawings and builds. Remember the
   songs, beats, and movements are part of the gallery too — they live in your voice
   and body.
3. **Walk the gallery (10 min).** Move quietly around and look. Find one thing that
   makes you curious. Ask its maker, "Tell me about this" — or point to it and smile.
4. **Share and celebrate (15 min).** One at a time, share your piece if you want to —
   show it, sing it, move it, or explain what it does and what you improved. You may
   also pass, or let a friend help you share. Everyone claps for everyone, because
   every piece has a story.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: your hands, your voice, your body, and your ideas can
   all make something new. That is making, music, and movement — and it is yours
   forever.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer a gentle prompt to share ("show us one color you used," or "show
  the beat with your hands") and let the learner point or gesture rather than speak.
- **Support (seated/adapted, non-speaking):** Share from where the learner is — point,
  show, sign, or have a friend help present the piece; that is full participation.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to name one thing they want to make next, and how
  they would make it better.

## Assessment

- **Summative (portfolio):** The learner's favorite piece, kept in the portfolio with
  a note of its story.
- **Performance (observation):** The learner shares one piece from the unit, in any
  form.

## Home connection

Make a tiny gallery at home: lay out a few things you made this week and give a
grown-up a "tour," telling the story of each one.
