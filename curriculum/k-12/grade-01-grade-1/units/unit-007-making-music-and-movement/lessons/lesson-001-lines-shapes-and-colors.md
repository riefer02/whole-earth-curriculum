# Lesson 1 — Lines, Shapes, and Colors

## Summary

Learners meet the three building blocks of pictures — **lines**, **shapes**, and
**colors** — and use them to make a drawing or painting of their own. Then they tell
what they made. The point is the making, not a "perfect" picture.

## Objectives

- Create a drawing or painting using lines, shapes, and colors and tell what they
  made. _(D07.S1.01.01)_

## Connection

Look around you. A roof is a line that slopes. A plate is a circle, a doorway is a
shape, the sky and the leaves are colors. Everywhere in the world, people make
pictures and patterns from lines, shapes, and colors — in the paint on a wall, the
weave of a cloth, the pattern on a pot. Today you will make your own with the same
three parts.

## Materials

- Paper (scrap or fresh)
- Crayons, colored pencils, or chalk
- Paint and brushes (optional)

## Preparation

- Set out paper and colors so every learner can reach them.
- Have a space to dry paintings, and a way to wash hands if paint is used.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. As facilitator, name the elements but never correct a child's
drawing — there is no wrong way to draw. Praise effort and curiosity ("look at that
long line you tried!"). A learner who is blind or has low vision makes **tactile**
lines and shapes — string, yarn, or glue-sand laid in lines and circles — and feels
them with their hands, then names each by touch. A learner who does not use a speaking
voice names what they made by pointing to each part. Watching and copying are full
participation.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of a picture you have seen — on a wall, a cloth, a
   pot, or in a book. Pictures are built from three things: **lines**, **shapes**,
   and **colors**.
2. **Meet the three parts (10 min).** Make a line in the air with your finger —
   straight, wiggly, zig-zag. Make a shape with your hands — a circle, a triangle, a
   square. Point to a color you love and say its name (or point to something in the
   room that color).
3. **Make your picture (20 min).** On your paper (or in the sand, with a stick), draw
   or paint using lines, shapes, and colors. You might make a house, a tree, a fish,
   a friend, or a whole invented thing. Use at least one line, one shape, and one
   color — but you can use many.
4. **Tell what you made (10 min).** Show a friend or grown-up. Name one line, one
   shape, and one color in your picture, and say (or point to) what it is. If your
   lines can be felt, let a friend close their eyes and feel the shape.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: every picture everywhere is made of lines, shapes,
   and colors — and now yours is too. Everything you make belongs.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer one simple prompt ("draw a big circle and a straight line") and
  let the learner add one color; name the parts together.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Use raised lines (string or glue-sand) so shapes can
  be felt; the learner names each by touch.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Name the parts by pointing, one at a time.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to combine shapes to make a new shape (two triangles
  make a diamond) and name what they discovered.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Does the learner use at least one line, one shape, and
  one color, and name or point to what they made?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The learner's picture (or a photo of it) kept in the
  portfolio.

## Home connection

At home, find a line, a shape, and a color somewhere — on a pot, a cloth, a wall, or
outside — and show a grown-up each one. Then draw one thing you saw.
