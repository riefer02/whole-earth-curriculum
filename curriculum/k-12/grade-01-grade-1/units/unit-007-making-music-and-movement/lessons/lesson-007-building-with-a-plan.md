# Lesson 7 — Building with a Plan

## Summary

Learners start with a **purpose**, make a simple **plan**, and **build** something
for it. They discover that builders everywhere — of homes, bridges, boats, and
baskets — think first about what the thing is *for*. A plan is an idea you can build
with your hands.

## Objectives

- Build something with a simple plan in mind and tell what it is for.
  _(D07.S4.01.01)_

## Connection

Think of the things people build everywhere — a house to keep out rain, a bridge to
cross a river, a basket to carry food, a boat to float. Every one begins with the
same question: *what is it for?* The answer is the **purpose**, and it shapes the
plan. Today you will choose a purpose, make a simple plan, and build something for
it.

## Materials

- Blocks, boxes, or cups
- Tape, string, or cloth for joining

## Preparation

- Gather building materials into piles for small groups or individuals.
- Set out a few simple joiners (tape, string, cloth) so structures can hold together.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Keep the plan simple — a spoken sentence or a few sketched
lines is enough; a written plan is not required. Ask "what is it for?" rather than
"what is it?" — **purpose** is the heart of this strand. If a build falls over, treat
it as information, not failure: "what could you change so it stands?" Honor every
structure; a wobbly tower is still a thinker's tower. A learner with limited fine
motor can direct a partner who stacks and presses, or build from larger, lighter
pieces. A learner who is blind or has low vision builds by feel, with a partner or
grown-up naming the parts out loud, and tells the purpose by touch. Every plan and
every build belongs.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Look at the materials. What could you build with them? Think
   of one thing with a **purpose** — a tower to reach high, a bridge to cross a gap,
   a boat to float, a little home to hold a toy.
2. **Make a plan (5 min).** Before you build, say or think your plan: what will it
   do? What will hold it up? You can say it, point to the parts, or sketch a few
   lines in the air or on the ground.
3. **Build (20 min).** Stack, join, and arrange to match your plan. If it wobbles,
   that is a clue — try a wider base or a tighter join. Build, look, change, build
   again. Your plan can change as you learn.
4. **Tell what it is for (10 min).** Show your build to a friend or grown-up and tell
   (or point to) what it is for and which part does the job.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: builders everywhere turn a purpose and a plan into
   something useful. Your hands just did that too.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer one purpose and one simple plan ("build a tower of five blocks")
  and model a steady base.
- **Support (fine motor):** Use larger, lighter pieces, or build with a partner who
  stacks while the learner plans and directs.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Tell the purpose by pointing to the part that does the
  job.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Build by feel; a partner names the parts and the
  learner tells the purpose by touch.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to plan two different builds for the same purpose and
  tell which one works better.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Does the learner build something with a plan in mind
  and tell (or show) what it is for?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** A photo or drawing of the build and its purpose, kept in
  the portfolio.

## Home connection

At home, pick a small purpose — carrying toys, holding water, keeping a door open —
and build something for it from what you find. Tell a grown-up what it is for.
