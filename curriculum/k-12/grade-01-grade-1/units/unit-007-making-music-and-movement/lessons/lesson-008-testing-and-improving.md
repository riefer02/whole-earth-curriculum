# Lesson 8 — Testing and Improving

## Summary

Learners **test** something they built, watch what happens, and **change one thing**
to make it work better. They learn that a test is a question you ask your build — and
a build that doesn't work yet is not a failure, but a clue.

## Objectives

- Test what they made and change one thing to make it work better.
  _(D07.S4.01.02)_

## Connection

Think of a door that sticks, or a bridge that sways, or a basket that tips over.
Builders everywhere test what they make, watch what happens, and change one thing to
make it work better — a smoother hinge, a stronger beam, a wider base. Testing is not
about being wrong; it is how things get better. Today you will test your build and
change one thing to improve it.

## Materials

- A build from last time (or a fresh one)
- A way to test it (a small weight, a rolling ball, water, or a toy)
- Extra building materials

## Preparation

- Have learners' builds (or fresh materials) ready.
- Set up a safe way to test each kind of build (a puddle for boats, a ramp for balls,
  a weight for towers).

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Frame every test as a **question, not a verdict**. When a build
falls or tips, say "interesting — what does that tell us?" and guide the learner to
one change. Protect the culture of "not yet": there are no broken ideas, only builds
that are not finished improving. Celebrate the *change* as much as the result. A
learner with limited fine motor can decide the change and have a partner carry it out
— deciding is building. A learner who is blind or has low vision tests by feel and
sound — feel the wobble, hear the splash, the thud, or the silence — and a partner or
grown-up describes what happened. Keep tests gentle and safe so no one fears the
"test" itself.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Bring the thing you built. Today you will find out if it does
   its job — that is a **test**.
2. **Test it (10 min).** Try the job: does the tower stand when you add a weight?
   Does the boat float? Does the bridge hold a stone? Watch carefully what happens.
   What you see is a clue, not a grade.
3. **Notice the problem (5 min).** What happened? Did it lean, tip, fall, or sink?
   Say or point to the part that needs help. A test that shows a problem is
   **information** — the best kind.
4. **Change one thing (15 min).** Choose **one** change to make it work better — a
   wider base, a tighter join, a lighter top, a stronger side. Make the change, then
   test again. Did it help? If not yet, that is fine — change one more thing and try
   again.
5. **Share your change (10 min).** Tell a friend or grown-up the one thing you
   changed and what happened when you tested again.
6. **Close (5 min).** Remember: "not yet" is not "wrong." Testing and changing is how
   everything good gets made — one try at a time.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer one clear test and one clear change ("it tips — let's make the
  base wider"), and test again together.
- **Support (fine motor):** The learner decides the change and a partner makes it.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Point to the part to change and show the test result
  with a gesture (thumbs, or a shake of the head).
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Test by feel and sound; a partner describes what
  happened, and the learner decides the change.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to change one thing, test, and keep improving until
  the job works, telling the story of each change.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Does the learner test their build and change one thing
  to make it work better, telling (or showing) what they changed?

## Home connection

At home, find something that almost works — a wobbly chair, a leaky cup, a tilted
shelf. With a grown-up, change one thing to make it work better, and test it again.
