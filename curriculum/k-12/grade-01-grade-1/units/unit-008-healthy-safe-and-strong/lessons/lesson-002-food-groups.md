# Lesson 2 — Food Groups: Fruits, Vegetables, Grains, and Proteins

## Summary

Learners sort foods into four groups — fruits, vegetables, grains, and proteins — and
discover that grouping is a tool for noticing the many different foods that help a
body. The four groups are one way to sort, never a rule that ranks any food or family.

## Objectives

- Name a variety of foods from different groups, such as fruits, vegetables, grains,
  and proteins. _(D03.S3.01.01)_

## Connection

When a cook or a market seller gathers food, they often sort it into kinds — a basket
of greens, a bag of rice, a tray of eggs, a pile of fruit. Sorting helps us notice the
many different foods that help our bodies. Today you will sort foods into four groups:
**fruits**, **vegetables**, **grains**, and **proteins**.

## Materials

- Four sorting baskets or mats labeled "fruits," "vegetables," "grains," and "proteins"
- Food picture cards (fruits, vegetables, grains, and proteins from many places)

## Preparation

- Prepare four labeled baskets or mats.
- Gather food picture cards covering all four groups, from many cultures.
- Remind learners there are no "bad" foods and no wrong answers that shame — sorting is
  about noticing.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). **Hold the line on no food shaming.** The
four groups are *one way* to sort, not the only right one — different schools and
cultures sort foods differently, and some families add a milk-foods group or group foods
by where they come from. Honor all of it. Let learners sort by their own family's real
foods, and celebrate differences. Do not rank any group or any food, and never tie food
to a child's size or appetite. A food may feel at home in more than one group (beans are
proteins, and some also think of them as a grain-like food) — that is fine; sorting is a
tool, not a test. A learner who is blind or has low vision sorts by touch (real foods or
textured cards); a learner who does not use a speaking voice sorts by pointing. A learner
who is deaf or hard of hearing sorts by pointing to the labeled baskets and watching the
demonstration from the front, since sorting here is shown with cards and baskets, not only
told. This lesson extends Kindergarten's everyday/sometimes sorting (U.00.002,
U.00.008) into naming food groups.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Remember all the foods we named last time. Today we will sort
   them into groups, like a cook sorting at the market.
2. **Meet the four groups (10 min).** Look at the four baskets. **Fruits** grow from a
   plant's flower and often hold seeds — mango, banana, apple, papaya. **Vegetables**
   are plant parts we eat — leaves like greens, roots like carrots or cassava, stems and
   more. **Grains** are seeds we eat — rice, maize, wheat (flatbread is made from
   wheat), millet. **Proteins** are foods that help your body grow, build, and mend —
   beans, lentils, eggs, fish, and meat. Every group has foods that help bodies.
3. **Sort together (15 min).** Pick up a food card. Which group does it belong to? Place
   it in a basket and say why: "A mango is a fruit because it grows on a tree and holds
   a seed." Talk with a partner about each food. If you are not sure, wonder together —
   sorting is about noticing, not being right.
4. **Some foods fit more than one place (5 min).** Beans are proteins — and some people
   also group them with grains. That is okay. Sorting is a tool to help us notice, not a
   rule that must be perfect.
5. **Close (5 min).** Look at the four full baskets. No group is "best" — bodies are
   helped by foods from every group. Which food from each group does your family enjoy?

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Sort just four familiar foods first; use real foods from the learner's
  own kitchen. Learners may point, gesture, or name foods aloud instead of picking up
  cards.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Use real or textured foods and sort by touch, naming
  each food as it is felt.
- **Extension:** Name one food from each of the four groups that their family eats, and
  wonder aloud about a food that could fit two groups.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner place a food into one of the four groups
  and give a simple reason, in their own way?
- **Self:** The learner names one food from two different groups.

## Home connection

At your next meal or at the market, point to one fruit, one vegetable, one grain, and
one protein. Say each food's name and its group.
