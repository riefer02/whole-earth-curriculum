# Lesson 6 — Dressing and My Daily Care

## Summary

Learners practice dressing skills — buttons, zips, snaps, and ties — with growing
independence, choose clothes for the weather, and assemble a "my day" self-care routine
that joins washing hands, brushing teeth, and dressing. Every body, every ability, and
every family's resources are honored; care is never a test.

## Objectives

- Perform daily self-care routines such as dressing with growing independence.
  _(D03.S4.01.01)_

## Connection

Getting dressed is one of the first ways you take care of your own body each day.
Buttons, zips, and ties need practice — and practice is how we grow. Dressing yourself
is a way of saying "I can care for my body." Some days you will need a little help, and
that is okay too — care is never a test.

## Materials

- A practice board or clothes with fasteners (buttons, zips, snaps, ties)
- Weather and clothing picture cards
- A "my day" routine chart

## Preparation

- Gather practice boards or spare clothes with buttons, zips, snaps, and ties.
- Prepare weather cards (sun, rain, cold, heat) and matching clothing cards.
- Prepare a "my day" routine chart with picture cards: wake, wash hands and face, brush
  teeth, dress, eat.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). **No poverty judgment, ever.** Some
learners have few clothes or need help dressing; some fasteners are hard for many hands.
Celebrate effort, never compare outfits or resources, and never imply there is a "right"
way to dress. Some clothing fastens with buttons, zips, and snaps; much of the world's
clothing wraps, ties, or uses sashes and drawstrings instead — all are honored here.
Honor motor differences: adaptive fasteners, Velcro, and help from a
caregiver or peer are all fine — the goal is caring for the body, not doing it perfectly
or alone. Weather and dress vary greatly around the world, and cultural dress is honored;
there is no single "right" outfit for a season. A learner who is blind or has low vision
practices fasteners by touch; a learner with a motor difference works on the fastener
that fits their next step. A learner who is deaf or hard of hearing follows the
demonstrations from the front, where the weather, clothing, and routine picture cards
carry each step visually. **Care is never a test.** This lesson completes the
S4.01.01 self-care trio (wash, brush, dress) and previews the unit's body-autonomy
lessons that follow.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Remember washing hands and brushing teeth — two ways we care for
   our body. There are more ways too. Getting dressed is one of them. Today we will
   practice, and practice is how we grow.
2. **Practice fasteners (15 min).** Work on a practice board or your own clothes.
   Buttons, zips, snaps, and ties — each one is a small puzzle for your fingers. Go slow
   and steady. If it is hard at first, that is normal; keep trying. Fingers learn by
   doing.
3. **Dress for the weather (10 min).** Match the clothing cards to the weather cards:
   sun, rain, cold, heat. People around the world dress for their own weather and their
   own life. There is no one "right" outfit — there are many ways to be dressed and
   ready.
4. **My day routine (10 min).** Put the day in order: wake, wash hands and face, brush
   teeth, dress, eat. Arrange the picture cards, or act it out with your body. This is
   your own care — a little more of it you can do yourself each day.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: every body and every ability is honored. Helpers,
   adaptive fasteners, and extra practice are all fine. Caring for your body is the
   goal, not being perfect.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Focus on one fastener the learner is closest to; practice together
  hand-over-hand, then let them try alone.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Practice fasteners by touch, naming each piece and each
  step as the fingers feel it; choose clothes by texture and by feeling the day's weather.
- **Support (deaf/hard-of-hearing):** Face the learner for each demonstration and let the
  weather, clothing, and routine picture cards carry the steps visually.
- **Support (motor-difference):** Offer Velcro or large buttons, and let a caregiver or
  peer assist while the learner leads.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to put the routine in order from memory and to teach a
  fastener to a peer who is still learning it.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner attempt a fastener and name or arrange one
  daily self-care step, with growing independence?
- **Self:** The learner names one self-care thing they will do on their own tomorrow.

## Home connection

Practice one fastener at home — a button or a zip. With a grown-up, choose tomorrow's
clothes for the weather, and try to dress yourself as much as you can.
