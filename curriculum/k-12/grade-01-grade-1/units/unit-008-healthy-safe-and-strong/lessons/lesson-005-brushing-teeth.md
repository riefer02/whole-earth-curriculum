# Lesson 5 — Brushing My Teeth

## Summary

Learners practice brushing their teeth with growing independence — morning and night,
all surfaces, for about two minutes — and discover that clean teeth help a body eat,
talk, and smile. They learn that people around the world keep teeth clean in different
ways, and that care is never a test.

## Objectives

- Perform daily self-care routines such as brushing teeth with growing independence.
  _(D03.S4.01.01)_

## Connection

Your teeth work hard every day — biting, chewing, and helping you talk and smile. They
are a part of your body worth caring for. People everywhere clean their teeth, though
not all the same way: some use a brush and paste, some use a clean finger, and in many
places people have long used a small chewing stick. Today you will practice cleaning
your teeth all by yourself.

## Materials

- A toothbrush and toothpaste (each learner's own, or a demonstration brush)
- A cup of water for rinsing
- A large tooth model or a picture

## Preparation

- Ensure each learner has their own clean toothbrush (never share brushes).
- Set up a place to practice, with water and a place to spit safely.
- Have a two-minute song ready to time the brushing.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Never share toothbrushes, and supervise
so toothpaste is not swallowed. **Honor every context:** where brushes or paste are
unavailable, teach a clean-finger or chewing-stick method without shame. Where a learner
cannot brush their own teeth, a caregiver or older peer can help — the goal is clean
teeth, not doing it alone. **Care is never a test** — celebrate effort, not perfection. A
learner who does not use a speaking voice points or acts out the steps; a learner who is
blind or has low vision is guided hand-over-hand through the motion. A learner who is
deaf or hard of hearing watches the demonstration from the front and follows picture step
cards or a hand-over-hand model; each brushing step is shown on the mouth, not only told.
The health facts are
from the World Health Organization: brushing removes food and plaque that can hurt teeth
and gums. This lesson extends Kindergarten's "Brushing My Teeth" (U.00.002) into
independent, self-checked practice.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Smile at a friend. Your teeth help you eat, talk, and smile.
   Today you will learn to care for them all by yourself.
2. **Meet the toothbrush (5 min).** Hold your brush. A little water and a small dab of
   toothpaste is all you need — a dot the size of a pea.
3. **Remember the steps (10 min).** Practice the motion with your finger first:
   - Brush the **tops** (the chewing part) with small circles.
   - Brush the **fronts**.
   - Brush the **backs**.
   - Brush the **tongue** side gently too.
   - Spit out the foam — do not swallow the paste.
4. **Brush for real, on your own (15 min).** Brush your own teeth, going around every
   part. Sing a short song in your head — about two minutes is enough. Rinse with a
   little water. Then check: run your tongue over your teeth — do they feel smooth and
   clean?
5. **When do we brush? (5 min).** Name the times: in the **morning** and before **bed**.
   Share with a partner one time you will remember.
6. **Close (5 min).** Clean teeth are a way of saying "I care for my body."

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Practice with a finger first, then brush together; use a picture card
  showing the steps.
- **Support (deaf/hard-of-hearing):** Face the learner for every step and pair each motion
  with its picture card; the finger-and-tongue check also works by feel.
- **Support (motor-difference):** A caregiver or peer helps reach back teeth while the
  learner leads as much as they can.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to make up their own two-minute brushing song and to
  count how many teeth they can feel with their tongue.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner brush with growing independence, showing
  the steps and naming one time to brush?
- **Self:** The learner feels their teeth and reports whether they feel clean.

## Home connection

Brush your teeth on your own, morning and night, this week. Ask a grown-up to watch and
cheer you on.
