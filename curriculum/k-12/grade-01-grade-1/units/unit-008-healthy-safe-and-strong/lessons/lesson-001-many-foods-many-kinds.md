# Lesson 1 — Many Foods, Many Kinds

## Summary

Learners discover that people around the world eat a wonderful variety of foods and
name many of them. They notice that different families eat different foods — rice and
beans here, flatbread and lentils there, fish and root vegetables somewhere else —
and that every family's food is worthy of honor, never ranked.

## Objectives

- Name a variety of foods from different groups, such as fruits, vegetables, grains,
  and proteins. _(D03.S3.01.01)_

## Connection

Everywhere in the world, people sit down to eat — but not the same foods. In one home
the everyday food might be rice and beans; in another, flatbread and lentils; in
another, fish and root vegetables; in another, maize porridge and greens. The world's
kitchens hold thousands of different foods, and each one belongs to a family, a place,
and a story. Today you will name the many foods you know and meet some you have never
seen.

## Materials

- Food picture cards (rice, beans, flatbread, maize, cassava, lentils, greens, mango,
  fish, root vegetables, eggs, milk foods, and more)
- A "world foods" basket or chart

## Preparation

- Gather or draw food picture cards showing a wide variety of foods from many places.
- Set up a basket or chart where named foods can be collected and counted.
- Set a warm tone: every family's food is welcome here, and no food is "better" than
  another.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). **Hold the line on no food shaming.** The
goal is to name and celebrate the *variety* of foods, never to rank them. A food that is
an everyday staple for one family may be rare or unavailable for another, and neither is
wrong — every family's eating is honored. Do not comment on what or how much a learner
eats, and never tie food to body size, weight, or appetite. For a learner who is blind or
has low vision, pass real foods (or strongly scented ones) to touch and smell and name by
feel. A learner who does not use a speaking voice names foods by pointing, signing, or
drawing. A learner who is deaf or hard of hearing joins by watching the speaker or signer
from the front and naming foods by pointing to the picture cards or signing the names.
This lesson builds on Kindergarten's food lessons (U.00.002, U.00.008) and opens
the spiral for this unit's food-group sorting.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of a food you love. Tell a partner what it is. Everyone
   has foods they love, and no two families eat exactly the same way. Today we will
   fill a basket with the many foods of the world.
2. **Name the foods you know (10 min).** Take turns naming foods — foods you eat, foods
   you have seen at a market, foods from celebrations. As each food is named, add its
   picture to the world-foods basket or chart. There are thousands of foods; together
   we can name many!
3. **Meet new foods (15 min).** Look at the picture cards. Some you will know; some may
   be new to you — a flatbread from one land, a root vegetable from another, a fruit
   you have never tasted. Say each food's name (or point to it) and wonder: where might
   it grow? Who might eat it?
4. **Foods are many, and all are honored (10 min).** Notice: some foods are **staples** —
   eaten almost every day in one place, like rice, maize, beans, cassava, or flatbread.
   A food that is everyday for one family may be a treat for another, and that is fine.
   No one's food is better or worse; every family's food matters.
5. **Close (5 min).** Count the foods on our chart. How many did we name? The world is
   full of wonderful food, and every kind is a gift.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Name and match just four or five familiar foods; a learner who does not
  use a speaking voice points, signs, or draws their food.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Pass real foods to touch and smell, and name each by
  feel and scent rather than by sight.
- **Extension:** Draw or describe one food special to their family and tell where it
  comes from and who cooks it.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name or point to a variety of foods from
  different places and show, in their own way, that different families eat different
  foods?
- **Self:** The learner names one food they already knew and one new food they learned
  today.

## Home connection

At your next meal, name every food on the table. Ask a grown-up the name of one food
your family eats that other families might not.
