# Lesson 4 — Washing My Hands

## Summary

Learners practice a complete handwashing routine with growing independence — wet,
lather, scrub every surface, rinse, and dry — and learn *when* to wash. They discover
how soap and water carry germs away, and they use water with care so there is enough
for everyone. Care is never a test.

## Objectives

- Perform daily self-care routines such as washing hands with growing independence.
  _(D03.S4.01.01)_

## Connection

Your hands touch everything: a door, a pet, the ground, a friend's hand, your food.
Washing your hands is one of the simplest, most powerful ways to keep yourself and the
people around you healthy. Whether you wash at a sink, a tap, a bucket, a pour jug, or
a stream, the trick is the same: water, soap, and a good scrub. Today you will practice
doing it all by yourself.

## Materials

- Soap and water (a sink, basin, bucket, or pour jug with safe water)
- A towel or clean cloth for drying

## Preparation

- Set up a handwashing station with safe water and soap (or ash, if soap is scarce).
- Have a short 20-second song ready to time the scrub.
- Plan how water will be conserved — where water is scarce, use the wet–lather–rinse
  method.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Model the steps slowly, then **step back** —
Grade 1 is about growing independence. Watch each learner and gently support, but let
them do the washing. **Honor every context:** where water is scarce, teach the
"wet, turn off (or put the jug down), lather, rinse" method that saves water, and note
that ash can work when there is no soap. Where a learner cannot use their hands, a
caregiver or older peer can help wash them — the goal is clean hands, not doing it
alone. **Care is never a test** — celebrate effort, not perfection. A learner who does not
use a speaking voice points or acts out the steps; a learner who is blind or has low
vision is guided through each motion hand-over-hand. A learner who is deaf or hard of
hearing follows the steps by watching the demonstration from the front and using picture
step cards or hand-over-hand modeling, since every step is shown, not only told. The
health facts here are from the
World Health Organization: clean hands with soap remove germs that cause sickness. This
lesson extends Kindergarten's "Washing Hands, Washing Me" (U.00.002) into independent
practice.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of all the things your hands touched today. Hands carry
   tiny things you cannot see — we call them **germs** — that can make bodies sick.
   Washing hands washes germs away.
2. **Remember the steps (5 min).** Say the steps and practice the motions in the air:
   - **Wet** your hands.
   - **Lather** them with soap (or rub with ash).
   - **Scrub** every part: palms, backs, between fingers, thumbs, wrists, and under
     nails.
   - **Rinse** the soap away.
   - **Dry** with a clean cloth.
3. **Save water (5 min).** Where water is precious, wet your hands, turn the water off
   (or put the jug down), then lather and scrub — and only then rinse. Water is a
   treasure; use just enough.
4. **Wash for real, on your own (15 min).** Go to the station and wash your hands by
   yourself, doing all six parts. Sing a short song while you scrub — about 20 seconds
   is enough. Then check your own hands: did I miss a spot?
5. **When do we wash? (10 min).** Name the times hands need washing: before eating,
   after the bathroom, after playing outside, after coughing or sneezing, and after
   touching an animal. Point to a partner and share one time you will remember.
6. **Close (5 min).** Show your clean hands. Washing your hands on your own is a small
   act of care — for you and for everyone around you.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Practice the motions in the air first, then wash together once before
  trying alone; use a picture step card if it helps.
- **Support (deaf/hard-of-hearing):** Stand where the learner can see your face and hands;
  pair each step with its picture card and a clear visual model.
- **Support (motor-difference):** A caregiver or peer assists with parts that are hard,
  while the learner leads as much as they can.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to teach the six steps to someone younger and to notice
  one more way to save water while washing.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner wash their hands with growing
  independence, showing the steps and naming one time to wash?
- **Self:** The learner checks their own hands for missed spots after washing.

## Home connection

Wash your hands on your own before one meal today. Show a grown-up that you can do all
the steps, and notice one way to save water while you wash.
