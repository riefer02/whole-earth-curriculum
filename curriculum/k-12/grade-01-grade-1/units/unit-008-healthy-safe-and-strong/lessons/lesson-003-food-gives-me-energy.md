# Lesson 3 — Food Gives Me Energy to Grow, Play, and Learn

## Summary

Learners discover that food is the body's fuel, giving energy to grow, play, and learn.
They match foods to the jobs of a busy body and build a "foods that give me energy"
chart — always without ranking any food or commenting on any body.

## Objectives

- Explain that food gives the body energy to grow, play, and learn. _(D03.S3.01.02)_

## Connection

A cooking fire needs wood to burn; a lamp needs oil or a battery; a bicycle needs your
legs to pedal. Everything that moves or grows needs some kind of **fuel**. Your body
needs fuel too — and that fuel is food. Food gives your body the energy to grow, to
play, and to learn.

## Materials

- Food picture cards (everyday foods from many places)
- A "foods that give me energy" chart
- A small plant or a sprouting seed

## Preparation

- Have a small plant or a bean sprouting in a jar to show growth.
- Prepare the "foods that give me energy" chart.
- Clear space for acting out grow, play, and learn.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). **Hold the line on no food shaming.** Keep
the focus on what foods *do* — give energy — never on amounts, weight, or appetite, and
never comment on what or how much a learner eats. Speak of food as fuel that all living
things need, and honor the many ways people get it — farming, fishing, gathering,
buying, growing in pots. Some children have little food at home; keep the tone warm and
matter-of-fact. It is basic biology that the body breaks food down and turns it into
energy for moving, thinking, and growing — a fact, not a value. The value we add is that
every body and every family's food is honored. The "foods that give me energy" chart is a
unit portfolio artifact; keep learners' additions. **Every body and every way of moving
is honored.** A learner who does not use a speaking voice points, signs, or draws their
foods and energy jobs; a learner who is deaf or hard of hearing follows the demonstration
from the front, where the picture cards and acted-out grow-play-learn motions carry the
meaning visually. A learner who is blind or has low vision feels the seed or plant, names
the three energy jobs aloud, and can model their energy-food in clay or choose a real food
to hold instead of drawing. This lesson extends Kindergarten's
"Foods That Help Me Grow" (U.00.002) and "Food for My Growing Body" (U.00.008).

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Put a hand on your tummy. Remember the food groups from last
   time. Food doesn't just fill you up — it is your body's **fuel**.
2. **Fuel and energy (10 min).** Look at the little plant. What does a plant need to
   grow? Water and food from the soil. A lamp needs fuel to shine; a fire needs wood to
   burn. Your body needs food to run, jump, think, and grow. Every food gives energy —
   no food is "bad" or "empty"; foods just help in different ways and different amounts.
3. **Grow, play, learn (10 min).** Your body's energy has three jobs. **Grow** — stretch
   tall like a seed becoming a plant. **Play** — run in place, jump, dance. **Learn** —
   touch your head and think hard about something you want to know. Food gives your body
   the energy for all three.
4. **Match foods to jobs (10 min).** Pick a food card. Say how it helps: "Rice gives me
   energy to play," "Beans help me grow," "Fruit gives me energy to learn." Add each food
   to the "foods that give me energy" chart.
5. **Draw your energy food (5 min).** Draw one food that gives you energy, and say (or
   write) what it helps you do — grow, play, or learn.
6. **Close (5 min).** Food and rest both give your body energy. Which food are you glad
   to have today?

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use three familiar foods and act out "grow," "play," and "learn" energy;
  let learners point or draw instead of speaking in sentences.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Feel the seed or plant to understand growth; name the
  grow-play-learn jobs aloud and describe one energy food by touch or scent.
- **Support (motor-difference):** Show grow-play-learn with whatever movement is
  comfortable — a nod, a blink, a partner moving alongside, or pointing to a picture — so
  every body can join.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to think of one food for each of the three jobs — grow,
  play, learn — and to explain their choice.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner say, in simple words or actions, that food
  gives the body energy to grow, play, and learn?
- **Portfolio:** The learner's "foods that give me energy" drawing or chart addition
  becomes a unit portfolio artifact.

## Home connection

At your next meal, ask a grown-up what you will do with the energy from that food — play,
learn, or grow. Tell them which food gives you energy to run and play.
