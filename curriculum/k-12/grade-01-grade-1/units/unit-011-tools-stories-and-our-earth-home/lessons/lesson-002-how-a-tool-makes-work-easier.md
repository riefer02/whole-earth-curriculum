# Lesson 2 — How a Tool Makes Work Easier or Faster

## Summary

Learners do a small task two ways — carrying by hand, then carrying with a basket or
cup — and describe how the tool changes the task. They learn to say *why* a tool
helps: it holds more, spills less, or lets one trip do the work of many. The lesson
keeps the message even-handed: a basket is just as much a tool as anything new, and no
tool is "better" simply because it is new.

## Objectives

- Describe one way a tool makes a task easier or faster than doing it by hand.
  _(D10.S1.01.02)_

## Connection

Try this: scoop up a big pile of dry beans with just your two hands and carry them
across the room. Some fall, your hands feel full, and you have to make many trips. Now
carry the same beans in a basket or a cup. One trip, nothing spills. That is what a
tool does — it makes a job easier or faster than your hands alone. People in every
place and every time have made tools for exactly this reason.

## Materials

- Small, dry objects to carry (dry beans, pebbles, or seeds)
- A basket, bag, cup, or folded cloth for each group
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Put a bowl of small, dry objects (beans, pebbles, or seeds) in each group's space.
  Use dry things so nothing is wasted — save water for the living things that need it.
- Set out one carrier (basket, bag, cup, or folded cloth) per group.
- Mark a short "start" and "finish" line for the carrying task.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The heart of it is the *comparison*, so
let every learner actually try both ways and say the difference in their own words.
Keep it hands-on and concrete: carrying, scooping, and sweeping are real tasks a
six-year-old knows. Do not use water for the demonstration — in places where water is
scarce, wasting even a little sends the wrong message and can be a real hardship; dry
beans, pebbles, or seeds work perfectly. Reinforce the unit's commitment: a basket, a
gourd, or a folded cloth is just as much a tool as anything new, and an older tool is
not "less." For a learner with motor differences, the choosing and the saying are the
task — they may direct a partner's hands and describe the difference; adapt the tool
too (a wide, light cup, a handled basket). A learner who is blind or has low vision can
feel how many dry things fit in cupped hands versus in the carrier, and describe the
difference by weight and fullness. A non-speaking learner can point to the fuller
carrier and to a picture for "easier" or "faster."

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Look at the bowl of dry beans and the basket. Today you will
   do one small job two ways, and notice the difference.
2. **Try it by hand (10 min).** Scoop as many dry beans as you can into your two
   cupped hands and carry them from the start line to the finish line. How many made
   it? How many trips did it take? How did your hands feel?
3. **Try it with a tool (10 min).** Now use the basket, bag, cup, or cloth. Carry the
   same beans the same distance. How many made it this time? How many trips?
4. **Compare and say why (10 min).** Talk with a partner: which way was easier? Which
   was faster? Say the reason with a sentence: "The basket makes carrying easier
   because it holds more and does not spill." Or: "The cup makes scooping faster
   because one scoop does the work of many."
5. **Try another pair (5 min).** Think of another job — scooping soup, sweeping a
   floor, cutting paper. Which tool helps, and how? Act it out or say it.
6. **Close (5 min).** A tool helps by holding more, reaching farther, or saving
   effort. A basket is as real a tool as anything new, and it has helped people carry
   for a very long time. Which tool would you choose if you had a big carrying job?

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Do the two ways side by side and compare one thing only — "which holds
  more?" — then say the sentence together.
- **Motor differences:** Choose the task and direct a partner's hands while saying
  which way is easier or faster; adapt the tool (a wide, light cup, a handled basket)
  so the learner's own body can join when possible.
- **Blind/low vision:** Feel how full the cupped hands are versus the carrier, and
  judge by weight and spill; a partner names the parts and says the sentence together.
- **Deaf/hard of hearing:** Compare "by hand" and "with a tool" with sign, pictures,
  and acting; the learner signs or points to "easier" or "faster" and shows why with a
  gesture or a drawn arrow.
- **Extension:** Name two different tools that do the *same* job (a basket and a bag
  both carry; a broom and a mop both clean) and tell which one you would pick and why.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner describe one way a tool made a task
  easier or faster than doing it by hand?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The learner's "by hand vs. with a tool" drawing or
  sentence, kept in their portfolio.

## Home connection

At home, try a job two ways with a grown-up — carry things by hand, then with a bag or
basket. Tell the grown-up which was easier or faster and why. Ask them about a tool
they use that saves them time or effort.
