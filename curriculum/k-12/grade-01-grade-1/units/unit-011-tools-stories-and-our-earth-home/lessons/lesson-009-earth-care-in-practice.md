# Lesson 9 — Earth Care in Practice

## Summary

Learners bring the two strands of stewardship together: wasting less and caring for a
living thing. They do both in one connected act — turning off a tap, then using just
enough water to water a plant — and discover that the two are really one care: when we
save water, we are caring for the living things that need it. Ecology is not a chapter
here; it is the ground they are standing on.

## Objectives

- Name one way to waste less, such as turning off water or reusing a bag, and do it.
  _(D10.S4.01.01)_
- Help care for a plant or animal and say what it needs each day. _(D10.S4.01.02)_

## Connection

The water you save and the plant you water are part of the same story. When you turn
off a tap so water is not wasted, you are keeping water for the plant, the bird, and
the people who need it. When you water a plant with just enough — not too much, not too
little — you are wasting less and caring at the same time. These are not two different
jobs; they are two faces of one act of care for our shared home.

## Materials

- Supplies for two connected acts of care (a tap or cup, and a plant or animal to care
  for)
- Paper and crayons for a two-part "Earth care" drawing

## Preparation

- Choose a connected pair of acts learners can really do: turn off a running tap and
  then water a plant with just enough; or use a cup carefully and fill a bird's water
  dish.
- Set out the supplies for both parts.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The key move is the *connection*: saving
water and caring for a living thing are one care, not two. Make both acts real and
connected — learners turn off a tap, then carry a small, just-enough cup to a plant or
a bird's dish. Keep the tone hopeful and agentic: learners are helpers, not saviors or
enemies, and no act is too small. Where water is scarce, this connection is especially
true and especially dear — a carefully used cup of water for a plant is both a
waste-less act and a caring act, and it is exactly right to celebrate. Never shame any
family or place for how they use water; honor the care people already show. For a
learner with motor differences, the choosing and the directing are the care — they may
guide a partner's hands to turn off the tap and pour the water while saying how the two
acts are one; adapt the tools (a light cup, a long-handled tap). A learner who is blind
or has low vision can hear the tap stop and feel the damp soil after watering, and say
the connection. A non-speaking learner can point from the tap to the plant to show the
two acts are connected. This lesson draws on the spirit of education for sustainable
development (see Resources).

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of two things you can do for the Earth: waste less, and
   care for a living thing. Are they two jobs, or one?
2. **Do the first act — waste less (10 min).** Turn off a running tap, or use just
   enough water from a cup. Say why it helps: "Turning off the tap saves water."
3. **Do the second act — care for a living thing (10 min).** Now use a little of that
   saved water to water a plant, or fill a bird's dish. Say why it helps: "This water
   helps the plant grow."
4. **Find the connection (10 min).** Talk with a partner: how are the two acts one
   care? (The water you save is the water the plant drinks; caring for water *is*
   caring for the plant.) Say it in your own words.
5. **Draw the two-part care (10 min).** Draw the two acts side by side — the tap
   turned off, and the plant watered with just enough — with one line joining them.
6. **Close (5 min).** Wasting less and caring for living things are one care for one
   home. When we save water, we save it *for* the living things we love. What will
   you do with your saved water tomorrow?

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Do the two acts together and say the connection as one shared sentence,
  hand-over-hand.
- **Motor differences:** Choose and direct both acts through a partner's hands while
  saying the connection; adapt the tools (a light cup, a long-handled tap).
- **Blind/low vision:** Hear the tap stop and feel the soil turn damp; a partner names
  the two acts and the learner says the connection.
- **Deaf/hard of hearing:** Show the two connected acts with sign, pictures, and
  acting; the learner signs or points from the tap to the plant to show they are one
  care.
- **Extension:** Name a third act that joins the two — reusing a container to carry
  water to a plant, or watering with leftover water instead of fresh — and tell why it
  is care twice over.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Did the learner do one waste-less act and one
  caring-for-a-living-thing act, and say how the two are connected?
- **Portfolio:** The two-part "Earth care" drawing, kept as a record of the connected
  acts.

## Home connection

At home, join the two acts with a grown-up: turn off a running tap, then use a little
water to water a plant or fill a dish for a bird or pet. Tell the grown-up how saving
water and caring for a living thing are one care for one home.
