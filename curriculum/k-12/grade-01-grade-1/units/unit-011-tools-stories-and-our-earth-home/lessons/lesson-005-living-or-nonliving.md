# Lesson 5 — Living or Nonliving?

## Summary

Learners learn the simple clues that tell a living thing from a nonliving thing:
living things grow and change, need food or water, and breathe air; nonliving things
do not. They sort real things and picture cards into the two groups, give an example
of each, and learn that living things are to be cared for and respected — never harmed.

## Objectives

- Tell the difference between a living thing and a nonliving thing, and give an
  example of each. _(D10.S3.01.01)_

## Connection

You are a living thing. So is the plant on the windowsill, the bird outside, the fish
in the water, and the bug under the stone. A rock, a spoon, a toy, and a puddle of
water are not alive — they are **nonliving**. How can we tell? Living things grow and
change, they need food or water, and they breathe air. Nonliving things do none of
those. Today you will learn to notice the difference.

## Materials

- A real plant (or a clear picture of one)
- "Living" and "nonliving" sorting cards
- Two sorting trays or hoops labeled "living" and "nonliving"

## Preparation

- Bring a real plant (or a clear picture) for learners to observe.
- Prepare sorting cards: living (a plant, a dog, a bird, a fish) and nonliving (a
  rock, a spoon, a toy, water), plus a few that can surprise (a seed, a feather).
- Label two trays or hoops "living" and "nonliving."

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Keep it gentle and concrete: the three
clues are *grows and changes*, *needs food or water*, and *breathes air*. A plant is
living even though it does not walk or speak — it grows toward the sun, needs water,
and takes in air. Water, sunlight, and air are nonliving, but living things need them.
Do not raise the topic of death in this lesson; the focus is noticing what is alive and
treating it with care. If a learner brings a living thing to observe (a bug, a leaf), it
is a guest, not a toy — we watch without harming and we never hurt a living thing just
to look at it. If a learner asks whether a seed or an egg is living, honor the wonder:
it can grow and change, and that is a clue it is living — but we can wonder together and
do not need to settle it today. For a learner who is blind or has low vision, sort by
touch, sound, and smell — a leaf is soft and smells green, a rock is hard and still — and
a partner names each thing as it is felt. A non-speaking learner can place each card in
the correct tray or point to show the answer.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Look at the plant and then at a rock or a spoon. One is alive,
   one is not. How can you tell?
2. **Learn the clues (10 min).** A living thing grows and changes. It needs food or
   water. It breathes air. A plant grows toward the sun and needs water; a bird eats
   food and breathes. A nonliving thing — a rock, a spoon, a toy — does not grow,
   does not need food or water, and does not breathe.
3. **Sort the cards (15 min).** Look at each card. Ask the three questions: Does it
   grow and change? Does it need food or water? Does it breathe? Put each card in the
   "living" tray or the "nonliving" tray. If a seed or a feather surprises you, that
   is good — wonder about it out loud.
4. **Give an example (5 min).** Point to one living thing and say, "___ is living
   because it grows and needs water." Point to one nonliving thing and say, "___ is
   nonliving because it does not grow or breathe."
5. **Draw one of each (5 min).** Draw one living thing and one nonliving thing side by
   side.
6. **Close (5 min).** Living things grow, need care, and breathe; nonliving things do
   not. When we know a thing is alive, we treat it gently and with respect. What
   living thing will you look at more kindly now?

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Sort just two things — one living, one nonliving — while touching the
  real plant and the real rock; say the clue together.
- **Blind/low vision:** Sort by touch, sound, and smell with a partner naming each
  thing; feel a soft living leaf and a hard nonliving rock.
- **Deaf/hard of hearing:** Share the three clues (grows, needs, breathes) with sign,
  pictures, and acting; the learner signs or points to "living" or "nonliving" and
  gestures the clue.
- **Non-speaking:** Place each card in its tray or point to show the answer; a partner
  says the clue aloud.
- **Motor differences:** Choose where each thing goes and direct a partner's hands to
  place it; draw one of each by directing a partner's hands while naming the clue.
- **Extension:** Name one living thing that does not move the way an animal does (a
  plant) and explain how you still know it is living.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner tell a living thing from a nonliving
  thing and give an example of each, with a simple reason?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The "one living, one nonliving" drawing.

## Home connection

At home or outside, find one living thing and one nonliving thing. Tell a grown-up
which is which and one clue that told you. Ask them to name one living thing they care
for and what it needs.
