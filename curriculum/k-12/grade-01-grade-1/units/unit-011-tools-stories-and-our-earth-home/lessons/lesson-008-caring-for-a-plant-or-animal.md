# Lesson 8 — Caring for a Plant or Animal

## Summary

Learners choose a plant or animal to help care for, learn what it needs each day —
water, food, and a clean, safe place — and actually do one caring task. They make a
simple daily care plan and learn that it is steady, everyday care — not one grand
gesture — that keeps a living thing well.

## Objectives

- Help care for a plant or animal and say what it needs each day. _(D10.S4.01.02)_

## Connection

A plant cannot water itself, and a pet cannot fill its own dish. Living things need
helpers — and you can be one. Caring for a living thing is not hard to understand: it
needs water, food, and a clean, safe place, **each day**, not just once. When you water
a plant a little every day, or give a pet food and clean water every day, you are doing
what keeps it alive and well.

## Materials

- A plant or animal to help care for
- Care supplies (a cup or watering can, a food dish, a water dish)
- Paper and crayons for a "daily care plan" drawing

## Preparation

- Choose a plant or animal that is safe and easy for learners to help care for: a
  classroom or home plant, a pet, a fish, or a bird feeder.
- Set out the care supplies each task needs.
- Make sure any animal is calm and safe, and that learners wash hands after handling.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Make the caring real, not symbolic —
learners should actually water, feed, or tidy for a living thing, with safe, clean
hands and gentle touch. The core idea is **each day**: steady care is what living things
need, and learners are helpers, not saviors or owners to command. Honor whatever living
thing is at hand — a windowsill plant is every bit as worthy of care as a pet. Never
frame a family without pets or plants as missing something; a wild bird given a dish of
water, or a street tree watered in the dry season, is just as real. For a learner with
motor differences, the choosing and the directing are the care — they may guide a
partner's hands to pour the water or fill the dish while saying what the living thing
needs; adapt the tool (a light cup, a long-spouted can). A learner who is blind or has
low vision can feel the plant's dry or damp soil and the filled dish, and listen for
the living thing's sounds. A non-speaking learner can point to the caring task and to
pictures of water and food to show what the living thing needs each day.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Meet the plant or animal you will help care for. Take one
   gentle, quiet look (or listen, or feel). It is alive, and it needs you.
2. **Learn its daily needs (10 min).** Say what this living thing needs each day: a
   plant needs water and light each day; an animal needs food, clean water, and a
   clean, safe place each day. Point to or touch each need as you name it.
3. **Do one caring task (15 min).** Choose one caring task and do it now — water the
   plant, fill the water dish, put out food, or tidy the safe place. Use gentle
   hands. If your body makes the doing hard, guide a partner's hands while you say
   what to do and why.
4. **Make a daily care plan (10 min).** Draw a simple plan: one caring task for each
   day — water, food, clean water, a clean safe place. Put the days in a row or a
   circle so you can follow it.
5. **Close (5 min).** A living thing needs care each day, not just once. When you
   care for a plant or an animal, you are a helper in its life. What caring task will
   you do first?

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Do one caring task together hand-over-hand and say the need aloud; make
  a two-day plan instead of a week.
- **Motor differences:** Choose the task and direct a partner's hands while saying
  what the living thing needs; adapt the tool (a light cup, a long-spouted watering
  can).
- **Blind/low vision:** Feel the soil before and after watering, or the dish empty
  then full; a partner names each step and the daily need.
- **Deaf/hard of hearing:** Share each caring task with sign, pictures, and acting;
  the learner signs or points to water, food, and clean place to show the daily needs.
- **Extension:** Watch the same plant or animal again tomorrow and tell one thing that
  is the same and one thing that changed, and check off the day's caring task.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Did the learner help do one caring task and say what
  the plant or animal needs each day?
- **Portfolio:** The "daily care plan" drawing, kept as a living record to follow over
  the coming days.

## Home connection

At home, help care for one living thing — a plant, a pet, a tree, or a bird you can
reach with water. Do one caring task with a grown-up and tell them what it needs each
day. Ask if you may help again tomorrow.
