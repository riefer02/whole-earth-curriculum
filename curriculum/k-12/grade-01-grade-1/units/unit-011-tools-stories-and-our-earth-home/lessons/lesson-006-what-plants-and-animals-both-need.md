# Lesson 6 — What Plants and Animals Both Need

## Summary

Learners name what a plant needs and what an animal needs, then discover the needs
they share: both need water and air. They sort need cards into "plant," "animal," and
"both," and learn that all living things — plant, animal, and us — depend on the same
few gifts the Earth gives. Caring for those shared needs becomes the quiet thread.

## Objectives

- Name two things a plant and an animal both need to live, such as water, food, light,
  or air. _(D10.S3.01.02)_

## Connection

A plant and an animal look very different — one is green and still, the other moves and
eats. But look closer: they both need the same few gifts to live. A plant needs water
to drink and air to breathe, and it needs light from the sun to grow. An animal needs
water to drink and air to breathe, and it needs food to eat. Two needs they share are
**water** and **air**. Every living thing, everywhere, depends on these.

## Materials

- A real plant and a picture of an animal (or a real animal to observe)
- "Need" cards (water, air, light, food, shelter; plus decoys like a toy, a hat, a
  song)
- Two large overlapping circles drawn as a "both" diagram

## Preparation

- Bring a real plant and a clear picture of an animal learners know.
- Prepare need cards: water, air, light (sun), food, shelter, and a few decoys (a toy,
  a hat, a song).
- Draw two overlapping circles: one labeled "plants," one labeled "animals," and the
  overlap labeled "both."

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The clean, faithful answer at this age
is that a plant and an animal **both need water and air**. Each also needs something
else — a plant needs light (and it makes its own food from it, a fuller story saved for
later grades); an animal needs food. Keep the distinction soft and true: water and air
are the shared needs; light and food are the special ones. Do not overcomplicate with
"plants make food from sunlight" as a system — at six it is enough to say a plant needs
light to grow and an animal needs food to eat. Frame the Earth as a home that gives all
living things what they need, not as a store to use up. For a learner who is blind or
has low vision, feel the plant's leaves and stem, and if an animal is present, feel its
fur or shell gently; a partner names each need as it is discussed. A non-speaking
learner can place cards in the "both" circle or point to show the shared needs.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Look at the plant and at the animal picture. How are they
   different? Here is a secret: they share some of the same needs.
2. **What does the plant need? (10 min).** Name what the plant needs to live: water
   to drink, air to breathe, and light from the sun to grow. Touch its leaves gently
   and notice how they reach for the light.
3. **What does the animal need? (10 min).** Name what the animal needs: water to
   drink, air to breathe, and food to eat. (Some animals also need a safe place to
   rest.)
4. **Find what both need (10 min).** Look at the two overlapping circles. Place each
   need card: water and air go in the middle, where **both** plants and animals need
   them. Light goes with plants; food goes with animals. Say it: "A plant and an
   animal both need water and air."
5. **Draw the shared needs (5 min).** Draw a plant and an animal side by side, with
   water and air drawn for both of them.
6. **Close (5 min).** Plant or animal, quiet or busy — every living thing needs water
   and air, and each needs something special too. The Earth gives all of us these
   gifts, and we help care for them.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Sort just two cards — water and air — into the "both" circle and say
  the shared-need sentence together.
- **Blind/low vision:** Feel the plant (and an animal, gently, if present) and hear a
  partner name the needs; place water and air in the overlap by touch.
- **Deaf/hard of hearing:** Share the needs with sign, pictures, and acting; the
  learner signs or points to the "both" overlap and to water and air to show the
  shared needs.
- **Non-speaking:** Place cards in the overlap or point to show the shared needs; a
  partner says the sentence aloud.
- **Motor differences:** Choose where each need card belongs and direct a partner's
  hands to place it; draw the shared needs by directing a partner's hands while naming
  water and air.
- **Extension:** Name one thing you need that is the same as what a plant or an animal
  needs (water, air) and one thing that is different (a story, a toy, a song).

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name two things a plant and an animal
  both need (water and air), and place them in the shared circle?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The drawing of a plant and an animal with their shared
  needs.

## Home connection

At home, look at a plant and think of an animal you know. Tell a grown-up the two
things they both need (water and air), and, with their help, give one of them a little
water — the plant, or a pet, or a dish of water left out for birds.
