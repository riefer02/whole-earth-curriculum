# Lesson 1 — Tools at Home and School

## Summary

Learners hunt for tools in the two places they know best — home and school. They name
each tool and the job it helps people do, then sort the tools into "home" and "school."
Along the way they learn that tools live in every place, that a spoon and a phone and a
mortar and pestle are all equally real tools, and that no place's tools are more
important than another's.

## Objectives

- Name a tool people use at home or at school and tell what job it helps them do.
  _(D10.S1.01.01)_

## Connection

Think about your morning. At home you may eat with a spoon, brush with a toothbrush,
or open a door with a handle. Later, at school, you may cut with scissors, write with a
crayon or chalk, or drink from a cup. Every one of those is a tool — a thing people
made to help them do a job. Tools are not hidden away; they are right where you live
and learn.

## Materials

- Picture cards of home tools and school tools
- Two sorting trays or hoops labeled "home" and "school"
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Gather or draw picture cards of home tools (a spoon, a broom, a pot, a basket, a
  water jug) and school tools (scissors, chalk or crayon, a book, a cup, a watering
  can).
- Label two trays or hoops "home" and "school."
- Clear a circle for acting out tool jobs.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). As facilitator, keep the message gentle
and true: tools are wonderful helpers, but they are not magic and they are not better
just because they are new. Honor every tool equally — a mortar and pestle is just as
much a tool as a phone, and a home's cooking pot matters just as much as a school's
chalk. Avoid presenting any one tool as the "real" or "modern" one. Honor the tools of
the learners' own homes, whatever they are, and never rank one home's or one place's
tools above another's. Honor every body: name and sort can be done by voice, sign,
touch, gesture, or a partner's hands, and each way counts equally. Supervise any
handling of tools so everyone stays safe. For a learner who is blind or has low vision,
let them hold and feel each tool and name it by shape and weight; a learner who does not
use a speaking voice can point, gesture, or place the card in the correct tray instead
of saying the answer aloud.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think back to your home and look around this learning space.
   What is a tool? (A thing people make to help them do a job.) Name one tool you
   already see.
2. **Home tools (10 min).** Look at the home-tool cards, or walk to where home tools
   are. For each one, name the tool and its job: "A spoon helps us scoop and eat. A
   broom helps us sweep the floor." If you can hold it, feel its shape and weight.
3. **School tools (10 min).** Now find the school tools. Name each one and its job:
   "Scissors help us cut paper. A book holds words and pictures for us to read."
4. **Sort home or school (10 min).** Look at each tool card. Does it live mostly at
   home or mostly at school? Some tools, like a cup or a spoon, live in both places —
   that is fine. Put each card in the "home" tray or the "school" tray, and say its
   job as you sort.
5. **Draw a tool and its job (5 min).** Draw one tool you found and the job it helps
   people do. Add the word or picture for "home" or "school" beside it.
6. **Close (5 min).** Every place has its own tools, and every tool helps someone do a
   job. No place's tools are more important than another's — a kitchen pot and a
   school chalk are both treasures. What tool did you notice today that you had never
   noticed before?

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Name just two tools and match each to "home" or "school," saying the job
  together aloud.
- **Blind/low vision:** Hold and feel each tool, naming it by its shape, weight, and
  texture; match it to "home" or "school" by touch and by the voice of a partner.
- **Deaf/hard of hearing:** Share tool names and jobs with sign, pictures, and acting;
  the learner signs or points to a tool and to "home" or "school" to answer, and reads
  the facilitator's face and gestures.
- **Non-speaking:** Point to, gesture toward, or place each tool card in its tray to
  show the answer; a partner or facilitator says the job aloud.
- **Motor differences:** Choose the tool and direct a partner's hands to hold and sort
  it while saying or pointing to its job; use big, light cards or real objects placed
  within easy reach.
- **Extension:** Name one tool that lives in both places (a cup, a spoon, a cloth) and
  tell one job it does at home and one it does at school.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name one home tool and one school tool
  and tell the job each helps people do?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The "tool and its job" drawing, kept in the learner's
  portfolio.

## Home connection

At home, go on a tool hunt with a grown-up in one room — the kitchen or the yard. Find
three tools, say each one's job, and tell whether you also have one like it at school.
Ask the grown-up which tool they use the most.
