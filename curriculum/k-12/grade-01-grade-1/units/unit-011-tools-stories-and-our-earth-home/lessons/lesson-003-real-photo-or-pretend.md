# Lesson 3 — Real Photo or Pretend?

## Summary

Learners look at pairs — a real photo and a drawing of the very same thing — and learn
to tell them apart and give one reason. A photo is made by a camera of something that
was really there; a drawing is made by a person's hand, and it may show something real
or something made-up. They also meet a pretend story, and learn that pretend is a
treasure to love, not a trick to fear.

## Objectives

- Tell the difference between a real photo and a drawing or a pretend story, and give
  one reason. _(D10.S2.01.01)_

## Connection

Hold a real leaf in your hand — you can see it, feel it, and smell it. A camera can
take a *photo* of that leaf, a picture made by a machine of something that was really
there. A person can also make a *drawing* of the leaf with a hand and a crayon. Both
are pictures, but they are made in different ways — and that is how we begin to tell
them apart.

## Materials

- Pairs of a real photo and a drawing of the same thing (a cat, a tree, a person)
- A short pretend story to read or tell
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Gather or make two or three photo-and-drawing pairs of the same subject.
- Choose or invent a clearly make-believe story (a talking animal, a flying house).
- Set out paper and crayons.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Keep it warm and wonder-full, never
cynical. A photo is a picture a camera makes of something that was really there; a
drawing is a picture a person makes by hand, and it may be of something real or
something imagined. That is the whole distinction at this age — noticing, not judging.
Make-believe is not "bad" or "a lie"; it is one of the great gifts of being human. If a
learner insists a dragon could be real, honor the wonder: "What a wonderful story! Some
people tell stories about dragons." Then gently return to "a photo is made by a camera
of something really there, and a drawing is made by a person's hand." Do not shame any
media or screen; frame everything as noticing. If a learner names a story their family
holds as true — a sacred story or a tale of their people — honor it and do not call it
make-believe; steer the practice back to clearly fictional examples like talking
animals and flying houses. For a learner who is blind or has low vision, use a real
object and a raised-texture drawing of it, and let them tell which was made by a camera
(a partner describes the photo) and which was made by hand (they feel the texture). A
non-speaking learner can point or gesture to show the answer.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Look at a real photo and a drawing of the same thing side by
   side. They look a little different — can you see how?
2. **Meet a photo (5 min).** A photo is a picture a camera makes. The camera sees
   something that is really there — a real cat, a real tree, a real person — and
   records it.
3. **Meet a drawing (5 min).** A drawing is a picture a person makes by hand, with a
   crayon or pencil or brush. A drawing can show something real (a real cat) or
   something made-up (a cat with wings).
4. **Sort the pairs (10 min).** For each pair, point to the photo and the drawing.
   Give one reason: "This is a photo because a camera made it of the real cat." Or:
   "This is a drawing because a person drew it by hand."
5. **Hear a pretend story (10 min).** Listen to the pretend story about a talking
   animal or a flying house. Is it a photo, a drawing, or a story someone made up?
   How do you know? (It is a story — someone imagined it, and animals do not talk,
   houses do not fly.)
6. **Make one of each (5 min).** Draw a real thing, and then imagine one small change
   that turns it into make-believe — a tree with a door in it. Which part is real and
   which part is pretend?
7. **Close (5 min).** Photos, drawings, and pretend stories are all treasures. We
   notice how each is made — by a camera, by a hand, by an imagination — and we love
   them all.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use just one photo-and-drawing pair and one reason, said together
  aloud, while holding a real object to compare.
- **Blind/low vision:** Compare a real object with a raised-texture drawing; a partner
  describes the photo while the learner feels which picture was made by hand.
- **Deaf/hard of hearing / non-speaking:** Tell the pretend story with sign, pictures,
  or acting; the learner signals "photo," "drawing," or "story" with a sign, a card,
  or a gesture.
- **Motor differences:** Point or gesture to choose the photo or the drawing, and
  direct a partner's hands to draw one of each while the learner says or signals which
  part is real and which is pretend.
- **Extension:** Make up one pretend creature and tell one real thing about it (it has
  fur, it breathes) and one pretend thing (it breathes fire).

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner tell a real photo from a drawing or a
  pretend story and give one reason?

## Home connection

At home, find one real photo and one drawing or painting. Tell a grown-up which is
which and give one reason — then ask them to tell you a pretend story they loved as a
child.
