# Lesson 4 — Who Made It, and for Whom?

## Summary

Learners learn that every picture, poster, and story has a maker — someone who made it
— and an audience — someone it was made for. They look at a real poster and a picture
book to find the maker and the audience, then make their own picture and say who made
it and who it is for. The lesson is about noticing, never about shaming: no message is
"hiding" anything here, it simply has a maker and a friend it was made for.

## Objectives

- Say who made a simple picture, poster, or story and who it was made for.
  _(D10.S2.01.02)_

## Connection

Everything you see that someone made — a sign on a door, a poster on a wall, a picture
in a book, a story told at bedtime — was made by a person and made for a person. The
person who made it is the **maker** (in a book, the **author** writes the words and the
**illustrator** draws the pictures). The person it was made for is the **audience**. A
"Wash your hands" sign was made by someone who cares about health, for everyone who
uses the sink. Today you will learn to ask two gentle questions about any picture or
story: *who made it?* and *who is it for?*

## Materials

- A simple poster or sign to look at together
- A picture book with a visible author and illustrator
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Choose a simple, real poster or sign whose maker and audience are easy to name.
- Pick a picture book whose cover or title page clearly names the author and
  illustrator.
- Set out paper and crayons.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The goal is a first, gentle step toward
media literacy: every message has a maker and an audience. Keep it warm and
matter-of-fact — we are noticing, never accusing. No message is being framed as
"sneaky" or "trying to trick you"; at six, the point is simply that pictures and
stories come from someone and go to someone. Do not shame any media or screen. Honor
the many kinds of makers: an author, an illustrator, a sign-writer, a grandparent who
tells stories, a child who draws. All are makers, and no kind of maker is better than
another. For a learner who is blind or has low vision, read the maker's name aloud and
describe the picture; the learner names the maker and audience from the description. A
non-speaking learner can point to the maker's name and to a picture of the audience (a
child, a grown-up, everyone) to show the answer. When learners make their own picture,
every audience is welcome — a sibling, a pet, a friend, the whole class.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Look at the poster on the wall. Someone made it. Who might
   that be, and who is it for?
2. **Meet the maker and the audience (10 min).** Look at the poster or sign. Say who
   made it (a grown-up, a shopkeeper, a learner, a doctor) and who it is for
   (everyone who uses the sink, people visiting the market, the class). Every
   picture has these two friends: the one who made it and the one it was made for.
3. **Find the maker in a book (10 min).** Look at the picture book. On the cover or
   title page, find the name of the person who wrote the words (the author) and the
   person who drew the pictures (the illustrator). Point to each name and say it.
   Now ask: who was this book made for? (Little children like you, or a baby, or
   everyone who loves stories.)
4. **Make your own (10 min).** Draw a picture — a sign, a card, or a picture for a
   friend. When you finish, say: "I made this, and I made it for ___." You are now
   the maker, and you chose the audience.
5. **Share and ask (5 min).** Show your picture to a partner. Ask them the two
   questions: "Who made it?" and "Who is it for?" Then answer for your own.
6. **Close (5 min).** Every picture and every story comes from a maker and goes to an
   audience. When you look at something someone made, you can wonder: who made this,
   and who is it for?

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Name the maker and audience of one picture together aloud, then draw a
  picture and name both with help.
- **Blind/low vision:** Hear the maker's name read aloud and the picture described;
  name the maker and audience from the description.
- **Deaf/hard of hearing:** Share the maker and audience with sign, pictures, and
  acting; the learner signs or points to the maker's name and to the audience to show
  both.
- **Non-speaking:** Point to the maker's name and to a picture of the audience to show
  who made it and who it is for.
- **Motor differences:** Say or signal the maker and audience while a partner draws;
  or direct a partner's hands to make the picture while the learner chooses who it is
  for.
- **Extension:** Make two versions of one picture — one for a baby and one for a
  grown-up — and tell how you changed it for each audience.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner say who made a simple picture, poster,
  or story and who it was made for?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The learner's own picture, with "made by ___ for ___"
  recorded by the facilitator.

## Home connection

At home, find one thing someone made — a sign, a picture, a book, or a song. Ask a
grown-up (or guess) who made it and who it is for. Then make a small picture for
someone at home and tell them: "I made this for you."
