# Lesson 9 — Learning from a Past Decision

## Summary

Learners practice reflecting on a **past decision** and its **consequences** — separating the
choice from what happened from the reasoning behind it — and turning the lesson into a concrete
**better next choice**. They run the decision loop on a worked example, then on one real, safe
decision of their own, and learn that reflection is what turns experience into wisdom.

## Objectives

- Reflect on a past decision and its consequences to make a better choice next time.
  _(D01.S3.07.01)_

## Connection

Every choice sends a ripple out into the world, and the ripple comes back carrying information.
You lend a tool you needed; you speak sharply and watch a face close; you choose a shortcut and
arrive late or early. Reflection is simply *reading the ripple*: what did I choose, what
happened, and what will I do differently now that I know? A person who reflects on a hundred
small decisions learns more than a person who rushes through a thousand — on a boat, in a
market, or anywhere choices get made.

## Materials

- Decision-loop poster
- Decision-reflection page
- Pencils

## Preparation

- Print or draw the decision-loop poster (four steps in a circle).
- Copy a decision-reflection page for each learner.
- Arrange pairs for peer check.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **reflection is a learnable
loop — decision → consequence → reflection → better next choice — and it feeds forward, not
backward.** The goal is never to shame a past choice but to *extract its lesson* and convert it
into a precise next step. Teach the loop as a worked example before independent practice
(Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006). The *ethics* lens asks what a choice did to others; the
*egalitarianism* lens asks whose needs it counted and whose it left out. Let learners choose
**small, safe, recent** decisions (not their heaviest regrets); never require disclosure of
painful material, and offer the option to use a made-up or role-played decision. Feedback is
built in as a peer check: the partner confirms that the "next time" step actually follows from
what was learned. Distinguish **evidence from values**: that reflection improves later judgment
is supported by learning science (retrieval and spacing deepen it); that a learner's past is
never a verdict — only information — is a value this curriculum holds — name it as one. See
`docs/facilitation.md` and `docs/art-style.md`.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** You have walked the unit from feelings to identity to the labels that
   would narrow it. Now the turn to *purpose*: how do you steer your own life? Try to recall
   one small decision you made this week. (Pause.) Today you learn to learn from it.
2. **Meet the loop (10 min).** Look at the poster: a circle of four steps — **DECISION**
   (what I chose), **CONSEQUENCE** (what happened), **REFLECTION** (what I learned), **BETTER
   NEXT CHOICE** (what I'll do next time). Worked example: *I stayed up late before a big day.
   Consequence: I was tired and grumpy, and I snapped at a friend. Reflection: my sleep shapes
   how I treat people. Next time: I set a wind-down time and honor it.*
3. **Run your own loop (15 min).** On your page, fill the four boxes for one small, safe
   decision of your own. Separate the three things that are easy to blur: the **choice**, what
   **happened**, and the **reasoning** behind the choice. Write your "next time" as a concrete
   action, not a wish ("I will ___," not "I should try to ___").
4. **Peer check (10 min).** Swap pages with a partner. Check each other's loop: does the "next
   time" step actually follow from what was learned? Is it concrete? Offer one improvement, and
   take one.
5. **Close (5 min).** A past decision is never a verdict — it is information. Reflection turns
   that information into a better next choice, and the loop starts again.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide sentence starters for each box ("I chose ___, what happened was ___, I
  learned ___, next time I will ___") and a worked example to copy first.
- **Extension:** Run the loop on a decision that affected *other people*, and add a fifth
  question: whose needs did my choice count, and whose did it leave out?

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner separate the decision from its consequence from
  the reasoning, and produce a concrete "next time" that follows from the lesson?
- **Portfolio (artifact):** The decision-reflection page, kept for the unit's
  decision-reflection note.

## Home connection

Ask someone at home about a choice they once made and what it taught them — and share one small
loop of your own.
