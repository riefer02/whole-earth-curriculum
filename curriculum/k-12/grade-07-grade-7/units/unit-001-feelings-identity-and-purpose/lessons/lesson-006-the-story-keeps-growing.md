# Lesson 6 — The Story Keeps Growing

## Summary

Learners **predict** ways their identity may keep growing, and discover that the future is not
a single fixed endpoint but a set of open **directions** they can help **author**. They sketch
several "possible selves," name the values that tie them together, and connect growth to a
growth mindset that is honest about both effort and uncertainty.

## Objectives

- Predict ways their identity may keep growing. _(D01.S2.07.01)_

## Connection

The person you are at thirteen is not the person you will be at twenty or forty — but you are
not a passenger either. You are the author. You can name the directions you hope to grow
toward: the maker, the caretaker, the leader, the lifelong learner. These are not boxes to be
sorted into; they are branches of a path that stays open. A seed does not know its exact
shape, but it grows toward the light it is given. So do you — in a fishing port, a mountain
farm, or anywhere people dream.

## Materials

- Growing-arrow poster
- Possible-selves page
- Pencils and crayons

## Preparation

- Print or draw the growing-arrow poster (one "me now" arrow branching into several open
  directions).
- Copy a possible-selves page for each learner.
- Arrange a circle for sharing.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **the future self is open,
not fixed — and the learner is its author, not its audience.** This builds directly on Lesson
5's "identity changes" to reach the objective's second half ("predict ways it may keep
growing"). Frame prediction as *direction* (values and skills to grow toward), never as a
single destiny that would pressure or narrow a learner — a learner's possible selves are many,
and none is a verdict. The growth-mindset framing (Dweck, 2006) is real but should be held
honestly: the research shows modest effects, and effort alone is not a guarantee, so do not
teach "you can be anything if you just try" as a promise. Say instead: **your self can keep
growing, and what you practice shapes the direction of that growth.** Model the growing-arrow
as a worked example (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006). Distinguish **evidence from values**:
that identity can keep developing is observable; that every learner's open future deserves to
be honored equally is a value this curriculum holds — name it as one. See `docs/facilitation.md`
and `docs/art-style.md`.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Last time you mapped how you have already changed. Now look the other
   way: who might you become next? (Pause.) The future is not one fixed box.
2. **Meet the growing arrow (10 min).** Look at the poster: an arrow starts at **"me now"** and
   branches into several **possible selves** — a maker, a caretaker, a leader, a learner. Worked
   example: *I am becoming a person who writes things that help people think — and also a
   person who can fix a machine, and a person who keeps learning new languages. These are
   directions, not a single destiny.*
3. **Sketch your branches (15 min).** On your page, write "me now" on the left. Then draw three
   or four branches — possible selves you can imagine growing toward. They can be practical
   (a mechanic, a nurse, a farmer) or qualities (kinder, braver, more curious). There is no
   wrong branch.
4. **Find the through-line (10 min).** Look at your branches. What **value** ties them together
   — helping others? making things? understanding the world? Write it under the arrow. This is
   the direction that stays the same no matter which branch grows.
5. **Share and close (10 min).** Share one branch and the value under it with a partner. Close
   by remembering: your story is not finished, and you hold the pen.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide branch starters ("a person who helps ___, a person who makes ___, a
  person who learns ___") and a list of values to choose from.
- **Extension:** Write a short "next chapter" naming one possible self, a first small step
  toward it, and the value that would guide the choices along the way.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name at least one way their identity may keep
  growing, as a direction rather than a fixed fate?
- **Portfolio (artifact):** The possible-selves page, kept for the unit's "my changing
  identity" reflection.

## Home connection

Ask someone at home what they once thought they would become and what they actually became —
and notice together that the story stayed open.

## Resources

- On identity as a developing, lifelong process: Erikson, *Childhood and Society* (1950) —
  cited for the framing that identity keeps forming across the life.
- On the growth-mindset idea that the self can keep developing (held with the research's own
  cautions about modest effects): Dweck, *Mindset: The New Psychology of Success* (2006).
- On modeling a new skill before independent practice: Kirschner, Sweller & Clark (2006),
  <https://doi.org/10.1207/s15326985ep4102_1>
