# Lesson 1 — Holding Two Feelings at Once

## Summary

Learners discover that two opposite feelings can be true **at the same time** — happy AND
sad, excited AND nervous — and begin to name that mix in precise language. They meet the
word **bittersweet**, practice a **both/and** sentence, and find a mixed-feeling moment in
their own lives — without judging either side as the "wrong" one.

## Objectives

- Describe mixed or conflicting feelings — such as happy and sad at once — in precise
  language. _(D01.S1.07.01)_

## Connection

You leave a place you love to begin something new: the last day of the season, a friend
moving to another town, a grandmother's house sold, the final game of a team you will never
play on again. Your chest holds two things at once — joy at what is beginning and grief at
what is ending. The word "and" does the honest work here: you are not *either* happy *or*
sad. You are happy **and** sad, in the same breath, in the same body. Whether you live in a
high-rise city or a mountain village, this is a feeling every person knows.

## Materials

- Both/and poster
- Both/and page
- Pencils and crayons

## Preparation

- Print or draw the both/and poster: one scene shown twice, with a happy face beside a sad
  face joined by a large "AND."
- Copy a both/and page for each learner.
- Arrange a circle for discussion.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land is that **two opposite
feelings can be true at once, and "and" is the honest word for it** — not that a mixed
feeling is a problem to solve. Normalize it without pathologizing: holding two feelings is
a sign of growing emotional complexity, and it appears in every culture, though the words
for it differ (English's "bittersweet," the idea of "mono no aware" — a gentle sadness at
the passing of things — in Japanese tradition, and many others). Keep cultures side by side,
never ranked. Model one both/and sentence as a worked example before learners try their own
(Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006). Invite only small, safe moments; never push a learner to
reveal a deep grief. See `docs/facilitation.md` and `docs/art-style.md`.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Last year you noticed that what a person *feels* and what they *show*
   can be different. Today you meet something new: a feeling that is **two feelings at
   once**. Has it ever happened to you — happy and sad in the same moment? (Pause and
   remember.)
2. **Meet the both/and (10 min).** Look at the poster. Same scene, two sides: on one side a
   smile, on the other a tear — joined by a big **AND**. Not "or." The word for this mix is
   **bittersweet**. Worked example: *I feel **happy** that my team won the final match, AND
   **sad** that it is the last time we will all play together.* Say it with me.
3. **Name your own mix (15 min).** Think of one small, safe moment this month when you held
   two feelings at once. On your page, write or draw one feeling on each side of the **AND**,
   and finish one sentence: *I feel ___ AND ___, because ___.* Share with a partner if you
   are comfortable.
4. **The body holds both (10 min).** Notice where each feeling lives in your body — a warm
   smile **and** a tight throat, a light chest **and** heavy shoulders. Two feelings, one
   body, both real. Write one word for where each one sits.
5. **Close (5 min).** Feeling two things at once is not confusion and it is not weakness. It
   is the honest size of being human. Tomorrow you will find even more precise words for it.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a word bank of feeling pairs (happy/sad, excited/nervous, proud/lonely)
  and the sentence starter "I feel ___ AND ___ because ___."
- **Extension:** Write two mixed-feeling moments and notice what is the same and what is
  different about how the two feelings sit together.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name two opposite feelings from the same
  moment and join them with "and" rather than "or"?
- **Self-check:** The learner rereads their sentence and checks that it names **two** feelings
  from **one** moment, and that the "because" makes sense.

## Home connection

Ask someone at home about a time they felt two opposite things at once — happy and sad in the
same breath — and share one small example of your own if you like.
