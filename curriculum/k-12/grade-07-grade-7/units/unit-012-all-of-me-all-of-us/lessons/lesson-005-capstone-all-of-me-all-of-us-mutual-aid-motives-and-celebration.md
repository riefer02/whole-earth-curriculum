# Lesson 5 — Capstone: All of Me, All of Us — Mutual Aid, Motives, and Celebration

## Summary

The unit and year-end capstone. Learners **retrieve** mutual aid and the three motives from Unit 3,
**plan or join** a small mutual-aid action to carry into Grade 8, **reflect honestly on their
motives**, and close by **celebrating** each whole person, the caring community they have built, and
the living Earth — looking ahead to Grade 8.

## Objectives

- Organize or join a mutual-aid action in their school or community. _(D02.S4.07.01)_
- Reflect on the motives for helping, such as care, duty, or recognition. _(D02.S4.07.02)_

## Connection

In Kenya, people speak of **harambee** — "all pull together" — when a community joins to build a
school or help a family. In Indonesia, **gotong royong** names neighbors sharing the burden of building
and gathering. In the Andes, **mink'a** (or *minga*) is a long-standing practice of farming the terraces
together so no one harvests alone. In rural North America, a **barn raising** gathered neighbors to lift
a family's barn in a day, the favor returning later. Different names, one thing: **mutual aid** —
helping where everyone both gives and receives, no one above and no one below. And behind every act of
help sits a motive — **care**, **duty**, **recognition**, often more than one at once. Today you carry
that practice forward and celebrate all of it.

## Materials

- All-of-me-all-of-us poster
- Carry-forward aid page
- Celebration items
- The completed week's portfolio pages

## Preparation

- Print or draw the all-of-me-all-of-us poster.
- Copy a carry-forward aid page for each learner.
- Gather the week's portfolio pages (then/now/next, decisions, routine, belonging) to display.
- Retrieval: from Unit 3, recall mutual aid and the three motives — care, duty, recognition. This
  lesson carries them forward and celebrates.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **mutual aid is helping among equals
— everyone gives and receives — and carrying it forward with honest motives (care, duty, recognition)
is how "all of me" meets "all of us"; the year ends in celebration of each whole person, the caring
community, and the living Earth, not in ranking.** This deepens Unit 3, Lesson 10 (which planned a
mutual-aid action) by pointing it into Grade 8 and joining it to the unit's identity work. Present
mutual-aid traditions with respect as living practices, never as curiosities or a ranking (harambee,
gotong royong, mink'a/minga, barn raising — S-164; the term's canonical source is Kropotkin, S-163).
Honor the egalitarian lens: mutual aid is solidarity among equals, not charity from above. Keep motives
non-judged — all three are human and common, and naming them honestly is the skill (inquiry over
creed). The ethics lens asks what is right toward others; the global lens holds many peoples' ways of
helping side by side; the technology lens asks how tools and messages can help or hinder mutual aid;
the environment lens grounds the action in care for the land people share. The action must be small,
safe, and genuinely doable, with every learner offered a real part. Model the planner once as a worked
example (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006, S-011). This is a **summative** moment, but mastery is
personal, never ranked — celebrate growth, never compare learners. Assess by what each learner can now
*explain, choose, and do*, not by a score.

## Procedure

1. **Gather and recall the unit (10 min).** With a partner, retrieve the whole week: the three panels
   of your identity (then, now, next); the decision pattern you found; the routine you will carry; and
   the loss and gain in a community change. Name them without looking — this is the year's harvest.
2. **Recall mutual aid and motives (8 min).** Look at the poster. Mutual aid: everyone gives and
   receives, no one only the helper and no one only the helped. Three motives: **care** (I help because
   I feel for you), **duty** (I help because it is right and owed), **recognition** (I help to be seen
   as good). All three are human.
3. **Plan your carry-forward aid (20 min).** Choose one small mutual-aid action you could join or start
   in Grade 8 — a study circle, a shared cleanup, a "help board," a thank-you to someone who serves the
   community, a letter for a fair cause. On your page, fill the **need**, the **people**, the **roles**
   (what each person gives and receives), and the **steps**. Then write one honest line for each motive:
   *My care says ___. My duty says ___. My want of recognition says ___.* There is no wrong answer; name
   which motive is strongest in you today.
4. **Run it through the lenses (7 min).** Check your action: Is it right (ethics)? Who benefits, and who
   is left out (egalitarianism)? How would another people or place do it (global)? What does it do to
   people and the Earth (technology & environment)? Adjust one line.
5. **Celebrate and look ahead (10 min).** Display the week's pages. In a circle, each person shares one
   thing they are proud of — one way they have grown. Remember: **you are each a whole person, and
   together a caring whole. Carry all of it — the self, the routine, the community, the care — into
   Grade 8, and into the living world.**

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a pre-chosen action and a roles word bank; ask the learner to fill only the
  "need" and their own role, plus one motives line.
- **Extension:** Distinguish mutual aid from charity in one or two sentences (mutual aid is among
  equals, everyone gives and receives; charity flows one way), and note why the difference matters.

## Assessment

- **Summative (performance + portfolio):** The completed carry-forward aid page — a real part in a
  mutual-aid action plus an honest motives note — is the unit's capstone artifact, kept in the
  year-end portfolio.
- **Self-check:** The learner confirms their motives note names care, duty, and recognition honestly,
  and marks one thing from the year they feel confident about and one they want to keep growing —
  "not yet" is information, never a verdict.

## Home connection

Share your carry-forward aid plan with someone at home and invite them to add one action of their own —
one way your household or street can pull together next year.
