# Lesson 3 — The Routine I'll Carry Forward

## Summary

Learners **retrieve** the routine wheel from Unit 1 and **deepen** it into a commitment to **carry
forward**: they name the goal of who they are becoming, draft the study–sleep–movement routine that
serves it, add an **if-then plan** for the hard moment, and set a way to **track and adjust** it. The
result becomes the second half of the year-end decision-and-routine note.

## Objectives

- Build and follow a personal routine (study, sleep, movement) that supports their own goals.
  _(D01.S3.07.02)_

## Connection

Big things are built of small, repeated things — a house of bricks, a field of rows, a skill of
practice returned to day after day. A routine is how a goal stops being a wish and starts being
*days*. Study grows your mind; sleep lets the body repair and the brain keep what you learned; movement
keeps the whole system clear. Choose the person you are becoming, then build the day that feeds it —
in a city apartment or a village house, the pattern is the same.

## Materials

- Carry-forward wheel poster
- My carry-forward page
- Pencils and crayons

## Preparation

- Print or draw the carry-forward wheel poster (study, sleep, movement, meals, rest, with an arrow
  into "next year").
- Copy a my carry-forward page for each learner.
- Prepare a short fact summary on sleep and movement amounts (see Resources).
- Retrieval: from Unit 1, recall the routine wheel and the three evidence-backed blocks — study
  (spaced and retrieved), sleep (about 8–10 hours for teens), and daily movement.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **a routine is how a goal becomes days
— and the routine you carry into Grade 8 should serve the person you are becoming, not just a checklist.**
This deepens Unit 1, Lesson 10 (which built the wheel); here the wheel is pointed **forward** and tied
to the "who I am becoming" goal from Lesson 1. Rest it on three evidence-backed blocks: study (with
breaks, spaced and retrieved), sleep (about 8–10 hours for teens — AASM, S-050), and daily movement
(WHO, S-069), plus rest and meals. Use if-then planning for the hard moments (Duckworth, Gendler & Gross,
S-097) and keep amounts flexible — every family, shift, and body differs; for a learner with limited mobility, "movement" means whatever their body does (stretching, range-of-motion, physio, breathing), and a routine that cannot
bend will break. The egalitarian lens is explicit: a learner with chores, care duties, or long travel
has different hours than one without, so teach the routine as "within your real day," never as a
middle-class default schedule. The environment lens: sleep and movement follow daylight and season;
many people's days follow the sun and the land, not a clock. The technology lens: name screens as one
real pressure on sleep and study, and plan for it rather than ignore it. Model one routine as a worked
example (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006, S-011). Distinguish **evidence from values**: that sleep,
movement, and spaced study support health and learning is documented; that every learner's goals deserve
equal honor is a value this curriculum holds — name it as one.

## Procedure

1. **Gather and recall (8 min).** In Unit 1 you built a routine wheel. Quick retrieval: name the three
   blocks that matter most — **study**, **sleep**, **movement** — and the amounts that fit your age.
   Today you point that wheel into next year.
2. **Meet the carry-forward wheel (10 min).** Look at the poster: the familiar wheel, with an arrow
   leaving it into a gate labeled **"Grade 8 — next year."** Worked example: *My goal is to keep
   growing as a musician and a student. My routine: 20 minutes of practice after school, screens off an
   hour before sleep, lights out by ten, a walk or game in the afternoon.* Every block serves the
   person I am becoming.
3. **Draft your carry-forward routine (15 min).** On your page, write the goal you named yesterday (or
   a new one). Then draft your real day — study, sleep, movement, meals, rest — with amounts that fit
   *your* life. Add one **if-then plan** for the hard moment: *If I feel like skipping practice, then I
   will do just five minutes.* And one way to **track** it next week (a tick chart, a note, a check-in).
4. **Check and adjust (7 min).** Swap with a partner. Does every block serve the goal? Is the if-then
   concrete? Plan *how you will know* next week what to keep and what to adjust — a routine is a living
   thing, not a cage.
5. **Close (10 min).** You have now named who you are becoming, the pattern in your choices, and the
   routine that carries your goal into next year. Small, repeated actions, day after day, add up to the
   life you want. **Start next week — or start today.**

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a partly filled carry-forward page and a menu of goals; write one if-then plan
  together as a class first.
- **Extension:** Connect each block to *why* it serves your specific "who I am becoming" goal, and plan
  one adjustment you would make if a block fails next week.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner draft a routine with study, sleep, and movement that
  serves a named goal, plus an if-then plan and a way to track and adjust?
- **Portfolio (artifact):** The my carry-forward page, kept as the second half of the unit's
  decision-and-routine note.

## Home connection

Share your carry-forward routine with someone at home and invite them to help you track it next week —
then adjust it together, as a living plan rather than a grade.
