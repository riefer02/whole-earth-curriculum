# Lesson 1 — My Identity: Then, Now, Next

## Summary

Learners open the year-end unit by **retrieving** the "me then / me now" identity map they built in
Unit 1 and adding a **third panel — "me next"** — to predict how their identity may keep growing.
They reflect on who they were, who they are, and who they are becoming, and keep the result as the
"who I am becoming" opening page of the year-end portfolio.

## Objectives

- Reflect on how their identity has changed over time and predict ways it may keep growing.
  _(D01.S2.07.01)_

## Connection

Think of yourself at eight, at ten, at twelve. The games, the fears, the dream jobs — some of it is
gone, some of it grew, and some of it is still quietly at your center. A river is still a river even
though the water is always new; a tree is still the seed it was, grown. Your identity is like that:
continuous *and* changing. Today you add one more step the younger you could not see yet — the one
that comes **next**.

## Materials

- Then/now/next poster
- Then/now/next page
- Pencils and crayons

## Preparation

- Print or draw the then/now/next poster (three panels joined by arrows).
- Copy a then/now/next page for each learner.
- Retrieval: from Unit 1, recall the **"me then / me now"** map and its four parts — interests,
  abilities, values, relationships. This lesson adds the third panel.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **identity is a continuous, changing
story, and because it changed before, we can predict (with honesty, not certainty) how it may keep
growing.** This closes the year by *deepening* Unit 1, Lesson 5 (which asked "how has my identity
changed?"); here learners complete the objective by looking **forward**. Identity as a lifelong,
re-forming process is developmentally real (Erikson, S-155); frame "next" as a *prediction with
reasons*, not a fortune-telling or a demand to commit. The ethics lens: who I am becoming raises the
question "who do I want to become, and what is right for others in that?" The egalitarian lens:
growth looks different in every life — no one's "next" is ranked against another's, and a learner
with fewer choices still authors their own story. The global lens: people everywhere understand the
self as a story that grows, in many different idioms (a river, a tree, a path, a weaving); honor many
ways of saying it rather than one. The technology lens: devices and media now shape the self — invite
learners to notice whether their "next" is theirs or borrowed from a screen. Retrieval here is
deliberate spaced recall (Dunlosky et al., 2013, S-039); model the three panels once as a worked
example (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006, S-011). Distinguish **evidence from values**: that identity
changes is observable; that every person's growth deserves equal honor is a value this curriculum
holds — name it as one. Keep identity work low-stakes and voluntary; it can brush against sensitive
material (family change, migration, loss), so let learners choose safe examples and never require
disclosure.

## Procedure

1. **Gather and recall (10 min).** Think back across the whole year — and all the way back to Unit 1.
   You once mapped "me then" and "me now." Quick retrieval, no notes: name one way your **interests**
   changed, one way your **abilities** grew, one **value** that deepened, and one **relationship** that
   shifted. Check against a partner.
2. **Meet the third panel (10 min).** Look at the poster: **"me then"**, **"me now"**, and now **"me
   next."** Worked example: *Then, I was shy and loved only drawing. Now, I speak up in class and play
   a sport — and I still draw. Next: my drawing may grow into design or animation, and I may learn to
   lead a small team.* Notice the "next" follows from evidence in the "then" and "now" — it is a
   prediction with reasons, not a guess.
3. **Map your own three panels (15 min).** On your page, write or draw three columns — then, now,
   next — for the four parts (interests, abilities, values, relationships), plus any part that matters
   to you (place, language, faith, body). For your "next" column, write what you *predict* may grow,
   and add one word about **why you think so**.
4. **Name your continuity (5 min).** Under all three columns, write the one thing that has stayed the
   same and likely will keep staying — a value, a love, a habit. Growth and staying are both true.
5. **Share and close (10 min).** Share one "next" prediction with a partner, and let them check: does
   it follow from something real about you? Close by remembering: **you are not the person you were,
   and that is the point of growing — and you are the author of the next page.**

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a partly filled then/now/next page, a word bank (interests, abilities, values,
  relationships, changed, stayed, next), and sentence starters ("Next, I may ___ because I now ___").
- **Extension:** Write one paragraph connecting a past change to a predicted future change, naming the
  evidence that links them — a small "story so far, and where it points."

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name at least one way their identity has changed, one
  thing that has stayed, and one future change with a reason that follows from the evidence?
- **Portfolio (artifact):** The then/now/next page, kept as the "who I am becoming" opening piece of
  the year-end portfolio.

## Home connection

Ask an older family member how they have changed since they were your age *and* what they think they
may still become. Notice that grown-ups are still writing their next page too.
