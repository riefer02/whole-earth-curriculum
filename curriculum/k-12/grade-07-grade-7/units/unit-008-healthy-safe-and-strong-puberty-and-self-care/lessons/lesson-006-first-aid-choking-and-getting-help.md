# Lesson 6 — First Aid: Choking & Getting Adult Help

## Summary

Learners explain the basic response to **choking** — recognize the sign, call for
emergency help and get an adult, and give back blows and abdominal thrusts only if
trained — and practice **how to call for help**, saying name, location, and what
happened. Help-seeking is framed as part of first aid, never a failure.

## Objectives

- Explain basic first aid for choking and when to seek adult or medical help.
  _(D03.S4.07.01)_

## Connection

Choking is different from a cut or a burn: a person who is truly choking cannot breathe,
talk, or cough, and every second matters. The single most important thing is to **get
help fast** — call the emergency number and get an adult — while doing what you are
trained to do. Knowing how to call for help is itself a first-aid skill, and it is one
you can practice until it feels calm and automatic.

## Materials

- Choking-and-calling-for-help diagram
- Emergency scenario cards

## Preparation

- Print or draw the choking-and-calling-for-help diagram.
- Copy the scenario cards.
- Confirm and share the correct emergency number(s) for your region.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Teach choking response **safely and
factually**, and lead with the rule: **call for emergency help and get an adult
immediately.** The facts to land: if someone is choking and cannot breathe, talk, or
cough — the "universal sign" is hands clutching the throat — call the emergency number
and get an adult at once; back blows and abdominal thrusts can help but must be learned
from a certified instructor, and learners at this age should be taught to seek help and,
if trained, assist (IFRC Global First Aid Reference Centre). Do **not** have learners
practice thrusts on each other — use a manikin or instructor demonstration only. Teach
the emergency call: say your name, where you are, and what happened, and stay on the line
until told to hang up. Emergency numbers differ by country (112, 999, 911, and local
numbers) — teach the correct one for the region. Frame getting help as skill and courage,
never as weakness. See `docs/facilitation.md`.

## Procedure

1. **Gather and recall (5 min).** Recall last lesson: what are the first steps for
   bleeding and for burns? Today: choking — and the skill of calling for help.
2. **Recognize the sign (10 min).** Look at the diagram. A person who is choking cannot
   breathe, talk, or cough, and often clutches their throat — the **universal sign**. If
   you see it, act: **call for emergency help and get an adult now.**
3. **Assist if trained (10 min).** **Back blows** (firm hits between the shoulder blades)
   and **abdominal thrusts** can clear the airway — but they are skills to learn from a
   certified instructor, not to improvise. Say the steps in order, and remember: calling
   for help comes first.
4. **Practice the emergency call (15 min).** Role-play with a partner. When you call, say
   three things: **your name, where you are, what happened** — and **stay on the line**
   until you are told to hang up. Take turns; make it calm and clear.
5. **Close (5 min).** First aid is not about being a hero — it is about acting calmly,
   getting help fast, and doing what you are trained to do. You now know the plan for
   choking and how to make the call.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use the frame "If someone is choking, I first ___, then I say ___." Focus
  on calling for help and saying the three things.
- **Extension:** Explain why calling for help comes first, and why back blows and
  abdominal thrusts should be learned from a certified instructor rather than improvised.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner state the choking response (recognize, call
  for help, assist if trained) and recite the three things to say when calling for help?
- **Portfolio:** A first-aid note (choking steps + the emergency-call script + local
  emergency number), kept as a portfolio artifact.

## Home connection

If it feels safe, learn and write down your local emergency number with a trusted adult,
and practice the three things to say. Being ready is a gift to the people around you.
