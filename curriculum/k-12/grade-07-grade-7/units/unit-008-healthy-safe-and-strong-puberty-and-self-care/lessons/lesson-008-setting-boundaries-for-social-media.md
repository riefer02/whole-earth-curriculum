# Lesson 8 — My Social Media Boundaries: A Plan for Healthy Use

## Summary

Learners set **personal boundaries** for healthy social-media use across four areas —
time, content, notifications, and people — and write a plan they can keep. Boundaries are
framed as self-care and choice, never as punishment or shame, and every plan is private.

## Objectives

- Set personal boundaries for healthy social-media use. _(D03.S4.07.02)_

## Connection

Last lesson you saw what social media can lift and what it can weigh down. Now you do the
part that turns awareness into action: you set **boundaries**. A boundary is not a rule
someone else forces on you — it is a promise you make to yourself, because you are worth
taking care of. Like closing a door to sleep, or turning a page down to rest your eyes, a
boundary protects the things that keep you well.

## Materials

- Social-media boundary wheel
- My-boundary-plan page

## Preparation

- Print or draw the social-media boundary wheel.
- Copy a "My-boundary-plan page" for each learner.
- Prepare to keep all personal plans private; share only general examples.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The goal is a **private, personal plan**,
not a set of class rules. The four areas: **time** (limits on when and how long), **content**
(curating what and who you follow), **notifications** (turning off what pulls you back),
and **people** (choosing who you connect with). The facts to land: healthy digital habits —
including protecting sleep and curbing mindless use — support adolescent mental health
(WHO adolescent mental health; UNESCO media and information literacy). The values held
*as values*: boundaries are self-care and choice, never punishment; there is no "correct"
amount of social media, only what protects each learner's wellbeing. Frame boundaries as
a promise to oneself, revisit them without judgment, and remind learners that a trusted
adult can help them keep a boundary. Never require a learner to disclose their plan or
their habits; keep plans private. See `docs/facilitation.md`.

## Procedure

1. **Gather and recall (5 min).** Recall last lesson: what can social media lift, and what
   can it weigh down? Today you turn that into a plan you choose for yourself.
2. **Meet the boundary wheel (10 min).** Look at the wheel. A healthy plan touches four
   areas: **time** (limits on when and how long), **content** (curate what and who you
   follow), **notifications** (turn off what pulls you back), and **people** (choose who
   you connect with). The center says it all: *my boundaries, my choice*.
3. **Write your plan (15 min).** On your page, write one boundary for each of the four
   areas — small, doable, and *yours*. Examples (choose your own): "I won't scroll after
   dinner," "I'll mute accounts that make me feel less-than," "I'll turn off non-essential
   notifications," "I'll only share with people I trust." This is private — you do not
   have to share it.
4. **Add a check-in (10 min).** Pick one way you will notice whether your boundary is
   helping — a moment each week to ask, *"how do I feel after using it?"* Write one
   trusted adult you could talk to if it ever feels hard to keep your boundary.
5. **Close (5 min).** A boundary is not a cage; it is a fence around a garden. It keeps
   the good in and the harm out — and it is yours to set, change, and keep.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use the frame "My boundary for ___ is ___." and write one boundary for one
  or two areas rather than all four.
- **Extension:** Add a "check-in" step to the plan — how and when you will notice whether
  the boundary is helping — and explain why check-ins make a boundary more likely to last.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name the four areas of a boundary plan and
  write one personal boundary in at least two areas, framed as self-care?
- **Portfolio:** The private boundary plan (one boundary per area + one check-in + one
  trusted adult), kept as a portfolio artifact.

## Home connection

If it feels safe, share one boundary from your plan with a trusted adult — you do not need
to share the whole plan. A boundary is easier to keep when someone you trust knows about
it.
