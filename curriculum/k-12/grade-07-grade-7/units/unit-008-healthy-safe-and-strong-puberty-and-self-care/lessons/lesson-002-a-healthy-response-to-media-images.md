# Lesson 2 — A Healthy Response: Reading Media Without Losing Myself

## Summary

Learners build a **healthy response** to media images — a pause-and-question habit, kind
self-talk, curating what they follow, and talking to a trusted adult — so that an edited
image no longer has power over how they feel about their own real body. No learner is
ever asked to judge or disclose their own body.

## Objectives

- Describe a healthy response to media images that could shape how a person feels about
  their body. _(D03.S1.07.02)_

## Connection

Last lesson you saw that media images are made — chosen, edited, and repeated. The
question now is what to *do* with that. When an image makes you feel smaller or less-than,
you do not have to simply feel it and carry on. You can **pause**, ask three questions,
and answer back. This is a healthy response: it is a skill, like any other, and it puts
you back in charge of your own feelings about your own body.

## Materials

- Pause-and-question diagram
- Healthy-response cards

## Preparation

- Print or draw the pause-and-question diagram.
- Copy and cut the healthy-response cards.
- Prepare to keep self-talk practice private; never require a learner to share feelings
  about their body.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The goal is a *usable skill*, not a lecture
on self-esteem. The facts to land: a person's body image — how they feel about their body —
is shaped in part by the images around them, and a person can respond in ways that protect
their wellbeing (UNESCO media and information literacy; WHO adolescent health). The
healthy responses to teach: **pause and question** (is it edited? who chose it? what is it
selling?), **kind self-talk** (speak to yourself as you would to a friend), **curating**
(choose what and who you follow), and **talking to a trusted adult** when it feels heavy.
These are framed as self-care, never as blame, and never as pressure to "love" a body. Hold
body neutrality as a valid option: it is enough to respect your body and treat it well
without feeling positive about it every day. Never comment on any learner's body, never
compare learners, and keep all practice general. See `docs/facilitation.md`.

## Procedure

1. **Gather and recall (5 min).** What did you learn last time? (Images are chosen,
   filtered, retouched, repeated.) Today you learn what to *do* when an image gets under
   your skin.
2. **Meet the pause-and-question (10 min).** Look at the diagram. When an image makes you
   feel less-than, **pause**. Then ask: **Is this edited? Who chose this? What is it
   selling?** Saying the questions out loud gives you a moment between the image and the
   feeling.
3. **Build your healthy responses (15 min).** Sort the response cards and practice each:
   pause and question; **speak to yourself like a friend** ("my body is allowed to look
   like my body"); **curate** what you follow (you can choose, mute, or unfollow accounts
   that make you feel worse); and **talk to a trusted adult** if it ever feels too heavy.
   Choose the one that fits you best and say why.
4. **Practice your answer (10 min).** Pick one image you often see. Write or say a
   healthy reply to it using the three questions and one kind sentence. Example: *"This
   photo is edited, and it is selling a product — not telling me what my body should look
   like."*
5. **Close (5 min).** You cannot control every image you see, but you can control your
   response to it. Pausing is power.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use the frame "When I see a hard image, I will pause, ask ___, and say
  ___." Practice one question and one kind sentence.
- **Extension:** Write a short "media response" that names the tactic an image uses
  (edited, chosen, repeated) and offers a healthy reply — then share it with a partner.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name the pause-and-question steps and
  describe at least one healthy response (pause, self-talk, curating, or asking an adult)?
- **Portfolio:** A written healthy-response note (one image + the three questions + one
  kind sentence), kept as a portfolio artifact.

## Home connection

If it feels safe, tell a trusted adult about the pause-and-question habit. You might even
practice it together on an image you both see. You do not need to share anything personal
about your body.
