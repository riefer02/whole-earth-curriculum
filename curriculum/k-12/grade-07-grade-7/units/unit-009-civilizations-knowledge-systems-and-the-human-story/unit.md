# Unit 9 — Civilizations, Knowledge Systems & the Human Story

## Overview

This unit steps into the wider human story with the tools of the historian, building on
Grade 6's primary-and-secondary sources, power, and movements for rights. Learners
explain why historians divide time into periods and compare two different calendars or
ways of counting years used by different peoples, and analyze the multiple causes and
consequences of one major change, such as the spread of an invention or a large
migration. They analyze a scientific or technological achievement from a non-European
civilization — mathematics, astronomy, medicine, engineering — and its effect on the
world, and compare how two different knowledge systems, such as indigenous ecological
knowledge and formal science, understand and care for the natural world. They compare
voluntary and forced migration and analyze their causes and effects, explain how
geography — rivers, oceans, deserts, mountains — helped connect or separate peoples and
shaped the spread of ideas and goods, analyze forms of resistance — protest, boycott,
refusal, art, storytelling — and how people choose among them, and explain how
colonization affected colonized peoples, including loss of land, culture, and
self-rule, with care and accuracy. History is told as a global story from many
perspectives, never one people's triumph.

## Essential questions

- Why do historians divide time into periods, and how do different peoples count the years?
- What are the many causes and consequences of great changes, and what has non-European achievement given the world?
- How do knowledge systems, migration, geography, resistance, and colonization shape the human story?
