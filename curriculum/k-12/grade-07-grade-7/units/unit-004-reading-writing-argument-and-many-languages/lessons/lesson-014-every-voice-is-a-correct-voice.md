# Lesson 14 — Every Voice Is a Correct Voice: Dialect and Accent

## Summary

Learners recognize **dialect** and **accent** as natural variation in every language, and
challenge the idea that one "standard" way of speaking is the only correct one. They name
varieties of several world languages with respect, and connect the idea to the unit's
closing theme: multilingualism is wealth, and every voice carries dignity.

## Objectives

- Recognize dialect and accent as natural variation, and challenge the idea that one way
  of speaking is correct. _(D04.S4.07.02)_
- Connect respect for every way of speaking to the work of translation and interpretation
  across languages. _(D04.S4.07.01)_

## Connection

There is no language on Earth spoken exactly the same way everywhere. Arabic sounds one
way in Casablanca, another in Cairo, another in Beirut — and each is fully, correctly
Arabic. Spanish in Mexico City and in Buenos Aires differ, and both are right. The idea
that one version is the "proper" one is a story people tell — usually about *power*, not
about language. Every way of speaking is a correct way of speaking, for the people who
speak it.

## Materials

- Many voices map
- One language, many ways cards
- Voice-rights poster

## Preparation

- Make or print the many-voices map and the voice-rights poster.
- Copy the "one language, many ways" cards for each group.
- Reflect on the class's own languages and varieties so the discussion can begin at home.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land is that **dialect and
accent are natural, rule-governed variation — every variety is "correct" for its speakers
— and the "one correct way" story is about prestige and power, not language itself**
(Everett, 2012). Model respect first: share your own way of speaking before asking
learners to share theirs. Guard against ranking — no "better" or "worse," ever (see
`docs/facilitation.md`). The four lenses: *egalitarianism* (whose way gets called
"standard" is a question of power, not merit); *ethics* (mocking an accent is harm, not
humor); *global* (every language has varieties — name several respectfully);
*social-emotional* (a learner's accent may be a tender part of identity — handle with
care). This closes the unit: evaluation, writing, and now honoring every voice.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Recall the unit: you learned to evaluate reasoning, build
   presentations, judge sources, write precisely, and bridge languages. Today's question:
   is there one "correct" way to speak a language?
2. **Meet variation (10 min).** Look at the many-voices map. A **dialect** is a regional
   or social variety of a language — its own words and grammar. An **accent** is a way of
   pronouncing. Both are natural, everywhere, in every language. No variety is "broken."
3. **Name many ways (15 min).** With a group, read the "one language, many ways" cards —
   several varieties of Arabic, or Spanish, or Chinese, or English. For each, say one
   respectful thing: where it is spoken, what makes it distinct. Then share one word or
   phrase you know in your own way of speaking.
4. **Challenge the story (10 min).** Together, ask: *who* decided one way was "standard"?
   *Who* benefits when other ways are called "wrong"? Write one sentence: *"___ is not
   wrong — it is the correct way for the people who speak it."*
5. **Close (5 min).** You have built the habits of a careful, fair, many-voiced world:
   evaluate reasoning, write with precision, bridge languages, and honor every voice.
   Carry these into every day.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide the sentence starter and one variety to describe; accept a spoken
  answer instead of a written one.
- **Extension:** Research one variety of a language you know and share two things that
  make it distinct — then name a time it was unfairly called "wrong."

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name a dialect or accent as natural
  variation and say why it is not "wrong"?
- **Self-check:** The learner rereads their sentence and confirms it names a real variety
  and challenges the "one correct way" idea.

## Home connection

Ask someone at home which ways of speaking your family or region uses, and share one
thing you love about the way your family speaks.
