# Lesson 6 — Performing a Scene or Dance: Timing, Expression, Ensemble — and Revising by Feedback

## Summary

Learners devise and perform a short **scene or dance** with attention to **timing** (together
versus staggered, pauses, shared cues), **expression** (face and body carrying feeling), and
**ensemble** (everyone fitting the scene, supporting rather than stealing it). They perform a
first run, receive specific feedback, choose one revision, and perform again. Performance is
open to every body: movement, sign, gesture, and written or spoken words are all welcome
channels.

## Objectives

- Perform a scene or dance with attention to timing, expression, and ensemble, and revise
  after feedback. _(D07.S3.07.01)_

## Connection

Watch any group of people do something together — a dance at a wedding, players entering a
field, friends acting out a story — and you are watching three skills at once. They move on
the same **timing** (everyone lands the beat together, or one after another on purpose); they
wear the scene on their faces and bodies (**expression**); and they fit each other — no one
blocks the view, no one steals the moment (**ensemble**). A great scene is not one star and a
background; it is a group breathing together. Today your group becomes that group — and uses
feedback, not guesswork, to make the scene land.

## Materials

- Performer-tools poster (expression / timing / ensemble)
- A short scene or movement phrase
- Feedback slip

## Preparation

- Print or draw the performer-tools poster.
- Offer groups a simple, devisable prompt (a meeting, a farewell, a celebration, a stand) that
  each can perform with the channels their bodies and voices allow.
- Copy a feedback slip for each learner.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land is that **timing, expression,
and ensemble are chosen and can be revised** — a learner should be able to name one deliberate
choice in each and one change made after feedback. Model the three tools with the poster
(explicit instruction for a novice skill — Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006). Keep performance
**open to every body**: no one must stand, leap, speak, or move in a way their body does not
do — offer signed, seated, gestural, and written parts, and let each performer choose their
channel. Frame other cultures' performance traditions (dance, drama, ritual) as living
traditions to learn *about* with respect, not to imitate for a grade — learners perform the
scene *they* devise. Feedback stays about the work and is always specific and kind; a group
chooses which note to act on (see `docs/assessment.md`).

## Procedure

1. **Gather and recall (5 min).** Last lesson you read and spoke the silent language of
   gesture, posture, and spacing. Today you add **timing, expression, and ensemble** — and put
   it all into a scene you perform and revise.
2. **Meet the three tools (15 min).** Look at the poster. **Expression** — the face and body
   carry feeling: a joyful face, a worried one, a determined one. **Timing** — three moves
   *together* make one strong moment; three moves *staggered* arrive one after another; a held
   pause makes the next move land harder. **Ensemble** — everyone faces out, leaves space so
   no one is blocked, enters and exits on a shared cue, and *supports* the scene instead of
   stealing it. Say each with a partner: point to where expression, timing, and ensemble would
   live in a simple scene.
3. **Devise the scene (10 min).** In your group, choose your prompt and devise a short scene —
   about 30–60 seconds — deciding together: what feeling do we show and how (expression)? which
   moments are together and which staggered, and where do we pause (timing)? who stands where,
   who enters when, and how do we support each other (ensemble)?
4. **First run (5 min).** Perform it once for a watching partner or another group. This is not
   a test — it is a first draft.
5. **Get feedback and revise (10 min).** Read your feedback slips. Look for one note you can
   act on — "we couldn't see the back person's face," "the entrance was late," "the feeling
   didn't show." Choose **one** change, rehearse just that part, and put it back together.
6. **Second run (5 min).** Perform again. Notice what the revision did — that is craft growing
   in real time.
7. **Close (5 min).** You made a scene with others, read feedback honestly, and made it
   stronger. That loop — devise, perform, revise — is how every troupe on Earth rehearses.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Limit the scene to one beat of action and one expression each, with a partner
  giving a single, spoken feedback note.
- **Extension:** Add one deliberate timing choice — a synchronized entrance or a held pause —
  and write one sentence naming the effect it created.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation/peer):** Can the learner demonstrate one expression, one timing
  choice, and staying in ensemble with the group — and name one change made after feedback?
- **Artifact (performance):** The two runs (or a partner's written description of the change),
  showing a revision made after feedback.
- **Self-check:** The learner asks, "Can I point to one thing I changed after feedback and say
  *why* it made the scene clearer or stronger?"

## Home connection

Watch one group moment today — people greeting, playing, or leaving together — and name one
timing, one expression, and one ensemble choice you saw.
