# Lesson 1 — Art with a Message: Finding My Personal Voice

## Summary

Learners identify a **personal message** they want to communicate and plan how to carry it
through an artwork using a **symbol, color, and composition**. They meet the idea that art
carries messages in living traditions around the world — **adinkra** cloth symbols are Akan
proverbs worn on cloth — then choose their own message and begin the artwork they will
revise after feedback in the next lesson.

## Objectives

- Create an artwork that communicates a personal message and revise it after feedback.
  _(D07.S1.07.01 — the "create and plan" half; revision comes in Lesson 2)_

## Connection

Messages travel in pictures everywhere you look. A symbol on a door says *welcome*; a
mural on a wall tells a neighborhood's story; a badge on a uniform names a role. Among the
Akan peoples of Ghana and Côte d'Ivoire, stamped cloth called **adinkra** carries a whole
sentence in each symbol — one stamp means *unity*, another *memory*, another *wisdom*. The maker isn't decorating; they are *speaking*. Today you become that kind
of maker: you choose what you want to say, then choose the symbol, color, and composition
that will carry it to another person's eyes.

## Materials

- Message-map poster (the worked example)
- Message-map page (one per learner)
- Drawing paper and mark-makers

## Preparation

- Print or draw the message-map poster, including the worked example ("I care for living
  things" → hands holding a seedling).
- Copy a message-map page for each learner.
- Recall Grade 6: learners used line, shape, color, balance, and contrast deliberately to
  express an idea or feeling. This unit adds the *message* — a meaning meant for another
  person.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The one idea to land is that **art is a
message with a maker**, not decoration: a learner should be able to say *what* they want to
communicate and name at least one visual choice (symbol, color, or composition) that carries
it. Model the message-map worked example fully before learners plan their own (explicit
instruction for a novice skill — Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006). Frame adinkra as a
*living* tradition to honor with respectful questions, never as a specimen to copy — the
curriculum's standing commitment (see `docs/facilitation.md`). A learner's message may be
private; invite, never require, sharing of anything personal, and offer the wonder-box or a
written slip for messages they want held quietly. Keep the artworks safe — they will be
revised after feedback next lesson.

## Procedure

1. **Gather and recall (5 min).** Last year you made pictures that carried a *feeling*,
   using line, shape, color, balance, and contrast on purpose. This year you add a
   **message** — something you want *another person* to understand. Think: what is one
   thing you believe, hope, or want people to remember about you?
2. **Meet the message map (10 min).** Look at the poster. Here is a worked example. The
   message is *"I care for living things."* To carry it, the maker chose a **symbol** (two
   hands holding a seedling), a **color** (greens and browns — life and earth), and a
   **composition** (centered, held up high). Same message, a hundred possible pictures — the
   choice is the maker's. People everywhere make this same move: one adinkra stamp says
   *unity*, another *memory*, and the cloth wears the message like a sentence.
3. **Find your message (10 min).** Choose your message. Prompts if you need one: *something
   I believe · something I hope for the world · something I want people to remember about me
   · a thank-you · a warning · a dream I am working toward.* Write it in one sentence. Say it
   to a partner — does it sound like you?
4. **Plan it (10 min).** Fill your message-map page: choose a **symbol** (an object, animal,
   place, or mark that stands for your message), a **color or value** (and why), and a
   **composition** (centered or off-center? big or small? close or far?). Sketch the plan
   lightly.
5. **Begin making (10 min).** Start your artwork from the plan. You do not have to finish
   today — you will look at it with fresh eyes and a partner's feedback next lesson.
6. **Close (5 min).** You did what makers do in every culture and time: you chose something
   to say and began finding the shape for it. Keep your plan and your artwork safe for
   Lesson 2.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a message stem — "I believe ___" or "I hope ___" — and a symbol bank
  (heart, tree, hands, path, bird, seed, star) to choose from.
- **Extension:** Plan two different symbols for the same message and write one sentence
  naming which carries the message more strongly and why.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation/self):** Can the learner state their message in one sentence and
  name at least one visual choice — symbol, color, or composition — that carries it?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The completed message-map page (message + plan + sketch), kept
  for the unit portfolio.
- **Self-check:** The learner asks, "Could a stranger look at my plan and guess, at least
  roughly, what I care about? If not, what one change would make it clearer?"

## Home connection

Find one message-carrying image in your day — a symbol on clothing, a sign, a mural, a
poster — and tell someone what you think it is "saying" and how it shows it.
