# Lesson 9 — Alone and Together: Individual and Collective Action

## Summary

Learners compare **individual actions** and **collective actions** for caring for the environment
and explain why **both matter**. They sort actions into the two kinds and see that individual
habits and collective changes strengthen each other.

## Objectives

- Compare individual actions and collective actions for caring for the environment, and explain why
  both matter. _(D10.S4.07.02)_

## Connection

Some changes are in your own hands: you can walk instead of ride, waste less, plant a tree, or
speak up. Other changes need many hands at once: cleaning a whole river, making a rule that
protects a forest, or changing how a whole town handles its waste. These are not rivals — a person
who recycles at home is the same person who might help pass a recycling rule at school. Caring for
the Earth takes both, and today you learn why.

## Materials

- Alone-and-together sheet
- Action cards

## Preparation

- Copy the alone-and-together sheet and action cards.
- Retrieval: from Lesson 8, recall the greenhouse effect and the human activities that add to it.
  Today we turn from *what is happening* to *what we can do about it*.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The ideas to land: **(1) an individual action is one
a person can do alone, now; a collective action needs many people — and often a rule, a group, or a
system; (2) they do different jobs — individual actions model values and add up, while collective
actions change the systems that shape everyone's choices; (3) both matter, and they feed each
other.** Model the sort once (worked example, S-011). The environment & technology lens: systems
(transport, energy, waste) are shaped by collective choices, so changing them is a collective act —
this is the unit's bridge from tools to stewardship. The egalitarian lens: collective action is how
people with little individual power get a voice; a rule protects everyone, not just those who can
afford to act. The ethics lens: caring for the Earth is a question of what we owe each other and
the future. The global lens: people everywhere act both ways — a Kenyan tree-planting movement
(the Green Belt Movement, S-059) and a household habit are the same two kinds — and stewardship is
far older than any of these: Indigenous and traditional peoples have cared for land and sea for
thousands of years through their own knowledge traditions, such as Aboriginal and Torres Strait
Islander cultural burning (S-320, S-321) and Māori kaitiakitanga (guardianship of the environment),
held here side by side with modern environmental action, never beneath it. Hold hope and agency
high; end on what learners *can* do, not despair.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** What makes the Earth warm? Today we ask: what can we *do* about it?
2. **Meet the two kinds (8 min).** An **individual action** is one you can do alone, now — walk,
   waste less, plant a tree. A **collective action** needs many people — clean a river, make a
   rule, plant a forest, change a system.
3. **Sort the actions (12 min).** With a partner, sort the action cards into the two columns. Some
   actions start alone and grow into a group — put those on the bridge.
4. **See the bridge (12 min).** Look at your sheet's bridge. A person who wastes less at home is the
   same person who can help start a school-wide waste rule. Individual habits **model** what matters
   and add up; collective action **changes the systems** that make good choices easy for everyone.
   Each makes the other more likely.
5. **Close (8 min).** Share one pair — one thing you can do alone and one thing you could do
   together. Remember: **both matter, because the Earth needs both our hands and our rules.**

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide the actions pre-sorted and ask learners to explain *why* each landed in its
  column.
- **Extension:** Design a mini-campaign for one goal — an individual habit plus a collective ask (a
  rule, a group, or a petition) — and explain how the two support each other.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name one individual and one collective action for a
  goal and explain why both are needed?
- **Portfolio artifact:** The completed alone-and-together sheet, kept in the portfolio.

## Home connection

Choose one environmental goal for your home or street. Name one thing your household can do alone
and one thing the neighborhood could do together — and why both matter.
