# Lesson 10 — Capstone: Our Earth Home, Our Choices

## Summary

The unit capstone brings all four strands together. Learners **retrieve** the unit's eight ideas,
**revisit the wonders** they wrote in Lesson 1, and assemble a personal **Earth-home action plan**
read through all four lenses — ethics, egalitarianism, global, and technology & environment.

## Objectives

- Synthesize the unit's eight goals into one coherent view of technology, media, ecology, and
  stewardship, and turn that view into a personal and collective action plan.
  _(D10.S1.07.01, D10.S1.07.02, D10.S2.07.01, D10.S2.07.02, D10.S3.07.01, D10.S3.07.02, D10.S4.07.01, D10.S4.07.02 — synthesis)_

## Connection

For ten days you have looked at one shared home through four windows: the tools people make, the
messages they share, the living systems they are part of, and the choices that care for all of it.
Now you bring those windows together. Understanding is not the finish line — it is the starting
point for choosing. Today you write down the choices you will make, alone and with others, for the
home we all share.

## Materials

- Earth-home action plan
- Unit map (from Lesson 1)

## Preparation

- Copy the Earth-home action plan.
- Return each learner's Lesson 1 unit map and wonder slips.
- Retrieval: this lesson is itself a **spaced, interleaved retrieval pass** across the whole unit
  (Dunlosky et al. 2013, S-039) — it works because learners must *recall*, not re-read.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The ideas to land: **(1) the four strands are one
thing — technology, media, ecology, and stewardship all meet in the choices we make; (2) the four
lenses (ethics, egalitarianism, global, technology & environment) are how we check those choices;
(3) a plan that pairs individual action with collective action is stronger than either alone.** This
is a **summative** moment, but mastery is personal, not ranked — celebrate growth, never compare
learners. The ethics lens: "what is right?" The egalitarian lens: "who benefits, who is left out?"
The global lens: "how would another people or place see this?" The technology & environment lens:
"what does this do to humanity and the Earth?" Ask learners to run their plan through all four.
Keep hope central — the unit ends on agency, not despair. Assess by what each learner can now
*explain and choose*, not by a score.

## Procedure

1. **Recall the whole unit (10 min).** With a partner, recall the eight ideas: how a technology
   spreads; intended vs. unintended effects; the five questions for a source; misinformation vs.
   disinformation; the carbon cycle; pollination; the greenhouse effect; and individual vs.
   collective action. Name them without looking.
2. **Revisit your wonders (8 min).** Open your Lesson 1 map and wonder slips. Which wonders can you
   now answer? Which have grown into new questions? Answer one you can, and mark one to keep
   wondering about.
3. **Build your action plan (20 min).** On your plan, write five lines: **(1)** one technology
   choice you will make with care; **(2)** one source habit you will practice; **(3)** one
   ecological fact you will remember and teach someone; **(4)** one individual action for the
   Earth; **(5)** one collective action you could join or start.
4. **Run it through the lenses (7 min).** Check your plan against the four lenses: Is it right
   (ethics)? Who benefits, and who is left out (egalitarianism)? How would another people or place
   see it (global)? What does it do to people and the Earth (technology & environment)? Adjust one
   line.
5. **Close (5 min).** Share one line you are proud of. Remember: **the Earth home is ours — and so
   is the choosing.**

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide sentence starters for each of the five lines ("One technology choice I will
  make is ___ because ___").
- **Extension:** Add a "who benefits" line and a "what I will check back on in a month" line to the
  plan.

## Assessment

- **Summative (portfolio + self):** The completed Earth-home action plan is the unit's capstone
  artifact, kept in the portfolio.
- **Self-assessment:** Learners mark one line they feel confident explaining and one line they want
  to keep learning about — "not yet" is information, never a verdict.

## Home connection

Share your action plan with someone at home, and invite them to add one line of their own — one
choice they will make for the Earth home you share.
