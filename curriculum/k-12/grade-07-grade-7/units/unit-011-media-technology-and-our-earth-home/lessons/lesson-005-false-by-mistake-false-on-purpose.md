# Lesson 5 — False by Mistake, False on Purpose

## Summary

Learners learn the difference between **misinformation** (false by mistake) and **disinformation**
(false on purpose). The dividing line is **intent** — did the person believe the false thing, or
know it was false and spread it anyway? They sort examples by intent.

## Objectives

- Explain the difference between misinformation (false by mistake) and disinformation (false on
  purpose), with an example of each. _(D10.S2.07.02)_

## Connection

Imagine two people share the same wrong notice: "The fair is on Saturday." The first person read
an old poster from last year and truly believed it. The second person knows the fair was moved,
but spreads the Saturday notice to make people show up at the wrong time. Both shared something
false — but one did it **by mistake** and the other **on purpose**. That difference matters, because
what we do about a mistake is not the same as what we do about a trick.

## Materials

- Mistake-or-purpose sheet
- Example strips

## Preparation

- Copy the mistake-or-purpose sheet and example strips.
- Retrieval: from Lesson 4, recall the five questions for checking a source. Today we add one more
  layer — not just *is it false?* but *was the falsehood meant?*

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The ideas to land: **(1) misinformation is false
information shared by mistake — the sharer believes it; disinformation is false information spread
on purpose — the spreader knows it is false and means to deceive; (2) the difference is intent,
which we often must infer from context rather than read off the message; (3) our response should
match the intent — correct a mistake kindly, resist and expose a trick.** Model the first example
once (worked example, S-011). The critical-thinking lens: this is the unit's "hold claims open"
habit applied to falsehood itself. The ethics lens: spreading a known falsehood to deceive is a
wrong against other people's ability to choose — but an honest mistake deserves correction, not
blame. The egalitarian lens: disinformation often targets people with less access to good sources;
teaching everyone to see intent protects the most exposed. The technology lens: digital sharing
makes both kinds travel farther and faster, so the habit of asking "mistake or purpose?" is now a
basic life skill. Frame the two terms accurately and consistently (S-217; S-063) and keep examples
low-stakes and non-frightening.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** What were the five questions for checking a source? Today we sort *false*
   things by one key question: was the falsehood meant?
2. **Meet the two words (8 min).** **Misinformation** is false information shared **by mistake** —
   the sharer believes it. **Disinformation** is false information spread **on purpose** — the
   spreader knows it is false and means to trick. The difference is **intent**.
3. **Watch it modeled (12 min).** Two people post the same false claim: "The well water here is
   poisoned." Person A heard it from a neighbor and, worried, repeats it to warn others — believing
   it true. Person B knows the water is safe but spreads the claim to scare people away from the
   market. Both are false. A's is **misinformation** (mistake); B's is **disinformation**
   (purpose). Sort them.
4. **Sort the examples (15 min).** With a partner, take the example strips. For each, ask: did the
   person believe it, or know it was false? Sort into the two columns and say why.
5. **Close (10 min).** Share one placement and your reasoning. Remember: **the same false words can
   be a mistake or a trick — intent is the difference.**

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide each strip with a one-line hint ("this person believed it / this person knew
  it was false") and ask learners to match.
- **Extension:** For one misinformation and one disinformation case, write what you would do
  differently in response to each.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner give one example of each kind and explain the
  intent behind each?
- **Portfolio artifact:** The completed sort sheet, kept in the portfolio.

## Home connection

Talk with someone at home about a time a false thing spread — was it a mistake or a trick? How did
people find out, and what did they do?
