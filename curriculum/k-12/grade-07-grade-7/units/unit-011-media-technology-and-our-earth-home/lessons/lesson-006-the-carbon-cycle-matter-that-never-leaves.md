# Lesson 6 — The Carbon Cycle: Matter That Never Leaves

## Summary

Learners trace how **matter and energy move through an ecosystem — the carbon in a forest — and
are reused rather than created new**. They follow a single thread of carbon through four steps:
photosynthesis, eating, respiration, and decomposition, and see that the loop closes.

## Objectives

- Explain how matter and energy move through an ecosystem, such as the carbon in a forest, and are
  reused rather than created new. _(D10.S3.07.01)_

## Connection

Take a breath in, then out. The carbon you just breathed out was not made fresh inside you — it
came from food you ate, which came from plants, which pulled that very carbon out of the air. The
same atoms have been going round and round through air, plants, animals, and soil for as long as
life has existed. Earth is a closed loop: matter is **reused**, never created new. Today you trace
one loop through a forest.

## Materials

- Carbon-cycle sheet
- Step cards

## Preparation

- Copy the carbon-cycle sheet and step cards.
- Retrieval: from Grade 6, recall what a food chain is (D10.S3.06.01 — predator and prey, and
  energy passing from plants up). Today we add the missing piece: the same matter *returns*.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The ideas to land: **(1) matter (like carbon) cycles
— it is reused again and again, never created new; energy flows in and is released as heat, but the
atoms themselves go round; (2) four steps carry carbon in a forest — photosynthesis (plants take
CO₂ from the air), eating (animals take in plant matter), respiration (living things release CO₂
back), and decomposition (dead matter returns carbon to the soil and air); (3) the loop closes —
that is why the amount of carbon on Earth stays the same while it moves around.** Model the loop
once (worked example, S-011). The environment lens: this is the ground truth of "our Earth home" —
nothing is truly thrown away. The global lens: the cycle is the same everywhere, though the living
things differ by place — and many peoples have long observed and honored this recycling of matter
through their own knowledge traditions, held side by side with the science here (philosophy §11).
The technology lens: human activity (burning buried carbon as fuel) pushes
extra carbon into the air faster than the loop removes it — a preview of Lesson 8. The
critical-thinking lens: "reused, not created new" is a checkable scientific fact, not a guess
(S-253). Keep the claim precise: Earth is effectively a closed system for carbon, so its total
carbon is nearly constant (S-253); energy, by contrast, flows through and is not recycled.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Grade 6: what is a food chain, and which way does energy move? Today we
   see that matter *returns*.
2. **Meet the loop (8 min).** Matter in an ecosystem moves in a **cycle** — it is **reused**, not
   made new. Energy flows in from the Sun and leaves as heat; the atoms themselves stay and go
   round.
3. **Watch it modeled — carbon in a forest (15 min).** Trace one carbon atom: **(1)
   Photosynthesis** — a tree pulls carbon dioxide (CO₂) from the air to build its leaves and wood.
   **(2) Eating** — a deer eats the leaves; the carbon becomes part of the deer. **(3)
   Respiration** — the deer (and the tree, at night) breathe out CO₂, returning carbon to the air.
   **(4) Decomposition** — dead leaves and wood decay; fungi and tiny living things return their
   carbon to the soil and the air. The loop closes — the same carbon, going round.
4. **Arrange the loop (15 min).** With a group, place the four step cards in order and draw arrows
   between them. Say, for each step, what the carbon *enters as* and *leaves as*.
5. **Close (7 min).** Share one step you can now explain. Remember: **nothing is thrown away on
   Earth — it only changes form.**

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide the four steps pre-ordered and ask learners to match each to its input and
  output.
- **Extension:** Trace the *same* carbon atom through all four steps, naming what it becomes at
  each one, and then say one way burning fuel disrupts the loop's balance.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name the four steps in order and explain that carbon
  is reused, not created new?
- **Portfolio artifact:** The completed carbon-cycle sheet, kept in the portfolio.

## Home connection

Look at any plant near home. Ask: where did the carbon in this plant come from, and where will it
go when the plant dies? Talk through the loop with someone.
