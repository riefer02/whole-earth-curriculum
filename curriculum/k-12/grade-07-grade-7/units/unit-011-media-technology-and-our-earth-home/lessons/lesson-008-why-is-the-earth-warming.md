# Lesson 8 — Why Is the Earth Warming?

## Summary

Learners learn the **basic science of climate change**: the Sun's energy reaches Earth, and a layer
of **greenhouse gases** traps some of the heat, warming the planet. They then identify the main
**human activities** that add greenhouse gases — burning fossil fuels, cutting forests, farming,
and industry.

## Objectives

- Explain the basic science of climate change (greenhouse gases trapping heat) and the main human
  activities that add to it. _(D10.S4.07.01)_

## Connection

Have you ever sat in a closed car or a covered spot on a sunny day and felt it grow warm inside?
The glass lets sunlight in but slows the heat from leaving. Earth's atmosphere does something
similar: certain gases — called **greenhouse gases** — let sunlight through but hold some of the
heat near the surface. That holding is natural and keeps our planet warm enough to live on. The
problem is that human activity has added so much more of those gases that the "blanket" has grown
thicker, and the planet is warming.

## Materials

- Greenhouse-effect sheet
- Activity cards

## Preparation

- Copy the greenhouse-effect sheet and activity cards.
- Retrieval: from Lesson 6, recall that carbon cycles — and that burning buried carbon (fossil
  fuel) pushes extra carbon into the air. Today we see what that extra carbon does to the
  temperature.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The ideas to land: **(1) the greenhouse effect is
real and natural — the Sun's energy comes in, and greenhouse gases trap some of the heat so Earth
is warm enough for life; (2) human activities — burning fossil fuels (coal, oil, gas), cutting
forests, some farming, and industry — add extra greenhouse gases, thickening the blanket; (3) that
is why the Earth is warming, and the warming is a fact, not an opinion.** Model the diagram once
(worked example, S-011). The environment & technology lens: the warming is a direct bridge between
human technology (burning fuel) and the living Earth — the unit's central connection. The ethics
lens: the science tells us *what is happening*; what we *owe* to others because of it is a value
question, kept distinct. The egalitarian lens: the warming falls hardest on people who contributed
least to it — an injustice we will return to in the next lesson. The global lens: this is a
planet-wide change, measured the same way everywhere — and communities who live close to the land and
sea, including many Indigenous peoples, have long noticed shifting seasons, weather, and wildlife
through their own observation traditions, held side by side with the climate science here
(philosophy §11). Hold the science precisely and cite it: the
greenhouse effect and the role of human emissions are documented by the IPCC (S-005) and NOAA
(S-309); warming also threatens health (S-124). End with agency, not despair — the next lesson is
about action.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** What happens to carbon when we burn buried fuel? Today we see what that
   extra carbon does to the temperature.
2. **Meet the greenhouse effect (12 min).** Sunlight reaches Earth and warms the surface. Some heat
   rises back toward space, but a layer of **greenhouse gases** — including **carbon dioxide** —
   holds some of it near the surface. This is natural and keeps Earth warm enough for life.
3. **Label the diagram (10 min).** On your sheet, label the Sun, the incoming energy, the surface,
   and the layer of greenhouse gases. Draw an arrow for the heat that is trapped.
4. **Sort the activities (15 min).** With a group, sort the activity cards: **burning coal, oil,
   and gas; cutting forests; some kinds of farming; and industry** all add greenhouse gases. For
   each, say what gas or change it adds.
5. **Close (8 min).** Share one activity you can now explain. Remember: **the warming is real, it
   has a cause we can name, and — as we will see next — we can act on it.**

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide the diagram labels in a word bank and ask learners to place them, then match
  each activity to the gas it adds.
- **Extension:** Using a simple data table, rank the main activities by how much they add and
  explain your ranking.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner explain how greenhouse gases trap heat and name at
  least two human activities that add them?
- **Portfolio artifact:** The labeled greenhouse diagram, kept in the portfolio.

## Home connection

Explain the greenhouse effect to someone at home using the blanket or closed-car example, and name
one thing your household does that adds greenhouse gases and one that does not.
