# Lesson 5 — Strategies in Play: Putting It Together

## Summary

Learners choose a game or sport and write a simple team plan that names **spacing**, **timing**,
and **teamwork**. They play one round, pause to check the plan against what happened, adjust,
and reflect — closing the movement-and-strategy arc with the whole trio used together.

## Objectives

- Apply strategies such as spacing, timing, and teamwork in a game or sport. _(D03.S2.07.01)_

## Connection

A strategy is just a plan you make *before* you move, so you do not have to decide everything
in the heat of the moment. The same three tools you have been practicing — spread out, move at
the right time, help each other — are the whole toolkit. A relay team plans who runs which leg;
a group crossing a busy market plans who leads and who watches; a crew hauling a fishing net
plans who pulls and when. The plan is not about being the best athlete; it is about thinking
together, and it works in every game and every place.

## Materials

- Team plan page
- Cones, a ball or object

## Preparation

- Copy a team plan page for each team.
- Set up the playing area and any equipment for the chosen game.
- Have a short list of low-equipment games ready (relay, tag, keep-away, an adapted version of
  one of the games from the last lesson) so teams can choose quickly.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land is that **a strategy is a
plan you can name, test, and improve — not a mystery talent**. Model the worked example: write
a tiny plan aloud ("spacing — spread wide; timing — pass on the third count; teamwork — one
caller, one helper") before teams write their own (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006). Keep
teams mixed and the game cooperative wherever possible; ensure every body and ability has a
role (planner, caller, timer, mover, helper), so no one is on the sidelines. The reflection
("what worked, what would we change") is the mastery moment — feedback, not ranking. See
`docs/facilitation.md` and `docs/assessment.md`.

## Procedure

1. **Gather and recall (5 min).** You now hold three tools: **spacing**, **timing**, and
   **teamwork**. Today you use all three at once, on purpose. Name a game or sport your group
   could play with what you have.
2. **Write the plan (10 min).** On your team plan page, fill the three boxes. *Spacing*: where
   will each person stand or move to keep open space? *Timing*: when will you move or pass?
   *Teamwork*: who has which role, and how will you help each other? Keep it to one short
   sentence each.
3. **Play one round (15 min).** Play a short round, sticking to your plan as best you can.
4. **Check and adjust (10 min).** Huddle. What worked? What surprised you? Change *one* thing
   in the plan — one spacing, one timing, or one teamwork idea — and play one more short round
   to test it.
5. **Reflect (10 min).** Write one line: *We planned ___. What worked was ___. We changed ___
   and it ___.* Share your team's one biggest discovery with the group.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use the sentence starters on the plan page; keep the game simple (a relay or
  tag) and change only one part of the plan.
- **Extension:** Change the plan twice, and explain which change mattered most and why — using
  what you saw, not just what you felt.

## Assessment

- **Formative (peer):** Each team reads its plan aloud; a second team checks that the plan
  names all three — spacing, timing, and teamwork.
- **Portfolio:** The completed team plan page and the one-line reflection, kept in the
  learner's portfolio as evidence of applying strategies in a game.

## Home connection

Explain your team's plan to someone at home in one sentence each: what you planned, what
worked, and what you changed. Ask if they have ever used the same three ideas — spacing,
timing, teamwork — in something they do.
