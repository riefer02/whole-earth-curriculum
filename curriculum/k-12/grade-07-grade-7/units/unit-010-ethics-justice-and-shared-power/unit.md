# Unit 10 — Ethics, Justice & Shared Power

## Overview

This unit turns questions of fairness and justice inward to the self and the group,
building on Grade 6's fact-versus-belief-versus-value and its democracy-versus-
alternatives work. Learners apply a simple framework — who is helped, who is harmed,
what is fair — to a moral question, and explain how someone's background, culture, or
position can shape what they think is right. They analyze a case of inequality or
injustice, identifying who is affected, who benefits, and what would make it more just,
and explain what human rights are and analyze a case where they were honored or
violated. They analyze how people influence decisions in their society — voting,
protest, petitions, organizing — and the limits of each, and explain what civil society
is and how groups and movements act for the common good when government does not. They
apply restorative steps — listening, taking responsibility, repairing harm — to a real
or role-played conflict, and explain how power and hurt can make conflict worse, and
how to address them without violence. Questions are held as questions, never answered
as dogma.

## Essential questions

- How do I reason about a moral question — who is helped, who is harmed, what is fair?
- What are human rights, and how do people influence decisions and act for the common good?
- How do I restore after harm, and address conflict without violence?
