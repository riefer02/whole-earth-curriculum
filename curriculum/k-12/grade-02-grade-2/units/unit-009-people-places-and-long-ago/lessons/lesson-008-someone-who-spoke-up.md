# Lesson 8 — Someone Who Helped and Spoke Up

## Summary

Learners hear the gentle, hopeful story of **Wangari Maathai**, who began a movement
of women planting trees in Kenya and spoke up to care for the land and the people who
live on it. They describe what she did and why it mattered — a first, age-appropriate
story of helping and speaking up, ending in growth, not blame.

## Objectives

- Describe a story of someone who helped others or spoke up when something was
  unfair, and what they did. _(D08.S4.02.02)_

## Connection

Sometimes people notice something that is not right — land being stripped of its
trees, people being left out — and instead of looking away, they **help** and
**speak up**. One such person was Wangari Maathai of Kenya. When she saw forests being
cut down, hurting the land and the people who depended on it, she did not just
worry. She planted.

## Materials

- A picture set of the tree-planting story
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Prepare the story accurately and gently (see Resources): Wangari Maathai founded the
  Green Belt Movement in Kenya in 1977, encouraging women to plant trees; it grew
  across countries and helped plant more than thirty million trees, and she received
  the Nobel Peace Prize in 2004. Keep the telling warm and blame-free — no frightening
  content.
- Prepare or draw the picture cards (trees cut down, a person planting with others,
  trees growing tall).

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). **Tell the story gently and
hopefully.** Wangari Maathai's story is real and well-documented (see Resources), and
its shape is perfect for this age: notice a problem, act with others, make things
grow, speak up — and win a prize for peace, not for fighting. **Keep it non-violent
and blame-free:** do not dwell on conflict or punishment; the lesson's power is in
planting, cooperating, and hope. **No child is ever the problem, and no one must
share a personal unfairness.** Emphasize that anyone can help: a child can plant a
seed, water it, and speak up for it. Honor every body: a learner who cannot see the
cards hears the story told clearly and describes it; a learner who does not use a
speaking voice acts the planting or points to the cards. Distinguish evidence from
values: *that* she planted trees and won the Nobel Prize is fact (see Resources);
*that* caring for the land and speaking up are good is a value we hold together.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Have you ever seen a plant grow because someone cared for it?
   What does a tree give us — shade, fruit, clean air, a home for birds?
2. **Hear the story (15 min).** Listen to the story of Wangari Maathai. In Kenya,
   forests were being cut down, and the land was growing bare and dry. Wangari saw
   this and began something simple and strong: she encouraged **women and children to
   plant trees**. One tree, then many. The trees held the soil, gave shade and fruit
   and firewood, and gave people work and hope. Her movement spread to other
   countries, and together people planted **millions of trees**. She also **spoke up**
   — for the land, and for the people who care for it. For all this, she won the
   **Nobel Peace Prize**.
3. **Name what she did (10 min).** Point to the cards and say it in order: she
   **noticed** the trees were gone; she **planted** with others; she **spoke up**;
   the trees **grew**, and so did hope.
4. **Draw and tell (10 min).** Draw one thing Wangari (or someone like her) did, and
   write or tell one sentence about why it mattered.
5. **Close (5 min).** Helping and speaking up are things anyone can do — even with one
   seed, one kind word, one question. That is how caring grows.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Tell just three beats (notice, plant, grow) and ask one question: "What
  did she do to help?"
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Hear the story told clearly and describe what she
  did aloud; plant a real seed and feel it in the soil.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Act out planting, watering, and growing, and point to
  the story cards in order.
- **Support (Deaf/HH):** Tell the story with the picture sequence and signs — the
  pictures carry it; sign "plant," "tree," and "help" as you go.
- **Support (motor-difference):** Direct a partner to act or point while you sign or
  eye-gaze; plant a seed in a cup that is easy to reach.
- **Extension:** Think of one small thing *you* could do to help your place — plant a
  seed, pick up litter, speak up kindly — and tell why it matters.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner retell at least two things Wangari
  (or the story's helper) did?
- **Formative (oral/performance):** Does the learner say why it mattered, in their own
  words, with hope rather than blame?

## Resources

- Nobel Prize, "Wangari Maathai — Facts" — documents that Wangari Maathai founded the
  Green Belt Movement in 1977, that it spread to other African countries and
  contributed to planting over thirty million trees, and that she received the Nobel
  Peace Prize in 2004 "for her contribution to sustainable development, democracy and
  peace."
  https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/peace/2004/maathai/facts/

## Home connection

At home, plant one seed in a cup or a corner of ground and care for it. As you water
it, tell someone the story of Wangari Maathai and why planting — and speaking up for
the land — matters.
