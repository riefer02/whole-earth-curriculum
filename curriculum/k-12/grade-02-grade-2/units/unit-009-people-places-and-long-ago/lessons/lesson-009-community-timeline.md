# Lesson 9 — Our Community Timeline: Then and Now

## Summary

Learners bring the unit's two time-skills together into one **portfolio artifact**: a
family and community **timeline** that orders recent events and marks one thing that
**changed** and one that **stayed the same**. It becomes the unit's recorded "simple
family timeline and then-and-now comparison."

## Objectives

- Order a few events from their family's or community's recent past on a simple
  timeline and explain the order. _(D08.S1.02.01)_
- Describe how one part of daily life, such as how people light their homes or get
  food, has changed over time, and one way it has stayed the same. _(D08.S1.02.02)_

## Connection

You have learned two big things about time: how to put events in **order** on a
timeline, and how to see what **changed and stayed the same** from long ago to today.
Today you will do both at once, on one page — the story of your family's and
community's recent past, told in order, with a "changed" mark and a "stayed the same"
mark. That page is a little history of the people closest to you.

## Materials

- A "family and community timeline" sheet
- Crayons, pencils, paste

## Preparation

- Prepare the timeline sheet: a long line with a "long ago" end and a "now" end,
  room for three or four events, and a small "changed / stayed the same" mark beside
  one event.
- Revisit the earlier lessons' ideas — ordering events, and comparing light or food
  then and now — so the retrieval is fresh.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). **This is a retrieval-and-integration
lesson, and a portfolio artifact — but it is practice, not a test.** Learners are
recalling two skills they already practiced (ordering events; comparing then and now)
and combining them — spaced retrieval across the unit (Dunlosky et al., 2013). Model
one full example first (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006): place three events on the
line, then mark one "changed" and one "stayed the same" beside a chosen event, saying
your reasoning aloud. **Honor every family shape:** no family is "correct," and a
learner may use events from their *community* (a new building, a festival, a harvest)
if family events feel private. Honor every body: a learner who is blind or has low
vision sequences objects by touch and says or signs the order; a learner who does not
use a speaking voice points, acts, or draws. The artifact is evidence of growth, never
a ranking.

## Procedure

1. **Gather and remember (10 min).** You know two skills. Say them together:
   - A **timeline** orders events from long ago to now, using **before** and **after**.
   - Some things **change** over time, and some things **stay the same**.
2. **Watch one example (10 min).** Look at the model. Three events in order: a new
   school opening, a festival, a new baby. Beside "the new school opening," a mark
   says: "**changed:** the road is now paved; **stayed the same:** children still walk
   to school together."
3. **Build your timeline (20 min).** On your sheet, draw or show three or four real
   events from your family's or community's recent past, in order from "long ago" to
   "now." For one event, add the mark: one thing that **changed** and one thing that
   **stayed the same**.
4. **Explain your order (10 min).** Point to each event and tell the order, then read
   your "changed" and "stayed the same" mark. You may speak, sign, act, or show your
   page.
5. **Close (5 min).** You just made a small history of your own people and place —
   told in order, with change and sameness side by side. That is what historians do.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Order just three events with help, and mark one "changed" (or one
  "stayed the same") with a partner.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Sequence real objects by touch and tell the order
  and the change/sameness aloud while a partner records it.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Draw the events and point to the "changed" and "stayed
  the same" marks, or act the events in order.
- **Support (Deaf/HH):** The timeline is visual — build it with pictures, and sign
  "before," "after," "changed," and "same" as you go.
- **Support (motor-difference):** Use a large, stable sheet and easy-grip crayons; a
  partner places the drawings while the learner points, signs, or eye-gazes.
- **Extension:** Add a fifth event and mark a second "changed / stayed the same" pair.

## Assessment

- **Formative (portfolio):** Does the timeline show three or four events in a sensible
  order, from furthest back to most recent?
- **Formative (oral/performance):** Does the learner explain the order and name one
  thing that changed and one that stayed the same?

## Home connection

Take your timeline home and share it. Ask someone to add one more event — where would
it go on the line, and why?
