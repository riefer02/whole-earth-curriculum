# Lesson 10 — Our People, Places & Long Ago Story

## Summary

The capstone: learners gather the whole unit into one **"my story" page** — a
timeline, a then-and-now pair, a child's day in another place, a knowledge people
developed, their own place, and a fair/unfair with someone who helped. It becomes the
unit's portfolio artifact and a first telling of their own place in the long,
many-voiced human story.

## Objectives

- Order a few events from their family's or community's recent past on a simple
  timeline and explain the order. _(D08.S1.02.01)_
- Describe how one part of daily life, such as how people light their homes or get
  food, has changed over time, and one way it has stayed the same. _(D08.S1.02.02)_
- Describe the daily life of children in another place or time, including food,
  homes, work, and play, using stories or pictures. _(D08.S2.02.01)_
- Identify one knowledge or skill a particular people has developed, such as farming,
  weaving, or wayfinding, and explain what it helps them do. _(D08.S2.02.02)_
- Explain what people need from their environment, such as water, food, shelter, and
  materials, and how people in different places meet those needs. _(D08.S3.02.01)_
- Describe how people adapt to a very different environment, such as a desert, a
  coast, or a cold region. _(D08.S3.02.02)_
- Identify a rule or a way of treating people that is fair or unfair and explain why,
  using a classroom or story example. _(D08.S4.02.01)_
- Describe a story of someone who helped others or spoke up when something was
  unfair, and what they did. _(D08.S4.02.02)_

## Connection

All this time you have been gathering pieces of a bigger picture: how time unfolds on
a timeline, how things change and stay the same, how children live in other places,
what knowledge people develop, what everyone needs from their environment, and how
noticing fairness and helping others can make things better. Today you will put the
pieces together into one story — *your* story of people, places, and long ago. Every
learner's story is different, and every one belongs.

## Materials

- A "my story" page divided into sections (my timeline, then and now, a child's day, a
  knowledge, my place, a fair/unfair and a helper)
- Crayons, pencils, paste

## Preparation

- Prepare the "my story" page template, or plan to divide a sheet into five or six
  labeled sections.
- Gather learners' earlier work from the unit (timelines, then-and-now drawings,
  child-life charts, knowledge drawings, place charts, fair/unfair sorts) to revisit.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). **This is the unit's portfolio
artifact and summative moment — but it is a celebration, not a test.** Retrieval
practice across the whole unit is the engine: learners are recalling and *using*
everything they learned, spaced across the unit (the science-of-learning payoff —
Dunlosky et al., 2013). The page has five or six sections, one per idea; a learner
may complete all, or tell some sections orally, by acting, or with a scribe. Honor
every body and every family shape: no one must disclose their own timeline events,
their own place, or a private unfairness — sections can be filled with *any* event,
place, child's day, knowledge, or helper met this unit. "No correct family," "no
correct place," "no child is ever the problem." Gather the pages into a class mural so
the whole becomes a shared, many-voiced story — history told from many perspectives,
never a single one.

## Procedure

1. **Gather and remember (10 min).** You have learned six things. Say them together:
   - A **timeline** orders events from long ago to now.
   - Some things **change and stay the same** from long ago to today.
   - Children everywhere **eat, live, help, and play** — the parts are alike, the ways
     are many.
   - Every people has developed **knowledge and skill** — like wayfinding, farming, and
     weaving.
   - Everyone needs **water, food, shelter, and materials**, and people **adapt** to
     their place.
   - I can **notice fair and unfair** and **help or speak up** to make things better.
2. **Make your story page (25 min).** Fill each section — drawing, or telling an older
   learner who scribes for you:
   - **My timeline:** three or four events from my family or community, in order.
   - **Then and now:** one thing that changed and one that stayed the same.
   - **A child's day:** one child's day in another place — food, home, work, play.
   - **A knowledge:** one knowledge or skill a people developed, and what it helps do.
   - **My place:** my environment and how it shapes how we live.
   - **Fair and unfair, and a helper:** one fair or unfair rule, and someone who helped
     or spoke up.
3. **Share your story (15 min).** Tell your story to a partner or the group — point to
   each section as you go. You may speak, sign, act, or show your page.
4. **Gather the mural (10 min).** Add your page to the class mural of people, places,
   and long ago. Stand back and see how many different stories now live side by side —
   each one true, each one worthy.
5. **Close (5 min).** Your story is part of the long human story — told from many
   perspectives, never just one. You are the author of your own page in it.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Complete two or three sections with heavy support, and tell the rest
  orally.
- **Non-speaking:** Build the story with picture cards, objects, or acting, with a
  peer or older learner scribing captions.
- **Blind/low-vision:** Tell the story aloud by section while an older learner or the
  facilitator records it; use textured markers to place each part.
- **Deaf/HH:** Build the story with pictures, objects, and a signed telling — the page
  itself is visual; sign each section as you point to it.
- **Motor difference:** Tell each section aloud or by pointing while a partner or older
  learner scribes or assembles the page; use larger sections and easy-grip crayons.
- **Extension:** Add a seventh section — "a second place, alike and different" — and
  tell one thing alike and one thing different.

## Assessment

- **Summative (portfolio):** Does the "my story" page (or its told/scribed version)
  show evidence across the four strands — a timeline, a then-and-now comparison, a
  child's day, a knowledge, a place/adaptation, and a fair/unfair with a helper?
- **Performance (oral):** Can the learner tell their story, moving through the
  sections, using the unit's ideas in their own words?

## Home connection

Take your story page home and share it with someone. Ask them: what is one thing they
would add to their own people, places, and long ago story?
