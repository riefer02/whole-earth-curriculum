# Lesson 2 — Light Then and Now: What Changed, What Stayed

## Summary

Learners compare how people light their homes **long ago** and **today** — from
candle and oil lamp, through kerosene and gas, to the electric bulb and the solar
lamp — and name one thing that **changed** and one thing that **stayed the same**.
They hold an honest, kind truth: many people still light their homes the older way,
and neither way is "better."

## Objectives

- Describe how one part of daily life, such as how people light their homes or get
  food, has changed over time, and one way it has stayed the same. _(D08.S1.02.02)_

## Connection

When it gets dark, people everywhere want the same thing: **light** to see by. Long
ago, that light came from a flame — a candle, an oil lamp, a lantern. Today, in many
homes, it comes from an electric **bulb** that turns on with a switch — and in many
homes it still comes from a flame or a lamp too. The job stayed the same (making
light in the dark); how people do it changed. That is what "changed and stayed the
same" means.

## Materials

- Light picture cards (candle/oil lamp, kerosene or gas lamp, electric bulb, solar
  lamp)
- A "same" spot, a "different" spot, and a "both" spot
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Gather or draw the light cards in order: flame lights first, then bulb lights.
- Set up the three labeled spots — "same," "different," and "both" (where the two
  overlap).
- Prepare to say it plainly: the price of light has fallen a very great deal over the
  centuries, and light moved from candles to lamps to bulbs — yet many people still
  lack light at night today (see Resources).

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). **Comparing is a foundational skill,
so model it once before independent work** (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006): hold a
candle and a bulb, and think aloud — "Both give light. That is the same. The candle
uses a flame; the bulb uses electricity. That changed." Then guide the same two moves
for a second pair. **Never rank:** do not say old ways were harder or worse. A
candle, an oil lamp, and a solar lamp are all real, living ways to light a home —
today, hundreds of millions of people still light the dark with flame and lamp, and
many homes use solar lamps, so a "new" light can be flame-free without being
electric. The point is noticing change and sameness, never "progress." Honor every
body: a learner who is blind or has low vision feels the objects or hears the story
told clearly; a learner who does not use a speaking voice points to the "same" and
"different" spots. For accuracy, light's history is a long, many-peopled story (see
Resources) — keep it simple and honest.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** When it gets dark, what do people need? Light. How do you get
   light at your home — a switch, a lamp, a flame?
2. **Meet "long ago" and "today" (10 min).** Look at the pair: a candle and a bulb.
   Both help you see in the dark. Say it together: "**The same:** both give light.
   **Different:** flame and bulb." A long time ago, almost all light came from a
   flame. Over time people found new ways — lamps, then bulbs, and now solar lamps
   that charge in the sun.
3. **Guided practice (10 min).** Take a second pair — an oil lamp and a solar lamp.
   Together, put the older one on the "long ago" spot, the newer one on the "today"
   spot, and the job they both do on the "both" spot.
4. **Compare your own pair (15 min).** Choose one pair and hold or look at both. Find
   one thing that **stayed the same** (they both give light). Find one thing that
   **changed** (flame or bulb; something you light or something you switch on). Draw
   both and mark your two findings.
5. **Share (5 min).** Tell a partner: "It is the same because... It is different
   because..." Point, sign, or draw if you like.
6. **Close (5 min).** Many people today still use the older light, and many use the
   newer one. Both give light; both are real. That is how things change and stay the
   same.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use just one pair and ask only two questions — "what is the same?" and
  "what is different?" — with clear gestures toward the "both" spot.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Compare by touch and sound — a smooth candle and a
  cool bulb — and describe what feels the same and different aloud.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Point to the "same," "different," and "both" spots to
  show each finding, or draw the pair and mark the two findings.
- **Support (Deaf/HH):** The picture cards are already visual — compare by looking
  and pointing, and sign "same" and "different" as you place each item on its spot.
- **Support (motor-difference):** Choose easy-to-hold objects or larger cards; a
  partner moves each item while the learner directs with a point, a word, or
  eye-gaze.
- **Extension:** Choose a second part of daily life — how people get food — and find
  one thing that changed and one that stayed the same (people still buy and share
  food; the market and the store are different ways).

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Does the learner name at least one thing that changed
  and one thing that stayed the same for a then-and-now pair?
- **Formative (oral/performance):** Does the learner say or show "same" and
  "different" without calling either way better?

## Resources

  lighting: people moved from candles to oil and gas lamps to kerosene and then
  electricity, the price of light fell dramatically over centuries, and many people
  still lack light at night today.

## Home connection

Ask someone at home how their family got light when they were little — a flame, a
lamp, a bulb, a solar light? What is the same about how you get light today, and what
is different?
