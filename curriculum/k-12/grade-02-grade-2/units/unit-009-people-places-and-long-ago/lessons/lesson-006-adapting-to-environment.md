# Lesson 6 — Living in a Very Different Environment

## Summary

Learners meet three very different environments — a **desert**, a **coast**, and a
**cold region** — and see how people **adapt** to each through clothing, shelter,
and travel. Each adaptation is honored as clever and fitting, never "primitive."

## Objectives

- Describe how people adapt to a very different environment, such as a desert, a
  coast, or a cold region. _(D08.S3.02.02)_

## Connection

Where you live shapes how you live. If you lived where it is very hot and dry, you
would want shade and light cloth and to know where the water is. If you lived by the
sea, you would want a boat and to know the tides. If you lived where it is very
cold, you would want warm clothes and a snug shelter. People everywhere have adapted
— found clever, fitting ways to live — in each of these places, over a very long
time.

## Materials

- "Environment and adaptation" picture cards
- A three-column sort chart (desert, coast, cold region)

## Preparation

- Prepare or draw the three environment cards and the adaptation cards (light robes
  and a tent; a canoe and a stilt house; warm clothing and a snow house).
- Prepare the three-column chart.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). **Hold each adaptation with respect,
as ingenuity, not struggle.** Deserts, coasts, and cold regions are homes where
people have lived for thousands of years, not places to feel sorry about. Be accurate
and name the peoples where you can, without overclaiming: in deserts, people often
wear long, loose, light robes that shield from sun and sand, live in tents that let
cool air through, and know where the wells and oases are (see Resources); on coasts,
people fish, build boats, and sometimes raise homes on stilts above the tide; in cold
regions, Inuit peoples make warm parkas, kayaks, and winter shelters, and hunt and
fish (see Resources). **Never say any place is "hard to live in" in a pitying way** —
say instead: each place asks something, and people answer with knowledge. Honor every
body: describe the cards in words for a learner who cannot see them; a learner who
does not use a speaking voice points or acts the adaptations.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of the hottest, or wettest, or coldest place you can
   imagine. What would you need to be comfortable there?
2. **Meet the three environments (10 min).** A **desert** is very dry, hot in the day
   and cool at night. A **coast** is where land meets the sea — windy, salty, and
   full of fish. A **cold region** is very cold much of the year, with snow and ice.
3. **See how people adapt (15 min).** Sort the cards:
   - **Desert:** long, loose, light **robes** that shield from sun and sand; **tents**
     that let cool air through; knowing where the **wells and oases** are.
   - **Coast:** **boats and canoes** for fishing and travel; **houses on stilts**
     above the tide; knowing the **tides and winds**.
   - **Cold region:** warm **parkas** of fur and hide; **kayaks** that glide on the
     water and over ice; winter **shelters** of snow; hunting and fishing for food.
4. **Match and explain (15 min).** For one environment, say how each adaptation fits
   the place: "In the desert, light robes keep the hot sun off your skin. On the
   coast, a stilt house stays dry when the tide comes up." Point, act, or sign.
5. **Close (5 min).** Each place asks something of the people who live there, and
   people everywhere have answered with knowledge. Adapting is a human gift.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Meet one environment (the desert) and sort two adaptations with heavy
  picture support.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Feel objects — light cloth for the desert robe,
  wood for the canoe, a thick wrap for the cold parka — and match each to its
  environment by touch and description.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Sort the cards onto the three columns and point to each
  environment and adaptation instead of speaking.
- **Support (Deaf/HH):** The cards are already visual — sign "hot," "water," and
  "cold" as you place each card.
- **Support (motor-difference):** Use larger cards and a stable chart; direct a
  partner to place each card while you point, sign, or eye-gaze.
- **Extension:** Choose a fourth environment (a forest, a mountain, a grassland) and
  tell one way people adapt to it.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner match at least one adaptation each to
  a desert, a coast, and a cold region?
- **Formative (oral/performance):** Does the learner explain how one adaptation fits
  its environment, in their own words, without ranking any place or people?

## Resources

- National Geographic Education, "Desert" — describes how people in deserts adapt
  with long, loose, light robes, tents, camels, and knowledge of wells and oases.
  https://education.nationalgeographic.org/resource/desert/
- Inuit Circumpolar Council — the united voice of Inuit across Alaska, Canada,
  Greenland, and Chukotka; documents Inuit knowledge and ways of living in the
  Arctic.
  https://www.inuitcircumpolar.com/
- Polynesian Voyaging Society (Hōkūleʻa) — documents how Pacific island and coastal
  peoples read the sea and sail canoes, supporting the coast example.
  https://hokulea.com/

## Home connection

Think of your own place: what is the weather like, and how does it shape what your
family wears, where you live, and how you get around? Tell a grown-up one way your
place has shaped your family's way of living.
