# Lesson 5 — What People Need From Their Environment

## Summary

Learners name the four things people need from their **environment** — **water**,
**food**, **shelter**, and **materials** — and discover that people in different
places meet those same needs in many different ways, all of them real and valid.

## Objectives

- Explain what people need from their environment, such as water, food, shelter, and
  materials, and how people in different places meet those needs. _(D08.S3.02.01)_

## Connection

Every person, everywhere, needs the same four things from the world around them:
**water** to drink, **food** to eat, **shelter** to stay warm and dry, and
**materials** — wood, stone, clay, cloth — to make the things they use. But the
*way* people get each one depends on where they live. A family by a river drinks from
the river; a family far from water draws from a well or catches the rain. The need is
the same; the way is shaped by place.

## Materials

- "Needs" picture cards (water, food, shelter, materials) plus "ways to meet them"
  cards
- A four-row "needs and ways" chart

## Preparation

- Gather or draw the four needs cards and several "ways to meet them" cards (a well,
  a river, a farm, a market, a tent, a house of wood or clay, a clay pot).
- Prepare the four-row chart (water, food, shelter, materials).

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). **Keep the needs universal and the
ways many.** The four needs — water, food, shelter, materials — are shared by every
human; this is a factual, cross-cultural claim (every person needs water, food, and
shelter to live). What differs is *how*, and every way is a real adaptation, never a
deficiency. **Do not rank:** a well is not "better" than a river; a tent is not
"worse" than a house of wood; each fits its place and its climate. Use examples from
many regions (a desert well, a forest home of wood, a high-mountain stone house, a
coastal fish dinner) so no single region is the default. Honor every body: describe
the cards in words for a learner who cannot see them; a learner who does not use a
speaking voice points or acts. A learner whose own way of meeting a need differs is
never "wrong" — their way belongs on the chart too.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Close your eyes and think of what you needed this morning —
   something to drink, something to eat, a place to be warm and safe. Where did each
   come from?
2. **Meet the four needs (10 min).** Say them together: **water**, **food**,
   **shelter**, **materials**. Every person, everywhere, needs all four. The world
   around us — the land, the water, the plants — is where they come from. That world
   is the **environment**.
3. **See the many ways (15 min).** Look at the cards. People meet the same need in
   different ways: water from a **river**, a **well**, or the **rain**; food from a
   **farm**, a **garden**, the **sea**, or a **market**; shelter as a **tent**, a
   **wooden house**, a **clay home**, or a **stone house**; materials from **wood**,
   **stone**, **clay**, or **plants**. Sort each "way" onto its need row.
4. **Match a place and a way (10 min).** For one need, say why a place shapes the
   way: "Where there is little rain, people dig **wells**. Where there is a river,
   people draw from the **river**." Point, act, or sign.
5. **Close (5 min).** The needs are the same for everyone; the ways are many. Every
   way is a real answer to a real place.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Focus on two needs (water and food) with one way each, using heavy
  picture support.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Feel the objects (a cup for water, a seed for food,
  cloth for shelter, a stick for materials) and name the need each one stands for.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Sort the cards onto the chart and point to each need
  and its way instead of speaking.
- **Support (Deaf/HH):** The cards are already visual — sign "water," "food,"
  "shelter," and "materials" as you place each one.
- **Support (motor-difference):** Use larger cards and a stable chart; direct a
  partner to place each card while you point, sign, or eye-gaze.
- **Extension:** For two needs, name two different places and tell how each place
  shapes the way people meet the need.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name the four needs — water, food,
  shelter, materials — and match at least one way people meet each?
- **Formative (oral/performance):** Does the learner explain, for one need, how a
  place shapes the way people meet it, without ranking any way as better?

## Resources

- National Geographic Education, "Desert" — notes that people have adapted to life
  in deserts for thousands of years, drawing water from wells, springs, and oases,
  and using tents and light robes — a concrete example of how a place shapes how
  needs are met.

## Home connection

At home, trace one need back to its source: where did your water come from today?
Where did your food come from? Tell a grown-up how your place shapes the way your
family meets that need.
