# Lesson 4 — Where Feelings Live in My Body

## Summary

Learners build a body map and learn that every feeling shows up somewhere in the
body — tense shoulders, a fast heartbeat, hot cheeks, a heavy chest, a wobbly tummy.
They practice a short body scan and mark their own feeling spots. The body is always
talking; learning to listen is the beginning of understanding ourselves.

## Objectives

- Notice where a feeling shows up in the body, such as tense shoulders or a fast
  heartbeat, and name the feeling. _(D01.S1.02.02)_

## Connection

Put your hand on your chest right after you run fast — your heart is pounding. That is
your body talking. Your body talks all the time, not just when you run. When you are
mad, your shoulders might pull up tight. When you are scared or excited, your heart
beats fast. When you are sad, your chest can feel heavy. Your body is a messenger, and
learning to listen to it helps you know what you are feeling before you even have the
words.

## Materials

- Body map poster (blank body outline)
- Shape stickers or colored dots (heart, diamond, circle, triangle, square, star)
- Small mirrors (optional)

## Preparation

- Draw or print the blank body map poster.
- Prepare the shape stickers or dots and a legend (shape → feeling spot).
- Set aside a quiet space for the body scan.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Some learners will feel a feeling
everywhere or nowhere, and some bodies signal differently — honor both. There is no
"wrong" spot; the goal is *noticing*, not matching a chart. Frame every description as
a possibility ("many people feel mad as tense shoulders — where do *you* feel it?").
Model the body scan with your own body. For learners with limited sensation or
mobility, the spot can be named and pointed to on the poster or on a partner's body
with consent.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** You have named look-alike feelings — mad, frustrated, sad,
   disappointed. Today you find out *where* those feelings live in your body.
2. **Meet the body spots (15 min).** Look at the body map. For each spot, hear where a
   feeling often lives — then check your own body: **tense shoulders** (mad,
   frustrated — your shoulders pull up toward your ears), **fast heartbeat** (scared,
   excited — put a hand on your chest and feel it), **hot cheeks** (mad — your face
   flushes warm), **heavy chest** (sad, disappointed — your chest feels full and slow),
   **wobbly tummy** (nervous — a fluttery, shaky feeling low in your belly).
3. **Body scan (10 min).** Sit or lie quietly. Close your eyes or soften them. Travel
   from your head down to your toes: shoulders, chest, cheeks, tummy, hands. Notice
   one thing your body is doing right now. Is anything tight, fast, heavy, or warm?
   Name it, even if it is small.
4. **Mark your own map (10 min).** On the big body map — or your own outline — put a
   shape on the spot where *you* feel mad, where *you* feel sad, where *you* feel
   excited. Your map may not match a friend's map, and that is exactly right: every
   body talks in its own way.
5. **Close (5 min).** Your body is always talking to you. Learning to listen is how
   you understand your feelings. Next time you notice tight shoulders or a fast heart,
   you can ask: "What am I feeling?"

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Introduce one or two body spots at a time; point to the spot on the
  poster and on your own body.
- **Extension:** Keep a one-day "body signal journal" — draw or write one body signal
  you noticed and the feeling it named.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner point to a body spot and name the
  feeling that often lives there?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The learner's marked body map.

## Home connection

Teach someone at home one body signal — for example, "mad shows up as tense
shoulders" — and ask them where *they* feel mad or sad.
