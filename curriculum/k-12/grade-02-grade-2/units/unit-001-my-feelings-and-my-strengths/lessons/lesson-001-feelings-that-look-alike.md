# Lesson 1 — Feelings That Look Alike

## Summary

Learners meet two pairs of feelings that look and feel almost the same: **mad** and
**frustrated**, and **sad** and **disappointed**. They learn a simple clue question
to tell each pair apart and practice sorting small everyday moments. This is the
first step toward naming feelings exactly, not just roughly.

## Objectives

- Use feeling words to tell the difference between similar emotions, such as mad and
  frustrated or sad and disappointed. _(D01.S1.02.01)_

## Connection

Imagine your little sibling knocked over the tower you just built. Your face gets
hot, your hands curl into fists — that is **mad**. Now imagine you tried and tried to
tie your own shoes and the knot keeps slipping loose. Your face is scrunched and your
chest is tight — that is **frustrated**. Both feel hot and tight, but they are not the
same. Two more: you lose a favorite toy — **sad**. You hoped for rain so you could
splash in puddles, and it stayed dry — **disappointed**. Learning to tell feelings
that look alike apart helps you say exactly what is happening inside you.

## Materials

- Look-alike feelings chart (mad/frustrated, sad/disappointed)
- Feeling scenario cards
- Chart paper and markers

## Preparation

- Print or draw the look-alike chart and the scenario cards.
- Write the two clue questions on chart paper: "Did something block me, or did my
  trying not work?" and "Did I lose something, or did something I hoped for not
  happen?"
- Arrange a circle for gathering and a space for the sorting game.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Prepare the chart, cards, and clue
questions, then guide the sorting game and closing reflection. Feelings are personal
— no answer is "wrong." If a learner names a different feeling, honor it and wonder
together about why. The goal is *noticing* the difference, not memorizing a chart.
Keep the clue questions simple and concrete; read them aloud for learners who are
still growing as readers.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think back: last year you learned feeling names like proud,
   lonely, and excited, and long ago you learned happy, sad, angry, and scared. Today
   you meet feelings that *look alike* — almost the same, but not quite.
2. **Meet the first look-alike pair (10 min).** Look at the two faces side by side.
   **Mad** is when something blocks you — someone grabs your toy, and you want it
   back *now*. **Frustrated** is when you try and try and it will not work — the
   button slips, the page rips, the ball will not balance. Both feel hot and tight.
   The clue question is: *did something block me, or did my trying not work?*
3. **Meet the second look-alike pair (10 min).** Now look at the other two faces.
   **Sad** is when you lose something you love — a pet, a friend moving away, a broken
   treasure. **Disappointed** is when you hoped for something and it did not happen —
   the rain did not come, the game was canceled. Both feel heavy and slow. The clue
   question is: *did I lose something, or did something I hoped for not happen?*
4. **Sort game (10 min).** Hear a moment, then point or say which feeling it is: "You
   build a sandcastle and a wave washes it flat." (mad — something blocked you, or
   frustrated — your trying was washed away? Talk about why you chose.) "The market
   ran out of the fruit you saved your coins for." (disappointed.) "Your dog ran
   away." (sad.) No answer is silly — the clue question is your tool.
5. **Close (5 min).** Look-alike feelings are like cousins — they resemble each other,
   but they are not the same person. The clue question helps you tell them apart. More
   exact words mean people can understand you better.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Work with one pair at a time (mad/frustrated first); use only the faces
  and clues, no written words.
- **Extension:** Add a third look-alike pair of your own (for example, excited and
  nervous) and write a clue question for it.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name a clue that tells mad from
  frustrated, or sad from disappointed, when shown a moment?

## Home connection

Teach one look-alike pair — mad and frustrated, or sad and disappointed — to someone
at home, and share a time you felt each one.
