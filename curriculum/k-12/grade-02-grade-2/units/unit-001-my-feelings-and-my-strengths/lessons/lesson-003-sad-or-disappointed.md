# Lesson 3 — Sad or Disappointed?

## Summary

Learners go deep on the second look-alike pair: **sad** and **disappointed**. Using
the clue question *"Did I lose something, or did something I hoped for not happen?"*
they sort everyday moments — including some tender ones — and learn that both heavy
feelings are welcome, nameable, and different from each other. Naming them exactly is
a gift you give yourself.

## Objectives

- Use feeling words to tell the difference between similar emotions, such as sad and
  disappointed. _(D01.S1.02.01)_

## Connection

When a favorite hen dies, or a friend moves far away, your chest feels heavy and you
cry — that is **sad**: you lost something you love. But when the harvest festival is
canceled because the rains came late, or the bus is full and you miss the trip you
waited all week for, your shoulders droop — that is **disappointed**: you hoped for
something, and it did not happen. Both feel heavy. The difference is loss or hope.

## Materials

- Sad vs. disappointed comparison card
- Scenario strips
- Two sorting baskets or hoops

## Preparation

- Print or draw the comparison card and the scenario strips.
- Set out the two labeled baskets or hoops: "sad" and "disappointed."
- Write the clue question large: "Did I lose something, or did something I hoped for
  not happen?"

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Some scenarios touch real loss (a pet,
a person moving away). Hold them gently and calmly — name the feeling, do not push for
disclosure, and never require a learner to share a personal loss. A learner may pass
or just listen. Reassure the group that sad feelings are not a problem to fix; they
are a signal of what matters to us. Read scenarios aloud for learners still building
reading fluency, and keep the tone warm, not heavy.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Yesterday you mastered mad and frustrated. Quick check: what
   clue tells them apart? (Blocked vs. trying.) Today a new pair: sad and
   disappointed.
2. **Model the clue (10 min).** Look at the comparison card. **Sad** is about *loss* —
   you lost something you love, or someone is gone. **Disappointed** is about *hope* —
   you hoped for something, and it did not happen. Watch: "Your cat has been missing
   for two days." That is sad — a loss. Now: "You hoped to fly your new kite, and
   there is no wind today." That is disappointed — a hope that did not happen.
3. **Sort together (10 min).** Hear a moment, then point to the sad basket or the
   disappointed basket. "You saved seeds to plant, and the birds ate them all."
   (Disappointed.) "Your grandmother who lived far away has passed." (Sad — and it is
   okay to feel this deeply.) "The sweets at the stall were all gone when you got
   there." (Disappointed.) Say your clue question out loud each time.
4. **Tender moments (10 min).** Some heavy moments can be both. "You move to a new
   home and miss your old friends — and you had hoped the move would not happen."
   Talk softly with a partner: sad, disappointed, or both? You do not have to share
   anything about your own life — you may just listen. Noticing both is brave and
   wise.
5. **Close (5 min).** Sad says "I lost something I love." Disappointed says "something
   I hoped for did not happen." Knowing which one it is helps you know what you need —
   comfort and remembering, or a new hope and another try.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer only picture scenarios and two choices at a time; keep tender
  moments brief and optional.
- **Extension:** Write your own moment (a hope that did not happen) and have a partner
  sort it, then share one thing you can hope for next.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner sort a new moment as sad or
  disappointed and give the clue ("loss" vs. "hope did not happen")?

## Home connection

Ask someone at home to tell you about a time they were disappointed, and listen for
the hope inside it — then share one new hope with them.
