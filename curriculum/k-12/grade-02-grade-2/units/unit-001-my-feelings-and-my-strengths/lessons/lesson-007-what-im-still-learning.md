# Lesson 7 — What I'm Still Learning

## Summary

Learners name one thing they are still learning and place it on a growth ladder:
**not yet**, **with help**, **on my own**. They discover that "not yet" is
information, never a verdict — it simply says where the next step is. Everyone is
growing, and no one's pace is a measure of their worth.

## Objectives

- Identify their personal strengths and one thing they are still learning to do.
  _(D01.S2.02.01)_

## Connection

When a baby bird first flaps its wings, it cannot fly — not yet. It flutters, falls,
tries again. "Not yet" does not mean "never" or "not good enough." It means "I am on
my way." Maybe you cannot whistle yet, or swim yet, or read a long word yet. That is
not a failure; it is the exact spot where you are growing. Even grown-ups have
"not yet" things — a gardener learning a new crop, a cook learning a new dish.

## Materials

- Not-yet ladder poster
- Index cards or paper slips
- Crayons and pencils

## Preparation

- Prepare the ladder poster (three rungs: not yet, with help, on my own).
- Set out the cards and crayons.
- Arrange a circle for sharing (sharing is invited, never required).

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The heart of this lesson is the
"not yet" mindset: a skill not yet learned is information, never a verdict. Never let
the ladder become a ranking — no one is "higher" than anyone else; different people
are on different rungs for different skills. A learner who shares a "not yet" is
showing courage; receive it warmly. "With help" and "on my own" are both good places
to be. Model your own "not yet" first to make it safe.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Yesterday you found your strengths — things you can do well.
   Today you name one thing you are *still learning*. Everyone has one, even grown-ups.
2. **Meet the ladder (10 min).** Look at the ladder. The bottom rung says **not yet** —
   I cannot do it yet, and that is okay. The middle rung says **with help** — I can do
   it when someone shows me or helps me. The top rung says **on my own** — I can do it
   by myself. You are on different rungs for different skills, and that is normal.
3. **Choose your "not yet" (10 min).** Pick one thing you are still learning — tying
   your shoes, writing your name neatly, swimming, reading a hard word, sharing when it
   is hard. Write it or draw it on a card. Then point to the rung you are on.
4. **Name the next small step (10 min).** For your "not yet," name one small step you
   could take next — ask someone to show you, try once more today, practice a little
   each day. Share with a partner if you like, or keep it to yourself.
5. **Close (5 min).** "Not yet" is not a bad grade — it is a signpost pointing to your
   next step. Say it together: "I can't do it *yet* — and I am growing."

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer a picture menu of common "still learning" skills to choose from;
  use only two rungs (not yet, with help) to start.
- **Extension:** Choose a second skill, and for each, name the next step and who could
  help you with it.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name one thing they are still learning
  and place it on a rung of the ladder?
- **Self-check:** The learner says, "I am still learning ___, and my next step is ___."

## Home connection

Ask someone at home what *they* are still learning, and tell them one thing you are
still learning too.
