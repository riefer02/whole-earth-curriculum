# Lesson 9 — Two Ways to Calm Down

## Summary

Learners name and practice two calming strategies — **take three slow breaths** and
**count slowly to ten** — then choose the one they will use on their own, marking it
on a choice card. They learn that calming is a helpful tool, never a punishment, and
that different strategies work for different people and different moments.

## Objectives

- Name two strategies for managing strong feelings and choose one to use on their
  own. _(D01.S3.02.01)_

## Connection

When a feeling gets very big — mad, scared, or so excited it feels like too much —
your body can go tight and fast. Last year you learned one calming tool, like a deep
breath. This year you grow a whole toolbox, and you get to *choose*. A calm-down
strategy is like a deep breath you keep in your pocket: it is there whenever you need
it, and no one can take it away from you. It is help, never a punishment.

## Materials

- Calm-down menu (two strategies)
- A soft object to hold
- A "my choice" card

## Preparation

- Prepare the two strategy cards and the choice cards.
- Set out the soft objects.
- Arrange a quiet space for practice.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Frame calming as help, never as
discipline — never send a learner to calm down as a punishment. Model both strategies
with your own body first, then guide practice, then let each learner *choose* — the
choice is the point of this objective. Some bodies calm with movement rather than
stillness; if the two stillness strategies do not fit a learner, offer a movement
option (slow sway, gentle stretch) as an equally good choice. The choice is theirs.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** You have learned to name your feelings and read your body's
   clues. Now, when a feeling gets very big, what can you *do*? Today you meet two
   calm-down tools and choose your own.
2. **Meet the two strategies (10 min).** Look at the menu. **Strategy one: take three
   slow breaths.** Breathe in through your nose like smelling a flower; breathe out
   through your mouth like blowing on hot soup. **Strategy two: count slowly to ten.**
   Count each number like a slow, soft step — 1… 2… 3… all the way to 10. Both help
   your body slow down so your heart and mind can catch up.
3. **Practice together (15 min).** Hold your soft object. Try the three slow breaths —
   in like a flower, out like soup — three times. Then try the slow count to ten,
   touching a finger for each number. Notice which one your body liked best.
4. **Choose your tool (10 min).** On your choice card, mark the strategy you will use —
   the breaths or the count (or, if you prefer, a slow sway or gentle stretch). This is
   *your* tool. Say when you might use it: before you yell, before you give up, before
   bed, when a game is too loud.
5. **Close (5 min).** You have a toolbox now, and you are the one who chooses which
   tool to use. Next time a feeling gets big, reach for your tool.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Practice one strategy only (the three breaths) until it is solid, then
  add the count.
- **Extension:** Teach your chosen strategy to a friend, and name a second moment when
  you would use it.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name two calming strategies and choose
  one to use on their own?
- **Self-check:** The learner marks their choice card and says when they will use it.

## Home connection

Teach your chosen calm-down tool to someone at home, and try it together once before
bed.
