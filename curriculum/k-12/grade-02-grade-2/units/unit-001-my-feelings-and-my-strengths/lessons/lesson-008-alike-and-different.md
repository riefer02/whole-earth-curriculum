# Lesson 8 — Alike and Different

## Summary

Learners explore how they are alike and different from others, sorting facts into two
overlapping circles: *just me*, *just my friend*, and *what we share*. They discover
that both sameness and difference matter — what we share joins us together, and what
is different makes each of us special. No one is "more" or "less"; we are each a
one-of-a-kind person who also belongs.

## Objectives

- Describe how they are alike and different from others and why both matter.
  _(D01.S2.02.02)_

## Connection

Children everywhere eat, sleep, laugh, and love to play — in a busy city, a mountain
village, a fishing town by the sea, a desert camp. In those ways you are alike. But
children also speak different languages, eat different foods, live in different kinds
of homes, and play different games. In those ways you are different. Both matter: what
you share with someone lets you play and work together, and what is different makes
the world interesting — and makes you, you.

## Materials

- Alike-and-different circles (Venn) poster
- Picture cards of people
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Draw the Venn poster (two overlapping circles: "just me," "just my friend," "we
  share").
- Gather the picture cards of many kinds of people and places.
- Arrange partners and a floor space for the big Venn if using rope or chalk.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Keep the frame egalitarian: differences
are facts to notice and celebrate, never a ranking — no skin tone, language, home, or
food is "better." If a learner shares a difference that carries stigma (a disability,
a different family shape), receive it as simply another part of who they are, with
warmth, not pity. Use the wide word "people" so every family and community shape is
honored. Both circles matter equally; there is no "best" circle to be in.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** You are a person, and you are also one of many people. Today
   you look at how you are **alike** (the same) and **different** from others.
2. **Meet the circles (10 min).** Look at the Venn. One circle is *just me* — things
   true only about you. The other is *just my friend* — things true only about them.
   The middle, where the circles overlap, is *what we share* — things true about both
   of you.
3. **Sort the facts (15 min).** With a partner, place facts into the circles: your
   name (just me), their name (just my friend), you both like to play (we share), you
   speak one language at home and they speak another (different), you both have
   families who love you (we share). Notice: no fact is "better" — each one simply
   tells something true about a person.
4. **People everywhere (10 min).** Look at the picture cards of people from many
   places. Name one way they are all alike (everyone eats, sleeps, laughs, loves).
   Name one way they are different (foods, homes, languages, games). Say it together:
   both matter.
5. **Close (5 min).** What you share joins you to others. What is different makes you
   special. You are alike *and* different — and that is exactly how a person should
   be.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use two circles with no overlap at first (same / different), then add
  the shared middle; use pictures instead of words.
- **Extension:** Fill your own Venn page comparing yourself with a friend, and write
  one sentence about why a difference you share with them matters.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name one way they are like another
  person and one way they are different, and say why both matter?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The learner's completed "same and different" page.

## Home connection

At home, find one thing you share with someone there and one thing that is different,
and tell them why both matter.
