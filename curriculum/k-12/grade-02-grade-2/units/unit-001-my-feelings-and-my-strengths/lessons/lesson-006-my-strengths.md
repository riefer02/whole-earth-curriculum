# Lesson 6 — My Strengths

## Summary

Learners identify their personal strengths — things they can do well — and record them
on a star chart. They learn that a strength is not about being "the best," but about
something you can do well and feel good doing. They also see that everyone has
strengths, and no one strength is better than another.

## Objectives

- Identify their personal strengths and one thing they are still learning to do.
  _(D01.S2.02.01)_

## Connection

Think of a time you did something and thought, "I am good at this." Maybe you shared
your snack with a friend who forgot theirs — you are good at being kind. Maybe you
climbed to the top of the mango tree, or read a whole page out loud, or made your
little cousin stop crying. A strength is something you can do well and feel good
doing. Everyone has some — not the same ones, and that is what makes a group strong.

## Materials

- "My Strengths" page (star chart)
- Crayons and pencils
- A small box or basket for strength notes

## Preparation

- Prepare the star chart (several empty stars, one labeled "still learning").
- Set out crayons and pencils.
- Arrange partners for sharing, and the box for strength notes.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Strengths come in many kinds — helping,
making, moving, listening, thinking, caring, telling stories, being patient. Never
rank them (a fast runner is not "better" than a kind friend). Some learners will say
they have no strengths; help them find one by noticing something they did that day.
Keep the focus on *capability*, not comparison. Ensure every learner receives at least
one note: assign each child a secret strength partner so every child writes and
receives exactly one. The empty "still learning" star is a preview of the next lesson —
leave it unfilled today if that feels right.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** You have been learning to read your feelings. Now you look at
   something just as important: what you can *do well*. Today you find your strengths.
2. **What is a strength? (10 min).** A strength is something you can do well and feel
   good doing. It can be big (building a bridge out of sticks) or quiet (listening when
   a friend is sad). It can be in your body (running, dancing), your hands (drawing,
   fixing), your heart (sharing, being kind), or your head (figuring things out,
   remembering). No kind is better than another.
3. **Find your strengths (15 min).** Look at your star chart. Fill each star with a
   strength — draw it or write it. Stuck? Try three ways to find one: **notice** (what
   do people thank you for?), **ask** (turn to a partner and ask, "What is one thing I
   am good at?"), and **try** (what did you do today that felt good and worked?).
4. **Strength notes (10 min).** Your facilitator gives you a secret "strength
   partner" — someone in the group, and someone has you, too. On a slip, write or draw
   one strength you see in your partner. Fold it and put it in the box. When the notes
   are read out, every person gets exactly one note — the one their partner wrote for
   them. Noticing strengths in others is a strength too.
5. **Close (5 min).** You are a person with strengths. They are part of who you are.
   Hold your star chart and say one strength out loud, or just look at it and smile.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer picture choices of strengths (a helping hand, a running figure, a
  drawing hand) and scribe the words the learner says.
- **Extension:** Add a strength you want to grow *more* of, and one small thing you
  could do today to practice it.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name at least one personal strength,
  in words, drawing, or movement?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The "My Strengths" star chart.

## Home connection

Ask someone at home to tell you one strength they see in you, and add it to your star
chart.
