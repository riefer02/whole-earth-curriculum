# Lesson 10 — Pause Before I Act

## Summary

The capstone lesson. Learners put the whole unit together into one routine: **Pause,
Name, Choose**. They practice stopping before they react, naming the feeling (using
their look-alike words and body clues), and naming the choice they are about to make.
They revisit their strengths and calm-down tools as the choices already in their
hands.

## Objectives

- Pause before reacting and name the choice they are about to make. _(D01.S3.02.02)_
- Use feeling words to tell the difference between similar emotions, such as mad and
  frustrated or sad and disappointed. _(D01.S1.02.01)_
- Name two strategies for managing strong feelings and choose one to use on their
  own. _(D01.S3.02.01)_
- Identify their personal strengths and one thing they are still learning to do.
  _(D01.S2.02.01)_

## Connection

A feeling can rush in fast — a toy grabbed, a turn skipped, a tower knocked over — and
before you know it, your hand is already moving. Pausing is the brave little stop in
the middle: a breath, a hand held open, a moment to think. Then you can *choose* what
happens next instead of just letting it happen. You have been building everything you
need for this all unit long: words to name feelings, clues from your body, and calm
tools to reach for. Today you put them together.

## Materials

- Pause, Name, Choose steps poster
- Choice cards
- A soft bell or chime (optional)

## Preparation

- Prepare the three-step poster and the choice cards.
- Choose a few simple scenarios for practice (toy grabbed, turn skipped, tower knocked
  over).
- Arrange space for role-play.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The capstone is a *routine*, not a
test. Model the three steps yourself with a real, mild scenario first, then guide
practice, then let learners role-play. Never shame a child whose first instinct was to
react — the point is that pausing is a skill that grows with practice. "Name" uses the
unit's vocabulary (mad vs. frustrated, sad vs. disappointed) and body clues; "Choose"
uses the calm tools and the learner's own strengths. A learner may choose anything
safe; there is no single "right" choice — the win is that they paused and named it.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** This is your last lesson in "My Feelings & My Strengths." Look
   back: you learned to tell look-alike feelings apart, to read your body's clues, to
   name your strengths and what you are still learning, and to calm a big feeling.
   Today you put it all into one superpower: **Pause, Name, Choose**.
2. **Meet the three steps (10 min).** **Pause** — stop, take one breath, hold your hand
   open. **Name** — say what you feel, using your exact words: "I feel *frustrated*,
   not just mad, because my trying is not working." **Choose** — name the choice you
   are about to make: "I could yell, or I could use my calm breath." You do not have to
   choose perfectly — you just have to see the choice.
3. **Practice together (15 min).** Hear a scenario: "Your friend knocks over the tower
   you worked so hard on." Walk the three steps out loud: Pause (breath), Name ("I feel
   mad — something blocked me — and also frustrated, because my trying got knocked
   down"), Choose ("I could push them, or I could take three slow breaths and say
   'That made me mad'"). Try it with two more scenarios.
4. **Role-play your own (10 min).** With a partner, act out a moment from your own day
   where a feeling rushed in. Practice Pause, Name, Choose together. Then name one
   *strength* you used — being brave, being kind, being patient.
5. **Close (5 min).** You are a person who can feel, name, and choose. That is your
   strength, and you are still growing it — and that is exactly right. Pause, Name,
   Choose. You can do this anywhere, all your life.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use only two steps (Pause, Choose) at first; provide the feeling word as
  a choice ("Is it mad or frustrated?") during Name.
- **Extension:** For each choice, name what might happen next ("If I yell…, if I
  breathe…"), thinking one step ahead.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner pause in a role-play, name the feeling,
  and name a choice before acting?
- **Performance:** The learner demonstrates Pause, Name, Choose in a role-play.
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The "Pause Before I Act" check-in — the learner's name for
  the choice they made — added to the unit portfolio alongside their strengths chart.

## Home connection

Teach Pause, Name, Choose to someone at home, and try it together the next time a
small thing goes wrong.
