# Lesson 12 — Sharing Into Halves, Thirds, and Fourths

## Summary

Learners fold and cut circles and rectangles into equal shares — **halves**, **thirds**,
and **fourths** — and name each share. They discover that equal shares are the heart
of fair sharing, and that the more people share, the smaller each share becomes.

## Objectives

- Partition circles and rectangles into equal shares and describe the shares as
  halves, thirds, and fourths. _(D05.S3.02.02)_

## Connection

Sharing fairly is one of the oldest human acts. Two children share a flatbread — each
gets a **half**. Three friends share a round cake — each gets a **third**. Four people
share a pizza — each gets a **fourth**. In every land, when people share one thing
among many, they cut it into equal parts so that everyone gets the same — because fair
is not just kind, it is the way we live together.

## Materials

- Paper circles and rectangles (several per learner)
- A round or square food or object to share (optional)

## Preparation

- Give each learner several paper circles and rectangles.
- Have scissors (or tearing by hand) ready.
- Plan to model folding into halves, thirds, and fourths as worked examples.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It introduces **equal shares** — the
first real meeting with fractions, taught through the ethics of fair sharing. Teach
with **worked examples** (philosophy §13): fold a circle into two equal parts and say
"two halves make the whole." Emphasize that shares must be **equal** for sharing to be
fair, and connect halves, thirds, and fourths to sharing among two, three, and four
people. This is a values lesson as much as a math lesson: **fairness means each person
gets the same, and no one is worth more than another** (egalitarianism). Avoid ranking
"good" and "bad" folders. For learners who are Deaf or hard of hearing, show folding
and name each share in sign; for learners who are blind or low-vision, fold real cloth
or a flatbread and feel the equal parts. A learner who does not speak points to the
share or holds up the number of parts. Every learner can share fairly.

## Procedure

1. **Gather — recall sharing (5 min).** Have you ever shared one thing with a friend?
   What did you each get? Today we learn to cut one thing into equal parts so sharing
   is fair.
2. **Worked example — halves (8 min).** Take a paper circle. Fold it so the two parts
   match exactly. Open it — two **equal** parts. Each part is a **half**; two halves
   make the whole. Fold it again into four equal parts: each is a **fourth**.
3. **Make thirds (8 min).** Take a rectangle. Fold it into **three equal parts**. This
   takes care — the parts must match. Each part is a **third**; three thirds make the
   whole.
4. **Cut and name (12 min).** Cut a circle into halves, thirds, and fourths (three
   different circles). Label each share: "one half," "one third," "one fourth." Put
   the parts back together to make the whole again.
5. **Notice the size (7 min).** Compare: which is bigger, a half or a fourth? Which is
   bigger, a third or a fourth? Why? The more equal parts, the smaller each share.
6. **Close (3 min).** Fair sharing is equal sharing. Halves, thirds, and fourths are
   the names of fair shares — and when everyone gets the same, everyone is cared for.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Fold and name only halves today; add thirds and fourths another day.
- **Support (motor):** Use larger paper or real flatbread, and have a partner fold
  while the learner names each share.
- **Extension:** Fold a rectangle into halves in two different ways and show they are
  still halves; or explain why a half is bigger than a fourth.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner partition a circle and rectangle into
  halves, thirds, and fourths and name each share?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** A labeled set of equal-share shapes, kept as a record of
  the learner's first fraction work.

## Home connection

At home, share one flatbread, fruit, or paper circle among two, three, and four people,
and name each person's share.
