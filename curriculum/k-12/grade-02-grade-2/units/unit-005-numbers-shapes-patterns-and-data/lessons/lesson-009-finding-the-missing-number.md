# Lesson 9 — Finding the Missing Number

## Summary

Learners solve one-step addition and subtraction stories where a number is missing —
"I had some seeds, I gave away 4, now I have 6." They act the story out with objects,
write it as a number sentence with a blank, find the missing number, and check by
putting it back.

## Objectives

- Solve one-step addition and subtraction problems with a missing number in a real
  context, such as sharing or spending. _(D05.S2.02.02)_

## Connection

Sometimes you know the ending and one part, but not the start. A child shares some
mangoes and has a few left — how many did they start with? A shopper spends some coins
and has some left — how many did they have at first? A fisher sells some fish and
keeps the rest. Finding the missing number is how we figure out the beginning of a
story when we only know the middle and the end.

## Materials

- About 50 small objects (counters, seeds, coins)
- Paper or slate and a writing tool

## Preparation

- Give each learner or pair about 50 objects.
- Prepare a few missing-number stories about sharing, spending, or gathering.
- Plan to model finding the missing number as a worked example.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It is the "missing number" lesson —
the unknown can be at the start, middle, or end of the story, which is a genuine
stretch for seven- and eight-year-olds. Teach with a **worked example** (philosophy
§13): tell the story, act it out, write a sentence with a blank, find the missing
number, and **check by putting it back**. The check is the key habit — the learner
proves the answer, not just states it. Keep stories small, real, and fair (sharing and
spending, never winning or losing as worth). **There is no "math person" and no
"not-a-math person"; every learner can find a missing number, each in their own way
and at their own pace.** For learners who are Deaf or hard of hearing, tell the story
in sign or pictures and act it out with objects; for learners who are blind or
low-vision, act it out with real objects by touch. A learner who does not speak shows
the missing number by holding up objects or pointing.

## Procedure

1. **Gather — recall the two moves (5 min).** You know adding (joining) and
   subtracting (taking away). Today a story hides one number, and you find it.
2. **Worked example — the hidden start (10 min).** Listen: "I had some seeds. I gave
   away 4. Now I have 6." How many did I start with? Act it out: make the "now" — 6
   seeds. Put back the 4 I gave away. That is 10. The missing number is **10**. Write
   it: □ − 4 = 6, and □ = 10. Check: 10 − 4 = 6. Yes!
3. **Guided practice — act and find (15 min).** Hear more stories, one at a time. For
   each: act it out, write the number sentence with a blank, find the missing number,
   and check by putting it back. Try stories where the missing number is at the start,
   in the middle, and at the end.
4. **Write your own (8 min).** Make up one sharing or spending story with a missing
   number. Write the number sentence with a blank. Trade with a friend and solve each
   other's.
5. **Check together (5 min).** Read your friend's answer to your story. Does putting
   the number back make the story true? If not, act it out together — kindly.
6. **Close (2 min).** A missing number is a little mystery, and you are the detective.
   Act the story, find the number, and check it back. Every story has an answer you
   can find.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use stories where the missing number is at the end ("6 + 4 = □") before
  hiding the start; act out every step.
- **Support (motor):** Have a partner move objects while the learner directs and finds
  the number.
- **Extension:** Write two different stories (one add, one subtract) with the same
  missing number, and show how the check works for each.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner act out a missing-number story, write
  the sentence with a blank, find the missing number, and check it?
- **Formative (self):** Can the learner put their answer back into the story to prove
  it is right?

## Home connection

At home, make up a sharing or spending story with a missing number for a grown-up, and
solve it together with small objects.
