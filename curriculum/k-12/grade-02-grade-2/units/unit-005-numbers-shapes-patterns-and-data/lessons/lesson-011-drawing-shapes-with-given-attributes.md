# Lesson 11 — Drawing Shapes With Given Attributes

## Summary

Learners draw shapes from given attributes — "draw a shape with four equal sides,"
"draw a shape with three angles." They check their drawings by counting sides and
angles, turning spoken words into pictures and back again.

## Objectives

- Identify and draw shapes with given attributes, such as a certain number of angles
  or equal sides. _(D05.S3.02.01)_

## Connection

A builder hears "make a frame with four equal sides" and draws a square; a weaver
hears "six-sided cells" and thinks of a honeycomb; a child hears "a shape with three
corners" and draws a triangle. Drawing from words is how an idea in one person's head
becomes a picture everyone can see — and a shape everyone can check by counting.

## Materials

- Paper or slate and a drawing tool
- Rulers or straight edges (optional)

## Preparation

- Prepare attribute cards or a list of drawing challenges.
- Have drawing tools and straight edges ready.
- Plan to model drawing one shape from attributes as a worked example.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It reverses Lesson 10: now the
attributes come first, and the learner draws the shape. Teach with a **worked example**
(philosophy §13): hear "four equal sides," draw a square, count to check. Accept every
drawing that honestly matches the attributes — a triangle can be wide or tall, and a
rectangle can be long or short; there is no single "correct" drawing, only shapes that
meet the attributes or do not. This is a gentle lesson in **precision without
punishment**: checking sides and angles is how we know a drawing is right, and
correcting a drawing is learning, not failing. **Every learner can draw; the goal is
matching attributes, not making "pretty" pictures.** For learners who are Deaf or hard
of hearing, show the attributes in writing or sign and let them draw; for learners who
are blind or low-vision, build the shape with a geoboard or clay and describe it. A
learner who does not speak draws the shape or builds it to answer.

## Procedure

1. **Gather — recall attributes (5 min).** Yesterday you named shapes by their sides
   and angles. Today it is a game: someone tells you the attributes, and you draw the
   shape.
2. **Worked example — hear and draw (8 min).** Listen: "Draw a shape with four equal
   sides." What could it be? A **square**. Draw it, then check: count the sides (4),
   check they are equal. Yes! Say it together.
3. **Draw from attributes (15 min).** One at a time, hear or read a challenge and draw
   the shape: "three angles," "four sides with two long and two short," "five sides,"
   "six angles." After each, check by counting sides and angles.
4. **Challenge a friend (10 min).** Write or say an attribute challenge for a friend.
   Draw their challenge; they draw yours. Check each other's drawings by counting.
5. **Check together (5 min).** For each pair of drawings, ask: does it have the right
   number of sides and angles? If not, draw again together — kindly.
6. **Close (2 min).** Words can become pictures, and pictures can be checked. When you
   can draw from attributes and count to check, you hold the whole shape in your mind.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Draw from one simple attribute at a time, with a model shape to copy
  first.
- **Support (motor):** Use a geoboard or large arm movements to make the shape before
  drawing, or have a partner trace while the learner directs.
- **Extension:** Draw a shape with two attributes at once (for example, "four sides
  AND all sides equal"), or draw two different shapes that both have four sides.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner draw a shape from given attributes and
  check it by counting sides and angles?
- **Formative (peer/self):** Can the learner check their own and a friend's drawing
  against the attributes and correct a mismatch?

## Home connection

At home, ask a grown-up to say some attributes ("three sides," "four equal sides"),
draw the shape, and let them check it with you.
