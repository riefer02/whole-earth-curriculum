# Lesson 16 — Our Data Fair

## Summary

The unit's capstone: learners ask a real question, collect data from the group, draw a
**picture graph** and a **bar graph**, and use adding and subtracting within 100 to
answer "how many more or less." They present one finding to the class in a **data
fair**, celebrating what the whole unit made possible.

## Objectives

- Draw and interpret picture graphs and bar graphs and answer questions about how many
  more or less. _(D05.S4.02.02)_
- Add and subtract within 100 using models, drawings, or written methods, and explain
  the strategy used. _(D05.S1.02.02)_

## Connection

This whole unit you have learned to count, group, add, subtract, see patterns, and
measure — and now it all comes together. A class that wants to plan a celebration asks
"how will we get there?" and graphs the answers; a village that wants to plant trees
counts who will help; a family plans a trip and counts the days. Data is people's
answers, and the math you know — adding and subtracting — is how you read what the
data says. Today you do what data-keepers do everywhere: ask, collect, graph, and
share.

## Materials

- A survey question and recording sheet (per pair or group)
- Paper or slate and a drawing tool
- Small objects or marks for a picture graph

## Preparation

- Help each pair or group choose one respectful question with no "right" answer.
- Prepare recording sheets and graph paper.
- Set up a space to share findings (a "data fair" wall or circle).

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It is the unit's **capstone**, weaving
together place value, adding and subtracting within 100, and graphs. Learners work in
pairs or small groups and take the role that fits them — asking, tallying, drawing,
counting, or presenting — so success is collective, not ranked (philosophy §8). The
one new skill is *integrating*: reading a graph and answering "how many more or less"
with subtraction. Teach it with a **worked example** (philosophy §13) if needed, but
let the groups drive. The fair is a celebration of mastery, not a competition: every
graph is honored, and every finding is real. **No one's graph is "better"; a clear,
honest graph is the goal, and every learner's contribution matters.** For learners who
are Deaf or hard of hearing, present findings in sign and point to the graph; for
learners who are blind or low-vision, build a tactile graph and present by counting
aloud. A learner who does not speak presents by pointing to bars and holding up the
number. Celebrate every group.

## Procedure

1. **Gather — the big question (5 min).** Today is our **data fair**. With a partner or
   small group, choose a real question to ask everyone (favorite fruit, how we get to
   school, a game we like, a pet at home).
2. **Collect the data (12 min).** Ask your question and tally the answers — one mark
   for each person, so everyone counts exactly once. Keep your tally neat, like a
   data-keeper.
3. **Graph it two ways (15 min).** Draw your data as a **picture graph** and as a
   **bar graph**, with a title. Check that both show the same counts.
4. **Find your finding (12 min).** Read your graphs. Which has the **most**? The
   **fewest**? Now answer "how many more or less" by subtracting: how many more chose
   the top answer than the bottom one? Write your finding as a sentence, using your
   adding and subtracting from this unit.
5. **Present at the fair (10 min).** Each group shares one finding with the class,
   pointing to the bars. Listeners ask kind questions. Everyone's graph is part of the
   fair.
6. **Close (6 min).** Look at everything you made. You asked, collected, graphed, and
   read the answer — with the numbers, shapes, patterns, and data of this whole unit.
   That is mathematics: making sense of the world, together, for everyone.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use a two- or three-answer question and count bars together; present
  one finding aloud with a partner.
- **Support (motor):** Have a partner draw while the learner tallies and counts.
- **Extension:** Write two findings, or combine two groups' data and compare them, or
  predict what a bigger group would answer and say why.

## Assessment

- **Summative (portfolio):** The learner's picture graph and bar graph, kept as the
  unit's data artifact.
- **Observation:** Can the learner collect data, graph it, and use adding/subtracting
  to answer "how many more or less"?
- **Peer:** Can the learner present a finding clearly and respond kindly to a
  classmate's graph?

## Home connection

At home, run a mini data fair: ask your family a question, graph the answers, and
present one finding to them.
