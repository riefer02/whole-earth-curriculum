# Lesson 14 — Choosing the Right Tool to Measure

## Summary

Learners match a measuring tool to the job — a ruler for small things, a meter stick
or measuring tape for big ones — and explain their choice. They estimate first,
measure, and discover that the right tool makes measuring easier and truer.

## Objectives

- Measure length using standard units such as centimeters, meters, and inches,
  choosing the right tool for the job. _(D05.S4.02.01)_

## Connection

You would not use a teaspoon to fill a water tank, and you would not use a bucket to
give a baby a drink. Tools fit their job. Measuring is the same: a seed wants a ruler,
a room wants a meter stick or a tape. A fisher measures a fish with a ruler, a builder
measures a wall with a tape, a tailor measures cloth with a tape measure. Choosing the
right tool is half of measuring well.

## Materials

- Rulers, meter sticks, and measuring tapes
- Things to measure, small and large (a seed, a book, a table, a room, a path)
- Paper or slate and a writing tool

## Preparation

- Gather several measuring tools and a range of objects, small and large.
- Prepare a simple chart: "small → ruler, big → meter stick or tape."
- Plan to model choosing a tool as a worked example.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It extends Lesson 13 from *how* to
measure to *which tool* to measure with — the "choosing the right tool" part of the
standard. Teach with a **worked example** (philosophy §13): "a seed is small, so a
ruler; a room is big, so a meter stick or tape." The key idea is that **a tool should
fit the size of the thing** — a meter is for big, a centimeter for small, and an inch
is one more shared unit some places use. Keep it concrete and exploratory; there is
often more than one sensible tool, and a learner's reason is worth more than a "right"
answer. **Choosing a tool is thinking, not a test, and every thoughtful reason is
honored.** For learners who are Deaf or hard of hearing, point to each tool and object
and show the match; for learners who are blind or low-vision, feel each tool and
object and reason about size by touch. A learner who does not speak points to the tool
they would choose.

## Procedure

1. **Gather — recall standard units (5 min).** Yesterday you measured with centimeters
   and inches. Today you become the chooser: which tool fits which job?
2. **Worked example — fit the tool to the thing (10 min).** Look at a seed. Is it
   small or big? Small — so a **ruler** in centimeters. Now look at a room. Big — so a
   **meter stick** or **measuring tape**. The rule: small things, small units; big
   things, big units.
3. **Choose and measure (18 min).** For each object — a seed, a book, a table, a room,
   a path — first choose your tool, and say why. Then estimate its length, measure,
   and record both. Did the tool fit the job?
4. **Compare tools (5 min).** What would happen if you measured a room with a ruler?
   (Many steps, more chances to slip.) What if you measured a seed with a meter stick?
   (Too big to see the answer.) Say why the right tool matters.
5. **Check with a partner (5 min).** Trade one measurement. A friend measures the same
   thing with their chosen tool. Do you agree? If not, talk through your tools —
   kindly.
6. **Close (2 min).** A good measurer chooses the right tool, like a good cook chooses
   the right spoon. Small things, small units; big things, big units. You can now
   measure anything, big or small.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer two tools to choose between and measure only clearly small or
  clearly big things.
- **Support (motor):** Have a partner hold the tool while the learner points and
  reads.
- **Extension:** Measure a curved or round thing (a ball, a tree trunk) and decide
  which tool works best, explaining why.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner choose a fitting tool for a small and a
  large object and explain their choice?
- **Formative (self):** Can the learner estimate, measure, and reflect on whether the
  tool fit the job?

## Home connection

At home, choose the right tool to measure something small and something big, measure
both, and tell a grown-up why you chose each tool.
