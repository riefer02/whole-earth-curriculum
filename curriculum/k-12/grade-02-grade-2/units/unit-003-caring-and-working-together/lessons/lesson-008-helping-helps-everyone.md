# Lesson 8 — Helping Helps Everyone

## Summary

Learners discover a surprising truth: helping others helps everyone, including
yourself. Through a class "helping web," they see that care flows in every direction —
when one person helps, the whole group is stronger, and the helper is helped too.

## Objectives

- Explain why helping others helps everyone, including themselves.
  _(D02.S4.02.02)_

## Connection

When you help water the garden, everyone gets vegetables — and you do too. When you
help tidy a shared space, everyone has a nicer place — and you do too. When you share
what you have, the whole group has enough. Helping is not a one-way gift that leaves
you with less; it is like a web where care travels in every direction. People
everywhere have always known this: when we help each other, we all rise. Today you
will see it with your own hands.

## Materials

- A ball of yarn or string
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Arrange a circle for gathering, with room to stretch the yarn across it.
- Set out paper and crayons.

## Facilitator note

This lesson names **mutual aid** — the idea that help flows between equals, not from
a giver above to a receiver below. That distinction matters: keep the lesson away
from "charity" framing (the strong helping the weak), and toward "we all need help,
and we all give it." The environment lens lives here too: caring for a shared plant,
a shared water source, a shared space helps everyone who depends on it. Keep the web
playful and non-ranking; no one is "the most helpful." Every learner has been helped
and has helped — that is the point. A non-speaking learner shows their help with a
gesture or a drawing; a learner who has been helped in a hard way shares only what
they wish. Read the procedure aloud; model one "I was helped… I helped…" pair first.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Sit in a circle. Think: name one time someone helped you. Name
   one time you helped someone.
2. **Meet the idea (10 min).** Wonder together: when you help, who is helped? Watch
   the garden example — you water the plant, the plant grows, everyone eats. Say it
   together: "When I help, it comes back to everyone — including me."
3. **Build the helping web (15 min).** Hold one end of the yarn. Say one way you have
   been helped, then toss the ball to someone across the circle. They say one way
   they help, hold the yarn, and toss it on. Keep going until everyone holds the web.
   Look at it: when we are all connected, the web is strong.
4. **Draw the return (10 min).** Draw one way you can help — and show how it comes
   back to everyone, including you.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: helping is not a gift that leaves you with less. In a
   helping web, everyone is held — and everyone is helped.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer sentence starters ("I was helped when…", "I helped when…"); a
  non-speaking learner holds the yarn and shows their help with a gesture or drawing.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to notice a shared resource everyone depends on —
  water, a garden, a tidy space — and name one way caring for it helps everyone.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner explain — in words, sign, or drawing —
  how helping one person helps everyone, including themselves?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The learner's "help comes back" drawing.

## Home connection

At home, do one thing that helps the whole family — and notice how it comes back to
you too — then tell a grown-up what you noticed.
