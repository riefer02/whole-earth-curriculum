# Lesson 4 — Caring When Someone Hurts

## Summary

Learners learn four caring steps — notice, ask, stay, include — and practice them in
three scenes: when a classmate is sad, left out, or hurt. Caring is taught as
something everyone can offer, not as a special talent for a few.

## Objectives

- Show caring when a classmate is sad, left out, or hurt. _(D02.S2.02.02)_

## Connection

Think of a time you felt sad, or a time you were left out of a game, or a time you
fell and hurt yourself. What made it better? Usually it was someone noticing, asking
if you were okay, staying with you, or pulling you into the circle. Caring is not
magic — it is a set of small things anyone can do. In every part of the world, when
someone is hurting, people come close, ask, and help. Today you will learn the steps
and practice them.

## Materials

- A caring-steps chart (notice, ask, stay, include)
- Scene cards (a classmate is sad; a classmate is left out of a game; a classmate is
  hurt)

## Preparation

- Arrange a circle for gathering.
- Post the four caring steps where all can see them.

## Facilitator note

Empathy becomes care through action — and action is a skill to teach, model, and
practice (CASEL's relationship-skills competency). Teach the four steps as explicit
instruction with a worked example first, then guided practice (Kirschner, Sweller &
Clark, 2006). Keep scenes fictional and gentle; do not ask a learner to act out a
real hurt they are living. Protect two commitments: **care is for everyone** (no
"most caring" prize — care is not a competition), and **caring does not mean fixing**
(a child does not need to solve a big problem; sitting nearby and asking is already
caring). A non-speaking learner shows care by staying close, holding out a hand, or
signing "are you okay?"; a learner with a motor difference cares in their own way —
by speaking, by staying, or by directing a partner — and their care counts fully.
Read the procedure aloud and model each step.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Sit in a circle. Think: when you were sad, left out, or hurt,
   what did someone do that helped?
2. **Meet the four steps (10 min).** Learn the caring steps and act each one out:
   **notice** (look around — who needs a friend?); **ask** ("Are you okay?" in a soft
   voice); **stay** (sit or stand close, wait); **include** (invite them in). Say them
   together until you know them.
3. **Watch a worked example (10 min).** Two volunteers show the scene "a classmate is
   sad." Watch them use all four steps: notice, ask, stay, and then — when the friend
   is ready — include.
4. **Practice in threes (15 min).** In a group of three, act out each scene — sad,
   left out, hurt. One person feels, one person cares, one person watches and names
   the steps they saw. Swap so everyone tries each part.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: caring is small and doable. Notice. Ask. Stay.
   Include. You can do all four.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Model one step at a time; offer the exact question "Are you okay?" to
  copy. A non-speaking learner signs or holds out a hand for "stay."
- **Extension:** Invite learners to add a fifth step of their own (offer a comfort
  object, get help from an adult) and explain when they would use it.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Does the learner use at least two caring steps in a
  role-play scene?
- **Performance:** The learner shows care — by word, sign, or action — for a
  classmate in the scene.

## Home connection

At home, notice someone who is sad, left out, or hurt, and try the four steps —
notice, ask, stay, include — then tell a grown-up what you did.
