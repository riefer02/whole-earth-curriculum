# Lesson 3 — Two Feelings, Same Moment

## Summary

Learners explore a surprising idea: two people can be in the very same moment and
feel different things — and both feelings are real and true. They practice describing
two different feelings about one shared situation.

## Objectives

- Describe how two people in the same situation might feel differently.
  _(D02.S2.02.01)_

## Connection

It starts to rain during playtime. One child frowns — they wanted to run outside.
Another child smiles — they love the smell of rain and know the garden needed it.
Same rain, two feelings. A dog visits the class: one child is thrilled, another is
afraid. Both feelings are true. The same moment can feel very different to different
people, and noticing that helps us be kind — because we stop thinking "you should
feel what I feel" and start wondering "how do you feel?"

## Materials

- Situation picture cards (rain at playtime, a dog visiting, a new baby, moving
  house, a birthday cake)
- Feeling tokens (counters or paper circles)

## Preparation

- Arrange a circle for gathering.
- Set out the situation cards and feeling tokens where all can reach them.

## Facilitator note

Perspective-taking is a cornerstone of empathy and of social awareness (CASEL). The
point to protect: **different is not wrong.** A child who feels afraid of the dog is
not "overreacting"; a child who feels glad about rain is not "silly." Name both as
true, and never rank feelings as better or worse. This is also where the global lens
lives: people in different places may feel differently about the same thing — rain is
welcome where fields are dry, unwelcome where it floods — and each feeling makes
sense in its own life. Keep examples light; avoid a situation that a learner is
living through painfully. Every learner can take part: a non-speaking learner places
a token or points to a feeling face; a learner with low vision describes the feeling
in words while you describe the card aloud. Read the procedure aloud and model the
first example.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Sit in a circle. Think: have you ever felt one way about
   something while a friend felt the opposite? What was it?
2. **Meet the big idea (10 min).** Look at the rain card. One person says "I'm sad,"
   another says "I'm glad." Wonder together: can both be true? Yes — the same moment
   can hold two feelings, and neither is wrong. Say it together: "Same moment, two
   feelings, both true."
3. **Feelings detective (15 min).** For each situation card — the dog, the new baby,
   moving house, the birthday cake — place a feeling token to show how *you* would
   feel. Notice how the tokens land in different places. Take turns describing two
   different feelings and why each one makes sense.
4. **Pair share (10 min).** With a partner, pick one card and tell two feelings two
   different people might have. Listen to your partner's two — did they match yours
   or differ?
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: when someone feels differently, they are not wrong.
   Ask "how do you feel?" before you guess. That is how caring begins.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Limit to two feeling faces (sad/glad); model each card's two feelings
  aloud. A non-speaking learner points to a feeling face and places a token.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to think of a third feeling the same moment could
  hold, or a moment from their own life when two people felt differently.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner describe two different feelings for the
  same situation and say why each makes sense?
- **Peer (pair share):** Did the learner listen to their partner's two feelings and
  treat them as true?

## Home connection

At home, ask two family members how they feel about the same thing — the weather, a
food, a song — and notice how the same moment can hold two feelings.
