# Lesson 9 — Teamwork Rehearsal

## Summary

This rehearsal lesson brings two skills from earlier in the unit back together:
giving sincere encouragement (Lesson 1) and doing a part in a shared task (Lesson 7).
Learners use both at once in a small team challenge — finishing together, not
competing against other teams.

## Objectives

- Give a sincere compliment or word of encouragement to a classmate during an
  activity. _(D02.S1.02.01)_
- Cooperate with others to finish a task that no one could do alone.
  _(D02.S4.02.01)_

## Connection

Remember the cheers from our first lesson — "You can do it!", "Nice try!" — and the
feeling of lifting something together that no one could lift alone? Teams everywhere
put these two together: a boat crew rows in time *and* cheers the stroke; a harvest
is gathered by many hands *and* by voices calling each other on. Today you will be a
team: each person does a part, and everyone cheers everyone on.

## Materials

- Blocks, cups, or a relay ball
- A soft turn marker

## Preparation

- Set up one small challenge per team (build a tower where each adds one block; pass a
  ball down a line without dropping it; carry a shared item a short way together).
- Arrange a circle for the closing reflection.

## Facilitator note

This is deliberate **retrieval and spacing**: learners are asked to *recall and use*
skills from Lessons 1 and 7, not re-read them (the science-of-learning retrieval and
spacing commitments). Keep the challenge cooperative, not competitive: teams are not
ranked against each other, and there is no "winning team." Celebrate that every team
finished together and every voice cheered. Name the two skills explicitly as they
appear ("I heard a real cheer," "I saw you steady the tower"). Watch that quiet
learners get a turn to speak and to act; a learner who does not speak with their
mouth cheers with a sign, a clap, or a gesture. Read the procedure aloud; model one
cheer and one part before teams begin.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Sit in a circle. Think: what two skills did we learn earlier —
   in the first lesson and in the "Together We Can" lesson?
2. **Recall the two skills (10 min).** Say them together. **Cheering:** name something
   true and specific ("I like how you…", "You can do it!"). **Doing a part:** each
   person takes one part, no one takes over, and the team finishes together.
3. **Team challenge (20 min).** In your team, do the challenge — the tower, the
   passing game, or the carrying. As you work, do your part *and* cheer your teammates
   on with real, specific words.
4. **Reflect in a circle (10 min).** Pass the turn marker. Tell one cheer you gave and
   one part you did. Listen to the others.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: a team is people doing their parts and cheering each
   other on. You did both today.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer a one-part challenge and a cheer sentence starter; a non-speaking
  learner does their part and cheers with a clap or sign.
- **Extension:** Invite teams to try a harder challenge and name the new parts it
  needs before they begin.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Does the learner give a sincere cheer and do their part
  during the challenge?
- **Peer (check):** Did every team member get a turn to act and to be cheered?

## Home connection

At home, be a teammate: do your part in one family job and cheer on a family member
who is trying something hard — then tell how it felt.
