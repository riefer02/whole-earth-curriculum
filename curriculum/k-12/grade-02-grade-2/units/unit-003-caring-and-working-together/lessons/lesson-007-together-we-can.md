# Lesson 7 — Together We Can

## Summary

Learners experience cooperation directly: they try a task alone, find it too hard,
and finish it together, discovering that cooperating means each person does a part —
and no part is too small to matter.

## Objectives

- Cooperate with others to finish a task that no one could do alone.
  _(D02.S4.02.01)_

## Connection

Some things are simply too big for one pair of hands — a heavy bench, a full basket,
a wide cloth, a pile of stones. One person cannot lift the bench; two or three can.
One person cannot hold the sheet for the tent; four can. This is true everywhere: a
boat crew rows together, a harvest is gathered by many hands, builders raise a house
side by side. When each person does a part, the group can do what no one could do
alone. Today you will feel this with your own hands.

## Materials

- A large, heavy-but-safe shared item (a mat, a bench, a full basket, a big sheet)
- Blocks or cups for a group tower

## Preparation

- Choose a safe cooperative task — carrying a heavy item, or a group tower where each
  learner adds one piece in turn.
- Set out the materials and clear a safe space.

## Facilitator note

Cooperation is the heart of mutual aid, and the lesson to protect is that **every
part matters and no part ranks above another** (philosophy §8: mastery is individual,
success is collective). Do not crown a "leader" and do not sort children into "strong"
and "weak" helpers — name each contribution as real: the one who steadies, the one
who lifts, the one who guides, the one who calls the rhythm. Let learners *feel* the
"too hard alone" moment first; that concrete experience carries the concept. Keep
tasks safe and age-appropriate; no one is forced to lift beyond their ability. A
learner with a motor difference contributes by directing, steadying, or holding an
edge, and their part counts fully; a non-speaking learner contributes with gestures
and rhythm. Read the procedure aloud; model counting or calling a rhythm together.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Sit in a circle. Think: what is something too heavy or too big
   for one person to do alone?
2. **Try it alone (10 min).** Try the task by yourself — lift the bench, carry the
   basket, hold the whole sheet. Notice: it is too hard. Now try it with one helper,
   then with three or four. Feel the difference: more hands, easier work.
3. **Each does a part (15 min).** Do the task together. Talk it over first: who will
   steady, who will lift, who will guide, who will call the rhythm? Then do it, and
   name each person's part as you go.
4. **Group tower (15 min).** In your team, build a tower where each person adds one
   block in turn — no one skips, no one takes over. When it stands, notice: every
   piece had a part.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: alone it was too hard; together it was done. Each part
   mattered, and yours did too.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer a lighter or seated cooperative task; a learner who cannot lift
  steadies or guides. Keep groups small so every learner has a clear part.
- **Extension:** Invite teams to plan a *second* task that needs everyone, and name
  each person's part before they begin.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Does the learner contribute a part and finish the task
  with the group?
- **Performance:** The task completed together, with each learner able to name their
  own part.

## Home connection

At home, find one job that needs more than one person — carrying, folding, tidying —
and do it together, each taking a part, then tell how it went.
