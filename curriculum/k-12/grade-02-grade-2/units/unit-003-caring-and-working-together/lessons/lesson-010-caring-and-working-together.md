# Lesson 10 — Caring and Working Together

## Summary

This capstone gathers everything from the unit into one caring act. Learners plan and
carry out a small act of care for their class community — welcoming a newcomer,
tidying a shared space, making a celebration, or building something together — using
encouragement, words, noticing feelings, caring, welcoming, traditions, cooperation,
and helping along the way.

## Objectives

- Give a sincere compliment or word of encouragement to a classmate during an
  activity. _(D02.S1.02.01)_
- Resolve a simple disagreement with a classmate using words instead of grabbing or
  shouting. _(D02.S1.02.02)_
- Describe how two people in the same situation might feel differently.
  _(D02.S2.02.01)_
- Show caring when a classmate is sad, left out, or hurt. _(D02.S2.02.02)_
- Explain what makes a group feel welcoming and inclusive to everyone in it.
  _(D02.S3.02.01)_
- Describe a community tradition or celebration and what it means to the group.
  _(D02.S3.02.02)_
- Cooperate with others to finish a task that no one could do alone. _(D02.S4.02.01)_
- Explain why helping others helps everyone, including themselves. _(D02.S4.02.02)_

## Connection

All the skills in this unit fit together like tools in one box: cheer someone on,
solve a problem with words, notice that two people can feel differently, care when
someone hurts, welcome a newcomer, honor a tradition, do your part together, and
remember that helping helps everyone. Today you use the whole box at once to make
your class a kinder, more welcoming place — the same way people everywhere build
caring communities with many small acts.

## Materials

- A caring-act menu (welcome a newcomer, tidy a shared space, make a celebration,
  build something together)
- Caring-act supplies (paper, markers, cloth, or blocks — whatever the act needs)

## Preparation

- Prepare the menu of caring acts learners can choose from.
- Set out supplies and a simple plan sheet for each small group.

## Facilitator note

This is a performance of the whole unit, not a test. Keep it joyful and low-stakes.
Let learners choose their caring act; your job is to notice and name the skills as
they appear ("I heard a real cheer," "I saw you make room," "I noticed you both felt
differently and talked it out"). Celebrate effort, not perfection, and celebrate
cooperation, not ranking — there is no winning group. No learner must speak in front
of the whole group; sharing can be by drawing, action, or a partner speaking on their
behalf. Every culture builds community with small acts of care; name that as you
close. Read the procedure aloud; model choosing an act and naming one skill first.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Sit in a circle. Think: what is one small thing that would make
   our class kinder or more welcoming today?
2. **Pick a caring act (10 min).** With your group, choose one caring act from the
   menu. Talk it over: what will we do, and how will each of us help? Use encouraging
   words, take turns speaking, and solve any small disagreement with words.
3. **Do it together (25 min).** Carry out your caring act. While you work: cheer your
   teammates on, notice how they feel, make room for everyone, do your part, and
   remember that helping helps everyone. If someone feels left out or sad, notice,
   ask, stay, and include.
4. **Share back (10 min).** Gather in a circle. Each group shares what it did — with
   words, pictures, or by acting it out — and the class listens without interrupting.
   Name one skill you used today and one part you did.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: caring and working together is a practice we do every
   day — with cheers, words, care, welcome, and help. Today you did it all at once.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer a two-option menu and a picture-based plan sheet; assign a
  "helper buddy" to each learner. A non-speaking learner contributes by acting,
  drawing, or signing.
- **Extension:** Invite groups to plan a *second* caring act for a community beyond
  the classroom (a sibling group, a neighbor) and describe how the same skills apply.

## Assessment

- **Performance (observation):** Does the learner use encouragement, caring words, and
  a part in the shared act during the caring act?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The group's plan sheet and a drawing of the caring act,
  kept as the unit's culminating artifact.
