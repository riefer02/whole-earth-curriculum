# Lesson 2 — Solving It with Words

## Summary

Learners learn a four-step way to solve a small disagreement — stop and breathe, use
words, listen, make a plan — and practice it with a shared object, choosing words
instead of grabbing or shouting.

## Objectives

- Resolve a simple disagreement with a classmate using words instead of grabbing or
  shouting. _(D02.S1.02.02)_

## Connection

Have you ever wanted the same thing as someone else — the same swing, the last piece
of fruit, the same turn with a toy? It happens to everyone, everywhere. The good news
is that there is a way out that keeps everyone's feelings safe: use words instead of
grabbing or shouting. "Can I have a turn when you are done?" "Let's share." "Let's
make a plan." Today you will learn the steps and practice them.

## Materials

- A solving-steps chart (stop and breathe, use words, listen, make a plan)
- A shared item or two to practice with (a ball, a crayon box, a picture book)

## Preparation

- Arrange a circle for gathering.
- Post the four solving steps where all can see them.

## Facilitator note

Conflict is not bad behavior; it is a normal part of living together, and solving it
is a skill to teach, not a misdeed to punish (CASEL's relationship-skills
competency). Teach the four steps as explicit, worked instruction — model the whole
sequence first, then guide, then let learners try (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006).
Keep the disagreement small and fictional in role-play so no learner relives a real
hurt. Frame "use words" as naming your feeling and what you need ("I feel upset. I
need a turn."), never as blaming ("You always…"). Watch for the learner who only ever
gives in — a fair plan is one where both get a turn or a share, not one where one
child always yields. Learners who do not speak with their mouth can use sign, a
picture card, or a gesture for each step. Read the procedure aloud and model each
step.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Sit in a circle. Think: when did you and a friend want the same
   thing? What happened next?
2. **Meet the four steps (10 min).** Learn the solving steps and act each one out:
   **one, stop and breathe** (hands on your belly, breathe in and out); **two, use
   words** ("I feel… I need…"); **three, listen** (cup your ear and look at the
   speaker); **four, make a plan** (take turns, share, or ask an adult for help).
   Say them together until you know them.
3. **Watch a worked example (10 min).** Two volunteers (or the facilitator and a
   puppet) both want the same ball. Watch them solve it: they stop, use words, listen,
   and make a plan — "I'll roll it first, then you."
4. **Practice in pairs (15 min).** With a partner, act out wanting the same thing.
   Use all four steps to reach a fair plan. Swap parts so each of you tries both
   sides.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: grabbing and shouting end in hurt; words end in a
   plan. You can solve a small problem with words.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Model one step at a time; offer the exact sentence "Can I have a turn
  when you are done?" to copy. A non-speaking learner signs or points to a picture
  for each step.
- **Extension:** Invite pairs to solve a slightly harder scenario (three people, one
  swing) and explain their plan.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Does the learner use words — not grabbing or shouting —
  to reach a plan in the role-play?
- **Peer (check):** Did both partners get a turn or a share in the plan they made?

## Home connection

At home, the next time you and someone want the same thing, try the four steps —
stop, use words, listen, make a plan — and tell a grown-up what you planned together.
