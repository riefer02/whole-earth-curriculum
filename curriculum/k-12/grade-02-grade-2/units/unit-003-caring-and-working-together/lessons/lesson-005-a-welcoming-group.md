# Lesson 5 — A Welcoming Group

## Summary

Learners explore what makes a group feel welcoming and inclusive — saying hi, learning
a name, making room, inviting someone in — and build a class welcome picture that
shows everyone belongs. Welcoming is seen as something every group, everywhere, does
in its own way.

## Objectives

- Explain what makes a group feel welcoming and inclusive to everyone in it.
  _(D02.S3.02.01)_

## Connection

Remember your first day in a new place — a new class, a new neighborhood, a new team.
What made you feel like you belonged? Probably someone said hello, learned your name,
made a space for you, and invited you into what they were doing. That is what a
welcoming group does. In every part of the world, people welcome newcomers in their
own way — a bow, a handshake, sharing tea, a song of welcome. Today you will name
what welcome looks like and build a picture of a group where everyone belongs.

## Materials

- Chart paper and markers
- Welcome-steps cards ("say hi", "learn my name", "make room", "invite me in")

## Preparation

- Arrange a circle for gathering.
- Set out chart paper and markers for the welcome picture.

## Facilitator note

Belonging is a deep human need, and inclusion is a practice, not a slogan (CASEL's
social-awareness competency). Teach the four welcome steps explicitly, with a worked
example, then let learners build the picture together (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark,
2006). Keep two commitments in view: **inclusion means everyone, not most** — name
language, ability, family shape, and body differences as reasons to adapt our welcome,
never as reasons to exclude; and **welcoming is mutual** — the group welcomes the
newcomer, and the newcomer's own way of greeting is honored too. A newcomer should
never be pressed to tell their story or name their difference; being welcomed is
enough. A non-speaking learner contributes a welcome with a sign, a wave, or a
picture; a learner with low vision describes what welcome sounds and feels like. Read
the procedure aloud; model each step with voice and body.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Sit in a circle. Think: when did you feel truly welcome
   somewhere? What did people do?
2. **Meet the welcome steps (10 min).** Learn and act out the steps: **say hi** (a
   wave or a smile); **learn my name** (say it, and try to say theirs); **make room**
   (open the circle and let someone in); **invite me in** ("Come join us!"). Wonder:
   how do people welcome others in different places and languages?
3. **Practice a welcome (10 min).** Pretend one of you is new. The rest of the group
   practices all four steps — say hi, learn the name, make room, invite in. Swap so a
   different person is new each time.
4. **Build the welcome picture (15 min).** Together, draw a picture of a welcoming
   group. Add everyone — every skin tone, every way of moving, every way of speaking.
   Label the welcome steps on your picture.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: a welcoming group is one where everyone belongs — and
   you are a part of making that true.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Model each step one at a time with a gesture; a non-speaking learner
  contributes a wave, a sign, or a drawn welcome. Offer sentence starters ("Hi, I'm…
  What's your name?").
- **Extension:** Invite learners to learn and share one way a different culture or
  language says welcome, and add it to the picture.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name and show at least two things that
  make a group welcoming?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The class welcome picture, with the learner's contribution
  visible.

## Home connection

At home, notice a time someone new is around — a visitor, a new neighbor — and try
the welcome steps: say hi, learn their name, make room, invite them in.
