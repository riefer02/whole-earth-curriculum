# Lesson 4 — A Seed Grows

## Summary

Every living thing grows and changes in a repeating pattern called a **life cycle**.
Learners put the stages of a plant's life cycle in order — seed, sprout, seedling,
flower, fruit with new seeds — and plant a real seed they will watch and record over
the coming weeks. They discover that a life cycle is a circle: the plant makes seeds,
and those seeds make new plants.

## Objectives

- Describe the life cycle of a plant or animal (such as seed to plant or egg to adult)
  in order. _(D06.S2.02.01)_

## Connection

You have seen a seed — a little hard thing that looks asleep. But inside it is a whole
plant waiting. Put it in soil, give it water and light, and it **grows**: first a tiny
sprout, then leaves, then a flower, then a fruit with new seeds inside. Then those seeds
can start again. That circle of growing is a **life cycle** — and you can watch it happen
with your own two eyes, wherever you live.

## Materials

- Pictures or cards of plant life-cycle stages
- A real seed to plant (a bean or another fast-growing seed)
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Prepare or draw the stage cards (seed, sprout, seedling, plant, flower, fruit with
  seeds) and shuffle them.
- Set up a planting spot with soil and a cup or small pot per learner; keep water and
  light within reach.
- Plan to revisit the planted seeds across the rest of the unit and beyond.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). A life cycle is a *circle*, so the order
can start anywhere — but at Grade 2 we anchor it at the seed and move forward, so the
learner has a clear sequence to hold. Model the ordering once (worked example), then let
learners re-order their own shuffled set. Planting a real seed makes the abstract cycle
concrete, and revisiting it over the weeks builds in **spacing** — real growth takes
time, which is itself the lesson. Note honestly that some plants are grown for food and
medicine in different ways around the world, and that caring for a seed is caring for a
living thing (a small ethical step). If a seed does not sprout, that too is information,
not failure — wonder about why.

## Procedure

1. **Gather and remember (5 min).** You have predicted, tested, and sorted. Now look at
   a seed and wonder: what is inside it, waiting to grow?
2. **Meet the stages (10 min).** A plant's life goes in a circle, called a **life
   cycle**. Meet the stages in order: **seed** → **sprout** → **seedling** → **flower**
   → **fruit with new seeds** → and back to a seed again. Say each one and point to its
   picture.
3. **Order the cycle (10 min).** Take a shuffled set of cards and put them in the right
   order. Check with a partner: did we both start at the seed and go around the same
   way?
4. **Plant a seed (15 min).** Plant your own seed in soil. Give it a little water and set
   it where it can get light. This is the very first stage of the cycle, happening in
   your own hands.
5. **Draw the cycle (7 min).** Draw the life cycle as a circle with the stages numbered
   1 to 5. Keep it — you will add to it as your seed grows.
6. **Close (3 min).** A life cycle is a circle that keeps going: seeds make plants, and
   plants make seeds. You have started one today.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Order just three stages (seed, sprout, plant) and say them with a
  partner.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Point to and move the stage cards into order; a partner
  says each name.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Feel a real seed, a sprout, and a leaf; order the
  stages by touch and description with a partner.
- **Extension:** Describe an animal's life cycle too (egg → caterpillar → chrysalis →
  butterfly, or egg → chick → hen) and tell how a plant's and an animal's cycle are
  alike and different.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner order the plant life-cycle stages and
  name them (or point to them)?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The numbered life-cycle circle drawing, kept to update as the
  seed grows.

## Home connection

Plant a seed at home in an old cup or a patch of soil. Water it a little and watch it
each day. Tell a grown-up what stage it is in now — seed, sprout, or seedling.
