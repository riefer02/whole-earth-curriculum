# Lesson 8 — The Sun Warms the Earth

## Summary

The Sun is a star — a giant ball of light and heat — and it gives the Earth two gifts:
**light** and **warmth**. Learners feel the difference between sun and shade, connect
those two gifts to the living things that need them, and learn that plants grow and
animals stay warm because of the Sun's energy. They also hold the Sun as a thing to
*wonder* at — where it came from is a big question with many stories, held as stories.

## Objectives

- Describe how the Sun warms the Earth and gives light that living things need.
  _(D06.S4.02.01)_

## Connection

Step into the sun and you feel warmth on your skin; step into the shade and it is cooler.
That warmth, and the light that helps you see, both come from one place: the **Sun**, a
star so big it could hold the Earth many, many times over. Without the Sun's light and
warmth, plants could not grow, and animals and people could not live. The Sun is far
away, but its gifts reach us every day.

## Materials

- Pictures of the Sun and Earth
- A sunny spot or a warm surface
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Find a place where learners can safely feel sun and shade (or set up a safe lamp to
  model it).
- Prepare a picture showing the Sun sending light and warmth to the Earth.
- Review safety: never look straight at the Sun; it can hurt eyes.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Teach the **safety rule early and
gently**: never look straight at the Sun — it is too bright for eyes. Keep the science
**concrete**: feel sun vs. shade; find a plant in the light; notice warmth on a dark
surface. The Sun is a star, and its light and heat travel about 150 million kilometres to
reach us — a fact to wonder at, offered lightly, not to memorize. Hold origins with
openness per `docs/philosophy.md` §11: people have told many stories about where the Sun
and the Earth came from, and scientists have a story too — they are all stories to wonder
at together, and we rank none above the others. Distinguish evidence from values: "the
Sun warms the Earth" is something we can feel and agree on; "I love sunny days" is a
feeling. In hot places the Sun can also be too strong — sunburn and heat are real, and
staying safe in strong sun (shade, cover, water) is a fair, hopeful note, not fear.

## Procedure

1. **Gather and remember (5 min).** Yesterday we met three kinds of energy. Today we
   meet the great giver of two of them: the **Sun**.
2. **Feel sun and shade (10 min).** Stand (or reach) into the sun, then into the shade.
   What do you notice? The sun side is **warmer**, the shade side is cooler. The Sun's
   **warmth** reaches your skin. (Never look straight at the Sun — keep your eyes safe.)
3. **The Sun's two gifts (10 min).** The Sun gives the Earth two gifts: **light** and
   **warmth**. Point to each in the picture. Which one lets you see? Which one warms
   your skin?
4. **Who needs the Sun? (10 min).** Think of a plant, an animal, and a person. What does
   the Sun give each one? "The plant needs light to grow. The cat needs warmth. I need
   both." Draw the Sun shining on a growing plant.
5. **Wonder about the Sun (10 min).** The Sun is a star, far, far away — so far that its
   light takes a long, long time to reach us. Where did the Sun come from? People have
   told many stories, and scientists have a story too. We hold them all as stories to
   wonder at together.
6. **Close (5 min).** The Sun gives the Earth light and warmth, and living things need
   both. Keep safe in its strong light — and keep wondering about it.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Match the two gifts (light, warmth) to a plant and a person using the
  frame "The Sun gives ___ and ___."
- **Support (non-speaking):** Point to the Sun, then to the warm skin and the bright
  light, then to a growing plant.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Feel sun vs. shade and describe the difference; a
  partner names the light that others see.
- **Extension:** Predict which feels warmer — a dark cloth or a light cloth left in the
  sun — then test it with a hand and tell what you found.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name the Sun's two gifts (light and
  warmth) and connect at least one to a living thing that needs it?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The "Sun shining on a growing plant" drawing, labeled light
  and warmth.

## Home connection

At home, feel a sunny spot and a shady spot with your hand. Tell a grown-up which is
warmer and why. Then watch a plant in the light — that is the Sun's gift at work.
