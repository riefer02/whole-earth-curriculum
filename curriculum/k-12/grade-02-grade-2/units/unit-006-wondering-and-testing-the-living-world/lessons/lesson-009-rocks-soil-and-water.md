# Lesson 9 — Rocks, Soil, and Water

## Summary

The land we stand on is made of three things a learner can touch: **rock** (the solid
Earth below), **soil** (the soft layer on top where plants grow), and **water** (the
rivers, lakes, and rain that flow through it). Learners feel and describe each, see how
they fit together in mountains, valleys, and rivers, and connect the land to the living
things that call it home. Where the land came from is held as a wonder with many stories,
scientific and cultural, side by side.

## Objectives

- Describe rocks, soil, and water and how they make up the land we live on.
  _(D06.S4.02.02)_

## Connection

Look down: the ground under your feet is the **land** you live on. Kick it gently — that
is **soil**. Under the soil, deep down, is solid **rock**. And flowing through it all, in
rain and rivers and puddles, is **water**. Rock, soil, and water are not three separate
things — they are the one land we stand on, and the home of every plant and creature on
it.

## Materials

- Samples of rock, soil, and water (a stone, a cup of soil, a cup of water)
- A picture of the land (mountains, valleys, and a river)
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Gather safe samples of rock, soil, and water for learners to touch and describe.
- Choose or draw a picture of the land showing mountains, a valley, and a river.
- Plan one worked example: touch soil, name it, say where it sits in the land.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Anchor everything in **touch**: rock,
soil, and water are concrete, and feeling them is the lesson. The gentle scientific idea
to offer is that soil is made of tiny pieces of worn rock mixed with old, broken-down
living things, and that water slowly wears rock down — but keep it as a wonder to
explore, not a definition to memorize; the full rock and water cycles arrive in later
grades. Hold origins with openness per `docs/philosophy.md` §11: many peoples have
stories about how the mountains, rivers, and land began, and geologists have a story
about very long, slow change — we hold them side by side and rank none above the others.
A light ethical and environmental thread: the land feeds us and gives us water, so we
care for it — this is a value we share, not a fact we prove. Touch with clean hands; use
safe, clean samples.

## Procedure

1. **Gather and remember (5 min).** You have met the Sun and the living things that need
   it. Now look down: what is the land you stand on made of?
2. **Touch rock, soil, and water (15 min).** Feel each one. **Rock** is hard and solid.
   **Soil** is soft and crumbly — plants grow in it. **Water** is wet and flows. Say one
   describing word for each.
3. **See how they fit (10 min).** Look at the picture of the land. Point to the solid
   rock in the mountains, the soil in the valleys where plants grow, and the water in the
   river. The land is all three, together.
4. **Wonder about the land (10 min).** Where did the mountains and rivers come from? Many
   peoples have told stories about how the land began, and scientists have a story about
   slow, long change. We hold them all as stories to wonder at together — and we can see
   the land changing slowly, a little at a time.
5. **Draw the layers (7 min).** Draw the land you live on: rock below, soil above, and
   water flowing through. Label the three.
6. **Close (3 min).** Rock, soil, and water make the land — and the land is our home and
   the home of every living thing. We care for it.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Touch each of the three and use the frame "Rock is ___, soil is ___,
  water is ___."
- **Support (non-speaking):** Point to the rock, soil, and water samples and to each in
  the land picture.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Describe the three by touch and by a partner's spoken
  description; feel water's coolness and soil's crumble.
- **Extension:** Use a hand lens to look closely at soil and tell what tiny things you
  find — bits of rock, bits of old plants.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name the three parts of the land and
  describe each (or point to each) and say how they fit together?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The labeled "layers of the land" drawing.

## Home connection

At home or outside, touch three things: a stone (rock), a pinch of earth (soil), and a
little water. Tell a grown-up which is hardest, which is softest, and which is wettest —
you just described the land.
