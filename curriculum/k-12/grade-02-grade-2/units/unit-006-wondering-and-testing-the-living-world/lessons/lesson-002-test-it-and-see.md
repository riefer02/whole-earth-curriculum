# Lesson 2 — Test It and See

## Summary

The day before, learners made a prediction. Today they **test** it by doing something,
watch what happens, and **record** the result — then tell plainly whether the result
**matched** their prediction or not. They learn the scientist's honesty: evidence is
what really happened, even when it is not what we hoped. A "not matched" result is a
gift of new knowing, never a failure.

## Objectives

- Make a prediction, test it by doing, and tell whether the result matched the
  prediction. _(D06.S1.02.01)_

## Connection

You predicted the leaf would float. So you drop it in the water and you **watch**. It
floats — your prediction **matched**. You predicted the stone would float too, but it
sank — it did **not match**. A scientist does not hide a "not matched" result; she says
it out loud, because now she knows something new. The test, and what really happened, is
the **evidence**.

## Materials

- One safe test to run (a float-or-sink bowl of water, a seed in a cup, or a toy car and
  a ramp)
- A "prediction and result" record sheet
- Pencils and crayons

## Preparation

- Set up the test so everyone can see it and, where possible, try it themselves.
- Prepare the record sheet with "I predicted…" / "What happened…" and a "matched / did
  not match" line.
- Review safety: water, ramps, and small objects need a clear, safe space.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The heart of it is a scientist's
**honesty about evidence**: hold up a "did not match" result with the same warmth as a
"matched" one — "wonderful, now we know." Never shame a wrong prediction; a wrong
prediction is what makes the test worth doing. Model the record once (worked example),
then guide pairs through it, then let learners try alone. Retrieval is built in: begin by
asking learners to recall their prediction from yesterday before testing it. Where
learners cannot write, they may draw, point, or tell a partner who scribes. Some
predictions take longer than one day to test (a seed growing); for those, set up a
"watch and wait" record that learners revisit across the unit — that is real science
too, and it spaces the learning.

## Procedure

1. **Gather and remember (5 min).** Yesterday you made a prediction. Say it again — what
   did you predict, and why?
2. **Do the test (20 min).** Carry out the test you planned. Drop the leaf, roll the car,
   plant the seed. Watch closely. What actually happened? Say it: "It floated." "It
   sank." "It did not roll far."
3. **Record the result (10 min).** On your record sheet, draw or write what happened.
   Then look at your prediction: did the result **match** it, or **not match**? Circle
   or tick the right box.
4. **Share the evidence (10 min).** Tell a partner your result with the honest words: "I
   predicted it would float. It floated — it matched." Or "…it sank — it did not match,
   and now I know it is heavier than I thought."
5. **Close (5 min).** A test turns a guess into **evidence** — what really happened.
   Whether it matched or not, you learned something true. That is science.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Run one test together and scribe the record for the learner while they
  point or tell.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Point to the matched / did-not-match box or to a picture of
  what happened.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Test something you can hear or touch (a bell that
  rings, a stone that sinks with a plop) and report by telling.
- **Extension:** Run the test a second time and compare — was the result the same both
  times? Why might a scientist test more than once?

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner carry out the test and tell whether the
  result matched the prediction?
- **Self:** The learner marks their own "matched / did not match" honestly and can say
  one thing they now know.
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The prediction-and-result record sheet.

## Home connection

At home, make a tiny prediction and test it — "I predict this spoon will sink." Do it
with a grown-up and tell them whether it matched. Being wrong is welcome there too.
