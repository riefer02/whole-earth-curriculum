# Lesson 5 — A Habitat Is a Home

## Summary

A **habitat** is a living thing's home — the place where it finds its food, water,
shelter, and a place to raise its young. Learners observe and describe a habitat (a
pond, a forest, a desert, a savanna, a tundra, or a real patch of ground outside) and
name the living things that call it home, and the things they need there. They learn
that homes differ all over the world, and each one fits the living things that live in
it.

## Objectives

- Observe and describe a habitat and the living things that call it home.
  _(D06.S2.02.02)_

## Connection

Think of your own home — the place where you sleep, eat, and are safe. Every living
thing has a home too. A frog's home is a **pond**; a cactus's home is the dry **desert**;
a bird's home is a **tree**; a zebra's home is the wide **savanna**; a polar bear's home
is the cold, icy **tundra**. That home is called a **habitat** — the place that gives a
living thing its food, water, shelter, and a safe place to raise its young.

## Materials

- Pictures of different habitats (a pond, a forest, a desert, a meadow, a seashore)
- Picture cards of living things (a frog, fish, bird, cactus, camel, tree, and others)
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Choose one habitat everyone can observe or look at closely (a real place outside, or a
  clear picture).
- Set out the living-thing cards to match to each habitat.
- Prepare to ask the guiding question: "Who lives here, and what does this home give
  them?"

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Keep it **concrete before abstract**:
begin with the learner's own home, then widen to the homes of other living things.
Model describing one habitat aloud — "this pond has water, mud, and plants; the frog
finds food and shelter here" — then let learners try. Use habitats from many biomes
(pond, forest, desert, seashore, meadow, savanna, rainforest, tundra) so no single
landscape or climate is treated as the default, and learners in Lagos, Lima, or Lahore
can find a home that looks like the one they know. A light ethical thread: a habitat is not ours to spoil — caring for a home means
caring for its living things. Where learners cannot go outside, a clear picture or a
story works just as well; where they can, a few minutes of close looking outdoors is the
richest version. Retrieval: link back to Lesson 3's sorting by "where it lives" — this
is that idea, grown.

## Procedure

1. **Gather and remember (5 min).** Last lesson we sorted by "where it lives." Today we
   give that place a name: a **habitat**.
2. **Name the parts of a home (10 min).** What does a home give you? Food, water, a safe
   place to sleep, a place to be with your family. A habitat gives living things the same
   things.
3. **Meet a habitat (15 min).** Look at one habitat together — a pond, a forest, or a
   real patch of ground outside. Say what you see: water? soil? plants? shade? Then name
   the living things that call it home: "The frog lives here. The fish lives here." What
   does this home give each one?
4. **Match living things to homes (10 min).** With the cards, match each living thing to
   its habitat. Tell a partner why it fits: "The cactus fits the desert because it can
   live with very little water."
5. **Draw one habitat (7 min).** Draw one habitat with its living things and label them —
   the water, the shelter, the food.
6. **Close (3 min).** A habitat is a home, and every living thing has one. Different
   homes, all over the world — each one just right for the ones who live there.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Describe one habitat with three named living things, using the frame "The
  ___ lives here, and it finds ___."
- **Support (non-speaking):** Point to the living things in a habitat picture and to the
  food, water, and shelter they use.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Describe a habitat you can hear and touch (a pond you
  can listen to, moss you can feel) with a partner naming each part.
- **Extension:** Compare two habitats — a desert and a pond — and tell how their living
  things and needs differ.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner observe a habitat and name two or more
  living things that call it home, and something the home gives them?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The labeled "one habitat" drawing.

## Home connection

At home or outside, pick one small habitat — a tree, a puddle, a patch of grass — and
watch it quietly. Tell a grown-up every living thing you found there and what the home
gives it.
