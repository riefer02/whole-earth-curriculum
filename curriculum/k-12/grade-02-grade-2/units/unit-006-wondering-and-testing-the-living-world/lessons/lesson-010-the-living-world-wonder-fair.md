# Lesson 10 — The Living World Wonder Fair

## Summary

A capstone celebration: learners gather everything they predicted, tested, sorted, and
found across the unit, share it with one another, and name the wonders they will carry
forward. This is a chance to remember, to teach each other, and to celebrate how much
they have grown as scientists — together, with no ranking.

## Objectives

- Review and show learning across all four strands of the unit. _(D06.S1.02.01,
  D06.S1.02.02, D06.S2.02.01, D06.S2.02.02, D06.S3.02.01, D06.S3.02.02, D06.S4.02.01,
  D06.S4.02.02)_

## Connection

Think back over these weeks: you turned wonder into a **prediction**, tested it and told
whether it **matched**, sorted things and explained how, followed a plant's **life
cycle**, named a **habitat** and its living things, matched **materials** to their jobs,
sensed sound, light, and heat as **energy**, felt the **Sun's** gifts, and touched the
rock, soil, and water of the **land**. You have become a scientist — someone who
wonders, predicts, tests, and finds out. Today you celebrate that, together.

## Materials

- Learners' own work from the unit (prediction-and-result records, sort drawings,
  life-cycle circles, habitat drawings, material matches, energy charts, Sun and land
  drawings)
- A display space (table, mat, or wall)
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Help learners gather their work from the unit.
- Set up a display space and decide how sharing will flow (gallery walk, circle share, or
  wonder walk).

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). This is a **celebration of mastery, not a
competition** — there are no winners, no rankings, no "best" work. Every learner shares;
every share is welcomed exactly as it is. Keep praise about effort and wonder, not
comparison ("you tested so carefully," not "yours is the best"). If a learner has gaps or
"not yet" moments, this is not the time to surface them — the fair is about what they
*did* learn and what they *still wonder*. Retrieval is built in: recalling and explaining
to a peer is the deepest kind of remembering, spaced and interleaved across the whole
unit. Honor every body: sharing may be by speaking, pointing, signing, acting, or simply
displaying work; a learner who prefers not to speak can point to their work while a peer
or adult reads a label. End by gathering the unit's questions — "what do you still
wonder?" — and honor that some questions we cannot answer yet, and that is wonderful.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** You are a scientist now. What is one thing you remember from this
   unit — something you predicted, tested, sorted, or found out?
2. **Set up your share (10 min).** Gather your work from the unit and choose the piece
   you are most proud of, or the one that holds your favorite wonder. Put it in the
   display space.
3. **The Wonder Fair (20 min).** Visit each other's work. When it is your turn to share,
   tell (or point to or show) two things: one thing you *predicted and found out*, and
   one thing you *still wonder about*. Listen to your friends with kind ears. Ask each
   other questions.
4. **One more wonder (10 min).** On fresh paper, draw or write your biggest question to
   carry into the rest of the year: "I still wonder…" Add it to the wonder wall or wonder
   book if you have one.
5. **Close (5 min).** Look at all we did together. You wondered, you predicted, you
   tested, you saw. Science is wondering and finding out — and you are good at it. Keep
   wondering.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Choose one piece and share one sentence ("I predicted…" or "I found
  out…") with a partner first.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Display work and point to it; a peer or adult reads the
  label aloud.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Share by telling and listening; a peer or adult
  describes each displayed piece aloud as you visit the fair.
- **Extension:** Write or draw three "I wonder" questions and star the one you most want
  to explore next.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name one thing they learned and one thing
  they still wonder, across the unit?
- **Self / peer:** Learners tell one another one thing they liked about each other's
  sharing.
- **Portfolio:** The gathered unit work plus the final "I still wonder…" page, as a
  record of growth.

## Home connection

Bring your "I still wonder…" question home and ask a grown-up what *they* wonder about.
Wonder together — and keep watching the living world.
