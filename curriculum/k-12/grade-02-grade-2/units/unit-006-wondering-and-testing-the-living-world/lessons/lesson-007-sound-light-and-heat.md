# Lesson 7 — Sound, Light, and Heat

## Summary

Energy is the power to make things happen — and three kinds of it, **sound**, **light**,
and **heat**, are right there in a learner's own body to sense: we **hear** sound, **see**
light, and **feel** heat. Learners find and sort examples of each, and discover that
sound comes from things **vibrating** (shaking back and forth), while light and heat
often come from the Sun or from things people make. Energy moves and changes things —
and we can observe it.

## Objectives

- Describe how sound, light, and heat are forms of energy we can observe.
  _(D06.S3.02.02)_

## Connection

Clap your hands — you **hear** sound. Step into the sun — you **see** light and **feel**
heat. These three are not just feelings; they are **energy** at work. Energy is the power
to make something happen: sound shakes the air to your ear, light lets your eyes see,
and heat warms your skin. You can sense all three with your own body, right now.

## Materials

- Things that make sound, light, and heat safely (a bell or a drum, a lamp or a window, a
  warm — not hot — surface or the sun on skin)
- A three-column chart ("sound / light / heat")
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Gather safe sound makers and a source of light and gentle warmth.
- Draw the three-column chart.
- Review safety: nothing hot enough to burn; no loud sounds near ears.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Keep energy **concrete**: name the sense
that tells us about each form — ear (sound), eye (light), skin (heat). The one careful
scientific step is that **sound comes from vibration** — a drum's skin shakes, a ruler
twanged on a desk wiggles, your throat hums when you touch it; you can offer this as a
wonder ("what do you feel on the drum when it booms?") rather than a definition to
memorize. Heat and light we describe by what they *do* (warm, brighten) at this age —
their deeper story (electromagnetic waves, molecules) waits for later grades. Model the
three-column sort once (worked example), then let learners find their own examples.
Distinguish evidence from values: "the Sun warms our skin" is something we can observe
and agree on; "I like sunny days" is a feeling, not evidence — both are fine, and they are
different. A technology and environment lens: people everywhere have made tools for
sound, light, and heat — drums, lamps, fires — and the Sun gives light and warmth for
free.

## Procedure

1. **Gather and remember (5 min).** You can hear, see, and feel. Today we meet three
   kinds of **energy** that come to those senses: **sound**, **light**, and **heat**.
2. **Sense each energy (15 min).** Ring the bell or tap the drum — that is **sound**, and
   you hear it. Look at the lamp or the window — that is **light**, and you see it. Hold
   your hand near the warmth (not touching anything hot) — that is **heat**, and you feel
   it. Say each one and the sense that catches it.
3. **Wonder about sound (10 min).** Tap the drum gently and touch its skin — do you feel
   it shake? That shaking is called **vibrating**, and vibrating is what makes sound. Hum
   and touch your throat — feel the vibration?
4. **Sort examples (10 min).** Think of more examples and put each into the chart:
   thunder and birdsong under **sound**; the Sun and lamps under **light**; the Sun and a
   warm bowl under **heat**. Draw one example in each column.
5. **Close (5 min).** Sound, light, and heat are energy we can observe with our own
   bodies. You carry the tools to sense them everywhere you go.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Match one example to each form using the frame "I hear ___, I see ___, I
  feel ___."
- **Support (non-speaking):** Point to the ear, eye, or skin and to the sound, light, or
  heat source.
- **Support (Deaf/HH):** Focus on light and heat you can see and feel, and on feeling a
  drum or speaker's vibration through the hands.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Meet sound by hearing it and feeling the drum or your
  own throat vibrate; meet heat by touch. For light, feel the Sun's warmth on your skin
  (light you can feel) and have a partner describe what is bright and dim, while you name
  the sound and heat you sense.
- **Extension:** Predict whether a big drum makes a bigger or smaller vibration than a
  small one, then test it gently with a fingertip.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name the three forms of energy and the
  sense (or body part) that observes each, with one example apiece?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The three-column "sound / light / heat" chart with one drawn
  example each.

## Home connection

At home, sit still for one minute and find one sound, one light, and one heat. Tell a
grown-up which sense caught each one. You are sensing energy.
