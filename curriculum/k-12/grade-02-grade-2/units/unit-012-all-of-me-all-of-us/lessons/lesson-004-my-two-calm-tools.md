# Lesson 4 — My Two Calm Tools

## Summary

Learners gather the year's calming strategies — balloon breath, counting to five,
finding a safe space, shaking it out, naming it with words — and choose the two that
help them most. They make a personal "calm card" and practice *choosing* a tool on
their own, pausing before they react. This is the year's self-regulation work, gathered
into something a child can carry forward.

## Objectives

- Name two strategies for managing strong feelings and choose one to use on their own.
  _(D01.S3.02.01)_

## Connection

A strong feeling is like a big wave — it can knock you over, or you can learn to ride
it. Everyone, in every place, has ways of meeting a big wave: some people breathe
slowly, some count, some pray or sing, some walk it off, some find a quiet spot. All
year you have collected calm tools of your own. Today you pick your two best ones, so
that when a strong feeling comes, you do not have to wait for a grown-up — you can
choose your own tool.

## Materials

- A "My Calm Card" template
- Crayons and pencils

## Preparation

- Prepare the calm card template with two spaces: "my tool 1" and "my tool 2."
- Have the year's list of calm tools ready on a chart (balloon breath, count to five,
  safe space, shake it out, name it with words, ask for help).
- Arrange a circle for practicing the tools together.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It gathers the year's self-regulation
work and gives it into the learner's own hands: **a strong feeling is okay, and the
learner can choose how to meet it.** No tool is better than another — the "right" tool
is the one that helps *this* child, and every child may pick a different one. Honor a
child's own invention as a real tool (a song, a squeeze, a hum). Practice each tool
slowly and without pressure; a learner who does not want to close their eyes or be
still may choose a moving tool (shake it out, walk, press feet into the floor). For a
learner who is blind or has low vision, describe each tool in body terms so it can be
felt. For a learner who is Deaf or hard of hearing, use visual and felt cues — a
counting hand, a breathing balloon drawn and shown — rather than a spoken chime. The
point is a tool the child can reach for *on their own*.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Remember the calm tools you met this year: balloon breath,
   counting to five, a safe space, shaking it out, naming it with words, asking for
   help. Say them together.
2. **Try them again (15 min).** Practice each tool as a group. Breathe in like a
   balloon growing big, and out as it grows small. Count slowly to five on your
   fingers. Notice how your body feels calmer each time. Which two help *you* the
   most?
3. **Choose your two (10 min).** On your calm card, write or draw the two tools that
   work best for you. These are *your* tools — no two cards need to be the same.
4. **Practice choosing on your own (10 min).** Close your eyes or look down. Think of a
   strong feeling you sometimes have. You cannot always wait for a grown-up — so
   *pause*, and choose one of your two tools, all by yourself. Take one slow breath or
   one slow count.
5. **Close (5 min).** A strong feeling is okay. What matters is that you now have two
   tools, and you know how to choose one on your own. That is a grown-up power.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Choose from two picture tools instead of a list; practice with a
  partner modeling each step.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Point to or draw a tool; a partner names it and helps
  practice it.
- **Support (Deaf/hard-of-hearing):** Use visual and felt cues — a counting hand, a
  drawn balloon — rather than a spoken chime.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Describe each tool in body terms and practice by
  feel; make the card tactually.
- **Extension:** Write *when* you would use each tool (before a hard task, after a
  disagreement) next to each drawing.

## Assessment

- **Formative (self + observation):** Can the learner name two strategies, and — when a
  strong feeling is imagined — choose and begin one on their own without a reminder?

## Home connection

Show someone at home your calm card and teach them one of your two tools.
