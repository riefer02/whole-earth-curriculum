# Lesson 7 — Our Care, Our Celebration

## Summary

The year ends as it began — with who we are and who we are to each other. Learners
display their year of work equally, share one remembered act of care, and think
together about why helping others helps everyone, including themselves. Then they
celebrate what they have grown into and look ahead to Grade 3. A celebration, never a
contest.

## Objectives

- Identify their personal strengths and one thing they are still learning to do.
  _(D01.S2.02.01)_
- Explain what makes a group feel welcoming and inclusive to everyone in it.
  _(D02.S3.02.01)_
- Explain why helping others helps everyone, including themselves. _(D02.S4.02.02)_

## Connection

All over the world, people gather to celebrate — a harvest, a season, a graduation, a
year's work — and at the heart of every good celebration is care: someone cooked,
someone set a place, someone helped a smaller one reach the table. When you help
someone, you do not just fill their need — you grow warmer and more connected too.
Helping helps everyone, including you. Today you remember the care you gave this year,
and you celebrate it.

## Materials

- Every learner's year work (writing, number work, predictions, made things) plus this
  unit's pages
- A space to display the work
- A shared way to celebrate (song, clap, movement)

## Preparation

- Gather each learner's chosen work and this unit's pages ("My Strengths, My Growing"
  and the calm card).
- Set up the display so every learner's work is shown equally — side by side, none
  higher or more special than another.
- Arrange a circle for the care-sharing and plan a simple shared celebration.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It is the year's **capstone**, and its
two commitments are **celebration, not competition** and **care helps everyone,
including the giver**. No best-work awards, no winners, no ranking — every learner's
work is displayed equally, and every child belongs. Honor every family shape too: a
child's caring circle may be parents, one parent, grandparents, extended kin,
guardians, foster or group care, or a trusted adult — and a learner may share their
celebration with any of these, or pass. In the care-sharing circle,
sharing is by choice; a learner may pass, and a pass is a full share. Let the question
"why does helping help everyone?" stay open and personal — the answer is felt, not
tested: helping connects us, warms us, and builds the group we want to live in. For a
learner who is Deaf or hard of hearing, make the celebration visual — clapping,
signing, a flag wave, a visual "bravo." For a learner who is blind or has low vision,
describe work aloud or offer a spoken or tactile tour. Keep the tone warm and
generous: the class has grown together, and everyone belongs.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Today is a celebration of all of you — all of me, all of us.
   There are no winners and no rankings, just everyone, together.
2. **Set up the gallery (10 min).** Lay out or hang every learner's year of work, plus
   this unit's pages. Every piece is shown equally — no one's is higher or more
   special. If a learner chooses not to show a piece, that choice is honored.
3. **Remember an act of care (10 min).** Think of one time this year you helped
   someone — a friend who was stuck, a smaller one who needed a hand, a job that
   needed many hands. Hold that memory. It is a real part of your year.
4. **Circle of care (15 min).** Sit in a circle. Each learner may share their
   remembered act of care — or pass. Then think together: *why does helping others
   help everyone, including you?* Helping fills a need, and it also connects and warms
   the helper. When we help, the whole circle grows stronger — and so do we.
5. **Celebrate together (15 min).** Make a shared celebration — a song, a clap, a
   movement the class creates together. You have grown all year, and you did it by
   caring for each other.
6. **Close (5 min).** Look ahead: next year, in Grade 3, you will keep growing — more
   feelings, more strengths, more community, more care. You are all of you, and you are
   all of us.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Allow a learner to celebrate by pointing to their work or holding up a
  partner's hand; a pass counts as sharing.
- **Support (Deaf/hard-of-hearing):** Make the celebration visual — clapping, signing,
  a flag wave, or a visual "bravo."
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Describe work aloud or offer a spoken or tactile
  tour of the gallery.
- **Extension:** Say in your own words why helping others helps everyone, and name one
  way you will care in Grade 3.

## Assessment

- **Summative (portfolio + performance):** The learner shares their year of work
  (displaying it or passing) and names one remembered act of care, and can say — by
  word, drawing, sign, or a partner voicing — one reason helping others helps everyone,
  including themselves.
- **Observation:** Does the learner show, in their own way, that they see themselves
  as a whole person and their class as a caring community they belong to?

## Home connection

Bring your year's work to the people who care for you — your family, whatever shape
it takes (parents, one parent, grandparents, aunts and uncles, kin, guardians, or any
trusted adult) — and tell them one act of care you remember, and why helping helped
you too.
