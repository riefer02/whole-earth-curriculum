# Lesson 3 — Alike and Different — and Why Both Matter

## Summary

Learners pair up and become detectives of each other: they find two true ways they are
alike and two true ways they are different. Then they explain why *both* matter — being
alike helps us understand each other, and being different brings gifts only that
person can give. This spirals back to the opening unit and gathers the year's lesson
that difference is not a ranking but a richness.

## Objectives

- Describe how they are alike and different from others and why both matter.
  _(D01.S2.02.02)_

## Connection

Everywhere people live, you can find two people who are alike in some ways and
different in others. Two farmers may both love to grow food — alike — while one grows
rice in a wet field and the other grows wheat on a dry plain — different. A city child
and a mountain child may both love stories — alike — yet tell them in different
languages and different ways. Alike helps us feel "you are like me." Different means
"you bring something I do not have." Both are true, and both are gifts.

## Materials

- A "Two True Things" partner sheet
- Crayons and pencils

## Preparation

- Prepare the "Two True Things" sheet with two boxes: "two ways we are alike" and
  "two ways we are different."
- Arrange learners in pairs, mixing pairs across the room so they meet someone new.
- Set out crayons and pencils.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It spirals back to the opening unit's
"alike and different" work, now with the added question *why both matter*. The
egalitarian commitment is firm: **difference is never a ranking**. No way of being is
better than another — alike is not "better" than different, and no learner's fact is
above another's. Guide pairs to find *true, kind* facts (likes, abilities, families,
languages, ways of moving) and steer away from facts that sort people into higher and
lower. For a learner who is blind or has low vision, focus on facts heard, felt, and
spoken rather than seen. For a learner who does not use a speaking voice, accept
pointing, signing, drawing, or a partner voicing. The goal is that every learner can
say, in their own way, one alike thing and one different thing that both matter.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Today you will be detectives — of how you are like a friend and
   how you are different. Both are true, and both are worth finding.
2. **Meet your partner (5 min).** Sit with a partner — someone you may not know well.
   You will discover two true ways you are alike and two true ways you are different.
3. **Find "alike" (10 min).** Talk together: what do you both love to do? What food,
   game, or story do you both enjoy? What makes you both smile? Write or draw two
   alike things in the "alike" box.
4. **Find "different" (10 min).** Now look for what is different: one of you draws and
   the other runs; one speaks a language the other is learning; one has a big family
   and the other a small one. Write or draw two different things. Remember: different
   is not better or worse — it is just different.
5. **Why both matter (10 min).** Tell each other: *why* do alike and different both
   matter? Alike helps us understand each other — "you are like me." Different brings
   new gifts — "you can do what I cannot." Say it in your own words.
6. **Close (5 min).** The room is full of people who are alike in some ways and
   different in others. That is not a problem to fix. It is the richness of the whole
   year, gathered in one room.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer a menu of example facts to choose from; scribe the words a
  learner says.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Point, draw, or sign the facts; a partner writes or
  voices them.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Use heard, felt, and spoken facts rather than visual
  ones.
- **Extension:** Name why both matter in your own words and give one example from the
  year where someone's difference was a gift to the group.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation + peer):** Can the learner name two ways they are alike
  with a partner and two ways they are different, and say in their own words why both
  matter?

## Home connection

Ask someone at home one way you are alike and one way you are different, and tell them
why both matter.
