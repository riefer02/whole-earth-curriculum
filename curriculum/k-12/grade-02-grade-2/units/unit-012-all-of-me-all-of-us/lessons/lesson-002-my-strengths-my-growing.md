# Lesson 2 — My Strengths, My Growing

## Summary

Learners name their personal strengths — not only things they can do well but kind
things they do — and one thing they are still learning, then reframe "I can't do it"
as "I can't do it *yet*." Each learner makes a two-sided page that gathers both sides
of one whole, growing person. This page becomes part of the year-end portfolio.

## Objectives

- Identify their personal strengths and one thing they are still learning to do.
  _(D01.S2.02.01)_

## Connection

Think of a thing you can do now that you could not do at the start of the year —
reading a page, riding a bike, counting to one hundred, sharing a turn without being
asked. That is a **strength**, and it grew from practice. Now think of a thing you
cannot do *yet*. Everyone — every person on Earth — has both: things they are good at
and things they are still growing into. Both are real parts of you, and both matter.

## Materials

- A two-sided "My Strengths, My Growing" page
- Crayons and pencils

## Preparation

- Prepare or fold the two-sided page: one side "I am good at," the other "I am still
  learning."
- Set out crayons and pencils.
- Have each learner's earlier "my strengths" and "what I'm still learning" work ready,
  to look back on (optional).

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It spirals back to the opening unit's
work on strengths and on "what I'm still learning." The heart of it is the reframe
**"not yet"**: a thing a learner cannot do is not a verdict on them; it is a direction
of growth. Celebrate strengths broadly — a strength can be a skill (counting, drawing,
running) or a quality of care (sharing, comforting, listening) — so every learner
finds at least one that is true of them, and no one's strength outranks another's.
Honor the growing edge too: naming it aloud is itself a strength. For a learner who
cannot write, accept drawing, dictation, pointing, signing, or a found object. The
page can be made many ways; the whole self is the point, not a perfect page.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of one thing you can do now that you could not do at the
   start of the year. That is a **strength**. Everyone has strengths — skills and kind
   things we do.
2. **Name your strengths (10 min).** On the "I am good at" side, write or draw two
   strengths: a thing you can do well, and a kind thing you do for others. Look back at
   your earlier work if you want a reminder.
3. **Name your growing edge (10 min).** Now think of one thing you are still learning —
   something you cannot do *yet*. Write or draw it on the other side, and add the word
   **yet**. "I can't" becomes "I can't *yet*."
4. **Make your page (15 min).** Finish both sides with drawings, words, or pasted
   pieces. Put your name on it. Your page shows the whole you: the strong you and the
   growing you.
5. **Close (5 min).** A thing you cannot do yet is not a failure — it is where you are
   growing next. Both sides of your page are worth celebrating.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide picture choices for strengths and growing edges; scribe the
  words a learner says.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Point to a picture or act out a strength; a partner
  writes it.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Build the page tactually — raised shapes, textured
  marks, or a spoken description of each side.
- **Support (motor differences):** Paste pre-made pieces or dictate while a partner
  places them.
- **Extension:** Add a "how I will grow it" line — one first step toward the thing you
  are still learning.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name at least one personal strength and
  one thing they are still learning, using "not yet"?
- **Portfolio:** The completed "My Strengths, My Growing" page, kept for the
  celebration.

## Home connection

Show someone at home both sides of your page — one strength and one thing you are
still learning — and tell them how you will grow it.
