# Lesson 6 — Many Hands, One Big Job

## Summary

Learners face a real task that no one person could finish alone — a heavy or long
thing to carry, or one big thing to build — and discover that cooperation is how it
gets done. They plan who does what, do the job together, and reflect that each person's
part mattered. This gathers the year's teamwork and prepares the class to make the
celebration together.

## Objectives

- Cooperate with others to finish a task that no one could do alone. _(D02.S4.02.01)_

## Connection

Think of a thing one person cannot do alone: lifting a heavy basket of grain, pushing
a boat into the water, building a house, carrying a harvest in from the field. All
over the world, people do such jobs by working together — many hands, one job. A task
that is "too heavy for one" becomes "just right for many," when each person takes a
part. Today your class becomes a team that can do what no single one of you could do.

## Materials

- A real cooperative task (a long pole, a heavy basket, a big puzzle, or a large
  picture to make together)
- Chart paper and markers

## Preparation

- Choose a task that is genuinely too big or heavy for one learner, but right for the
  group (carry a long bench or pole; move a heavy basket; finish a big floor puzzle;
  color one large picture).
- Set out chart paper for the plan.
- Arrange space to move and work together safely.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It makes cooperation **real and
physical**, not a slogan. Choose a task that is truly a one-person-cannot-do-it — if a
child *could* do it alone, the lesson loses its point. Safety first: pick tasks with
no pinch points or falls, and guide the lift (bend knees, lift together, on a count).
The egalitarian note is that **every part matters and no part outranks another**: the
person holding the far end is as needed as the one holding the near end. A learner who
uses a wheelchair or has limited strength can direct, count, clear the path, steady
the load, or cheer the team — all real parts. Make sure every learner has a part to
point to in the reflection ("that was my part"). The reflection, not the lifting, is
where the learning lands.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Here is a job. Try it alone — you cannot finish it. That is not
   because you are not strong or smart; it is because some jobs need many hands.
2. **Plan together (10 min).** Look at the job and plan as a team: who holds where,
   who guides, who clears the way, who counts "one, two, three, lift!" Write or say
   the plan. Every person needs a part.
3. **Do the job (15 min).** Do it together, following the plan. Listen, adjust, and
   help. If one part wobbles, another person steps in — that is cooperation.
4. **Check your work (10 min).** Did you finish what no one could do alone? Yes. Say
   each person's part out loud: "You held the far end, I held the near end, you
   cleared the path." Every part mattered.
5. **Close (10 min).** A job too heavy for one is just right for many. Tomorrow, at
   our celebration, the whole class will make something together — and now you know
   exactly how that works.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Give each learner one clear, simple part and a partner to do it with.
- **Support (mobility/strength):** Offer non-lifting parts — directing, counting,
  steadying, clearing the path, cheering — and name them as real parts.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Use pointing, signing, or a pictured plan to choose and
  show a part.
- **Extension:** Write the plan before doing it, then after, name one thing you would
  change next time.

## Assessment

- **Formative (performance + observation):** Did the learner take a part in a shared
  task that no one could do alone, and can they name their own part and one other
  person's part?

## Home connection

Find one job at home that is too big for one person and do it together, then tell
someone how each person's part mattered.
