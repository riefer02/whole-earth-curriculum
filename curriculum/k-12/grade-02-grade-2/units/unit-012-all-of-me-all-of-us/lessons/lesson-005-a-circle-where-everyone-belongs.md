# Lesson 5 — A Circle Where Everyone Belongs

## Summary

Learners think back to a moment they felt truly welcome, name the small things that
made it so — a smile, a space, their name said with care, an invitation, a helping
hand — and build a real welcoming circle. Then they practice the harder part: making
room, so that no one is left outside and everyone belongs. This gathers the year's
work on community and carries it into the celebration.

## Objectives

- Explain what makes a group feel welcoming and inclusive to everyone in it.
  _(D02.S3.02.01)_

## Connection

In every community, people make newcomers feel welcome in small, real ways: a bowl of
food set out, a door held open, a name learned and said right, a space made in the
circle. A group that feels welcoming is one where nobody has to squeeze in or be left
out — there is a place for everyone. All year your class has been learning to be that
kind of group. Today you name how you do it, so you can keep doing it on purpose.

## Materials

- Chart paper and markers
- A long ribbon or rope (optional)

## Preparation

- Set out chart paper to list "what makes a group welcoming."
- Arrange space for a real circle, big enough for everyone plus one more.
- Have the class's earlier "welcoming group" ideas ready to look back on (optional).

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It spirals back to the "caring and
working together" unit's work on welcoming groups, now made real in the body. The
commitment is **everyone belongs — no one is left outside**. Guide learners toward
concrete, doable welcomes (a smile, a space, a name said right, an invitation, a
helping hand, listening) rather than grand gestures. Be tender here: a learner may
remember a time they were *not* welcomed; honor that gently and turn it into agency —
"what would have helped you?" Make the circle wide enough for a wheelchair to enter,
and accept a learner's own way of joining (standing, sitting, signing "welcome,"
patting the space beside them). The invitation is the lesson: belonging is something
we *do*, and we can all do it.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of a time you felt welcome somewhere — a home, a
   playground, a class, a celebration. What did someone do that made you feel you
   belonged?
2. **Name what welcomes (10 min).** Together, list what makes a group welcoming: a
   smile, a space made for you, your name said with care, an invitation to play, a
   helping hand, someone listening. Write them on the chart.
3. **Build the circle (10 min).** Stand in a real circle, holding hands or a ribbon.
   Look around — this is your class community. Notice that everyone is inside.
4. **Practice making room (15 min).** Now make a gap in the circle and choose one
   person to step outside. That person waits, just for a moment. Then, from inside,
   welcome them back the way your list says: smile, say their name, make a space,
   reach a hand. Watch the circle close around them — no one left out.
5. **Close (5 min).** A welcoming group is not an accident; it is something people do
   on purpose, every day. Your class has become that group. At our celebration, make
   sure everyone has a space — because everyone belongs.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Act out one welcome at a time; give a simple script ("Welcome, [name].
   Here is a space for you.").
- **Support (non-speaking):** Welcome with a smile, a wave, a pat of the space beside
  you, or a sign for "welcome."
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Welcome with a spoken name and a held hand or arm,
  describing the space made.
- **Support (mobility):** Keep the circle wide and open so a wheelchair or walker can
  enter; invite a learner to pat the space beside them instead of standing.
- **Extension:** Explain in your own words what happens to a person left outside the
  circle, and one way you will make sure that does not happen at the celebration.

## Assessment

- **Formative (performance + observation):** Can the learner name at least two things
  that make a group welcoming and *do* one of them (say a name, make a space, offer a
  hand) to include someone?

## Home connection

At home, practice one welcoming thing — learning and saying a visitor's name with
care, or making a space at the table — and tell someone why it matters.
