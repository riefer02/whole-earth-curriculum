# Lesson 5 — Why We Wash Our Hands

## Summary

Learners already know how to wash their hands from Grade 1; now they learn *why* it
works. Soap and water carry away germs that cause sickness, and washing at the right
times — before eating, after the bathroom, after coughing or sneezing, and after touching
an animal — protects the washer and everyone around them. Water is honored, never wasted.

## Objectives

- Explain why handwashing, tooth-brushing, and rest help prevent sickness and keep the
  body well. _(D03.S4.02.01)_

## Connection

Your hands touch doors, pets, the ground, and your food — and they pick up tiny
**germs** too small to see. Germs can make bodies sick. Washing your hands with soap and
water carries those germs away. Today you will learn the *why* behind the washing you
already know how to do.

## Materials

- Soap and water (a sink, basin, bucket, or pour jug with safe water)
- A towel or clean cloth for drying

## Preparation

- Set up a handwashing station with safe water and soap (or ash, if soap is scarce).
- Have a short 20-second song ready to time the scrub.
- Plan how water will be conserved — where water is scarce, use the wet–lather–rinse
  method.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Grade 1 taught the *how*; Grade 2 adds the
*why*, so this is explanation building on a known skill — use explicit instruction and a
worked example of the reason ("soap lifts germs off the skin, and water carries them
away"), then let learners explain it back in their own words. **Honor every context:**
where water is scarce, teach the "wet, lather, rinse" method that saves water, and note
that ash can work when there is no soap. Where a learner cannot wash their own hands, a
caregiver or older peer can help — the goal is clean hands, not doing it alone. **Care is
never a test.** A learner who does not use a speaking voice points or signs; a learner who
is blind or has low vision is guided hand-over-hand and explains the reason aloud. A
learner who is deaf or hard of hearing follows the demonstration and picture step cards
from the front, since every step is shown, not only told. The health facts are from the
World Health Organization: washing hands with soap removes germs and is one of the most
effective ways to prevent the spread of sickness. This lesson starts the D03.S4.02.01
"why" trio (wash, brush, rest).

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of everything your hands touched today. Hands pick up tiny
   **germs** — living things too small to see — that can make bodies sick.
2. **Why does washing work? (10 min).** Soap loosens and lifts germs off your skin, and
   water carries them away down the drain. That is why we scrub every part: palms, backs,
   between fingers, thumbs, wrists, and under nails — so no germs are left behind.
3. **Practice the motions and the reason (5 min).** In the air, practice wet, lather,
   scrub, rinse, dry. As you scrub, say why: "Soap and water carry germs away."
4. **Wash for real (10 min).** Go to the station and wash your hands, scrubbing about 20
   seconds — the length of a short song. Then check: did I miss a spot?
5. **When do we wash, and why? (10 min).** Name the times washing matters most: before
   eating, after the bathroom, after coughing or sneezing, and after touching an animal.
   Washing then stops germs before they can reach a mouth, a nose, or another person.
   Tell a partner one time and why it matters.
6. **Close (5 min).** Clean hands protect you *and* the people around you. Washing is a
   small act of care for everyone.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Practice the motions in the air first, then wash together once before
  trying alone; use a picture step card.
- **Support (deaf/hard-of-hearing):** Stand where the learner can see your face and hands;
  pair each step with its picture card and a clear visual model.
- **Support (motor-difference):** A caregiver or peer assists with parts that are hard,
  while the learner leads and explains the reason as much as they can.
- **Extension:** Teach the "why" to someone younger, and name one more way to save water
  while washing.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner explain why washing helps prevent sickness
  and name one key time to wash, in their own way?
- **Self:** The learner checks their own hands after washing and says why they washed.

## Home connection

Wash your hands before one meal today and tell a grown-up why washing keeps you and your
family from getting sick.
