# Lesson 7 — My Trusted Adults at Home and School

## Summary

Learners identify trusted adults at home and at school — grown-ups who listen and help —
and fill a "help hand" with five of their own. They learn that they get to choose who
they trust, that asking for help is a strength, and that a trusted adult makes them feel
safe. The help hand becomes a portfolio artifact.

## Objectives

- Identify trusted adults at home and at school to ask for help in different situations.
  _(D03.S4.02.02)_

## Connection

When you fall and scrape your knee, you tell a grown-up. When you feel sick, lost,
scared, or unsafe, you tell a grown-up too. Some grown-ups are **trusted adults**: people
who listen to you and help you. You have trusted adults at home *and* at school. Today
you will name yours.

## Materials

- A "help hand" template (a hand outline with five fingers)
- Crayons
- Picture cards of adults (caregiver, grandparent, teacher, nurse, coach, neighbor)

## Preparation

- Prepare a "help hand" template for each learner.
- Prepare picture cards of adults to sort into "home" and "school."
- Read the facilitator note fully, including the safeguarding guidance, before teaching.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). **Read this note fully before teaching.**

- Keep the tone warm and reassuring. Asking for help is a strength, never a weakness, and
  telling is never wrong.
- Help each learner name trusted adults who are realistically available to them. If a
  learner names no one at home, gently guide them toward a teacher, nurse, school helper,
  or neighbor — without pressure and without implying anything about their family.
- A trusted adult is someone the learner feels safe with; the learner chooses. Adults
  vary across families and cultures — caregivers, grandparents, aunts, uncles, neighbors,
  teachers, nurses, coaches, and religious or community helpers can all be trusted adults.
- Honor every way of naming and asking: speaking, signing, pointing, drawing, or tapping a
  shoulder all work. A learner who is deaf or hard of hearing practices in their sign
  language and points to the picture cards; face the learner for every demonstration. A
  learner who is blind or has low vision names trusted adults aloud and traces the help
  hand in the air or by feel, naming one trusted adult per finger.
- **If a child discloses abuse or an unsafe situation:** stay calm; believe them; thank
  them for telling you; do **not** promise secrecy; do not press for details or ask
  leading questions; tell your designated safeguarding lead and follow your local
  child-protection procedure immediately.
- The help hand is a unit portfolio artifact; keep learners' drawings. This lesson builds
  on Grade 1's "Trusted Adults and Asking for Help" (U.01.008) and leads into the unit's
  capstone on asking for help in different situations.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Remember last year: your body belongs to you, and if something
   feels wrong you tell a trusted adult. This year we learn *who* your trusted adults are
   at home and at school.
2. **Who is a trusted adult? (10 min).** A trusted adult is a grown-up who listens to you
   and helps you. At **home**, that might be a caregiver, a grandparent, an aunt, or an
   uncle. At **school**, a teacher, a nurse, a coach, or a school helper. Sort the picture
   cards: which adults are at home, and which are at school?
3. **You choose who you trust (10 min).** You get to decide who feels safe to you. A
   trusted adult is someone who listens, believes you, and helps you when you ask. Tell a
   partner one thing a trusted adult does (listens, helps, keeps you safe).
4. **Fill your help hand (10 min).** Trace your hand. On each finger, draw or write one
   trusted adult — some from home, some from school. This is your help hand. Aim for five,
   and it is okay if you start with two or three and add more later.
5. **Close (5 min).** Say it together: "I have trusted adults at home and at school.
   Asking for help is a strength."

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Sort just two or three cards first; fill the hand with drawn faces rather
  than words.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Point to picture cards and draw faces on the hand; practice
  asking with a sign or a tap.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Name trusted adults aloud and trace the help hand in
  the air or by feel, saying one trusted adult per finger.
- **Extension:** Name a third trusted adult and tell which situations each one could help
  with — a bridge to the capstone lesson.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name at least two trusted adults (home and
  school) and say what a trusted adult does, in their own way?
- **Portfolio:** The "my trusted adults" help-hand drawing becomes a unit portfolio
  artifact.

## Home connection

With a grown-up, name the trusted adults at your home and school, and practice saying
"I need help" in a clear, calm voice. Keep your help hand where you can see it.
