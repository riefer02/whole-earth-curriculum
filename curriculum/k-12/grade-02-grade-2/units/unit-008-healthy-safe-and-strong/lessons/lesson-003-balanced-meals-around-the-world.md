# Lesson 3 — Balanced Meals Around the World

## Summary

Learners study balanced meals from several food traditions — rice, beans, and greens;
flatbread, lentils, and vegetables; maize, fish, and fruit — and find the food groups in
each. They then draw their own balanced-meal plate, which becomes a portfolio artifact.
The lesson closes the meal-building objective by showing that every tradition has its own
balanced meal, and every one is worthy of honor.

## Objectives

- Build a simple balanced meal by choosing foods from several different groups.
  _(D03.S3.02.01)_

## Connection

A cook in a rice-growing land builds a meal from rice, beans, and greens. A cook in a
wheat-growing land builds one from flatbread, lentils, and vegetables. A cook in a
maize-growing land builds one from maize, fish, and fruit. The foods change with the
land, but the idea stays the same: **a balanced meal reaches into several groups**. Today
you will travel the world's kitchens and find that idea everywhere.

## Materials

- World meal cards (balanced meals from several traditions)
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Prepare world meal cards showing several balanced meals, each labeled with its foods.
- Prepare a class "meals of the world" chart to collect learners' ideas.
- Remind learners: no meal and no tradition is ranked; all are honored.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). **Celebrate variety; rank nothing.** The
point is that "several groups" shows up in every food tradition, not that one tradition
eats "better." Some learners' families may not have all foods available; honor what they
do have, and never imply a family is failing. Vegetarian, plant-based, and fish- and
meat-eating families are all honored — beans, lentils, eggs, and fish are proteins, so no
meal needs meat to be balanced. If a learner's family sometimes has little food, hold
this gently and without comment on their family; the lesson speaks of variety and choice,
never of scarcity as shame. A learner who is blind or has low vision explores the meals by
touching real foods and hearing each food named; a learner who does not use a speaking
voice points to the foods and groups. A learner who is deaf or hard of hearing follows
the meal cards and group labels visually, since every food is shown and labeled. The
balanced-meal plate drawing is a unit portfolio artifact — keep it. This lesson completes
the D03.S3.02.01 meal-building strand before the unit turns to water.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Remember how we built a balanced meal last time. Today we will see
   that cooks everywhere build meals the same way — with foods from several groups.
2. **Meet meals from three lands (10 min).** Look at the world meal cards. Find the
   groups in each meal: rice (grain), beans (protein), greens (vegetable);
   flatbread (grain), lentils (protein), vegetables (vegetable);
   maize (grain), fish (protein), fruit (fruit). Each meal reaches into several groups.
3. **Wonder together (10 min).** What balanced meal might a family near you eat? What
   grows where you live — a grain, a root, a fruit, a fish, a bean? Build a meal in words
   from the foods of your own place, and name the group each food belongs to.
4. **Draw your balanced plate (15 min).** Draw your own balanced meal using foods from at
   least three different groups, and add water to drink. Your plate may look like your own
   home and your own land.
5. **Share and celebrate (5 min).** Share your plate with a partner. Notice how many
   different balanced meals the class has made — each one reaching into several groups.
6. **Close (5 min).** Every tradition has its own balanced meal. Every one is worthy of
   honor.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Find the groups in just one world meal together, then draw a plate using
  three familiar foods.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Build with real foods by touch and describe the groups
  aloud; the plate drawing can be made with textured materials.
- **Extension:** Draw a second balanced meal from a different tradition and write which
  group each food belongs to.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner find the food groups in a meal and draw a
  plate that reaches into several groups, in their own way?
- **Portfolio:** The balanced-meal plate drawing becomes a unit portfolio artifact.
- **Self:** The learner names the groups they included and one group they could add next
  time.

## Home connection

At home, talk with a grown-up about one balanced meal your family makes. Name the group
each food belongs to, and draw it together if you like.
