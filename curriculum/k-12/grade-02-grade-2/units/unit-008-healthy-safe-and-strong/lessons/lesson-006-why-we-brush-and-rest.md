# Lesson 6 — Why We Brush and Rest

## Summary

Learners already know how to brush their teeth from Grade 1; now they learn *why*
brushing and rest keep the body well. Brushing washes away food and **plaque** that can
hurt teeth, and sleep helps the body grow, heal, and fight sickness. People clean teeth in
many ways, and every body's rest can look a little different — all are honored.

## Objectives

- Explain why handwashing, tooth-brushing, and rest help prevent sickness and keep the
  body well. _(D03.S4.02.01)_

## Connection

You brush your teeth morning and night, and you rest when your body is tired. But do you
know *why*? Brushing washes away sticky bits that can make teeth hurt, and sleep is when
your body grows, heals, and gets ready to fight sickness. Today you will learn the reasons
behind two habits you already practice.

## Materials

- A toothbrush and toothpaste (each learner's own, or a demonstration brush)
- A cup of water for rinsing
- A "rest and sleep" chart

## Preparation

- Ensure each learner has their own clean toothbrush (never share brushes).
- Set up a place to practice, with water and a place to spit safely.
- Prepare a "rest and sleep" chart (sleeping, quiet time, a dim room, a bedtime routine).

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Grade 1 taught the *how*; Grade 2 adds the
*why* for both brushing and rest. Teach each reason as a worked example, then let learners
say it back in their own words. **Honor every context:** where brushes or paste are
unavailable, teach a clean-finger or chewing-stick method without shame. Where a learner
cannot brush their own teeth, a caregiver or older peer can help — the goal is clean
teeth, not doing it alone. **Honor every body's rest:** most children this age need about
9 to 12 hours of sleep (per the American Academy of Sleep Medicine), but families rest in
different ways and at different rhythms — no family is shamed for how or how much they
rest. Some learners cannot sleep in a bed of their own; hold this gently and without
comment. **Care is never a test.** A learner who does not use a speaking voice points or
signs; a learner who is blind or has low vision is guided hand-over-hand and explains
aloud. A learner who is deaf or hard of hearing follows the demonstration and picture
charts from the front, since each step is shown, not only told. The health facts are from
the World Health Organization (brushing removes food and plaque that can hurt teeth and
gums) and the American Academy of Sleep Medicine (children 6–12 need about 9–12 hours of
sleep). This lesson completes the D03.S4.02.01 "why" trio.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Smile at a friend. Your teeth help you eat, talk, and smile — and
   your whole body needs rest. Today we learn *why* brushing and sleeping keep you well.
2. **Why does brushing work? (10 min).** Sticky bits of food stay on teeth and turn into
   **plaque**, which can hurt teeth and gums. Brushing with a small dab of paste washes
   the plaque away. Practice the motion with your finger: tops, fronts, backs, and tongue
   side — every surface.
3. **Brush for real (10 min).** Brush your own teeth for about two minutes, singing a
   short song in your head. Rinse, then run your tongue over your teeth: do they feel
   smooth and clean?
4. **Why does rest help? (10 min).** While you sleep, your body grows, heals little hurts,
   and gets strong to fight sickness. That is why being tired is your body asking for
   rest. Look at the rest chart: a dim room, a calm routine, a quiet body. Most children
   your age need about 9 to 12 hours of sleep — but every body's rest can look a little
   different, and that is okay.
5. **Connect it (5 min).** Tell a partner one reason we brush and one reason we rest, in
   your own words.
6. **Close (5 min).** Brush and rest — two ways to care for the body that cares for you.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Practice the brushing motion with a finger first; model the "why" and have
  the learner echo it.
- **Support (deaf/hard-of-hearing):** Face the learner for every step and pair each motion
  and reason with its picture card.
- **Support (motor-difference):** A caregiver or peer helps reach back teeth while the
  learner leads and explains as much as they can.
- **Extension:** Draw or tell a bedtime routine that helps a body rest, and explain why
  each part helps.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner explain why brushing and rest help prevent
  sickness and keep the body well, in their own way?
- **Self:** The learner names one reason to brush and one reason to rest.

## Home connection

Before bed tonight, brush your teeth and tell a grown-up why brushing and sleeping help
your body stay well.
