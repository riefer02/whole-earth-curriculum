# Lesson 1 — Many Ways to Eat Well

## Summary

Learners recall the four food groups they met in Grade 1 and discover that families
around the world build their meals from several groups in many different ways. This
retrieval opens the unit's path toward building a balanced meal, and it holds the
line that every family's way of eating is honored, never ranked.

## Objectives

- Build a simple balanced meal by choosing foods from several different groups.
  _(D03.S3.02.01)_

## Connection

Last year you learned to sort foods into groups: **fruits**, **vegetables**,
**grains**, and **proteins**. A rice-and-beans meal, a flatbread-and-lentils meal, a
fish-and-root-vegetables meal — they look different, but each one pulls foods from
several groups. Before we build our own balanced meals, we will remember the groups and
notice the many wonderful ways families eat well.

## Materials

- Food group reminder cards (fruits, vegetables, grains, and proteins from many places)
- Four sorting mats labeled "fruits," "vegetables," "grains," and "proteins"

## Preparation

- Prepare four labeled sorting mats or drawn circles.
- Gather food picture cards covering all four groups, from many cultures.
- Set a warm tone: every family's food is welcome here, and no food or meal is "better"
  than another.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). **Hold the line on no food shaming.** The
goal is to recall the groups and notice *variety*, never to rank a food, a meal, or a
family. A meal that is everyday for one family may be rare for another, and neither is
wrong — the four groups are *one* way to sort, not the only right one. Do not comment on
what or how much a learner eats, and never tie food to body size, weight, or appetite.
A learner who is blind or has low vision sorts real foods by touch and scent; a learner
who does not use a speaking voice sorts by pointing or signing. A learner who is deaf or
hard of hearing sorts by pointing to the labeled mats and watching the demonstration from
the front, since every step is shown, not only told. This lesson retrieves Grade 1's
food groups (U.01.008) and opens the spiral toward this unit's balanced-meal work.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of a meal your family eats. Tell a partner what foods are
   in it. No two families eat exactly the same way, and every family's food matters.
2. **Remember the four groups (10 min).** Look at the sorting mats. **Fruits** grow from
   a plant's flower and often hold seeds. **Vegetables** are plant parts we eat — leaves,
   roots, stems. **Grains** are seeds like rice, maize, wheat, and millet. **Proteins**
   help your body grow, build, and mend — beans, lentils, eggs, fish, and meat. Point to
   the mat that matches each food card.
3. **Sort together (15 min).** Take a card. Which group does it go in? Place it and say
   why: "A mango is a fruit because it holds a seed." If you are not sure, wonder
   together — sorting is for noticing, not for being right. Some foods, like beans, can
   fit more than one group, and that is fine.
4. **Many ways to eat well (10 min).** Look at the four plates in the picture. A
   rice-and-beans meal, a flatbread-and-lentils meal, a fish-and-roots meal, a
   maize-and-fruit meal. Each one reaches into several groups. There is no single "right"
   meal — there are many ways to eat well.
5. **Close (5 min).** Say one food from each group that your family enjoys. Eating well
   can look a hundred different ways, and each one is a gift.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Sort just four familiar foods first; use real foods from the learner's own
  kitchen. Learners may point, sign, or gesture instead of speaking.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Use real or textured foods and sort by touch, naming
  each food as it is felt.
- **Extension:** Name one food from each of the four groups that their family eats, and
  draw a meal that uses foods from at least three groups.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner place a food into one of the four groups
  and, in their own way, show that different families build meals from several groups?
- **Self:** The learner names one food they already knew and one new way they saw a meal
  built.

## Home connection

At your next meal, point to one food from each group on the table — a fruit, a
vegetable, a grain, and a protein. Tell a grown-up which group each food belongs to.
