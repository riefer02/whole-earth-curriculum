# Lesson 4 — Water, My Body's Drink

## Summary

Learners discover why water matters for the body, notice their own thirst, and practice
drinking water when thirsty. They also learn, with care and honesty, that clean water is
precious and not available to everyone — so water is honored, never wasted, and shared
when there is enough.

## Objectives

- Explain the importance of drinking water and drink water when thirsty.
  _(D03.S3.02.02)_

## Connection

Your body is mostly water, and it needs water every single day — to play, to think, to
stay cool in the heat, and to stay well. When your mouth feels dry and your body says
"drink," that is **thirst**, your body's way of asking for water. Today you will learn why
water matters and practice listening to your own thirst.

## Materials

- A clear cup or bottle of water for each learner
- A "thirsty body" chart

## Preparation

- Ensure there is safe drinking water for learners to drink.
- Prepare a "thirsty body" chart (playing, hot weather, waking up, during a meal).
- Plan for any learner who cannot easily drink on their own — a caregiver, older peer, or
  a cup with a straw may help; the goal is water when thirsty, not drinking alone.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). **Water is a human need and a question of
justice, not a personal failing.** Teach why water matters and how to answer thirst,
then hold the global truth gently: many people do not have clean water to drink, and that
is a fairness problem we can work on — not something to make any learner feel ashamed of
their own home. Never shame a learner for being thirsty or for having little water at
home. How much water a body needs varies with weather, activity, and the person, so
"drink when thirsty" is the rule, not a fixed amount. A learner who cannot drink on their
own is helped without comment; a learner who does not use a speaking voice signs or
points "drink" and shows thirst. A learner who is blind or has low vision feels the
cup and the water's coolness and hears thirst named by body signs — a dry mouth, a warm
tired feeling — so the idea lives in the body, not only in the chart. A learner who is
deaf or hard of hearing follows the chart and demonstrations from the front, since every
idea is shown in pictures. The
health facts are from the World Health Organization: water is essential to life, and
safe drinking-water keeps bodies well. This lesson begins the unit's D03.S3.02.02 water
objective and links forward to handwashing, which also needs clean water.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Put your hand on your chest and breathe. Your body is mostly
   water — and it needs water every day to work well.
2. **Why does my body need water? (10 min).** Water helps your body play, think, stay
   cool when it is hot, and stay well. Without water, a body quickly feels bad. Water is
   your body's everyday drink.
3. **What is thirst? (10 min).** A dry mouth, a tired feeling, wanting a drink — that is
   **thirst**, your body's way of asking for water. Point to the "thirsty body" chart:
   you may be thirsty after playing, in hot weather, when you wake up, and during a meal.
4. **Drink when thirsty (10 min).** Notice your own body right now. Are you thirsty? If
   you are, drink some water. Drinking when thirsty is how you answer your body's call.
   If you are not thirsty now, remember the feeling so you will know it later.
5. **Water is precious and not for everyone (5 min).** In some places, clean water comes
   from a tap; in others, from a well or a boiled kettle; in others, people must walk far
   to carry water, and the water may not be clean. That is not fair. We can honor water by
   using just enough, never wasting it, and sharing when there is enough.
6. **Close (5 min).** Say it together: "Water keeps my body well. I drink when I am
   thirsty, and I honor water by not wasting it."

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use the thirsty body chart to practice one "I'm thirsty" signal; drink
  together, then alone.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Practice a sign or a point for "drink" and "thirsty"; use
  the picture chart to show thirst.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Name thirst by body feel — a dry mouth, a warm tired
  feeling — and let the learner feel the cup and the water; name the drink-when-thirsty
  times aloud together.
- **Support (motor-difference):** A caregiver or peer helps hold the cup or offers a
  straw; the learner leads by showing thirst and drinking what they can.
- **Extension:** Draw where water comes from at home and one way to avoid wasting it.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner say or show why the body needs water and
  notice or name their own thirst?
- **Self:** The learner drinks when thirsty and names one time their body asks for water.

## Home connection

At home, notice one time your body feels thirsty today and drink water when it does. Ask
a grown-up where your family's water comes from and one way to save it.
