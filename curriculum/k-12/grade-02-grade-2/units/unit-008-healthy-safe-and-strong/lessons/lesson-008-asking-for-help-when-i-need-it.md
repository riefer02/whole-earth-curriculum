# Lesson 8 — Asking for Help When I Need It

## Summary

Learners practice asking a trusted adult for help in different situations — feeling
sick, hurt, lost, scared, or unsafe — and learn to keep telling until someone listens.
As the unit's capstone, the lesson gathers everything learned: balanced meals, water,
hygiene, rest, and trusted adults all keep a body healthy, safe, and strong.

## Objectives

- Identify trusted adults at home and at school to ask for help in different situations.
  _(D03.S4.02.02)_

## Connection

Some situations are small and some are big, but they have one thing in common: asking a
trusted adult for help makes you safe. If you feel sick, hurt, lost, scared, or unsafe,
you tell a trusted adult — and if the first one is busy or doesn't understand, you tell
another, and another, until someone listens and helps. Today you will practice asking for
help, and then celebrate all the ways you keep your body healthy, safe, and strong.

## Materials

- Scenario cards (feeling sick, hurt, lost, scared, unsafe or uncomfortable)
- Each learner's "help hand" from last lesson

## Preparation

- Prepare simple scenario cards of different situations.
- Have learners bring (or re-trace) their "help hand" from the last lesson.
- Read the facilitator note fully, including the safeguarding guidance, before teaching.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). **Read this note fully before teaching.**

- Keep the tone warm and empowering. Asking for help is a strength, and telling is never
  wrong. Never frighten learners with graphic content; stay with "tell a trusted adult."
- Frame "keep telling" as persistence, not as "adults won't believe you": if the first
  grown-up is busy or doesn't understand, tell another, and another, until someone listens
  and helps.
- Practice every situation with the doll or a role-play partner, never with real bodies.
  Greetings and touch differ around the world — hugs, handshakes, joined palms, bows — but
  consent applies to all: a child may decline any touch they do not want, and "no" and
  "stop" are always okay.
- Honor every way of asking: speaking, signing, pointing, showing a picture, or tapping a
  shoulder all work. A learner who is deaf or hard of hearing practices in their sign
  language and points to the scenario cards; face the learner for every demonstration.
- **If a child discloses abuse or an unsafe situation:** stay calm; believe them; thank
  them for telling you; do **not** promise secrecy; do not press for details or ask
  leading questions; tell your designated safeguarding lead and follow your local
  child-protection procedure immediately.
- This lesson closes the unit; the help hand and scenario drawings are portfolio
  artifacts. Celebrate how much each learner can now do for their own health and safety.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Look at your help hand. These are the trusted adults you can ask.
   Today we practice *asking* for help in different situations.
2. **Meet the situations (10 min).** Look at the scenario cards: feeling **sick**,
   feeling **hurt**, feeling **lost**, feeling **scared**, and feeling **unsafe or
   uncomfortable**. In every one, the strong thing to do is the same: **tell a trusted
   adult**.
3. **Practice asking (15 min).** Practice the words in a clear, calm voice: "I need
   help." "I feel sick." "I feel lost." "I feel unsafe." You can also sign, point, or
   show a picture. Take turns with a partner or a doll — one asks, one listens. Then
   match each situation to a trusted adult on your help hand.
4. **Keep telling (5 min).** If the first grown-up is busy or doesn't understand, tell
   another one — and another — until someone listens and helps. Telling is never wrong,
   and you are never in trouble for telling.
5. **Gather the whole unit (10 min).** Remember everything that keeps you healthy, safe,
   and strong: a **balanced meal** with foods from several groups, **water** when thirsty,
   **washing hands**, **brushing teeth**, **rest**, and **trusted adults** to ask. Point
   to each one on a chart or name it aloud — you know how to care for your body.
6. **Close (5 min).** Say it together: "I can build a balanced meal and drink water. I
   wash, brush, and rest. I know my trusted adults and I can ask for help. My body is
   healthy, safe, and strong — and so am I."

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use two or three situations and practice one help phrase many times; model
  the words and a calm voice.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Practice asking with a sign, a picture card, or a point;
  match situations to trusted adults by pointing to the cards.
- **Extension:** Practice telling two adults in order for one situation, as they would if
  the first were busy, and explain which adult fits which situation.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name a trusted adult for at least two
  different situations and practice asking for help, in their own way?
- **Portfolio:** The help hand and any scenario drawings become unit portfolio artifacts.
- **Self:** The learner names one situation and the trusted adult they would tell.

## Home connection

With a grown-up, practice asking for help in one situation — feeling sick, hurt, lost,
scared, or unsafe. Say the words clearly and name the trusted adult you would tell.
