# Lesson 2 — Building a Balanced Meal

## Summary

Learners build a simple balanced meal by choosing at least one food from several
different groups and adding water to drink. They watch a worked example, build a plate
together, then build their own. A balanced meal can look many ways across the world's
kitchens; no food and no meal is ranked.

## Objectives

- Build a simple balanced meal by choosing foods from several different groups.
  _(D03.S3.02.01)_

## Connection

A single food is not a whole meal. Your body is helped by *many* foods, so cooks
everywhere build meals that reach into several groups at once — a grain, some vegetables
or fruit, a protein, and water to drink. Today you will build a meal like that, the way
a cook does, choosing foods from several different groups.

## Materials

- A large balanced-plate poster (sections for grains, vegetables and fruits, and protein,
  with a water cup)
- Food picture cards from every group
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Prepare the balanced-plate poster, or draw the plate on the board or ground.
- Gather food picture cards covering all groups, from many cultures.
- Prepare a simple worked example to model (for example: rice, greens, a mango, beans,
  and water).

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). **Model first, then release.** Show one
complete worked example step by step — "I choose rice for grains, greens and a mango for
vegetables and fruit, beans for protein, and water to drink" — then build one together,
and only then let learners build their own. This is explicit instruction for a new skill,
the way the evidence says novices learn best (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006). **No
food shaming, ever:** a balanced meal can look many ways, and "several groups" is a
gentle aim, never a rule that ranks any meal or family. Do not comment on how much a
learner eats or tie food to body size. Honor vegetarian and other ways of eating — beans,
lentils, and eggs are proteins, so a meal needs no meat to be balanced. A learner who is
blind or has low vision builds with real foods by touch; a learner who does not use a
speaking voice builds by pointing. A learner who is deaf or hard of hearing follows the
worked example from the front, where every choice is shown on the plate, not only told.
The health facts are from the
World Health Organization: eating a variety of foods from several groups each day helps
bodies grow and stay well.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Remember the four food groups. Today we become cooks and build a
   balanced meal — a meal with foods from several different groups, plus water to drink.
2. **Watch a worked example (10 min).** Watch as the plate is filled one part at a time:
   a **grain** (rice), a **vegetable and fruit** (greens and a mango), a **protein**
   (beans), and **water** to drink. Each food came from a different group.
3. **Build one together (10 min).** Now build a meal as a class. One person chooses a
   grain, another a vegetable or fruit, another a protein — and together you choose water
   to drink. Say each choice aloud: "We chose flatbread for grains."
4. **Build your own (15 min).** Draw or arrange your own balanced plate. Choose at least
   one food from several different groups, and remember water. You may use foods your
   family eats — your balanced meal can look like your own home.
5. **Check and share (5 min).** Look at a partner's plate. Did they choose from several
   groups? Wonder together: is there another food you could add to reach one more group?
6. **Close (5 min).** A balanced meal is foods from several groups and water to drink —
   and it can look a hundred different ways.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Build beside the worked example one section at a time; use real or
  familiar foods from the learner's own kitchen.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Arrange real foods on a divided tray, feeling each
  section and naming each food.
- **Extension:** Draw two different balanced meals from two different traditions and tell
  which group each food belongs to.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner build a meal that includes foods from
  several different groups, and name the groups, in their own way?
- **Self:** The learner checks their own plate and names one food from each group they
  chose.

## Home connection

At home, help build one meal with a grown-up. Choose at least one food from several
groups and a glass of water, and name the group each food comes from.
