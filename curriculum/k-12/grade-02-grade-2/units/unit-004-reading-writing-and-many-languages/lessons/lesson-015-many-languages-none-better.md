# Lesson 15 — Many Languages, None Better

## Summary

Learners close the unit with a multilingual festival: they share greetings and
treasures in many languages and come to see clearly that people speak many
languages and that no language is better than another. This is the unit capstone —
a celebration of everything learned about reading, writing, and many languages.

## Objectives

- Recognize that people speak many different languages and that no language is
  better than another. _(D04.S4.02.02)_
- Celebrate and review the whole unit: listening and retelling, taking turns,
  reading fluently, answering the five questions, writing stories and reasons, and
  sharing home languages. _(D04.S1.02.01, D04.S1.02.02, D04.S2.02.01, D04.S2.02.02,
  D04.S3.02.01, D04.S3.02.02, D04.S4.02.01)_

## Connection

Walk through any market, any port, any schoolyard, and you will hear many
languages — each one a whole way of naming the world. A greeting in one language is
not bigger or better than a greeting in another, just different, the way one flower
is not better than another flower. When we say hello in many languages and mean it,
we say the truest thing about people: we are many, and we belong together.

## Materials

- The unit's collected work (each learner's portfolio)
- A multilingual greeting chart or word wall

## Preparation

- Gather learners' portfolios and arrange a sharing space.
- Prepare the greeting chart or word wall in the classroom's languages, each
  greeting the same size, none above another.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. The capstone celebrates, reviews, and cements the unit's
deepest value: multilingualism is wealth, and no language — spoken or signed — is
better than another. UNESCO holds that valuing every child's language strengthens
learning and dignity; this festival is that value made visible. Keep it a
celebration, never a contest: no ranking, no "best" greeting, no high-stakes
anything. Every voice, accent, and way of speaking is honored equally. Review is
light and joyful — a glance back at the portfolio, a few of the five questions, one
story read aloud — not a test. A learner who is Deaf or hard of hearing shares in
sign, and a sign language greeting stands on the wall exactly as high as any spoken
one.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Look back at your portfolio from this unit. You have
   listened, retold, read, written, and shared. Today we celebrate it all.
2. **Greet in many languages (15 min).** Around the circle, each person says or
   signs a greeting in one language they know. Everyone says it back. Notice: the
   greetings sit side by side, all the same size, none above another.
3. **Share your treasures (15 min).** Share one more language treasure — the song,
   rhyme, or family word you brought from home. Listen with full attention; try a
   classmate's treasure once.
4. **Remember the journey (10 min).** Together, name what we learned: listen and
   retell in order; take turns; read smoothly; ask who, what, where, when, why;
   write a story in order; give an opinion and a reason. Point to each in your own
   work.
5. **Close (5 min).** Many languages, one circle, none better. Every language is a
   treasure, and every learner is the richer for them all.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** A learner may share by pointing to a greeting on the wall and having
  the group say it together; passing is always allowed.
- **Support (sign language):** Share a signed greeting; the whole circle signs it
  back, honoring it exactly as a spoken one.
- **Extension:** Write one greeting from the wall and one line about why every
  language is equal, to add to the class word wall.

## Assessment

- **Formative (portfolio/performance):** Can the learner greet in at least one
  language, share a home-language treasure, and say in their own words that no
  language is better than another?

## Home connection

Teach someone at home one greeting you learned today, and tell them the festival's
big idea: many languages, none better.
