# Lesson 9 — Asking All Five Questions

## Summary

Learners bring all five reading questions together — who, what, where, when, and why
— to understand a whole text and retell it completely. This is the comprehension
capstone of the reading thread.

## Objectives

- Answer who, what, where, when, and why questions about a text.
  _(D04.S2.02.02)_

## Connection

Five questions, working together, are like five senses on a text: who and what give
you the people and the happening, where and when place it in the world, and why gives
it meaning. Asked together, they turn a page of words into a whole story you could
tell to someone who was not there. That is what it means to truly understand what
you read — in any language.

## Materials

- A short, rich text (people, happenings, a place, a time, and a reason)
- A five-question frame (who, what, where, when, why)

## Preparation

- Choose a short text that supports all five answers.
- Prepare the five-question frame (five labeled spaces).

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. This is a spaced, integrating lesson: it retrieves the four
earlier lessons (who/what, where/when, why) and joins them into one skill. Model
filling the whole frame once, then guide pairs, then release. The frame is a tool,
not a test — a learner may answer by speaking, signing, drawing, or writing, and the
five answers become the bones of a retelling. Work in the home language. A learner
who struggles with one question is not "behind"; the frame shows exactly which
question to practice next, and that is information for growth, never a verdict.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Hold up one hand. Each finger stands for one reading
   question: **who, what, where, when, why**. Say all five.
2. **Meet the five-question frame (5 min).** Look at your frame with five spaces.
   Today you will fill every space and use them to retell a whole text.
3. **Read the text (10 min).** Read the short text once, all the way through.
4. **Fill the frame (15 min).** Read again, and answer each question: Who is it
   about? What happened? Where? When? Why? Write, draw, or say each answer in the
   right space, and check each against the words.
5. **Retell the whole text (5 min).** Use your five answers to retell the text to a
   partner as one connected story. Let them retell it back.
6. **Close (5 min).** Five questions, one understanding. You can now unlock any text
   — in your language, in any script.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Fill the frame with drawings, one box at a time, then retell using
  the pictures.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Answer each question by signing, pointing, or drawing;
  the five answers together are the understanding.
- **Extension:** Read a second text and fill the frame again, then compare how the
  five answers differ between the two.

## Assessment

- **Formative (performance):** Can the learner answer who, what, where, when, and
  why about a text and use the five answers to retell it completely?

## Home connection

Read or hear a short story tonight and answer all five questions — who, what, where,
when, why — to someone at home.
