# Lesson 1 — Listening and Retelling in Order

## Summary

Learners listen to a partner tell a short, three-step happening, then retell it
back in the correct order using first, next, and last. Order is the goal — not
exact words — and every language has its own words for "first," "next," and "last."

## Objectives

- Listen to a partner and retell what they said in the correct order.
  _(D04.S1.02.01)_

## Connection

All day people tell you things that happen in steps: how to plant a seed, the way
to school, what happened at the market or on the farm. If the steps come out of
order, the story breaks — like putting the roof on a house before the walls.
Listening for the order and retelling it back is how people everywhere pass along
know-how, one generation to the next.

## Materials

- A short, simple happening to share (three clear steps, in the home language)
- Three-step retell cards (first, next, last)
- Three small objects, such as stones or seeds (low-tech)

## Preparation

- Choose or remember a short happening with three clear steps.
- Prepare the retell cards or three small objects.
- Arrange partners so each pair can sit facing one another.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Model one retelling in three clear moves before partners
work, then guide the practice. Retelling honors the speaker's meaning, not their
exact words — celebrate a learner's own wording, in their own language and their own
way of speaking; there is no single "correct" accent. Oral traditions everywhere
carry stories by retelling; this is an ancient and universal human skill. A learner
who is Deaf or hard of hearing retells in sign, in pictures, or with objects moved in
order; a learner who does not use a speaking voice retells by pointing, signing,
drawing, or using a communication device — holding the order is what counts.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Sit facing your partner. Remember what listening feels like:
   your full attention on the speaker — with your eyes, your ears, or both. If you
   listen by watching faces and hands in sign, that is listening too.
2. **Meet the order words (5 min).** Learn the three words that hold a story
   together: **first**, **next**, **last**. Say them in your home language. Every
   language has its own words for these three steps.
3. **Listen once (5 min).** Your partner tells a short happening with three steps.
   Listen all the way through, without talking, with your whole body.
4. **Listen again and mark the order (10 min).** Hear it a second time. As you hear
   each step, move one stone or touch one card: **first**… **next**… **last**.
5. **Retell it back (15 min).** Now retell the happening to your partner in order,
   using your own words and the three order words. Then trade roles: your partner
   tells, you listen, and you retell.
6. **Close (5 min).** Order is what keeps a story whole. First, next, last — the
   shape of every telling, in every language.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Retell with just two steps first (first, last), then add the middle;
  use objects instead of words if helpful.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Retell by moving three objects in order, signing, or
  pointing to three pictures — the order is the retelling.
- **Extension:** Retell a four-step happening and notice where the extra step
  belongs in the order.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner retell a partner's three-step
  happening in the correct order, in their own words and in their own language?

## Home connection

Ask someone at home to tell you how to do one small task in three steps, then retell
it back to them in order and ask, "Did I keep the order?"
