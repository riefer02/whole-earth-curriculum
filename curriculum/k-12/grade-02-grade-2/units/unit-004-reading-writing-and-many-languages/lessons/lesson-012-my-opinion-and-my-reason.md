# Lesson 12 — My Opinion and My Reason

## Summary

Learners state an opinion about a familiar, friendly choice and give one reason for
it, using an "I think… because…" frame. An opinion with a reason is the beginning of
thinking that others can weigh and answer.

## Objectives

- Write a simple opinion piece that states an opinion and gives a reason.
  _(D04.S3.02.02)_

## Connection

Every day you hold opinions: this food is tastier, this game is fairer, this path
home is shorter. An opinion alone is just a feeling named out loud; an opinion with
a reason is something a friend can think about and answer. When you say "I think…
because…," you let others meet your thinking instead of just hearing it — the start
of every fair decision people make together.

## Materials

- Friendly opinion prompts (everyday choices to have an opinion about)
- An opinion frame ("I think… because…")

## Preparation

- Prepare a few friendly, low-stakes opinion prompts (no right answer, nothing
  personal or hurtful).
- Prepare the opinion frame in the home language.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Opinions here are friendly and low-stakes — favorite fruits,
games, seasons — never about people's worth, and never used to rank learners. Model
one full "I think… because…" before learners write, then guide and release. The
reason must actually support the opinion; a reason that just restates the opinion
("I think mango is best because I like mango") is gently guided toward a real reason
("because it is sweet and cool"). Disagreement is welcome and kind: learners may
hold different opinions with different good reasons, and both can be right. Work in
the home language; every language has its own words for "I think" and "because." A
learner may speak, sign, dictate, or write the opinion — the reasoning is the
learning.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of a food, game, or place you love. That is your
   **opinion** — what you think or feel.
2. **Meet opinion and reason (5 min).** An **opinion** is what you think. A
   **reason** tells why you think it. When you add "because," your opinion becomes
   something others can think about.
3. **Watch a model (5 min).** Listen to one full example: "I think… because…"
   Notice how the reason explains the opinion.
4. **Say it first (10 min).** Pick a prompt. Say your opinion aloud with your
   reason: "I think… because…" Say it in your home language.
5. **Write it (15 min).** Write your opinion and your reason on the frame. Read it
   back and check: does the "because" part really explain why?
6. **Share and listen (5 min).** Read your opinion to a partner and listen to
   theirs. It is fine to **disagree** — you can both have good reasons.
7. **Close (5 min).** An opinion with a reason is thinking you can share. Next, we
   use a reason to explain how something works.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Complete the frame aloud and have a scribe write it; begin with a
  two-word reason, then grow it.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Sign, point to pictures, or use a device to give the
  opinion and its reason — the reasoning is the learning.
- **Extension:** Give two reasons for one opinion, and tell which reason is the
  stronger one.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation/peer):** Can the learner state an opinion and give a
  reason that truly supports it, in their home language?

## Home connection

Tell someone at home one opinion and one reason tonight — "I think… because…" — and
listen to theirs.
