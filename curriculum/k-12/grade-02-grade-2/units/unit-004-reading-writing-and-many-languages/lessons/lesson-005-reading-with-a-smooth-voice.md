# Lesson 5 — Reading with a Smooth Voice

## Summary

Learners practice reading fluently — smoothly, at a talking pace, and with
expression — by re-reading a familiar text and tracking how their reading eases.
Fluency grows through repetition, not through racing or ranking.

## Objectives

- Read common sight words and regularly spelled words accurately and with growing
  fluency. _(D04.S2.02.01)_

## Connection

When you first walk a path, you go slowly and watch your feet. After a few trips,
you walk it without thinking. Reading is the same: the first time through a sentence
is bumpy, but after you re-read it, the words flow — like talking. A smooth voice
lets the meaning come through, so a story sounds the way it would if the teller were
speaking it to you.

## Materials

- A short, well-known text (at the learner's level, in the home writing system)
- A fluency chart to mark re-readings

## Preparation

- Choose a short passage the learner already knows well.
- Prepare a simple fluency chart (three or four rows to mark re-readings).

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Fluency means accuracy plus a smooth, expressive pace — the
goal is meaning, not speed, and there is no competition and no "correct" accent.
Reading should sound like the learner's own natural way of speaking, in their own
language. Re-reading is the engine of fluency; learners who re-read a familiar text
grow smoother each pass, so keep the text easy enough to be enjoyed, not struggled
with. A learner who is Deaf or hard of hearing builds fluency in sign — a smooth,
expressive signing of the text is fluent reading, and re-reading in sign builds it
the same way. Celebrate each learner's growth against their own yesterday, never
against a peer.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of a path or a song you know so well you can do it
   without thinking. Reading gets like that with practice.
2. **Meet fluency (5 min).** **Fluency** means reading **smoothly**, at a talking
   **pace**, with **expression** — so the meaning comes through.
3. **Read it once (5 min).** Read the familiar text once. It is okay if it is
   bumpy — that is the first walk on the path.
4. **Re-read and smooth it out (15 min).** Read it again, a little smoother. Then a
   third time. Slide a finger or pointer along the line to keep a steady pace. Mark
   each re-reading on your fluency chart.
5. **Listen to yourself (10 min).** If you can, record your reading or read to a
   partner. Notice: where is it still bumpy? Where does it already flow like
   talking? Re-read the bumpy part once more.
6. **Close (5 min).** You did not race anyone — you grew against your own first try.
   Smooth reading lets the meaning sing.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Re-read a single short, very familiar line several times; smoothness
  is the only goal.
- **Support (Deaf/Hard of Hearing):** Practice a smooth, expressive signed reading
  of the text; re-signing builds fluency the same way.
- **Extension:** Read with a partner, taking turns by sentence, and match each
  other's smooth, expressive pace.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation/self):** Does the learner's reading grow smoother and
  more expressive across re-readings, sounding like natural talk in their own
  language?

## Home connection

Read the same short favorite text aloud at home twice tonight, and notice how much
smoother the second reading feels.
