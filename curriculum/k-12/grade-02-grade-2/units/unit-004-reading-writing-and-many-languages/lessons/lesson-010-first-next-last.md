# Lesson 10 — First, Next, Last

## Summary

Learners plan a short story in three ordered parts using first, next, and last.
Before writing, a story needs a plan — three boxes that hold the beginning, the
middle, and the end.

## Objectives

- Plan a short narrative in order, using words such as first, next, and last.
  _(D04.S3.02.01)_

## Connection

Every recipe, every journey, and every day has an order: first you wake, next you
eat, last you sleep. A story is the same — if its parts come out of order, it falls
apart, like telling the ending before the beginning. Planning with first, next, and
last is how cooks, builders, and storytellers everywhere keep a thing whole.

## Materials

- A three-part story planner (first, next, last)
- A short model story to study

## Preparation

- Prepare the three-box planner.
- Choose or tell a short three-part model story to study together.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. This is the planning lesson; writing comes next, so keep
today's product a plan — three ordered parts, drawn or written. Use a worked example
first: study the model story's three parts together, then let learners plan their
own (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006). The order words differ across languages;
learners use their home language's words for first, next, and last. Plans may be
pictures, spoken sentences, signed sequences, or written words — the order is the
point. No story is too small: how to wash a hand, feed a goat, or walk to the well
is a fine story.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of something you do every day in three steps. What do
   you do **first**? **next**? **last**?
2. **Study a model (10 min).** Listen to or read a short model story. Notice its
   three parts: what happened first, next, and last.
3. **Meet the planner (5 min).** Look at your three boxes. Box one is **first**,
   box two is **next**, box three is **last**. A good story lives in all three.
4. **Plan your story (15 min).** Choose a small happening from your own life — real
   or imagined. In box one, draw or write what happens first; in box two, next; in
   box three, last. Say the three parts aloud using the order words.
5. **Tell your plan (5 min).** Tell your three-part plan to a partner in order.
   Listen as they tell theirs.
6. **Close (5 min).** A story with first, next, and last is a story that holds
   together. Tomorrow, we turn this plan into writing.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Plan with two boxes first (first, last), then add the middle; use
  pictures instead of words.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Plan by laying three pictures or objects in order and
  signing or pointing to each part — the order is the plan.
- **Extension:** Plan a four-part story and decide which of the four parts is the
  most important, and why.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner plan a story in three ordered parts
  and tell it using first, next, and last?

## Home connection

Plan tonight in three parts — first, next, last — how you will help with one small
task at home, and tell someone your plan.
