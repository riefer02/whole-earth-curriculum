# Lesson 13 — An Explanation with a Reason

## Summary

Learners write a simple explanation: a topic (something they know how to do or how
something works) and a reason (why it works that way). Explaining is the other side
of the opinion coin — the informative side.

## Objectives

- Write a simple informative piece that states a topic and gives a reason.
  _(D04.S3.02.02)_

## Connection

You already know how to do many things: wash a pot, feed an animal, find your way
home, make a friend smile. To explain is to share that knowing — to tell **what** it
is or how it works, and **why**. A recipe, a field guide, a neighbor's advice: these
are all explanations. When you can explain a thing with a reason, your knowledge can
travel to someone else, in your language, in your script.

## Materials

- Something the learner knows how to do (or how something works)
- An explanation frame (topic + reason)

## Preparation

- Prepare the explanation frame (a line for the topic, a line for the reason).
- Gather a few real objects or actions learners can explain.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. This lesson pairs with yesterday's opinion: an opinion says
"I think… because…," while an explanation says "this is how/what… because…" — both
state a topic and give a reason, so both serve the same standard (D04.S3.02.02).
Model one full explanation before learners write, then guide and release. The topic
should be something the learner genuinely knows — no research needed at this level,
just honest know-how. The reason must explain the topic, not decorate it. Work in
the home language. A learner may speak, sign, dictate, draw a diagram, or write —
the explaining is the learning.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of one thing you know how to do well. Today you will
   **explain** it to someone else.
2. **Meet explanation (5 min).** To explain is to tell two things: the **topic**
   (what it is or how it works) and the **reason** (why it works that way). "This is
   how… because…"
3. **Watch a model (5 min).** Listen to one full explanation: a topic and a reason
   that explains it.
4. **Say it first (10 min).** Choose your topic — something you know. Say your
   explanation aloud: "This is how… because…" Say it in your home language.
5. **Write it (15 min).** Write your topic on one line and your reason below. Add a
   small diagram if it helps. Read it back and check: does the reason explain the
   topic?
6. **Close (5 min).** You turned your knowing into words someone else can use. That
   is the gift of explanation — it lets knowledge travel.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Explain a single, very familiar action (washing hands); dictate to a
  scribe if writing is hard today.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Show the action or point to a diagram and give the
  reason by sign, picture, or device — the explanation is the learning.
- **Extension:** Add a second reason, or a "then" step that tells what happens next.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation/portfolio):** Can the learner state a topic and give a
  reason that truly explains it, in their home language?

## Home connection

Explain to someone at home how you do one small thing — with the reason — and ask
them to try it your way.
