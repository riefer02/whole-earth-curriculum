# Lesson 14 — My Home Language and Culture

## Summary

Learners share one treasure from their home language and culture — a song, a
counting rhyme, or a family word — and learn one treasure from a classmate. Home
language is honored first, with pride, before any other language is added.

## Objectives

- Tell one thing about their home language and culture, such as a song, a counting
  rhyme, or a family word. _(D04.S4.02.01)_

## Connection

Before you learned to read and write, you learned to belong — through a song your
grandparent sang, a rhyme for counting, a special word your family uses that no
dictionary holds. These are treasures of your home language and culture. Sharing
them is like opening a chest of gold: the treasure grows when it is given away, and
everyone in the circle leaves richer.

## Materials

- A sharing circle space
- A memory card for each learner

## Preparation

- Prepare the sharing circle and a talking object.
- Prepare memory cards and, if available, a class treasure map.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. This is a lesson of belonging: every learner's home language
— spoken or signed — is honored first, with no language ranked above another, and no
"correct" accent or way of speaking. UNESCO and a wide evidence base hold that
education in a child's mother tongue strengthens learning and identity; multilingual
classrooms that honor home languages first build stronger readers and stronger
belonging. If a learner does not want to share today, that is their right — they may
pass, listen, and share another day. A learner whose language is a sign language
shares it as a language, fully equal to any spoken one. Keep the sharing warm and
unhurried; this is celebration, not performance or ranking.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Sit in the circle. Think of one treasure from your home
   language — a **song**, a counting **rhyme**, or a special family **word**.
2. **Remember your treasure (5 min).** Draw or write it on your memory card. Say it
   quietly to yourself, in your own language, your own way.
3. **Share in the circle (20 min).** When the talking object comes to you, share
   your treasure: sing it, say it, sign it, or show it. Everyone listens with full
   attention. If you want to pass today, pass — and listen with the same heart.
4. **Learn one new treasure (10 min).** Choose one classmate's treasure and try it
   once — a word, a rhyme, a melody. Ask them to say it again until you get it.
   Trying is respect, not perfection.
5. **Close (5 min).** Your home language is a treasure. Shared, it made everyone
   richer today.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Share just one family word, or draw the treasure and show it without
  speaking; a shy learner may share through a grown-up or a recording.
- **Support (sign language):** Share a sign or a signed rhyme — a sign language is a
  home language, equal to any spoken one.
- **Extension:** Teach the class a two-line rhyme in the home language and explain
  what it means, so the whole group can try it together.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation/portfolio):** Can the learner share one treasure from
  their home language and culture with pride, and try one from a classmate?

## Home connection

Ask someone at home for one more treasure — a song, rhyme, or family word — and bring
it tomorrow for the festival.
