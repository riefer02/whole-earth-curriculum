# Lesson 6 — Who and What

## Summary

Learners read a short text and answer the first two of the five great reading
questions: **who** the text is about and **what** happened. These two questions are
the doorway into every story and every notice.

## Objectives

- Answer who and what questions about a text. _(D04.S2.02.02)_

## Connection

When a friend comes home bursting with news, you ask two things right away: "Who was
there?" and "What happened?" Those two questions work on everything you read — a
story, a market sign, a message from a neighbor. They tell you the people and the
happening, and everything else in a text hangs on those two hooks.

## Materials

- A short text with clear people and happenings (in the home writing system)
- Question cards for "who" and "what"

## Preparation

- Choose a short text with one clear "who" and one clear "what."
- Prepare the two question cards.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. The five questions (who, what, where, when, why) are taught
two at a time so each gets real practice; today is who and what. Model answering
once with evidence from the text, then guide, then release. "Who" may be a person,
an animal, or a character in the learner's culture of stories; "what" is the main
happening, not every small detail. A learner answers who/what by speaking, signing,
pointing to a picture, or writing — the answer is what matters. Work in the learner's
home language; every language asks these same questions, with its own words for
them.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** When someone tells you news, what do you want to know first?
   Usually **who** was there and **what** happened.
2. **Meet the two questions (5 min).** Meet the first two reading questions:
   **Who?** — who is the text about? **What?** — what happened?
3. **Read the text (10 min).** Read the short text once, all the way through.
4. **Find who and what (15 min).** Read it again, hunting for the answers. Who is
   the text about? What happened? Point to, say, or write each answer — and find the
   place in the text that shows you.
5. **Ask each other (5 min).** Trade who/what questions with a partner about the
   same text, and answer with evidence from the page.
6. **Close (5 min).** Who and what are the first two keys. Two more questions —
   where and when — are coming next.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Answer just "who" first, with a picture; then add "what."
- **Support (non-speaking):** Point to the person and the happening in the text or
  in pictures — pointing with evidence is answering.
- **Extension:** Find a second, smaller "what" detail and tell which one is the main
  happening and why.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name who a text is about and what
  happened, with evidence from the text?

## Home connection

After hearing or reading a short story today, tell someone at home who it was about
and what happened.
