# Lesson 7 — Where and When

## Summary

Learners read a short text and answer the next two great reading questions: **where**
the happening took place and **when**. Place and time are what locate a story in the
world.

## Objectives

- Answer where and when questions about a text. _(D04.S2.02.02)_

## Connection

Every happening sits somewhere in space and some moment in time — under the mango
tree or in the kitchen, at dawn or after school. When you know **where** and
**when**, a story stops floating and becomes real: you can picture the place and
feel the time of day. These two questions root a story in the world, the way a
market stall sits on a particular street corner.

## Materials

- A short text with clear place and time (in the home writing system)
- Question cards for "where" and "when"

## Preparation

- Choose a short text that clearly names a place and a time.
- Prepare the two question cards.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Build on yesterday's who/what: today adds where and when, and
a quick recall of who/what at the start keeps earlier learning spaced and alive.
Place and time can be told in words, pictures, or numbers; honor every way the
learner's language and culture mark time — the question is "when," not "what does
your clock say," and many ways of marking time are valid. A learner answers by
speaking, signing, pointing, drawing, or writing. Work in the home language.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Quick recall: what were the first two reading questions?
   (Who and what.) Today we meet two more.
2. **Meet where and when (5 min).** **Where?** — what place does the text name?
   **When?** — what time or moment? Say each question word in your home language.
3. **Read the text (10 min).** Read the short text once, all the way through.
4. **Hunt for place and time (15 min).** Read again, searching for the answers.
   Where did it happen? When? Say, point to, draw, or write each answer — and find
   the words that show you.
5. **Ask each other (5 min).** Trade where/when questions with a partner about the
   same text, and answer with evidence from the page.
6. **Close (5 min).** Now you hold four keys: who, what, where, when. One more
   question — why — is coming next, and it is the deepest of all.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Answer just "where" first, with a picture of the place; then add
  "when."
- **Support (non-speaking):** Point to the place in a picture and mark the time with
  a gesture or a drawing — that is answering.
- **Extension:** Notice how the place or time changes the story; retell the same
  happening at a different time and tell what would change.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name where and when a text's
  happening takes place, with evidence from the text?

## Home connection

Ask someone at home where and when one small thing in their day happened, and listen
for both answers.
