# Lesson 8 — Why

## Summary

Learners read a short text and answer **why** something happened — the question that
asks for a reason. They learn to answer with "because" and to find the words in the
text that show the cause.

## Objectives

- Answer why questions about a text. _(D04.S2.02.02)_

## Connection

"Why" is the question that turns a list of facts into understanding. A pot boils
over *because* the fire is too hot; a friend runs *because* it is starting to rain.
Finding the reason is how you make sense of things, not just notice them. Of the
five reading questions, "why" asks you to think — and it is the one that never runs
out of answers to wonder about.

## Materials

- A short text with a clear cause (in the home writing system)
- A "why" question card

## Preparation

- Choose a short text where one thing clearly happens because of another.
- Prepare the "why" card and, if available, a "because" answer frame.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. "Why" is the question of cause, and the answer is a reason —
often the hardest of the five because it asks for inference, not just location.
Model one because-answer fully, showing where the text hints at the reason, then
guide and release. Some why-questions have more than one honest answer; honor a
reason the text supports, and keep genuinely open whys open — "I wonder too" is a
fine answer. Work in the home language; every language has its own word for "why"
and for "because." A learner answers by speaking, signing, drawing, or writing.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Quick recall: name the four reading questions we have met.
   (Who, what, where, when.) Today comes the deepest: **why**.
2. **Meet why (5 min).** **Why?** — what is the reason it happened? The answer often
   begins with **because**. Say both words in your home language.
3. **Read the text (10 min).** Read the short text once, all the way through.
4. **Hunt for the reason (15 min).** Read again. Ask: why did that happen? Find the
   words that hint at the reason, then say the answer with "because." If there is
   more than one good reason, share both.
5. **Ask each other (5 min).** Trade why-questions with a partner about the text,
   and answer with "because" and evidence.
6. **Close (5 min).** You now hold all five keys: who, what, where, when, why.
   Next, we will use all five at once.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Answer with a two-word reason first ("the rain"), then grow it into a
  because-sentence.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Draw the cause and its effect and point from one to
  the other — showing the reason is answering why.
- **Extension:** Wonder a why the text does not answer, and guess two possible
  reasons, telling which the text supports better.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner answer why something happened in a
  text, using "because" and pointing to evidence in the text?

## Home connection

Ask someone at home "why" about one small thing today, and listen for their reason.
