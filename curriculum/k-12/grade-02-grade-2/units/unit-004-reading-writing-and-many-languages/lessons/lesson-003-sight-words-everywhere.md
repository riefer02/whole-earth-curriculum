# Lesson 3 — Sight Words Everywhere

## Summary

Learners collect the common words they see every day and learn to read them "by
heart" — quickly and accurately, without sounding them out. Every language has its
own common words, and every writing system has its own set; the learner works in
their own.

## Objectives

- Read common sight words accurately and with growing fluency. _(D04.S2.02.01)_

## Connection

Some words you meet so often that your eyes and brain learn to know them the way you
know a friend's face — at a glance. The words on a shop sign, the words in a favorite
book, the words on a food packet: after you see them many times, you read them
instantly. Knowing these words by heart makes reading smoother and faster, so you can
spend your energy on the words that are new.

## Materials

- A set of common sight-word cards (in the home writing system)
- A short text with many sight words (a sentence set or little book)

## Preparation

- Choose 8–10 common words the learner sees often, written clearly in the home
  writing system.
- Prepare the word cards and a short text that uses several of them.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. The goal is recognition that is fast and automatic, not
memorized in a fixed order — shuffle the cards so the learner reads the word itself,
not the position. Work in the learner's home writing system; never assume the Latin
alphabet or English. A sight word in one script is a sight word in any script, and
recognizing whole words quickly is a skill that transfers to any language the learner
reads. Reading is taught with explicit instruction and guided practice (Kirschner,
Sweller & Clark, 2006), so model first, then guide, then let the learner practice
independently. For a learner who reads braille or uses large print, prepare the same
common words in their medium — the words are the same.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of a word you have seen so many times you know it the
   instant you see it — on a sign, in a book, on a packet. That is a **sight word**.
2. **Meet your words (5 min).** Look at your set of common words. Say each one.
   These are words you will learn to know **by heart**, without sounding them out.
3. **Read together (10 min).** Read each word aloud as it is shown. Then read them
   again, faster. Let your eyes take in the whole word at once.
4. **Practice on your own (15 min).** Shuffle the cards and read them one by one, as
   quickly and accurately as you can. If a word slows you down, set it aside and
   come back to it — that word is your "next" word to grow.
5. **Read the words in a text (5 min).** Find your sight words inside a short text.
   Reading them inside a sentence is how they become truly yours.
6. **Close (5 min).** Words you know by heart free your mind for words you do not.
   Every language has its own set of common words — and now you know some of yours.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Begin with 3–4 words; read them as a whole (look, cover, say, check)
  before adding more.
- **Support (braille/large print):** Prepare the same words in the learner's medium;
  reading by touch is reading by heart too.
- **Extension:** Collect new sight words from the learner's own world and add them
  to the personal set; read them inside new sentences.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation/self):** Can the learner read the common words quickly
  and accurately, by heart, without sounding them out?

## Home connection

Hunt for three sight words around home or the street today, and read them aloud to
someone.
