# Lesson 11 — My Story in Order

## Summary

Learners turn yesterday's plan into a written story — several sentences in order,
using first, next, and last — then read it back. The finished story becomes a
portfolio artifact and a small act of authorship.

## Objectives

- Write a short narrative of several sentences in order, using words such as first,
  next, and last. _(D04.S3.02.01)_

## Connection

Every writer was once a child telling a story for the first time. Writing your story
in order — first, next, last — is how you hand a piece of your day to someone who
was not there. The words on the page keep the story safe, so it can be read again
tomorrow, or far away, in your language and your script.

## Materials

- Your three-part plan from last lesson
- Writing paper (in the home writing system, with room for a picture)

## Preparation

- Have learners' plans ready to return.
- Prepare writing paper appropriate to the home writing system.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. This is a writing lesson built on explicit modeling: write
one model story from a plan together, sentence by sentence, before learners write
their own (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006). Several sentences is the goal — not a
fixed count; a learner may write two clear sentences today and grow to four. The
story is written in the learner's home language and script; never require the Latin
alphabet or English. Spelling grows through use; celebrate the attempt and note one
pattern to grow, rather than marking errors as failure. A learner who uses an
alternative or augmentative communication device, or who dictates to a scribe, is
still the author — the ideas are theirs. Reading back is part of writing: it lets
the author check the order.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Look at your three-part plan from last lesson. Today it
   becomes a real story on the page.
2. **Watch a model (10 min).** Watch a plan become a story: one sentence for
   **first**, one for **next**, one for **last**. Notice how each sentence matches a
   box in the plan.
3. **Write your story (20 min).** Write your story in order. Start with **first**,
   then **next**, then **last**. Use your plan. Draw a picture beside your sentences
   if you like.
4. **Read it back (10 min).** Read your story aloud to yourself, then to a partner.
   Check: does the order match first, next, last? Fix anything that is out of order.
5. **Close (5 min).** You are an **author**. You wrote a story in order, and it
   will keep its meaning every time it is read.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Write one sentence per box directly on the plan, then copy the three
  sentences onto paper; dictate to a scribe if writing is hard today.
- **Support (AAC/dictation):** The learner dictates the story to a scribe or
  composes it on a device — they are the author; the tool only writes.
- **Extension:** Add a fourth sentence that tells how the story felt at the end, and
  read the whole story with expression.

## Assessment

- **Formative (portfolio/performance):** Does the learner's written story contain
  several sentences in first-next-last order, and can they read it back accurately?

## Home connection

Read your story to someone at home tonight, and ask them to tell you which part was
"next."

## Resources
