# Lesson 4 — Words I Can Read Myself

## Summary

Learners read regularly spelled words by sounding them out: breaking a word into its
sounds, then blending the sounds back into a word. They work in their own writing
system, whatever script that is.

## Objectives

- Read regularly spelled words accurately and with growing fluency.
  _(D04.S2.02.01)_

## Connection

A word is like a line of beads: each bead is a sound, and when you run your finger
along the line, the sounds join into a word. Sounding out is how readers unlock a
word they have never seen before — no grown-up needed. Once you know your language's
sound rules, you can read many new words all by yourself, the way a key opens many
locks.

## Materials

- A list of regularly spelled words (following the home writing system's sound rules)
- Sound-tap counters (one per sound)

## Preparation

- Choose 6–8 words that follow the regular sound rules of the learner's writing
  system.
- Prepare counters and, if available, sound boxes.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. "Regularly spelled" means the word follows the sound rules of
the learner's own language and script — the rules differ across writing systems, so
choose words that are regular in the learner's system, and never assume an alphabet.
Sounding out is a foundational skill taught with explicit instruction and guided
practice (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006): model one word fully, guide a second
together, then release learners to practice. The point is independence — the learner
reads a new word without being told it. A learner who is Deaf or hard of hearing can
map the written word to a signed word and to mouth shapes; a learner who reads
braille sounds out by touch. Every path to the word is honored.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Remember the last new word you read all by yourself. How did
   you do it? You probably broke it into its **sounds** and joined them again.
2. **Meet sounding out (5 min).** A **regularly spelled** word follows your
   language's sound rules. You can **sound it out**: say each sound, then blend the
   sounds into the word.
3. **Watch and listen (5 min).** Watch one word sounded out slowly: each sound
   tapped with a counter, then the sounds joined into the whole word.
4. **Sound out together (10 min).** Take a second word. Tap one counter for each
   sound as you say it. Then blend the sounds — run them together — into the word.
   Read the whole word aloud.
5. **Practice on your own (15 min).** Sound out each word in your list: tap the
   sounds, blend them, read the word. If a word is tricky, try it again slowly.
6. **Close (5 min).** Sounding out is your own key. With it, you can open new words
   yourself — in your language, in your script, every day.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Begin with two-sound words, then three; model blending with a slow
  finger-sweep under the word.
- **Support (Deaf/Hard of Hearing):** Pair each written word with a sign and a
  mouth-shape; sounding out can be signing out the parts.
- **Extension:** Sound out a longer, unfamiliar regular word and use it in a
  sentence of the learner's own.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner break a regularly spelled word into
  its sounds and blend them back into a correctly read word, without help?

## Home connection

Find one new regularly spelled word around home, sound it out, and read it to
someone.
