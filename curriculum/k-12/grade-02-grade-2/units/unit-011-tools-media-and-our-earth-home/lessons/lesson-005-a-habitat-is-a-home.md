# Lesson 5 — A Habitat Is a Home for Living Things

## Summary

A **habitat** is a living thing's home — the place where it finds food, water, and
shelter. Learners describe a habitat (a pond, a desert, a forest, a seashore, or a real
patch of ground outside) and name one **plant** and one **animal** that live there and
**why each suits it**: a reed grows in wet mud and a frog's wet skin suits the water; a
cactus stores water and suits the dry desert. Homes differ all over the world, and each
one fits the living things that live in it.

## Objectives

- Describe a habitat and name one plant and one animal that live there and why it suits
  them. _(D10.S3.02.01)_

## Connection

Think of your own home — the place where you sleep, eat, and are safe. Every living thing
has a home too. A frog's home is the wet **pond**; a cactus's home is the dry **desert**; a
squirrel's home is the **forest**. That home is a **habitat** — and each living thing
**suits** its home: the frog's wet skin suits the water, and the cactus stores water so it
suits the dryness.

## Materials

- Habitat picture cards (pond, desert, forest, seashore, savanna)
- Plant and animal cards to match (a reed and a frog; a cactus and a lizard; a tree and a
  squirrel)
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Choose one habitat everyone can observe or look at closely (a real place outside, or a
  clear picture).
- Set out the plant-and-animal cards to match to each habitat.
- Prepare to ask the guiding question: "Who lives here, and why does this home suit them?"

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Learners met a habitat earlier this year in
another unit; this lesson **deepens** the idea by asking *why each living thing suits its
home* — the first gentle step toward how living things fit their places. Keep it
**concrete before abstract**: begin with the learner's own home, then widen to the homes of
other living things. Model one habitat aloud — "this pond has water and mud; the reed
grows in the wet mud, and the frog's wet skin suits the water" — then let learners try. Use
habitats from many biomes (pond, desert, forest, seashore, savanna) so no single landscape
or climate is the default, and learners in Lagos, Lima, or Lahore can find a home that
looks like the one they know. A light ethical thread: a habitat is not ours to spoil —
caring for a home means caring for its living things (this returns in Lessons 7–8). Honor
every body: describe each card in words for a learner who is blind or has low vision; a
non-speaking learner points to the plant and the animal and to a picture for "water" or
"dry" to show why each suits.

## Procedure

1. **Gather and remember (5 min).** You have met habitats before — a living thing's home.
   Today we ask a bigger question: *why does each living thing suit its home?*
2. **Meet a habitat (15 min).** Look at one habitat together — a pond, a forest, or a
   real patch of ground outside. Say what you see: water? soil? plants? shade? Then name
   one plant and one animal that live there: "The reed lives here. The frog lives here."
   Say why each suits it: "The reed grows in wet mud. The frog's wet skin suits the
   water."
3. **Match living things to homes (10 min).** With the cards, match each plant and animal
   to its habitat. Tell a partner why it suits: "The cactus suits the desert because it
   stores water."
4. **Draw one habitat (12 min).** Draw one habitat with its plant and animal, and write
   or say one "it suits because..." sentence for each.
5. **Close (3 min).** A habitat is a home, and every living thing suits its home.
   Different homes, all over the world — each one just right for the ones who live there.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Describe one habitat with one plant and one animal, using the frame "The
  ___ lives here, and it suits this home because ___."
- **Blind/low vision:** Describe a habitat you can hear and touch (a pond you can listen
  to, moss you can feel) with a partner naming each part and why it suits.
- **Deaf/hard of hearing / non-speaking:** Point to the plant and animal in the picture
  and to a card for "water" or "dry" to show why each suits; sign the living things'
  names.
- **Motor differences:** Point to the plant and animal and direct a partner's hands to
  draw the habitat while the learner says or signals why each suits.
- **Extension:** Compare two habitats — a desert and a pond — and tell why their living
  things differ, and how each suits its own home.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner describe a habitat and name one plant and
  one animal that live there, and say why one of them suits the home?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The labeled "one habitat" drawing with a "why it suits"
  sentence.

## Home connection

At home or outside, pick one small habitat — a tree, a puddle, a patch of grass — and
watch it quietly. Tell a grown-up one plant and one animal you found, and why each suits
that home.
