# Lesson 7 — Water and Energy — Use and Save

## Summary

Learners name one way people **use** water or energy — for a drink, for washing, for
light, for cooking — and one way to **save** some of it: turning off the tap, turning off
the light, using just enough. They actually do one saving action and say why it helps,
learning that saving is care and agency, not guilt, and that in places where water is
scarce, every drop saved truly matters.

## Objectives

- Name one way people use water or energy and one way to save some of it.
  _(D10.S4.02.01)_

## Connection

Think of a tap running while no one uses it, or a light left on in an empty room. That is
using more than we need. Now think of turning the tap off, or the light off. That is
**saving**. We all use water and energy every day — to drink, to wash, to see, to cook —
and we can all save some, too. In places where water is hard to come by, every drop saved
truly matters.

## Materials

- Use-and-save picture cards (using water or energy, and saving it)
- Paper and crayons for a "use and save" promise card

## Preparation

- Choose one or two safe, simple saving actions learners can really do: turn off a tap,
  turn off a light, or take just a little water.
- Set out the use-and-save picture cards.
- Prepare to ask: "How do we use it? How can we save some?"

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Make the action **real, not symbolic** —
learners should actually turn off a tap or a light, or pour just enough water. Keep the
tone hopeful, never shaming: saving is care and agency, not guilt, and no act is too
small. This is especially true where water is scarce — in such places, "wasting less
water" may be the single most important act, and it is just as real as any other; adapt
the examples to the learners' own lives (a precious jug of water used carefully, a lamp
used only when needed, a walk instead of a ride). Never frame any family as wasteful;
honor the care people already show. State plainly that many people around the world still
lack safely managed drinking water (see Resources), and that saving water is an act of
fairness to them. Distinguish evidence from values: "turning off the tap saves water" is a
fact; "we should save water" is a value we hold because we care for all life — say it
that way. Honor every body: for a learner with motor differences, the choosing and the
saying are the act — they may direct a partner's hands to turn off the tap while saying
why it helps; adapt the tool (a long-handled tap, a light switch within reach). A learner
who is blind or has low vision can feel the tap turned, hear the water stop, and touch the
light switch; a non-speaking learner points to the action and to a picture for "saves."

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** What are two things every home uses every day? (Water and energy.)
   Name one way your home uses water, and one way it uses energy.
2. **Meet uses and savings (10 min).** Look at the cards. We **use** water for a drink and
   for washing; we **use** energy for light and for cooking. For each, name a way to
   **save** some: "We turn the tap off when we are done." "We turn the light off when we
   leave the room." "We use just enough."
3. **Choose and do one saving (15 min).** Pick one saving action you can really do right
   now, and do it. If your body makes the doing hard, choose the action and guide a
   partner's hands while you say why it helps. Feel what it is like to use just enough.
4. **Make a "use and save" promise (10 min).** Draw a promise card: one way you use water
   or energy, and one way you will save some of it, and why it helps.
5. **Close (5 min).** One person saving helps a little; many people saving helps a lot.
   Saving is a way of caring — for water, for energy, for everyone and everything that
   shares our Earth home.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Do one saving action together hand-over-hand and say the "why" together
  aloud.
- **Motor differences:** Choose the action, direct a partner's hands, and say why it
  helps; the choosing and the saying are the act of care. Adapt the tool too (a
  long-handled tap, a light switch within reach).
- **Blind/low vision:** Feel the tap turn and hear the water stop, or touch the light
  switch; a partner names the action and its reason.
- **Deaf/hard of hearing / non-speaking:** Show each use and saving with sign, pictures,
  and acting; the learner signs or points to the action and to a picture for "saves."
- **Extension:** Name a second way to save water or energy at home or in the
  neighborhood, and tell who or what it helps.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Did the learner name one way people use water or energy
  and one way to save some of it, and do the saving with a reason?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The "use and save" promise card.

## Home connection

Take your promise card home. Do the saving action with a grown-up — turn off a tap, turn
off a light, or take just enough water — and tell them why it helps our shared Earth home.
