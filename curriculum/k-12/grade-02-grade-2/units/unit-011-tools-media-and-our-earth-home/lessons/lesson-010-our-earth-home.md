# Lesson 10 — Our Earth Home: Tools, Media, and Care

## Summary

This capstone brings the whole unit together. Learners build a four-part "Earth home"
picture: a tool and the problem it solves, with an old-and-new comparison; a true fact and
its source, told as true information not selling; a habitat and who eats what for energy;
and a saving act of care for water, energy, or litter. They celebrate that tools, media,
living things, and care all belong to one shared home.

## Objectives

- Explain that tools and machines are made by people to solve a problem, and give an
  example. _(D10.S1.02.01)_
- Compare an old tool and a new tool for the same job and say what changed.
  _(D10.S1.02.02)_
- Tell where a piece of information came from, such as a book, a grown-up, or a screen,
  and ask one question about it. _(D10.S2.02.01)_
- Tell whether a simple message is trying to give you true information or to sell you
  something. _(D10.S2.02.02)_
- Describe a habitat and name one plant and one animal that live there and why it suits
  them. _(D10.S3.02.01)_
- Explain that animals get energy from eating plants or other animals, and give one
  example. _(D10.S3.02.02)_
- Name one way people use water or energy and one way to save some of it.
  _(D10.S4.02.01)_
- Follow what happens to a piece of litter and explain why putting it in the right place
  matters. _(D10.S4.02.02)_

## Connection

You have spent this unit learning four big things: **tools** solve problems and come old
and new; **media** carries true information or tries to sell, and always comes from a
source; **living things** have habitats and get energy from eating; and we can **care** by
saving water and energy and putting litter in the right place. Today we put them all
together, because they all live in the same home — the Earth we share.

## Materials

- Large paper for a class "Earth home" picture (a big circle divided into four parts)
- Paper, crayons, glue, and found natural materials

## Preparation

- Draw or mark a big circle — the Earth — divided into four parts, labeled: Tools, Media,
  Living, Care.
- Gather paper, crayons, glue, and found natural materials for adding to each part.
- Prepare to recall one thing from each strand with each learner.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). This is a **celebration, not a test.** Help
each learner recall one thing from each of the four strands and add it to the shared Earth
home picture. Keep the tone joyful, hopeful, and inclusive — every tool, every fact, every
living thing, and every act of care is welcome, and none is ranked. Hold the unit's whole
stance gently: technology met with neither worship nor fear, media met with judgment not
suspicion, the Earth met as home not storehouse, and learners met as helpers not saviors.
Let retrieval do its quiet work: each learner naming one thing from each strand is spaced
practice of the whole unit. Honor every body: a learner who is blind or has low vision may
add a found object by feel to each part while a partner describes the part; a non-speaking
learner adds marks and points while a partner names them; a learner with motor differences
directs a partner's hands. Every body is honored, and every body's contribution counts.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Look at the big Earth circle. This is our shared home. Today we
   fill it with everything we learned.
2. **Part one — Tools (10 min).** Name one tool and the problem it solves, and say one way
   an old tool and a new tool for the same job are different. Draw or place a mark for it
   in the "Tools" part.
3. **Part two — Media (10 min).** Name one true fact, where it came from, and one question
   about it; then say how you know it is true information and not selling. Draw a mark in
   the "Media" part.
4. **Part three — Living (10 min).** Name a habitat with one plant and one animal that
   suit it, and say who eats what to get energy. Draw them in the "Living" part.
5. **Part four — Care (10 min).** Name one way to save water or energy, or put a piece of
   litter in the right place, and do one small act of care right now. Add a mark to the
   "Care" part.
6. **Celebrate (5 min).** Step back and look at the full Earth home. Tools, media, living
   things, and care — all together, in one home, cared for by many hands.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Work on one part of the Earth at a time, or let the learner add just one
  mark to a shared class picture with a partner's help.
- **Motor differences:** Direct a partner's hands to add each mark while the learner says
  what it stands for; the choosing and the saying are the contribution.
- **Blind/low vision:** Add found objects by feel to each part while a partner describes
  the part and names what the learner chose.
- **Deaf/hard of hearing / non-speaking:** Share each part with sign, pictures, and
  acting; the learner signs or points to the mark they add and to the part it belongs to.
- **Extension:** Make a small four-page "Earth home" book, one page for each part, and add
  a fifth page about a new way the learner wants to help.

## Assessment

- **Portfolio:** The four-part "Earth home" picture or book becomes a portfolio artifact
  capturing the whole unit.
- **Observation (formative):** Can the learner recall one thing from each of the four
  strands — a tool and its problem (and old vs new), a fact and its source (true info vs
  selling), a habitat and who eats what, and a saving act of care?

## Home connection

Show your "Earth home" picture to a grown-up and tell them the four things you learned: a
tool and its problem, a true fact and its source, a habitat and who eats what, and one
saving act of care you did. Ask them what small act of care they like to do for the Earth.
