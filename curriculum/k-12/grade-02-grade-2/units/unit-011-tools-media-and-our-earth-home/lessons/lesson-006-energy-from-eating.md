# Lesson 6 — Energy from Eating

## Summary

Learners discover that every living thing needs **energy**, and that **animals get their
energy from eating plants or other animals**. They follow a simple food chain — the **sun**
gives the grass its start, the **rabbit** gets energy from eating the grass, and the
**fox** gets energy from eating the rabbit — and draw it with arrows showing which way the
energy moves.

## Objectives

- Explain that animals get energy from eating plants or other animals, and give one
  example. _(D10.S3.02.02)_

## Connection

Where does your energy come from? From the food you eat. Every animal is the same: a
**cow** gets energy from eating **grass**; a **rabbit** gets energy from eating plants; a
**fox** gets energy from eating other animals. All animals get energy from eating —
plants, or animals that ate plants. That is the chain of energy that runs through every
meadow, forest, and sea.

## Materials

- Food-chain picture cards (sun, grass, a rabbit, a fox)
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Prepare the food-chain cards: sun → grass → rabbit → fox, plus arrow cards showing the
  direction energy moves.
- Prepare examples of plant-eaters (a cow, a rabbit, an elephant) and animal-eaters (a
  lion, an owl, a fox).
- Set out paper and crayons.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Keep it **concrete and gentle**: energy is
what lets a body move, grow, and live, and eating is how animals take it in. The standard
asks only that learners say animals get energy from eating plants or other animals and give
one example — do not push toward the full detail of how plants make food (that comes in
Grade 3). Use a food chain as a *story* of energy moving, not a scary story of eating:
say "the fox gets energy from the rabbit," not gory detail. Offer chains from many biomes —
grass → rabbit → fox; sea plants → fish → shark; grass → zebra → lion — so no single
place is the default. A light ethical thread: every living thing takes energy to live, and
all of it belongs to one connected living world (this returns in Lessons 7–8 and the
capstone). Honor every body: describe each card in words for a learner who is blind or has
low vision; a non-speaking learner places the cards in order and points to the arrow to
show the direction energy moves; acting out each part works for learners who learn best by
moving.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** What gives *you* energy to run and play? (Food.) Every animal gets
   energy the same way — by eating.
2. **Meet the chain (10 min).** Lay out the cards. The **sun** gives the **grass** its
   start. The **rabbit** eats the grass and gets energy from the plant. The **fox** eats
   the rabbit and gets energy from the animal. The **arrows** show which way the energy
   moves.
3. **Say where the energy comes from (10 min).** Together, finish the sentences: "The
   rabbit gets energy from ___ (eating plants)." "The fox gets energy from ___ (eating
   other animals)." Point or act each one.
4. **Meet more eaters (8 min).** Name a few more: a **cow** and an **elephant** are
   plant-eaters; a **lion**, an **owl**, and a **shark** are animal-eaters. Say where each
   gets energy.
5. **Draw a chain (10 min).** Draw a food chain of your own — sun, a plant, a
   plant-eater, an animal-eater — with arrows showing which way the energy moves. Label
   each part.
6. **Close (2 min).** Animals get energy from eating plants or other animals. Every
   meadow, forest, and sea is threaded with this chain of energy — all connected, all
   alive.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Order the given chain (sun → grass → rabbit → fox) and say one sentence:
  "The rabbit gets energy from eating plants."
- **Blind/low vision:** Order the chain by feel (raised cards or named objects) and say
  each part and where its energy comes from.
- **Deaf/hard of hearing / non-speaking:** Place the cards in order and sign or point to
  the arrow to show the direction energy moves; a partner says the sentence.
- **Motor differences:** Direct a partner's hands to lay out the chain and draw while the
  learner says or points to where each part gets energy.
- **Extension:** Build a second chain of your own choosing and tell where its plant-eater
  and animal-eater each get energy.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner explain that animals get energy from eating
  plants or other animals, and give one example with the direction energy moves?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The drawn food chain with labeled parts and arrows.

## Home connection

At home, name one food you ate today and trace it back: did it come from a plant, or from
an animal that ate a plant? Tell a grown-up the chain of energy that ended on your plate.
