# Lesson 9 — Our True-Info Care Card

## Summary

Learners bring the unit's two halves together by **making a message that cares**. Each
writes a "true-info care card": one true fact about saving water, energy, or litter, with
**where they learned it**, a check that it is **true information and not selling**, and an
**audience** to share it with. Making a message teaches the message — and sharing it makes
care real.

## Objectives

- Tell where a piece of information came from, such as a book, a grown-up, or a screen,
  and ask one question about it. _(D10.S2.02.01)_
- Tell whether a simple message is trying to give you true information or to sell you
  something. _(D10.S2.02.02)_

## Connection

You have learned two big things this unit: how to tell **true information** from
**selling**, and how to **care** for water, energy, and the land. Today you put them
together. You will make a small message that shares one true fact about caring for the
Earth — with its source, and with no selling. A message that cares tells the truth and
asks for nothing.

## Materials

- Care card template (space for one fact, "where I learned this," a "true information"
  check, and "who is it for")
- Crayons and pencils

## Preparation

- Prepare or fold the care card template, one per learner.
- Model one example aloud: "Turn off the tap when you are done, to save water. I learned
  this from a grown-up. It is true information, not selling. It is for my family."
- Set out crayons and pencils.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). This is a **making and sharing** day, not a
test. The heart of it is that a message can *care*: it can tell a true fact, name its
source, and ask for nothing in return. Retrieval: learners recall Lesson 3 (source + one
question), Lesson 4 (true information vs selling), and Lessons 7–8 (saving water and
energy, litter's right place). Keep the media stance warm — making a truthful message is
the joyful opposite of fear. Honor all facts equally: a fact from a grandmother's spoken
word is as real a source as one from a book or a screen. Distinguish evidence from values
gently: the fact ("turning off the tap saves water") is evidence; wanting to share it is a
value of care — both are welcome. No wealth assumption: a card on any scrap of paper, or a
fact told aloud and acted out, counts just as much as a printed card. Honor every body: a
non-speaking learner can draw and point while a partner writes, or record the fact in
sign; a learner who is blind or has low vision can say the fact aloud and have a partner
scribe it, and feel the card's shape; a learner with motor differences can direct a
partner's hands to write. Peer check: partners check each other's card for "true
information, not selling."

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** You can tell true information from selling, and you can care for
   the Earth. Today you make a message that does both at once.
2. **Model one care card (10 min).** Watch or make one together: a true fact ("Turn off
   the tap when you are done, to save water"), where it came from ("a grown-up told me"),
   a check for "true information, not selling," and who it is for ("my family").
3. **Choose your fact (5 min).** Pick one true fact about caring for water, energy, or
   litter — from Lesson 7 or 8, or one you know. Say where you learned it.
4. **Make your card (15 min).** Write and draw your care card: the fact, its source, the
   check, and your audience. Ask one question about your own fact: *How do I know it is
   true?*
5. **Peer check (5 min).** Trade cards with a partner. Is the fact true information, or
   is it selling? Is the source written? Give one kind word.
6. **Share (8 min).** Read or act your care card to someone — a partner, a small group, or
   the class. Tell them the fact, the source, and who it is for.
7. **Close (2 min).** A message that cares tells the truth, names its source, and asks for
   nothing. You are now a maker of caring messages.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use the frame "___ is a true fact. I learned it from ___. It is true
  information, not selling. It is for ___," with a partner's help.
- **Blind/low vision:** Say the fact aloud and have a partner scribe it; feel the card and
  share it by voice.
- **Deaf/hard of hearing / non-speaking:** Draw the fact and share it in sign or by
  pointing while a partner reads the words aloud.
- **Motor differences:** Direct a partner's hands to write and draw while the learner says
  or points to the fact, source, and audience.
- **Extension:** Add a second true fact with a second source, or check a partner's card
  and explain why it is true information and not selling.

## Assessment

- **Formative (peer):** Does the partner's card name a true fact, its source, and show it
  is true information (not selling)?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The finished "true-info care card" becomes a portfolio
  artifact, capturing the media and stewardship threads together.

## Home connection

Show your care card to a grown-up and share your true fact and its source. Ask the
grown-up: what is one true fact about caring for the Earth that they would put on a card,
and who would they share it with?
