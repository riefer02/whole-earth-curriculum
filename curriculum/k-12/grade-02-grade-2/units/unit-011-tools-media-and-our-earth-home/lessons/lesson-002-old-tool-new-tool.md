# Lesson 2 — Old Tool, New Tool

## Summary

Learners compare an old tool and a new tool that do the **same job** and name what
**changed**. A mortar and pestle and an electric grinder both grind grain; a needle and a
sewing machine both join cloth; a water jug and a hand pump both move water. They notice
that the new tool is often faster or more powerful, and often needs **electricity** — and
that the old tool still works where there is no power. New is not better; it is just
different.

## Objectives

- Compare an old tool and a new tool for the same job and say what changed.
  _(D10.S1.02.02)_

## Connection

How did your family make food this morning — by hand, or with a machine? In many homes a
person grinds grain by hand with a stone mortar and pestle; in others an electric grinder
does it. Both do the same job. The difference is what changed: the machine is faster, but
it needs electricity, and the hand tool works anywhere. That is what it means to compare
an old tool and a new tool.

## Materials

- Old-and-new tool picture cards (pairs for the same job)
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Prepare or draw old-and-new pairs: mortar and pestle → electric grinder; needle →
  sewing machine; water jug → hand pump; hand broom → vacuum.
- Have ready one real old tool to hold (a mortar and pestle, or a needle) if available.
- Prepare to say, for each pair, the shared job and what changed.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Hold the unit's stance carefully: teach
technology with **neither worship nor fear**. A new tool is not "better" simply because it
is new — it is often faster or more powerful, and it often needs electricity that not
everyone has. An old tool is not "worse" simply because it is old — it works anywhere,
never runs out of power, and people still use it every day. Both are real tools made by
people to solve a problem (retrieval: link back to Lesson 1). Use examples from many
places so no one home is the default: a hand water pump in a village, a mortar and pestle
in a kitchen, a sewing machine in a workshop. Distinguish evidence from values: "the
machine is faster" is a fact; "the machine is better" is a value judgment — and at this
age we hold them apart gently. Honor every body: a learner who is blind or has low vision
can feel the old tool and hear the new one described; a non-speaking learner can point to
old or new and to a picture for "faster" or "needs electricity."

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Remember Lesson 1: tools solve problems. Sometimes there is an
   **old** way and a **new** way to solve the same problem.
2. **Meet old and new (15 min).** For each pair, name the shared job first: "Both the
   mortar and the grinder grind grain." Then say what changed: "The grinder is faster,
   and it needs electricity. The mortar works by hand, with no power."
3. **Sort old and new (10 min).** With the cards, put each tool in the "old" pile or the
   "new" pile. For one pair, say the job and what changed.
4. **Say it in a sentence (5 min).** Finish the frame together: "The ___ and the ___
   both ___. What changed is ___."
5. **Draw one pair (8 min).** Draw an old tool and a new tool for the same job, and write
   or say what changed.
6. **Close (2 min).** New is not better and old is not worse — they are two ways to solve
   the same problem. Some places have electricity; some do not. Both tools matter.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Compare one pair only, saying the job and one change together aloud with
  the frame.
- **Blind/low vision:** Feel the old tool and hear the new one described; tell what
  changed (faster, needs electricity) by voice.
- **Deaf/hard of hearing / non-speaking:** Sort cards by pointing, and signal "faster" or
  "needs electricity" with a sign or picture to say what changed.
- **Motor differences:** Direct a partner's hands to sort and draw while the learner says
  or points to the job and the change; use big, light cards.
- **Extension:** Tell why the old tool still matters where there is no electricity, and
  name one job your family does by hand.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner compare one old tool and one new tool for
  the same job and name what changed (speed, power, or electricity)?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The "old tool, new tool" drawing with the "what changed"
  sentence.

## Home connection

At home, find one job your family does with a tool. Ask a grown-up: how did people do this
job long ago, before the new tool? Tell them what changed and which way your family uses.
