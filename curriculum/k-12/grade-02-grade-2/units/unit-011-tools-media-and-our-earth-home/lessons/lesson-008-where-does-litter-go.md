# Lesson 8 — Where Does Litter Go?

## Summary

Learners follow what happens to a piece of **litter**. Dropped on the ground, **rain** can
wash it into a **river** and out to the **sea**, where animals may mistake it for food. Put
in the **right place** — a bin — it can be **reused**, **recycled**, or **composted**.
Learners learn that putting litter in the right place keeps water, soil, and animals safe,
and that this small act of care is one anyone can do.

## Objectives

- Follow what happens to a piece of litter and explain why putting it in the right place
  matters. _(D10.S4.02.02)_

## Connection

Think of a sweet wrapper or a paper scrap dropped on the ground. It does not stay there.
Rain can wash it into a gutter, then a river, then the sea, where a fish might mistake it
for food. The same piece, put in a **bin**, can be carried away and reused, recycled, or
composted. The **right place** for litter keeps water, soil, and animals safe — and putting
it there is something anyone can do.

## Materials

- Litter-path picture cards (the dropped path and the bin path)
- A small piece of clean litter and a bin
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Prepare the two-path picture cards: (1) dropped on the ground → rain → river → sea →
  an animal that could eat it; (2) put in the bin → reused, recycled, or composted.
- Have a small piece of clean litter and a bin ready for the real action.
- Prepare to ask: "Where does it go from here, and what happens along the way?"

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Keep it **hopeful and full of agency, never
fearful or shaming**: litter is a problem every child can help with, and the message is "I
can put it in the right place," not "the Earth is ruined." Follow the litter *faithfully*:
rain washes litter into gutters, rivers, and the sea; some kinds (especially plastic) last
a very long time and can be eaten by animals that mistake it for food (see Resources). But
the right place — a bin — lets it be reused, recycled, or composted, and keeps living
things safe. Distinguish evidence from values: "rain can wash litter into the sea" is a
fact; "we should put litter in the right place" is a value we hold because we care for
living things — say it that way. No wealth assumption: a bin, a basket, or a cloth bag all
count as the right place, and in some places people reuse or compost nearly everything
already. Honor every body: for a learner with motor differences, the choosing and the
pointing to the right place are the act, and they may direct a partner's hands to carry
the litter; a learner who is blind or has low vision can feel the litter and the bin and
follow the path by listening to the story; a non-speaking learner points to each step of
the path in order.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** What happens to a piece of litter after it is dropped? Pause and
   wonder. Does it stay put, or does it travel?
2. **Follow the dropped path (10 min).** Trace the first path: litter dropped on the
   ground → **rain** washes it along → into a **river** → out to the **sea** → an animal
   may **mistake it for food**. Say each step as you move the paper litter along.
3. **Follow the bin path (10 min).** Now trace the second path: litter in the **bin** →
   carried away → **reused**, **recycled**, or **composted**. Say each step.
4. **Say why it matters (10 min).** Finish the sentence: "Putting litter in the right
   place matters because ___." (It keeps water, soil, and animals safe.) Then actually
   carry a piece of litter to the right place.
5. **Draw the two paths (10 min).** Draw the dropped path and the bin path for one piece
   of litter, with arrows, and label why the right place matters.
6. **Close (3 min).** A piece of litter can travel far and harm living things — or, in the
   right place, it can be turned into something useful again. Putting litter in the right
   place is a small act of care that anyone can do.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Trace one path step by step together and say one reason the right place
  matters.
- **Blind/low vision:** Feel the litter and the bin, and follow the path by listening to
  the story told step by step; retell it by voice.
- **Deaf/hard of hearing / non-speaking:** Move the paper litter along each path and point
  to each step in order; a partner says the steps aloud.
- **Motor differences:** Direct a partner's hands to move the litter and draw the paths
  while the learner says or points to each step.
- **Extension:** Name one thing at home that your family reuses or composts, and tell how
  that keeps litter out of the wrong place.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner follow what happens to a piece of litter
  on both paths and explain why putting it in the right place matters?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The "two paths" drawing with arrows and a "why it matters"
  label.

## Home connection

At home, watch where a piece of litter goes. With a grown-up, carry one piece to the right
place — a bin, a basket, or a compost pile — and tell them why the right place keeps water,
soil, and animals safe.
