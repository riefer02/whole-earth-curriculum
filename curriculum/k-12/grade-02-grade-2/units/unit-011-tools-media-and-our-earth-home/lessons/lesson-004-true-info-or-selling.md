# Lesson 4 — True Information or Selling?

## Summary

Learners tell whether a simple message is trying to give **true information** or to
**sell** something. They use clues: a selling message asks you to *buy*, shows a *price*,
and says *"best ever!"* or *"buy now!"*; a true-information message tells a fact and asks
for nothing. They learn that selling is not bad — grown-ups buy things they need — but
*noticing* when a message wants our money is a power everyone can have.

## Objectives

- Tell whether a simple message is trying to give you true information or to sell you
  something. _(D10.S2.02.02)_

## Connection

Think of a message you saw today — a sign in a shop, a label on a packet, a picture on a
screen. Some messages tell you a true fact: "Brushing your teeth twice a day helps keep
them clean." Other messages want you to buy: "BUY Sparkle Paste — the BEST ever! Only five coins!"
One gives information; the other is selling. We can tell them apart by their clues.

## Materials

- Message cards (pairs of a true-information message and a selling message)
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Prepare or write message pairs: a true-information message (a fact, no price) and a
  selling message (asks to buy, shows a price, says "best ever").
- Prepare the clue list: *asks you to buy · shows a price · "best ever!" · "buy now!"*
- Set out paper and crayons.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Keep it fair and warm: **selling is not
bad, and noticing it is not suspicion.** People buy food, soap, and shoes, and sellers
help them find these things. The skill is simply to *notice* when a message wants our
money rather than only telling us a fact. That skill matters everywhere, and it protects
families with little to spend just as much as anyone else — knowing the clue is a kind of
fairness. Hold the curriculum's stance: media is met with judgment, not fear. Use simple,
everyday examples with no wealth assumption — a market stall, a street sign, a label on
rice or soap — so every learner recognizes a selling message from their own life. Distinguish
evidence from values: "this message shows a price" is a fact; "selling is bad" is a value
judgment we do not make. Honor every body: read each message aloud for a learner who is
blind or has low vision, and let them name the clue by voice; a non-speaking learner points
to "buy," "price," or "fact" on a clue chart. A learner who is Deaf or hard of hearing can
read the printed messages and sign "buy" or "fact" to answer.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Some messages tell you a fact; some want you to buy. How can you
   tell them apart?
2. **Meet the clues (10 min).** A message that is **selling** asks you to *buy*, shows a
   *price*, and may say *"best ever!"* or *"buy now!"* A message of **true information**
   tells a fact and asks for nothing.
3. **Sort the messages (10 min).** For each message card, say which it is and give one
   clue: "This is selling because it shows a price." Or: "This is true information
   because it tells a fact and asks for nothing."
4. **Make one of each (12 min).** Write or draw one true-information message (a fact you
   know is true) and one selling message (about something you would like to sell), with a
   price and a "buy" word. Label each: *true information* or *selling*.
5. **Close (3 min).** Selling is not bad — but noticing it is a power. A careful reader
   sees the price and the "buy now!" and says: "This is selling. Do I need this, or am I
   just being asked to buy?"

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Sort one pair and name one clue together aloud, using the clue chart.
- **Blind/low vision:** Hear each message read aloud and name the clue by voice (price,
  buy, or fact).
- **Deaf/hard of hearing / non-speaking:** Read the printed messages and point to "buy,"
  "price," or "fact" on a clue chart to answer.
- **Motor differences:** Direct a partner's hands to write and draw while the learner
  says or points to which message is true information and which is selling.
- **Extension:** Tell why a family might buy something even when the message is selling
  (they truly need it), and name one clue that helped you see it was selling.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner tell whether a simple message is giving
  true information or selling, and give one clue?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The "true information" and "selling" message pair, labeled.

## Home connection

At home, find one message on a packet, a sign, or a screen. Tell a grown-up whether it is
true information or selling, and give one clue. Ask the grown-up how they decide when
something is worth buying.
