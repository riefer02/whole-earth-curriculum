# Lesson 1 — Tools Are Made to Solve Problems

## Summary

Learners discover that a **tool** is a thing people **make** to **solve** a problem, and
that a **machine** is a tool with moving parts. They match a problem to the tool that
solves it — a heavy water jug to a pump, food to cut to a knife, soil to dig to a hoe —
and draw one problem and the tool that solves it. Along the way they learn that people
everywhere make tools, that no people's tools matter more than another's, and that a tool
is not magic: it is a problem made easier.

## Objectives

- Explain that tools and machines are made by people to solve a problem, and give an
  example. _(D10.S1.02.01)_

## Connection

Think of a problem you met today. A jar lid was too tight to open. A bag was too heavy
to carry. A piece of food was too hard to cut with your fingers. People made tools to
solve problems exactly like these: a gripper to open the jar, a basket or a cart to carry
the bag, a knife to cut the food. Every tool you see was made by a person who wanted to
make a problem easier.

## Materials

- Problem-and-tool picture cards (a problem and the tool that solves it, in pairs)
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Prepare or draw problem-and-tool pairs: a heavy water jug → a pump; food to cut → a
  knife; soil to dig → a hoe; cloth to join → a needle.
- Set out one real, safe tool to hold and name (a spoon, a cup, a digging stick).
- Clear space to act out a problem and its tool.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Keep the message gentle and true: tools
are wonderful, but they are not magic and they are not better just because they are new.
The heart of the idea is *made by people, to solve a problem* — a rock is not a tool until
a person picks it up to grind or dig; a spoon is a tool because a person made it. Honor
every tool equally: a mortar and pestle solves the same kind of problem as a blender, and
a water jug is just as real a tool as a pump. Never rank one people's tools above
another's, and never present any one tool as "the real modern one." A machine is simply a
tool with moving parts (a whisk, a pump, a bicycle); introduce it only as an extension of
the same idea. Honor every body: naming and matching can be done by voice, sign, touch,
gesture, or a partner's hands, and each way counts equally. Supervise any handling of
tools. For a learner who is blind or has low vision, let them hold and feel each tool and
name the problem it solves by its shape and weight; a learner who does not use a speaking
voice can point, gesture, or place the card to show the answer.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Look around you. A tool is a thing people make to help them solve
   a problem. Name one tool you can see right now and the problem it helps with.
2. **Meet problems and tools (10 min).** Look at the problem cards one by one. For each,
   name the problem: "This person has a heavy water jug to carry." Then find the tool
   that solves it: "A pump helps lift the water so it is easier to carry."
3. **Meet a machine (5 min).** Some tools have moving parts — they are called machines. A
   whisk, a pump, and a bicycle are machines. They still solve a problem; they just move
   while they do it.
4. **Match problem to tool (10 min).** With the cards, match each problem to the tool
   that solves it. Say it in a full sentence: "The knife solves the problem of food that
   is hard to cut."
5. **Draw a problem and its tool (10 min).** Draw one problem and the tool a person made
   to solve it. Write or say one sentence: "People made the ___ to solve ___."
6. **Close (5 min).** People everywhere make tools to solve problems. A tool is not
   magic and not better just because it is new — it is a problem made easier by a person's
   hands.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Name one problem and one tool together aloud, using the frame "People
  made the ___ to solve ___," while holding the real tool.
- **Blind/low vision:** Hold and feel each tool, naming the problem it solves by shape,
  weight, and texture, with a partner describing each card.
- **Deaf/hard of hearing:** Share each problem and tool with sign, pictures, and acting;
  the learner signs or points to the tool and to the problem it solves.
- **Non-speaking:** Point to or gesture toward the tool and the problem it solves, while
  a partner says the full sentence aloud.
- **Motor differences:** Choose the tool and direct a partner's hands to match and draw
  while the learner says or points to the problem; use big, light cards placed within
  reach.
- **Extension:** Name a machine with moving parts (a pump, a whisk, a bicycle) and tell
  the problem it solves.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner explain that a tool is made by people to
  solve a problem, and give one example of a problem and its tool?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The "problem and its tool" drawing with a sentence.

## Home connection

At home, find one problem in a room — a lid to open, a floor to sweep, water to carry —
and name the tool your family uses to solve it. Ask a grown-up which tool they are most
glad someone invented, and why.
