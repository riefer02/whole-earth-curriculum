# Lesson 4 — Hearing Instruments and Mood

## Summary

Learners listen with their eyes closed and **identify an instrument by its sound**,
then say whether the music **feels happy, sad, or another way**. They discover that
every culture has its own instruments — drums, flutes, strings, shakers — and that
the ear can tell them apart and feel the mood they make.

## Objectives

- Identify one instrument by its sound and say whether the music feels happy, sad, or
  another way. _(D07.S2.02.02)_

## Connection

Have you ever heard a drum before you saw it, or known a flute was playing from
across a field? Our ears can name a sound-maker without our eyes. And music carries
a **mood** — a song can make you feel like dancing, or make you feel quiet and a
little sad. All over the world, people make instruments from wood, skin, metal, and
gourd, and play them to share feelings. Today you will listen, name the instrument,
and feel the mood.

## Materials

- A few instruments or sound makers (a drum, a shaker, a flute, a plucked string)
- A blindfold, or just closed eyes

## Preparation

- Gather a few instruments or sound makers with clearly different sounds.
- Arrange the group so the sound can be heard but not seen (behind a cloth or with
  eyes closed).

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Honor the instruments you use as living people's music-making,
not curios — name the culture and the kind of sound respectfully, and do not treat
any instrument as exotic or sacred in a way that tokenizes it. There is no wrong
mood; one learner hears "happy" where another hears "excited," and both are welcome —
ask "what made you feel that?" to draw out the listening. A learner who is Deaf or
hard of hearing identifies the instrument by **feel and sight**: feel the vibration of
a drum through the floor or a table, and watch how the sound-maker is played; mood
can be felt as fast or slow, strong or gentle vibration. A learner who does not speak
points to the instrument and to a mood face or gesture. A learner who is blind or
has low vision is a star at this — listening is their home ground.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Close your eyes (or look down). Listen. A sound is a clue that
   names its maker — a **drum** thumps, a **flute** whistles, a **shaker** rattles, a
   **string** plucks. Today your ears will do the identifying.
2. **Name the instrument (15 min).** With eyes closed, hear one sound. What made it —
   a drum, a flute, a shaker, a string? Say its name, or point to a picture of it. If
   you can, feel its vibration through the floor or a table — a drum shakes big and
   slow, a shaker shakes fast and small.
3. **Feel the mood (15 min).** Now hear a little music. Does it feel **happy** — bright
   and bouncing? **Sad** — slow and low? Or another way — calm, excited, brave? Show
   the mood with your face or hands, and try to say its name. There is no wrong
   feeling; the same music can feel different to different people.
4. **Share (10 min).** Take turns naming one instrument you heard and one mood the
   music gave you. Listen to a friend — is their mood different from yours? Both are
   true.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: your ears can name a sound-maker and feel its mood.
   Every instrument is a voice, and every culture has its own — and you can hear them
   all.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer one clear sound and one clear question ("drum or flute?") and
  accept pointing or a hand signal.
- **Support (Deaf/HH):** Identify by feel (vibration) and sight (how it is played);
  feel mood as fast/slow or strong/gentle.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Point to the instrument picture and show the mood with
  face or gesture.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to identify two instruments played one after another,
  and to say whether the music's mood changed and why they think so.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Does the learner identify one instrument by its sound
  and say (or show) whether the music feels happy, sad, or another way?

## Home connection

At home or outside, close your eyes and listen for one sound — a pot tapped, a voice,
a bird. Name the sound-maker and tell a grown-up what mood it gives you.
