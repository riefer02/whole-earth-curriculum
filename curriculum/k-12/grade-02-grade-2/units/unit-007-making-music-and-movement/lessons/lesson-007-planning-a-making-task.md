# Lesson 7 — Planning a Making Task

## Summary

Learners **plan** a making task before they build: they choose **materials** and say
the **steps** in order. They discover that a plan is thinking made visible, and that
makers everywhere — potters, weavers, boat-builders — plan before they make.

## Objectives

- Plan a making task by choosing materials and saying the steps before starting.
  _(D07.S4.02.01)_

## Connection

Think of someone cooking a meal, sewing a cloth, or building a small boat. Before they
begin, they think: *what will I make? what will I use? what do I do first, next,
last?* That thinking is a **plan**. A plan keeps you from getting lost in the middle,
and it is how every maker begins. Today you will choose what to make, choose your
materials, and say your steps — all before you start.

## Materials

- A small pile of making materials (paper, cloth, string, tape, boxes, sticks, cups)
- A simple plan chart (three boxes: "what I'll make," "materials I choose," "steps")

## Preparation

- Gather a small pile of varied materials so learners have real choices to make.
- Display or draw the plan chart where everyone can see the three boxes.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Planning before making is a **skill**, so model it once — "I
will make a little boat; I choose sticks and string; my steps are gather, join, test"
— before learners plan their own (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006). A plan can be
spoken, pointed to, or drawn; writing is not required. The plan may change as the
learner builds, and that is fine — a plan is a guide, not a cage. A learner with
limited fine motor plans and directs while a partner handles the materials — choosing
and sequencing are the heart of the task. A learner who is blind or has low vision
chooses materials by feel and names the steps aloud; a partner can describe each
material. A learner who does not speak points to the materials and to the step boxes
in order. Every plan belongs.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Look at the materials in front of you. Today you will **plan**
   before you make — the way a cook or a builder does.
2. **Choose what to make (5 min).** Decide what you will make — a little boat, a
   shelter for a toy, a path for a rolling ball, a basket. Say it, or point to a
   picture of it. That is box one: **what I'll make**.
3. **Choose your materials (10 min).** Look at the pile. Which materials will you use
   to make it? Pick them out and set them beside you. That is box two: **materials I
   choose**. You do not need many — only the ones your plan needs.
4. **Say the steps (10 min).** Before you touch anything to build, say or point to
   your steps in order: first, next, last. That is box three: **steps**. You can say
   them aloud, draw them, or point to them on the chart.
5. **Now make it (10 min).** Build your plan. If a step does not work, change the
   plan — that is a maker's power. When you finish, show what you made and tell (or
   point to) the steps you planned.
6. **Close (5 min).** Remember: a plan is thinking you can see. Choosing materials
   and saying the steps before you start is how every good thing gets made.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer one clear task ("make a boat") and a two-step plan, and let the
  learner choose one material from two.
- **Support (fine motor):** The learner plans and directs while a partner handles the
  materials.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Point to the materials and the step boxes in order.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Choose materials by feel and name the steps aloud;
  a partner describes each material.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to write or draw a simple plan chart of their own,
  and to make a second plan if the first changes.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Does the learner plan a making task by choosing
  materials and saying (or showing) the steps before starting?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The plan chart (or a photo of it) and the finished thing,
  kept in the portfolio.

## Home connection

At home, plan a small making job with a grown-up — a paper boat, a place for toys.
Choose the materials together and say the steps before you start, then make it.
