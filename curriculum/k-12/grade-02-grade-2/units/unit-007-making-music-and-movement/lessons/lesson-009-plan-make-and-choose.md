# Lesson 9 — Plan, Make, and Choose

## Summary

Learners put the last two lessons together into one **flow**: they **plan** a making
task by choosing materials and steps, **make** it, then **compare two ways** and
**choose** the better one, explaining why. They discover that making is not one shot
but a flow they can travel again and again.

## Objectives

- Plan a making task by choosing materials and saying the steps before starting.
  _(D07.S4.02.01)_
- Compare two ways to solve a making problem and choose one, explaining why.
  _(D07.S4.02.02)_

## Connection

Think of a potter shaping a bowl, a weaver choosing a thread, a boat-builder deciding
between two joins. Each one does the same flow: **plan** what to make and how,
**make** it, then **compare** two ways and **choose** the one that works better. The
flow has no wrong stop on it — every stop teaches you something. Today you will travel
the whole flow on one making task.

## Materials

- A full set of making materials (paper, cloth, string, tape, boxes, sticks, cups)
- A simple flow chart: plan → make → compare and choose

## Preparation

- Set out a full set of materials so learners can plan, make, and try two ways.
- Display or draw the flow chart where everyone can see the three boxes.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. The flow is a **skill structure**, so model it once — you plan
aloud, make, compare two ways, and choose — before learners run it themselves
(Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006). Keep the three boxes as pictures or spoken words;
reading and writing are not required. Let learners travel the flow at their own speed —
some will go once, some several times; both are complete. Treat "choose" as a step
that always has a reason, not a search for a single right answer. A learner with
limited fine motor narrates and directs while a partner builds — deciding is making. A
learner who is blind or has low vision travels the flow by feel and spoken steps; the
flow chart can be made tactile (a string line with a stone at each box). A learner who
does not speak points to each box as they reach it and to the chosen way. Honor every
journey through the flow.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Look at the flow chart. These three boxes are a path:
   **plan**, **make**, **compare and choose**. Makers everywhere travel this path.
2. **Plan (10 min).** Decide what you will make and what it is for. Choose your
   materials, and say or point to your steps in order. That is the **plan** box.
3. **Make (10 min).** Build your plan. Let the plan change as you learn — a plan is a
   guide, not a cage.
4. **Compare and choose (15 min).** If your make has a problem — it sags, tips, or
   will not join — try **two ways** to fix it. Set them side by side, compare, and
   **choose** the one that works better, saying **why**. You have now traveled the
   whole flow once.
5. **Travel again (10 min).** Go through the flow once more — improve one more thing,
   or plan a new, better make. The flow never has to stop; it is how things keep
   getting better.
6. **Close (5 min).** Remember: making is a flow, not a single try — plan, make,
   compare, choose — and you can travel it again and again. Every stop taught you
   something, and what you made is yours.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Guide one full flow step by step, saying each box's name as the learner
  reaches it ("now compare — which holds better?").
- **Support (fine motor):** The learner narrates and decides while a partner builds.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Point to each box on the chart as the learner reaches
  it, and to the chosen way.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Travel the flow by feel and spoken steps; use a
  tactile chart (string line and stones) to mark each box.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to run the flow on a harder job (a bridge that holds
  a heavier stone) and tell which box taught them the most.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Does the learner travel the flow — planning a making
  task, making it, comparing two ways, and choosing one with a reason?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** A note, drawing, or photo of the make and the choice made,
  kept in the portfolio.

## Home connection

At home, choose a small making job and travel the flow with a grown-up: plan, make,
compare two ways, and choose. Tell them which step was your favorite.
