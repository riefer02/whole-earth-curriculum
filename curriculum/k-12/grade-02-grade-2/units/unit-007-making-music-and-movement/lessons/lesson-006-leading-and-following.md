# Lesson 6 — Leading and Following a Scene

## Summary

Learners act out a small scene with a **partner**, taking turns **leading** and
**following**. They discover that leading is a gift you pass along, following is an
active skill that takes attention and care, and both belong to every good
partnership.

## Objectives

- Act out a scene with a partner, taking turns leading and following.
  _(D07.S3.02.02)_

## Connection

Think of the times you move with someone else — dancing with a partner, carrying a
heavy thing together, playing a mirror game. One person **leads** for a while, and the
other **follows** — then they trade. Everywhere, people work and play this way: taking
turns so both get to lead and both get to follow. Today you will act a small scene
with a partner, leading and following in turn.

## Materials

- A simple scene idea (a market visit, carrying water, a greeting)
- A turn marker (a stone or cloth knot)
- Open floor space

## Preparation

- Choose a small, everyday scene with two clear roles, told as a scene to play.
- Clear a space for pairs, and set out the turn marker.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Keep the scene simple and everyday, and never co-opt sacred or
ceremonial roles from any culture. Use a neutral **turn marker** (a stone or cloth
knot) so no single culture's object carries the turn. Model leading and following once
— you move, a learner mirrors — before pairs try (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006).
Emphasize that following is active, not passive: a good follower watches, listens,
and matches. Never push a shy learner to perform; watching and mirroring is full
participation. A learner who uses a wheelchair or moves with support leads and follows
from where they are — a raised hand, a nod, a lean, and their partner mirrors it. A
learner who does not speak leads with movement and face, and follows the same way. A
learner who is blind or has low vision leads with voice and touch (a partner mirrors
the sound or holds a hand to feel the move). A learner who is Deaf or hard of hearing
leads and follows through visible movement and gesture. Celebrate both roles equally —
there is no "better" part.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Find a **partner**. Today you will act a small scene together.
   One of you will **lead**, and the other will **follow** — then you will trade.
2. **Practice mirroring (10 min).** Face your partner. One of you leads a slow move —
   raise a hand, tilt your head, reach out. The other **follows** by copying it at the
   same time, like a mirror. Go slowly so your partner can stay with you. Then trade:
   the follower becomes the leader.
3. **Act the scene (15 min).** Act a small everyday scene together — visiting a
   market, carrying water, greeting a friend. The leader begins and sets the moves;
   the follower stays with them. Hold the turn marker to show it is your turn to
   lead, and pass it when you trade. Both of you get a turn to lead and a turn to
   follow.
4. **Share (10 min).** Show a little of your scene to another pair if you wish. Tell
   (or point to) who led first and who followed, and how it felt to trade.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: leading is a gift you pass along, and following takes
   care and attention. Every good partnership needs both — and now you have done
   both.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer one clear action to lead ("raise one hand, and your partner
  mirrors it") and trade right away so both feel each role.
- **Support (seated/adapted):** Lead and follow from a chair — a raised hand, a nod, a
  lean; the partner mirrors it, and that is full participation.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Lead and follow with movement and face.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Lead with voice and touch; follow by holding a hand
  to feel the move.
- **Support (Deaf/HH):** Lead and follow through visible movement and gesture.
- **Extension:** Invite pairs to act a scene where the lead changes in the middle —
  the follower feels the moment and becomes the leader — then tell how they knew.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Does the learner act out a scene with a partner,
  taking turns leading and following?
- **Performance:** The learner's scene with a partner, observed live (in any adapted
  form).

## Home connection

With someone at home, play a mirror game — one of you leads a slow move and the other
follows, then trade. Tell each other which role you liked and why.
