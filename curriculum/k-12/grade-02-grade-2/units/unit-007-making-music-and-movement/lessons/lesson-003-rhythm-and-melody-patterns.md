# Lesson 3 — Rhythm and Melody Patterns

## Summary

Learners play or sing a short **rhythm or melody pattern** and **repeat it
accurately**. They discover that a pattern is sound you can say again, and that
echoing back a pattern — call-and-response — is how people everywhere share music.

## Objectives

- Play or sing a short rhythm or melody pattern and repeat it accurately.
  _(D07.S2.02.01)_

## Connection

Think of the sounds that repeat in your day — footsteps, a heartbeat, a bird's call,
a song someone sings over and over. A **pattern** is sound that comes back the same
way. All over the world, people share music by echoing: one person plays a short
pattern, and others play it back exactly. Today you will play or sing a short pattern
and repeat it so it comes back the same.

## Materials

- Hands, voices, and feet
- A small set of sound makers (a pot, a shaker, a cup, a bell)

## Preparation

- Gather a few sound makers that can tap or shake a clear pattern.
- Arrange the group in a circle so everyone can hear and see the leader.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Repeating a pattern is a **skill**, so teach it with explicit
modeling and guided practice (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006): play one short
pattern, have the group echo, then let learners lead. Keep patterns very short at
first (two to four sounds). "Accurately" means "the same the second time" — not
perfectly fast, and never "correct" vs "wrong." A learner who is Deaf or hard of
hearing repeats the pattern by **feel and sight**: watch the leader's hands and feel
the vibration of the beat through a drum, the table, or the floor, then tap it back
the same way. A learner who does not sing or speak repeats the pattern by clapping,
tapping, or humming. A learner who is blind or has low vision leads and repeats by
ear and touch. Watching and echoing is full participation.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Pat your knees in a little pattern — pat, pat, rest, pat. That
   is a **rhythm pattern**. Now hum a few notes that go up and down — that is a
   **melody pattern**. A pattern is sound you can say again.
2. **Learn to echo (10 min).** Listen to a short pattern a leader plays or sings. Play
   it back exactly the same — same sounds, same order, same length. That is an
   **echo**. Try a rhythm pattern (clap, clap, rest, clap) and a melody pattern (a few
   hummed notes).
3. **Repeat it accurately (10 min).** Play or sing your own short pattern, then repeat
   it the same way a second time. Does the second time match the first? If it does,
   you have repeated it accurately. If it drifts, that is okay — try it again, because
   accuracy grows with practice.
4. **Lead and echo (10 min).** Take a turn leading a short pattern while a friend
   echoes it back. Then trade. Leading and following are how patterns travel from one
   person to another.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: a pattern is sound that comes back the same. When you
   echo a friend's pattern, you carry their music for a moment — and that is how music
   is shared everywhere.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use a two-sound pattern (clap, pat) and echo it slowly together, one
  sound at a time.
- **Support (Deaf/HH):** Repeat by feel and sight — watch the leader's hands and feel
  the vibration through a drum or the floor, then tap it back.
- **Support (non-speaking/non-singing):** Repeat by clapping, tapping, or humming.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Lead and repeat by ear and touch.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to make a four-sound pattern and repeat it twice in a
  row without stopping, checking it stays the same.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Does the learner play or sing a short rhythm or melody
  pattern and repeat it accurately?
- **Performance:** The learner's pattern, played or sung live and repeated the same
  way.

## Home connection

Teach someone at home a short pattern — clap, clap, rest, clap — and have them echo
it back. Then let them lead a pattern for you to repeat.
