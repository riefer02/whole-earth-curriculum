# Lesson 10 — Making, Music & Movement

## Summary

This capstone gathers everything the unit explored. Learners lay out their work like a
gallery and share one thing they made — a mixed color, a rhythm or melody pattern, a
movement sequence, a partner scene, or a planned build. They see, all at once, that
colors, music, movement, and making are all **making** — and that every piece has
value and a story.

## Objectives

- Mix two colors to make a new one and use it in a picture. _(D07.S1.02.01)_
- Compare two artworks and tell one way they are alike and one way they are
  different. _(D07.S1.02.02)_
- Play or sing a short rhythm or melody pattern and repeat it accurately.
  _(D07.S2.02.01)_
- Identify one instrument by its sound and say whether the music feels happy, sad, or
  another way. _(D07.S2.02.02)_
- Create and repeat a short movement sequence with a clear beginning and end.
  _(D07.S3.02.01)_
- Act out a scene with a partner, taking turns leading and following.
  _(D07.S3.02.02)_
- Plan a making task by choosing materials and saying the steps before starting.
  _(D07.S4.02.01)_
- Compare two ways to solve a making problem and choose one, explaining why.
  _(D07.S4.02.02)_

## Connection

All through this unit, you have made things with your hands, your voice, your body,
and your ideas — a color you mixed, a pattern you played, a sequence you moved, a
scene you led, a build you planned. Today you get to look at it all together, the way
a family or a village shows its work at a fair or a festival. Making is how you share
who you are with the world — and every piece you made has a story worth hearing.

## Materials

- Each learner's favorite piece from the unit
- A simple display space (table, wall, or cloth)

## Preparation

- Help learners choose one favorite piece (a mixed-color picture, a rhythm or melody
  pattern, a movement sequence, a scene, or a planned build).
- Arrange a display space and a circle for sharing.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Make this a true celebration, not a contest. There are no
"best" pieces — the point is that every learner shares and every learner is heard. Let
learners share in whatever form suits them: talk, point, sing, move, or show. Sharing
is a choice, never a duty — a learner may pass, and watching and clapping for others
is full participation. Never single out a shy learner to share alone. Honor every
culture's arts as living heritage, and every learner's work as belonging. Close by
naming the unit's big idea together: making is a voice, and every voice matters.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Bring your favorite thing from this unit. Look around at what
   everyone brought. It is a gallery of making.
2. **Set up the gallery (10 min).** Lay out the pictures and builds. Remember the
   patterns, movements, and scenes are part of the gallery too — they live in your
   voice and body.
3. **Walk the gallery (10 min).** Move quietly around and look. Find one thing that
   makes you curious. Ask its maker, "Tell me about this" — or point to it and smile.
4. **Share and celebrate (15 min).** One at a time, share your piece if you want to —
   show your mixed color, play your pattern, move your sequence, act your scene, or
   explain your planned build and the way you chose. You may also pass, or let a
   friend help you share. Everyone claps for everyone, because every piece has a
   story.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: your hands, your voice, your body, and your ideas can
   all make something new. That is making, music, and movement — and it is yours
   forever.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer a gentle prompt to share ("show us the color you mixed," or "play
  your pattern for us") and let the learner point or gesture rather than speak.
- **Support (seated/adapted, non-speaking):** Share from where the learner is — point,
  show, sign, move a hand, or have a friend help present the piece; that is full
  participation.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to name one thing they want to make next, and how
  they would make it better.

## Assessment

- **Summative (portfolio):** The learner's favorite piece, kept in the portfolio with
  a note of its story.
- **Performance (observation):** The learner shares one piece from the unit, in any
  form.

## Home connection

Make a tiny gallery at home: lay out a few things you made this unit and give a
grown-up a "tour," telling the story of each one.
