# Lesson 9 — Conflict to Repair: A Role-Play

## Summary

Learners put the whole peace path together in one role-play: **describe** a conflict,
**choose a peaceful solve** (talk, turns, or help), and **make a real apology** that
fixes the harm. This is guided practice of the unit's restorative skill, from start
to finish, with no blame and no one left out.

## Objectives

- Describe a conflict and a peaceful way to solve it, such as talking, taking turns,
  or getting help. _(D09.S4.02.01)_
- Explain what makes an apology real, such as meaning it and trying to fix the harm.
  _(D09.S4.02.02)_

## Connection

Think back to what you have practiced: naming a conflict, choosing a peaceful way,
and making an apology that fixes the harm. Real peace is all three, in a row — not
just one piece. When two people disagree, they can talk or take turns or get help,
and then the one who hurt can make it right, and both can feel better. People
everywhere do this every day: two children over a swing, two neighbors over a wall,
two friends over a toy. Today you will practice the whole path, from conflict to
repair.

## Materials

- A three-step "conflict to repair" strip (describe the conflict, choose a peaceful
  solve, make a real apology that fixes the harm)
- Two or three puppets or soft toys

## Preparation

- Prepare the three-step strip and a small space for acting out.
- Have roles ready: the one who was hurt, the one who hurt, and (if you like) a
  helper.

## Facilitator note

Written to the learner ("you"). This lesson is **guided practice** — the learner has
seen each piece (conflict and peaceful solve in Lesson 7; real apology in Lesson 8)
and now puts them together with scaffolding. Model first: a small, gentle conflict (a
knocked-over tower, both wanting the same ball), then name each step aloud as the
group or a volunteer works it. Use the three-step strip as a worked example, then let
learners rehearse in small groups or pairs. Keep conflicts small, invented, and
blame-free; never ask a learner to act out a real, private hurt, and never require a
forced apology — the apology in the role-play is practiced as a skill, not extracted
as a confession. The helper role (a peer or a trusted grown-up) is optional and shows
that getting help is a real peaceful solve, not a last resort. A learner who does not
speak aloud points to each step on the strip or signs; a learner who is deaf or hard
of hearing follows the role-play through the strip and sign; a learner who is blind
or low-vision hears each step described and names what comes next; a learner with a
motor difference can direct a partner or play the helper. Every learner may pass. The
aim is that each learner can say, in their own way, "I can go from conflict to
repair."

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Remember Lessons 7 and 8: a conflict can be solved peacefully,
   and a real apology fixes the harm. Today you will do both, in a row.
2. **Meet the three steps (10 min).** The path has three steps: (1) describe the
   conflict — who wants what; (2) choose a peaceful solve — talk, take turns, or get
   help; (3) make a real apology — say what you did, say how they felt, mean it, fix
   the harm.
3. **Watch it modeled (10 min).** Watch (or act) one whole path: two children want
   the same ball. They take turns, and the one who grabbed says a real apology and
   offers the ball back. Name each step as it happens.
4. **Rehearse it (15 min).** In a small group or pair, choose a small conflict and
   walk the three steps. Take turns being each side, and use the helper if you like.
   A learner who does not act can point to the steps or direct a partner.
5. **Close (5 min).** You can go from conflict to repair — peacefully, with a real
   apology. That is how both people become well again.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use a scripted two-line scene with the steps written out; a
  non-speaking learner points to each step as the group does it; a learner with
  limited movement directs a partner or plays the helper.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to try a second conflict where talking alone is not
  enough and getting help is needed, and wonder why different conflicts need
  different solves.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner move through the three steps — describe
  the conflict, choose a peaceful solve, and make a real apology?
- **Performance (portfolio):** The role-play, with the steps noted (or photographed
  or drawn), kept as a portfolio artifact of the unit's restorative skill.

## Home connection

At home, teach someone the three steps: describe the conflict, choose a peaceful
solve, make a real apology. Try them the next time a small conflict comes up.
