# Lesson 10 — Fairness & Our Shared Rules, All Together

## Summary

Learners gather everything from the unit into one five-part "fairness" page: a right
choice and its reason, same-is-not-always-fair, a right every child has, a fair
decision and a helping hand, and a peaceful solve with a real apology. They share
their page and build a class "Fairness & Our Shared Rules" book.

## Objectives

- Tell the difference between something that is right to do and something that is
  wrong to do, and give a reason. _(D09.S1.02.01)_
- Explain how a kind or unkind act affects another person's feelings. _(D09.S1.02.02)_
- Explain why treating everyone the same is not always fair, using an example like
  different needs. _(D09.S2.02.01)_
- Name a right that children have, such as the right to play or learn, and why it
  matters. _(D09.S2.02.02)_
- Explain how a group makes a fair decision together, such as talking it through or
  taking a vote. _(D09.S3.02.01)_
- Identify a way people in their community help one another, such as a food drive or
  cleanup, and how they could join in. _(D09.S3.02.02)_
- Describe a conflict and a peaceful way to solve it, such as talking, taking turns,
  or getting help. _(D09.S4.02.01)_
- Explain what makes an apology real, such as meaning it and trying to fix the harm.
  _(D09.S4.02.02)_

## Connection

Fairness is not one big thing — it is many small things you can do every day: telling
right from wrong with a reason, remembering that same is not always fair, honoring
every child's rights, deciding together, lending a helping hand, and going from
conflict to repair with a real apology. When you put them together, you are practicing
how to live well with other people — at home, in class, and anywhere on Earth people
are together. Today you will put them all together.

## Materials

- A five-part "fairness" page per learner
- Crayons and drawing tools
- Stapler, string, or clips for a class book

## Preparation

- Prepare a five-box page for each learner (or one large shared sheet): "right choice
  and reason," "same is not always fair," "a right every child has," "our fair
  decision and a helping hand," and "a peaceful solve and a real apology."
- Set up a gathering place and a spot to assemble the class book.

## Facilitator note

Written to the learner ("you"). This is the unit's capstone: each learner authors
their own small statement of fairness and shared rules. Keep all five boxes optional
in depth — a word, a mark, or a drawing counts. This is a celebration of what learners
can *do*, not a test of what they know; there is no "correct" page. Celebrate each
page as a real contribution, and never rank pages against one another. A learner who
does not speak aloud shares by pointing to a box and to a picture or by signing; a
learner who is blind or low-vision describes their page aloud and the boxes are read
back to them; a learner with a motor difference can have a partner draw while they
choose and direct. Every voice matters — not just the loudest or fastest — and a
learner may always pass. The decision chart, the community-help plan, and the
"conflict to repair" strip stay live after this lesson, so the unit's habits continue
beyond the page. Questions stay questions: fairness is practiced, not preached.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Remember the unit: right and wrong with a reason, how acts
   reach feelings, same-is-not-always-fair, children's rights, deciding together,
   helping hands, conflicts and peaceful solves, and real apologies. You have
   practiced all of it.
2. **Fill the five boxes (20 min).** On your page, draw or write one thing in each
   box: a right choice and its reason; why same is not always fair; one right every
   child has and why it matters; a fair decision you helped make and one helping hand;
   and a peaceful solve with a real apology.
3. **Share (10 min).** With a partner or small group, show your page and tell about
   one box you are proud of. Listen to theirs.
4. **Make the class book (5 min).** Add your page to the "Fairness & Our Shared
   Rules" book or wall. Now everyone's fairness is together.
5. **Close (5 min).** Fairness is many small things you can do every day. Your choices
   matter — to your friends, your community, and you.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Complete one box at a time with a grown-up scribe; a single word or
  mark in each box counts. A non-speaking learner points to pictures for each box.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to add a sixth box: "tomorrow" — one fair or caring
  thing they will do next.

## Assessment

- **Summative (portfolio):** The five-part "fairness" page shows a right choice and
  reason, same-is-not-always-fair, a child's right, a fair decision and helping hand,
  and a peaceful solve with a real apology.
- **Observation:** Does the learner connect each box to the right idea from the unit
  and share it in their own words?

## Home connection

Take your "fairness" page home and share one box with your family. Ask them what
fairness means to them, and listen.
