# Lesson 5 — Deciding Fairly Together

## Summary

Learners explain two ways a group makes a fair decision together — **talking it
through** and **taking a vote** — and practice making a real shared decision where
every voice matters, not just the loudest or fastest.

## Objectives

- Explain how a group makes a fair decision together, such as talking it through or
  taking a vote. _(D09.S3.02.01)_

## Connection

Think of a time your family or friends had to choose one thing together — what to
eat, which game to play, which way to walk. How did you decide? A group can **talk it
through**, hearing everyone's idea and reason, or **take a vote**, letting everyone
show their choice and counting. People everywhere decide together: a council of
elders under a tree talking until all agree, a fishing crew choosing where to sail,
a village choosing when to plant, a classroom choosing a game. Today you will make a
real decision together, fairly.

## Materials

- A decision chart (large sheet or poster)
- A soft object as a turn marker
- Choice tokens (a pebble, seed, or mark per learner)

## Preparation

- Choose a real, small decision the group can make (which game to play, which name
  for the class pet, where to sit for story time).
- Prepare the decision chart; have the turn marker and tokens ready; arrange a circle.

## Facilitator note

Written to the learner ("you"). This lesson teaches **deciding together**, not
obeying a decision handed down. Offer two fair ways and let learners practice both:
**talk it through** (everyone's idea and reason is heard before choosing) and **take a
vote** (everyone shows a choice and the group counts; the choice most people picked
wins, but the group also listens to the ones who did not win). Neither way is a
dogma — a good group often does both. Use a passing object as a **turn marker** so one
voice is heard at a time; it is a reminder to listen, not a decoration. Every voice
matters — not just the loudest or fastest; invite quieter voices, and show that a good
idea can come from anyone. A learner who does not speak aloud shows their choice with
a token, a raised hand, a step into a circle, or by pointing; a learner who is deaf
or hard of hearing follows the choices through the chart and sign; a learner who is
blind or low-vision hears the choices read back and shows a choice by voice or touch;
a learner with a motor difference shows their choice by directing a partner or by eye
gaze, and their choice is counted. A learner may always pass. The goal is a decision
the group *made together* — and can say how it was made.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of a decision your group needs to make today. What are
   the choices?
2. **Meet two fair ways (10 min).** Learn two ways to decide together: **talk it
   through** — hear everyone's idea and reason — and **take a vote** — everyone shows
   a choice, then you count. Sometimes a group does both.
3. **Talk it through (10 min).** Pass the turn marker. When you hold it, share your
   idea and a reason (or point, sign, or have a peer voice it). Everyone's idea is
   heard.
4. **Take a vote (10 min).** Show your choice — a token, a raised hand, a step into a
   circle, or a nod. Count together and record the count on the chart. The choice most
   people picked is the group's choice.
5. **Close (5 min).** A fair decision is made together — by talking it through and
   taking a vote — and every voice matters, not just the loudest.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer two ready-made choices with pictures; a non-speaking learner
  points or nods to choose. A learner with limited movement directs a partner to place
  their token.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to wonder what could happen if a decision were made
  with no talking and no vote — and why hearing everyone's voice matters.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner explain how a group makes a fair
  decision (talking it through, voting) and take part?
- **Performance:** The learner's part in the decision, recorded on the chart.

## Home connection

At home, help your family make one small decision fairly — talk it through, then
vote. Say which way you used and how it felt.
