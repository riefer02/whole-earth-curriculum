# Lesson 7 — A Conflict and a Peaceful Way

## Summary

Learners describe a small conflict and name a peaceful way to solve it — talking it
through, taking turns, or getting help. A conflict is just a disagreement; it can be
solved without hurting anyone, and both sides can end up okay.

## Objectives

- Describe a conflict and a peaceful way to solve it, such as talking, taking turns,
  or getting help. _(D09.S4.02.01)_

## Connection

Think of a time two people both wanted the same thing — the same swing, the same
ball, the last piece of fruit. That is a **conflict**: a disagreement, when two people
want different things or want the same thing. A conflict does not have to end in
hitting or grabbing or crying. There are **peaceful** ways to solve it: talk it
through, take turns, or get help. People everywhere solve conflicts this way — two
farmers deciding who waters first, children passing a ball, neighbors asking an
elder to help. Today you will describe a conflict and name a peaceful way.

## Materials

- Conflict picture cards (two children wanting the same swing, a knocked-over tower,
  both wanting the last of something, a friend left out)
- Two puppets or soft toys

## Preparation

- Prepare the conflict picture cards.
- Set up a small space for acting out a conflict and a peaceful solution.

## Facilitator note

Written to the learner ("you"). This lesson separates **conflict** (a disagreement,
which is normal and healthy) from **violence** (hurting, which is not needed to solve
it). Keep conflicts small, everyday, and blame-free — no one is "bad" for wanting the
same swing; the question is only *how* we solve it. Offer three peaceful ways and let
learners choose: **talk it through** (both sides say what they need), **take turns**
(share the thing or the time), and **get help** (ask a trusted grown-up or peer to
help both be heard). Getting help is strength, not weakness — people everywhere turn
to an elder, a neighbor, or a friend. A learner who does not speak aloud describes
the conflict by pointing to a card and shows the peaceful way by pointing, signing,
or acting; a learner who is deaf or hard of hearing follows each conflict through the
cards and sign; a learner who is blind or low-vision hears the conflict described and
names the peaceful way aloud; a learner with a motor difference can direct a partner
to move the puppets. Never ask a learner to confess a real, private conflict; invented
or acted conflicts are welcome, and a learner may always pass.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of a time two people wanted the same thing. What
   happened? Did it end in hurt, or in a peaceful solve?
2. **Meet "conflict" and "peaceful" (10 min).** A **conflict** is a disagreement — two
   people wanting different things or the same thing. **Peaceful** ways solve it
   without hurting: talk it through, take turns, get help.
3. **Describe and solve (15 min).** For each conflict card, describe it: who wants
   what? Then name one peaceful way to solve it (say, sign, point, or act it).
   Wonder together: which way fits this conflict best?
4. **Act it out (10 min).** With the puppets, show a conflict — both puppets want the
   same ball. Practice one peaceful way: talk, take turns, or get help. A learner who
   does not move the puppets can point to a way or direct a partner.
5. **Close (5 min).** A conflict is just a disagreement. It can be solved peacefully —
   with talking, turns, or help — so no one has to be hurt.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use one conflict and model one peaceful way with the puppets; a
  non-speaking learner points to the peaceful way; a learner with limited movement
  directs a partner.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to compare two peaceful ways for the same conflict
  and wonder which fits better and why.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner describe a conflict and name a
  peaceful way to solve it?
- **Performance:** The acted-out conflict and peaceful solution, shared with the
  group.

## Home connection

At home, if a small conflict comes up, describe it out loud ("two people want the
same…") and try one peaceful way — talk, take turns, or get help.
