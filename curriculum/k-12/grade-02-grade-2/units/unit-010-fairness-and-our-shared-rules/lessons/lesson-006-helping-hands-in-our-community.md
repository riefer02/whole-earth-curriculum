# Lesson 6 — Helping Hands in Our Community

## Summary

Learners identify ways people in their community help one another — a food drive, a
cleanup, reading to a younger child, watering a shared garden — and plan one way they
could join in. Helping is something anyone can do, whatever their body can do.

## Objectives

- Identify a way people in their community help one another, such as a food drive or
  cleanup, and how they could join in. _(D09.S3.02.02)_

## Connection

Think of a place near you where people help each other — a neighbor who shares food
when someone is sick, a group that cleans the stream or the street, an older child
who reads to a younger one, someone who waters the shared garden so everyone can
eat. That is a **community**: people helping one another. And you can **join in** —
even a small help counts. People everywhere do this: a village clears the well, a
city shares food, a family waters the plot so all can harvest. Today you will name
one way people help near you, and one way you could join in.

## Materials

- Community-help picture cards (a food drive, a cleanup, reading to a younger child,
  watering a shared garden, carrying water for a neighbor)
- Crayons and paper

## Preparation

- Prepare the community-help picture cards, each with a simple scene and label.
- Arrange a circle for acting out and sharing.

## Facilitator note

Written to the learner ("you"). This lesson teaches **collective action** at the size
a 7–8-year-old can hold: people help one another, and a child can join in. Keep it
concrete and local — name real helps learners can see (a shared garden, a cleanup, a
food share, reading to a younger child). Global and anthropological framing: every
human community has ways of mutual aid (sharing harvests, mending shared things,
carrying water) — helping is not one culture's idea. Emphasize that a child's help is
real, not play-acting, and that every body can help in its own way: a learner who
does not speak aloud points to a card or signs; a learner who is blind or low-vision
hears each help described and names how they could join; a learner with a motor
difference plans a help they can do (telling the story, holding the bag, being the
lookout). Never require anyone to do physical work they cannot; "joining in" includes
suggesting, cheering, and caring. Do not send learners to do unsupervised tasks
outside — the plan is for *later*, with a grown-up. A learner may always pass.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of one time someone helped you or your family. What did
   they do? That is community help.
2. **Meet the helps (10 min).** Look at the cards: a food drive, a cleanup, reading to
   a younger child, watering a shared garden, carrying water for a neighbor. Name each
   help and who it helps.
3. **Choose one and plan to join in (15 min).** Pick one help (point, sign, or say
   it). Say how it helps, then plan your first step: "I could join in by…" — carrying
   a can to the box, picking up one piece of litter, reading one page, pouring one cup
   of water.
4. **Draw your "join in" plan (10 min).** Draw or write one way people help near you
   and one step you could take to join in. If drawing is not your way, build it, act it
   out, or have a partner draw while you direct. Tell a partner your plan.
5. **Close (5 min).** A community is people helping one another — and you can join
   in. Even a small help counts.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer two helping cards and model one sentence "I could join in by…"
  A non-speaking learner points to the card and the step; a learner with limited
  movement directs a partner.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to think of a help their community needs that is not
  on the cards, and a first step they could take with a grown-up.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner identify a way people help one another
  and say how they could join in?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The "join in" plan (drawn, built, acted, or
  partner-scribed), kept in the learner's portfolio.

## Home connection

At home, ask what way your family or neighbors help one another. With a grown-up,
take one small step to join in — and tell someone how it helped.
