# Lesson 5 — Combining Movements

## Summary

Learners move from single movements to sequences. They learn that a sequence is two or
more movements joined in an order, then practice combining movements — hopping then
turning, running then catching — with control, in their own body's way.

## Objectives

- Combine movements in a sequence, such as running and catching or hopping and
  turning. _(D03.S2.02.01)_

## Connection

Think of the things your body already does in an order without you even planning it:
you run, then catch a ball; you hop, then turn to go back; you climb, then slide. A
sequence is just movements joined in order, like the steps of a dance. Today you will
put two movements together on purpose.

## Materials

- Cones, chalk, or rope to mark a path
- Soft scarves or beanbags
- A beat source (clapping or drum, optional)

## Preparation

- Mark a clear space and a simple path with cones, chalk, or rope.
- Set out soft scarves or beanbags for the catch part.
- Clear enough room for everyone to move without bumping.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Prepare the space, then model each two-movement sequence slowly
before learners try. This spirals up from Grade 1's single movements (skip, gallop,
hop, jump) — now two movements join into a sequence. This is movement, not a race:
celebrate control and order, never speed or distance. Honor every body: a learner who
uses a wheelchair can run with their wheels and catch with a lap tray; a learner who
needs support can do the sequence seated or with a partner's help. Model adapted
versions as real choices, never second choices. Call the order out loud and keep a
visual beat going (a bouncing hand) so learners who are deaf or hard of hearing can
follow. Invite everyone to rest and go at their own pace.

## Procedure

1. **Warm up (5 min).** March in place, then shake out your arms and legs. Your body
   is awake and ready.
2. **Learn the first sequence: hop, then turn (10 min).** Try each part alone first.
   Hop on one foot. Then stand and turn all the way around. Now put them in order:
   **hop, then turn.** Do it slowly at first, arms out for balance.
3. **Learn the second sequence: run, then catch (10 min).** Run a few steps in place
   or to a mark. Then catch a scarf or beanbag you toss to yourself. Now put them in
   order: **run, then catch.** Eyes on the scarf, hands ready.
4. **Put it together (15 min).** From the starting mark, do your first sequence — hop,
   then turn. Then your second — run, then catch. Say the order out loud as you move:
   "hop, turn. Run, catch." Do every part in your own body's way — with wheels or
   seated springs if that is you.
5. **Close (5 min).** Slow down and catch your breath. You just joined movements into
   a sequence — a movement story with a beginning and an end.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Practice each movement alone first, then join only two parts; clap or
  count the order slowly.
- **Extension:** Ask learners to reverse the order (turn, then hop) or add a third
  movement to the sequence.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner combine two movements in an order with
  control, in their own body's way?

## Home connection

Show someone at home a two-movement sequence — hop then turn, or run then catch — and
teach them the order.
