# Lesson 2 — What Each Sense Tells Me

## Summary

Learners go one step deeper than matching: they describe one thing each of the five
senses actually tells them. Using a "sense basket" of safe everyday objects, they name
what they see, hear, smell, taste, and feel, then record it on a chart.

## Objectives

- Describe one thing each sense tells us. _(D03.S1.02.01)_

## Connection

Close your eyes for a moment and think of a market or a kitchen you know. You can see
the colors of the fruit, hear the sizzle and chatter, smell the spices, taste a sample
on your tongue, and feel the smooth or bumpy skin of a vegetable. Each sense tells you
a different part of the story. Together they tell you the whole thing.

## Materials

- A "sense basket" of safe everyday items (something to see, hear, smell, taste, and
  touch)
- Sense cards (reuse from Lesson 1)
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Gather a "sense basket": one item per sense — e.g., a flower or leaf to see and
  smell, a bell or shaker to hear, a soft cloth to touch, a clean fruit slice to taste.
- Reuse the sense cards from Lesson 1.
- Prepare paper and crayons for the chart.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Prepare the sense basket, then guide the describing and the
chart. Safety first: choose only items that are safe to smell and taste for every
learner, and never require a learner to taste anything. If food or taste samples are
not available, the taste row can be done from memory ("a lemon tastes sour") — memory
counts as knowing. Honor every body: a learner with low vision may describe what their
skin and ears tell them in rich detail, and a learner who cannot taste or smell that
day still has sight, hearing, and touch. A learner who is deaf or hard of hearing can
know the hearing sense as vibration — hold the bell or shaker and feel it buzz, or
feel a drum hum — and describe sound as a felt shake; hold up each object as you name
it so everyone can follow. There is no single correct answer — each learner's honest
description is the point.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** You matched your five senses to their doors last time. Today
   each sense tells you something. What do your eyes tell you right now?
2. **The sense basket (15 min).** One by one, meet the items. Look at the flower —
   your **eyes** tell you its color and shape. Shake the bell — your **ears** tell you
   it is ringing, and your hand can feel the buzz that sound makes. Sniff the flower — your **nose** tells you it is sweet. Touch the
   cloth — your **skin** tells you it is soft. Taste the fruit (if you choose) — your
   **tongue** tells you if it is sweet or sour.
3. **One thing each (10 min).** Hold up a sense card. Say one thing that sense tells
   you: "Sight tells me the sky is blue." "Hearing tells me the bell rings." "Smell
   tells me the flower is sweet." "Taste tells me the lemon is sour." "Touch tells me
   the cloth is soft."
4. **Make your chart (10 min).** Fold your paper into five boxes and write one sense
   in each box, with a picture of the one thing it tells you.
5. **Close (5 min).** Your five senses each tell you one part of the world's story.
   Together, they tell you the whole thing.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Focus on one sense at a time and use the sentence frame "___ tells me
  ___" out loud before writing.
- **Extension:** Ask learners to describe one thing using two senses at once, like "the
  orange is round and it smells sweet."

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner describe one thing each of the five
  senses tells them, using their own honest words?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The "what each sense tells me" chart, kept in the
  learner's portfolio.
