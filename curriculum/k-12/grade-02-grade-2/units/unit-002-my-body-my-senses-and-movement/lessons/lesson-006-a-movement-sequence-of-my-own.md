# Lesson 6 — A Movement Sequence of My Own

## Summary

Learners become authors of their own movement. They plan a three-part sequence —
first, then, last — choose three movements, rehearse them in order, and perform the
sequence with control for the group. Every sequence is the learner's own, in their own
body's way.

## Objectives

- Combine movements in a sequence, such as running and catching or hopping and
  turning. _(D03.S2.02.01)_

## Connection

Every day you already do little sequences — you wake, then wash, then eat; you pick up
a ball, then run, then throw. A sequence is just "first, then, last." Today you get to
choose the order yourself and make a movement story that is all yours.

## Materials

- Sequence cards or a drawing sheet with three numbered boxes
- Cones, chalk, or rope
- A beat source (optional)

## Preparation

- Prepare a sequence sheet with three numbered boxes labeled first, then, last.
- Mark a performance space.
- Optional: prepare movement picture cards (hop, turn, balance, jump, skip, spin).

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Prepare the sequence sheets and space, then guide planning,
rehearsal, and performance. The goal is authorship and control, not a flawless show —
praise order and effort, never compare performances. Honor every body: a sequence can
be done seated, with wheels, with a partner, or with arms only, and it counts fully.
Learners who do not want to perform for the group may perform for a partner, a mirror,
or a recording, or narrate their sequence aloud. Keep the audience kind — watching
and cheering is part of the skill.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Last time you joined two movements into a sequence. Today you
   will build one of your own, with three parts.
2. **Plan (10 min).** Look at your sequence sheet — three boxes: **first, then,
   last.** Choose three movements. Maybe first you hop, then you turn, last you
   balance. Or first you skip, then you jump, last you spin. Draw or write your plan
   in the three boxes.
3. **Rehearse (15 min).** Practice your sequence in order, slowly at first. Say the
   words as you move: "first hop, then turn, last balance." Check each part for
   control. If a part is hard, swap it or do it in your own body's way — that is your
   choice.
4. **Perform (10 min).** Perform your sequence for the group, a partner, or a mirror.
   Watch your friends and cheer them on. Every sequence is different, and every one is
   good.
5. **Close (5 min).** You are the author of your own movement story. How did it feel
   to plan and perform your own sequence?

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Plan just two movements (first, then) and rehearse with a partner
  calling the order aloud.
- **Extension:** Ask learners to add a fourth movement, or to perform the sequence
  once fast and once slow, keeping the order.

## Assessment

- **Formative (performance/observation):** Can the learner plan and perform a
  three-movement sequence in order with control, in their own body's way?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The completed sequence plan, kept in the learner's
  portfolio.

## Home connection

Teach your sequence to someone at home and perform it together, then let them make one
up too.
