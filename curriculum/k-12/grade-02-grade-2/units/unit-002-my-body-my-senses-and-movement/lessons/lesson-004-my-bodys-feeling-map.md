# Lesson 4 — My Body's Feeling Map

## Summary

Learners turn what they learned about feeling spots into a personal tool: a body map.
They write or draw the feeling that shows up in their cheeks, chest, tummy, hands,
shoulders, and legs, then keep the map as a private check-in they can use any day.

## Objectives

- Notice where a feeling shows up in the body and name the feeling. _(D03.S1.02.02)_

## Connection

A map helps you find your way. Your body has a map too: excitement lives in a fast
heart, worry in a fluttery tummy, sadness in heavy shoulders. When you draw your own
body's feeling map, you build a tool for finding your way back to calm — because once
you can name a feeling, you can take care of it.

## Materials

- Body-map worksheet or a drawn body outline
- Crayons or markers
- Feeling word cards (excited, nervous, sad, mad, calm, happy)

## Preparation

- Print or draw a body-map worksheet: a simple body outline with a blank box beside
  the cheeks, chest, tummy, hands, shoulders, and legs.
- Set out crayons and feeling word cards.
- Prepare a place for learners to keep their finished maps.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Prepare the worksheets and word cards, then guide the mapping
and the closing check-in. This is a private, personal artifact — a learner may keep it
to themselves and share only what they choose. Never grade or rank a body map, and
never correct a learner's answer: where a feeling shows up is different for every
person, and every answer is honest. Honor every body — the map is the learner's own
body, not a "standard" one. Learners who cannot write may draw a face in each box or
point and say the feeling; those are equal ways to make the artifact. The map becomes
a quiet tool the learner can return to all year.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** You have learned that feelings show up in the body. Today you
   will draw a map of your own body's feelings — a tool you can keep.
2. **Look at the map (10 min).** Look at the body outline. Find the six spots: cheeks,
   chest (heart), tummy, hands, shoulders, and legs. Point to each one on your own
   body.
3. **Fill in your map (15 min).** In each box, draw or write the feeling that shows up
   there for you. Maybe your cheeks get warm when you are mad, your heart beats fast
   when you are excited, your tummy flutters when you are nervous, your shoulders feel
   heavy when you are sad. Use the feeling word cards if they help. Your map is yours —
   every body is different.
4. **Check in (10 min).** Look at your finished map. Which spot is your body feeling
   right now? Name it quietly to yourself. If you want, show a partner one spot and the
   feeling that lives there.
5. **Close (5 min).** Keep your map somewhere safe. Naming a feeling is the first step
   to caring for it.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Fill in one spot together first, with the learner choosing the feeling
  and the facilitator or partner writing it.
- **Extension:** Ask learners to add a seventh spot of their own — a place only they
  know a feeling shows up — and name it.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner point to a body spot and name the
  feeling that shows up there for them?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The completed body feeling map, kept in the learner's
  portfolio as a private check-in tool.
