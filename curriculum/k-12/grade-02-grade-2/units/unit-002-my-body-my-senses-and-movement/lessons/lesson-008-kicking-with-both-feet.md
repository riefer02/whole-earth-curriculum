# Lesson 8 — Kicking with Both Feet

## Summary

Learners build ball control with their feet. They learn to plant one foot and kick
with the other toward a goal, then practice with both feet so each foot grows its own
control, in every body's way.

## Objectives

- Kick a ball with developing control using both feet. _(D03.S2.02.02)_

## Connection

People kick balls in games on every continent — a football in a field, a ball in a
courtyard, a rolled-up bundle on a dirt road. Kicking takes balance: one foot plants
to hold you steady while the other foot does the kicking. Both feet can learn, and
practice is how they do.

## Materials

- Soft balls
- A goal (two cones, a box, or chalk lines)
- Floor markers to mark a kicking line

## Preparation

- Set up a kicking line and a goal at a reachable distance.
- Set out soft, light balls.
- Clear the kicking lane so no one is in the path of a kick.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Prepare the goal and line, then model a kick slowly — plant one
foot, swing the other — before learners try. This is skill-building, not competition:
celebrate control and growing accuracy, never power or distance. Use soft, light balls
so no foot gets hurt. Honor every body: a learner who uses a wheelchair can push the
ball with a wheel or a foot, and that counts fully; a learner who needs support can
kick from a seated position or with a partner steadying them. Offer adapted versions
as real choices. Supervise kicks so everyone faces the same direction.

## Procedure

1. **Warm up (5 min).** Stand tall and practice standing on one foot, then the other.
   Balance is what a good kick stands on.
2. **Learn to kick (10 min).** Stand beside the ball. Plant one foot next to it, look
   at the goal, and gently swing the other foot to push the ball toward the goal. Keep
   your eyes on the ball. Try it slowly first.
3. **Try both feet (15 min).** Kick once with your left foot, then once with your
   right. Which foot feels more sure? Practice the other one a little more — that is
   how it learns. Aim for the goal each time.
4. **Step back (10 min).** Move back a step or two and kick again. Control is more
   important than distance. If you use wheels or are seated, push the ball with a wheel
   or your foot toward the goal.
5. **Close (5 min).** Shake out your legs. You kicked with both feet, and both of them
   learned something today.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use a light, larger ball and a close, wide goal; kick from a seated
  position if that is more stable.
- **Extension:** Ask learners to kick through a narrower goal or to stop the ball with
  one foot before kicking with the other.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner kick a soft ball toward a goal with
  both feet, with growing control, in their own body's way?

## Home connection

At home, kick a soft ball or a rolled-up cloth toward a goal made of two stones or
shoes, and try both feet.
