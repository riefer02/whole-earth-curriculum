# Lesson 7 — Throwing and Catching with Both Hands

## Summary

Learners build ball control with their hands. They learn to throw with both hands
toward a target and to catch with both hands ready and eyes on the ball, practicing at
their own distance with the ball size that fits them.

## Objectives

- Throw and catch a ball with developing control using both hands. _(D03.S2.02.02)_

## Connection

Think of a game you have played with a ball, or a time you tossed something to a
friend. Throwing and catching are how people play together all over the world — in a
courtyard, a field, a beach, or a schoolyard. Using both hands, and practicing over
and over, is how your hands learn to be ready and sure.

## Materials

- Soft balls or beanbags
- A target (hoop, bucket, or chalk circle)
- Floor markers to mark a throwing line

## Preparation

- Set up a throwing line and one or more targets at a reachable distance.
- Set out a range of soft balls and beanbags so each learner can choose what fits.
- Clear the throwing lane so no one is in the path of a throw.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Prepare the targets and line, then model a two-hand throw and a
ready-hands catch slowly before learners try. This is skill-building, not competition:
celebrate control and growing accuracy, never distance or speed. Honor every body: a
learner who uses a wheelchair throws and catches seated, and that counts fully; a
learner who needs a larger, softer ball or a scoop catches with a tool, and that counts
too. Offer adapted versions as real choices. Supervise throws so everyone faces the
same direction and no one is hit. Pair learners so a more confident catcher can toss
gently to a partner still learning.

## Procedure

1. **Warm up (5 min).** Wiggle your fingers and roll a ball between your hands. Your
   hands are ready to work.
2. **Learn to throw (10 min).** Hold the ball with both hands. Look at the target.
   Push or toss the ball toward it, releasing with both hands together. Try it slowly
   first. Then step back a little and try again — small steps build control.
3. **Learn to catch (10 min).** Toss the ball a little up in the air and catch it with
   both hands. Keep your hands open and ready, and watch the ball all the way in.
   Catch it softly, like you are hugging it with your hands. If a friend tosses to you,
   keep your eyes on the ball.
4. **Practice both (15 min).** Take turns with a partner: one throws with both hands,
   the other catches with both hands, then switch. Or throw at the target and catch
   your own rebound. Try the throw with your left hand forward, then with your right —
   both hands learn.
5. **Close (5 min).** Shake out your hands. You threw and caught with both hands, and
   with every try your hands get a little surer.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Stand close to the target and use a large, soft ball or beanbag; catch
  with a scoop or mitt before bare hands.
- **Extension:** Ask learners to throw at a smaller target or from farther back, or to
  catch a ball tossed from the side instead of straight ahead.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner throw toward a target and catch a soft
  ball with both hands, with growing control, in their own body's way?

## Home connection

At home, throw and catch a rolled-up pair of socks or a soft ball with someone, using
both hands.
