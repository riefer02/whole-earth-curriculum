# Lesson 1 — Starting with Care: Honoring Every Body, Every Pace

## Summary

Learners build the shared agreements that make this unit safe and honest, name what
"accurate information, dignity, and care" mean in practice, and set up a wonder box
so every question can be asked without fear. This lesson does not teach sexual
content yet; it builds the container that makes the rest of the unit possible.

## Objectives

- Analyze the risks and responsibilities of sexual activity using accurate
  information, beginning with the responsibility to talk about bodies with dignity
  and care. _(D03.S4.10.01)_

## Connection

When you need medical help — a fever, a broken arm, a worry that will not go away —
you would never want to be lied to or shamed for asking. You would want the truth,
said plainly and kindly, and the chance to ask anything. That is exactly what we are
building here: a place where questions are met with accurate information and care,
never fear. Every body is different, every person moves at their own pace, and every
family lives in its own way — all of it honored.

## Materials

- Class norms chart
- Wonder box or question jar
- Paper and pen

## Preparation

- Prepare the norms chart with four starting words: Dignity, Accuracy, Consent, Care.
- Set up the wonder box where learners can drop questions privately.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Your job is to co-build a safe
container. **Land these four commitments and keep returning to them all unit long:**
dignity (no shame about any body), accuracy (we use real, sourced information, never
rumor or fear), consent (everyone may share or pass; no one is forced to disclose),
and care (questions get honest answers and a path to help).

**Sensitive-topic guidance (non-negotiable):** learners may **opt out of sharing** at
any time with a signal — no forced disclosure of personal experience, ever. If a
learner's question or comment suggests they may be unsafe, hurt, or in need of help,
do **not** promise secrecy; follow your setting's safeguarding procedure and connect
them with a **trusted adult or local health service** (school counselor, clinic,
helpline). State the limits of confidentiality plainly at the start so no one is
surprised.

The **ethics** lens: dignity and honest information are the *right* way to care for
people. The **egalitarian** lens: these agreements protect everyone equally — the
quiet, the different, the unsure. The **global** lens: different cultures and
families have different ways of living, and none is above another. The
**social-emotional** lens: naming that this topic can feel tender is itself a skill.
Use the wonder box for every lesson; answer a few questions each day with accurate,
non-shaming information.

## Procedure

1. **Gather and breathe (5 min).** Sit in a circle. Notice your body: is it relaxed
   or tense? Both are okay. This unit is about your body and your health, and it
   starts with how we treat each other.
2. **Name the four commitments (10 min).** Look at the four words — **Dignity,
   Accuracy, Consent, Care.** With a partner, say what each one means in one
   sentence. Then share one idea aloud or in writing.
3. **Build the agreements (10 min).** Together, turn the four words into real rules:
   "No shame about any body." "We use accurate information, not rumor." "Everyone
   may share or pass." "Questions get honest answers." Add one agreement of your own.
4. **Meet the wonder box (5 min).** This box is for any question, asked in writing,
   with no name required. Write one question now if you have one — or write a
   question you imagine someone else might be afraid to ask.
5. **The pass signal (5 min).** Practice it: a raised hand, a folded arm, or a card.
   Anyone may use it at any time, no explanation needed. You always own your own
   story.
6. **Check yourself (10 min).** Write two sentences in your journal: "One thing I
   need from this room is…" and "One question I hope this unit answers is…" Keep it
   private or share by choice.
7. **Close (5 min).** Say one word that describes how you want this room to feel.
   Remember: every body, every pace, every family's way — honored.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer sentence starters for the two journal sentences; allow drawing
  or voice notes instead of writing.
- **Extension:** Ask learners to draft a short "why these agreements matter" note
  for a younger learner, in plain words.

## Assessment

- **Formative (self + observation):** Can the learner name the four commitments and
  use (or witness) the pass signal? Did they contribute one agreement or one
  question — aloud, in writing, or anonymously?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The two-sentence journal entry, kept private or added
  to the portfolio by choice.

## Home connection

If it feels safe in your family, ask an adult: "What does our family believe about
how to talk about bodies and health?" If that is not safe or comfortable, that is
okay — write the question for your wonder box or a trusted adult instead. No one is
required to have this conversation at home.
