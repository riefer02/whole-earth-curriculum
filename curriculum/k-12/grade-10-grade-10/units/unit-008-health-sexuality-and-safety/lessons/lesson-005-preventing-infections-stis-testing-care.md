# Lesson 5 — Preventing Infections: STIs, Testing, and Care

## Summary

Learners learn that sexually transmitted infections can be prevented, detected, and
often cured or managed, that many show **no symptoms**, and that protection, testing,
and treatment are acts of care — never of shame.

## Objectives

- Analyze the risks and responsibilities of sexual activity using accurate
  information, specifically how infections can be passed and how to prevent, detect,
  and treat them. _(D03.S4.10.01)_

## Connection

Some infections — a cold, a sore throat — you notice right away. Others give no sign
at all while they quietly do their work, which is exactly why check-ups exist even
when you feel fine. Sexually transmitted infections are like that: many people who
have one feel nothing, which is why **testing** matters as much as **protection**.
Knowing the facts — and getting care — is ordinary self-care, like any other kind.

## Materials

- Prevent–test–treat pathway
- Fact cards

## Preparation

- Copy the pathway and fact cards.
- Retrieval (spiral): from Lesson 4, that condoms protect against STIs including
  HIV; from Lesson 3, accurate vs. rumor information.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). **Land the accurate, non-shaming
facts** (from WHO, S-454): STIs are caused by **bacteria, viruses, or parasites**
and can pass during sex; some are **curable** with medicine (syphilis, gonorrhoea,
chlamydia, trichomoniasis) and some are **manageable** with treatment (the viral
ones — hepatitis B, herpes/HSV, HIV, HPV). Many STIs are **asymptomatic** — a person
can have one and not know. **Condoms offer one of the most effective protections**
against STIs, including HIV, and **vaccines exist for hepatitis B and HPV**. The
three-step frame: **prevent** (condoms, vaccines), **test** (regular testing,
especially with a new partner), **treat** (medicine, and telling partners so they
can be cared for too).

**Sensitive-topic guidance (essential):** keep this **non-frightening and
non-stigmatizing.** Do not show graphic images. Emphasize that having an STI is
common, treatable, and never a mark on a person's worth — the opposite of the shame
many cultures attach to it. Learners may **opt out** of discussion. If a learner
discloses a possible infection or unsafe circumstances, respond privately, without
alarm, and follow your safeguarding procedure to a **trusted adult or health
service** where they can get confidential testing and care.

The **ethics** lens: protecting a partner and telling them if there is risk is a form
of honesty and care. The **egalitarian** lens: access to testing and treatment is
unequal worldwide, and shame falls unevenly — both are justice problems, not moral
verdicts on any person. The **global** lens: prevention, testing, and treatment
availability differ hugely by country; the facts are the same everywhere. The
**social-emotional** lens: talking about testing with a partner takes courage and
clarity — a skill this unit builds.

## Procedure

1. **Retrieve (5 min).** From Lesson 4: which method also protects against STIs,
   including HIV? Say the three words of today's frame: **prevent, test, treat**.
2. **Meet the pathway (10 min).** Study the diagram. In your own words: **prevent**
   = condoms and vaccines; **test** = regular testing, especially with a new partner;
   **treat** = medicine, and telling partners so they can be cared for too.
3. **Curable vs. manageable (10 min).** Some infections are cured with medicine;
   some are managed over time. Neither is shameful — both are ordinary medical care.
   Name the key fact: **many STIs have no symptoms**, which is why testing matters.
4. **Sort the facts (15 min).** In small groups, place each fact card on the correct
   step of the pathway, and correct any myth cards with the accurate version.
5. **Say it without shame (5 min).** Practice a calm sentence for a hard moment:
   "I'm getting tested, and I want you to be cared for too." Notice: no blame.
6. **Reflect (5 min).** Write one sentence: "The reason testing is as important as
   protection is…"
7. **Close (5 min).** Reminder: prevention, testing, and treatment are self-care and
   mutual care — never shame. Next lesson, we weigh risks and responsibilities
   together.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Focus on the three-step frame and the "no symptoms, so test" fact;
   use the drawn pathway with fewer cards.
- **Extension:** Research (with a trusted source) where confidential testing and
   care are available locally or nationally, and add that to the pathway.

## Assessment

- **Formative (peer + self):** Can the learner place prevention, testing, and
  treatment in order, state that many STIs are asymptomatic, and name one fact
  about curable vs. manageable infections?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The "why testing matters" reflection.

## Home connection

If it feels safe, ask a trusted adult or health worker how people in your area can
get confidential testing and care. If not comfortable, write the question for your
wonder box. No home conversation is required.
