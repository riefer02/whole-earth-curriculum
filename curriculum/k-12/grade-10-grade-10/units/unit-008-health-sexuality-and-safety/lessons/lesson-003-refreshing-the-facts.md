# Lesson 3 — Refreshing the Facts: Conception, Anatomy, and Rumor vs. Reality

## Summary

Learners refresh the accurate basics of how pregnancy can begin — one egg and one
sperm meeting — with dignity and without shame, then practice telling fact from rumor
about bodies. This readies them for the contraception and infection-prevention
lessons that follow.

## Objectives

- Analyze the risks and responsibilities of sexual activity using accurate
  information, beginning with an accurate account of how pregnancy can happen.
  _(D03.S4.10.01)_

## Connection

Most of what you "know" about bodies may have arrived as rumor — a joke, a meme, a
friend's confident guess. When it comes to pregnancy and health, rumor is expensive:
it changes decisions. An accurate picture is simple and freeing: a pregnancy can
begin only when one egg and one sperm meet and implant. Nothing else — not a
particular position, not a first time, not a lucky day — changes that basic fact.

## Materials

- Rumor vs. reality cards
- Conception diagram

## Preparation

- Copy the conception diagram and the rumor/reality cards.
- Retrieval (spiral): from Grade 9, the basics of reproductive anatomy; from Lessons
  1–2, the commitments and the consent conditions.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). **Land the accurate, non-shaming
facts:** a pregnancy can begin only when a **sperm** meets an **egg (ovum)** and the
fertilized egg **implants** in the **uterus**. The egg is released around
**ovulation**, roughly mid-cycle for people who ovulate; sperm can survive in the
body for several days. Use correct anatomical terms plainly and without giggling or
shame — the body is taught with dignity. This is a *review* of Grade 9 anatomy, not a
new graphic deep-dive; keep it simple and correct. Keep it inclusive too: pregnancy
can begin only this way, but sexual intimacy is broader than egg-meets-sperm, and
the consent and infection-prevention skills this unit teaches apply to every kind of
relationship — learners of any gender or sexuality are neither assumed nor ranked.

**Sensitive-topic guidance:** no learner is asked to share anything personal. If a
question suggests a learner is pregnant or unsafe, respond privately and with care —
do not announce it, do not shame, and follow your safeguarding procedure to connect
them with a **trusted adult or health service**. Correct rumors without ridiculing
anyone who believed them; rumor is common because accurate education is not evenly
available.

The **ethics** lens: giving people true information is a form of respect. The
**egalitarian** lens: unequal access to accurate education is a justice problem — not
a personal failing of the learner who heard a rumor. The **global** lens: what is
considered "known" or "acceptable to say" about bodies varies widely across cultures;
accuracy is a tool everyone deserves. The **critical-thinking** lens: every claim
about bodies should be checked against a source — the exact skill this unit sharpens
next.

## Procedure

1. **Retrieve (5 min).** From Grade 9: what did you learn about how bodies are made
   and how pregnancy can begin? Say one accurate fact aloud or write it.
2. **Meet the diagram (10 min).** Study the conception diagram. Say the sequence in
   your own words: egg released → sperm meets egg → fertilization → implantation in
   the uterus. Notice what is *not* required: no particular timing myth, no special
   position, no "first time" rule.
3. **Sort rumor from reality (15 min).** In small groups, sort each card as **fact**
   or **rumor**, and for each rumor, say what the accurate version is. Use the
   diagram and a health source to check.
4. **Correct together (10 min).** Share your sorting. For each rumor, give the
   corrected fact — said kindly, because rumor is common and unlearning it is the
   win.
5. **The one-fact rule (5 min).** Commit: "When a claim about bodies surprises me,
   I will check it against a trustworthy source before I believe or share it."
6. **Reflect (5 min).** Write one fact you now hold accurately, and one rumor you
   can now correct.
7. **Close (5 min).** Preview: now that we know how pregnancy can begin, we can
   understand how contraception works — next lesson.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide the diagram already labeled; work through the fact/rumor sort
  as a whole group. For learners who cannot see the diagram, describe each step aloud
  and offer a tactile model — a small seed or stone for the egg, a grain or pinch of
  sand for the sperm — so the "meeting" can be felt, not only seen.
- **Extension:** Ask learners to write a short "myth-buster" card for a younger
  learner, in plain, non-shaming words.

## Assessment

- **Formative (peer + self):** Can the learner state the egg–sperm–implantation
  sequence and correctly identify at least one rumor with its corrected fact?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The one-fact/one-rumor reflection.

## Home connection

If it feels safe, ask a trusted adult what *they* were told about how pregnancy
happens and how accurate it turned out to be. If not comfortable, write the question
for your wonder box. No home conversation is required.
