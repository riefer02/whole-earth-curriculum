# Lesson 6 — Risks, Responsibilities, and Decisions

## Summary

Learners pull together the unit's first objective: they weigh the **risks** of
sexual activity (pregnancy, infections, emotional and relational outcomes) against
its **responsibilities** (consent, communication, protection, and care), and
practice a four-step decision frame that works with their own values.

## Objectives

- Analyze the risks and responsibilities of sexual activity, including contraception
  and preventing infections, using accurate information. _(D03.S4.10.01)_

## Connection

Every important choice — moving to a new place, taking a job, trusting someone with
a secret — involves what could go well, what could go wrong, and what you owe to the
people involved. A decision made with accurate information and your own values is
one you can stand behind; a decision made on rumor or pressure is one that decides
itself. Sexual activity is a choice like any other in this way: it deserves the same
clear thinking, the same honesty, and the same freedom to say "not yet."

## Materials

- Decision framework worksheet
- Scenario set

## Preparation

- Copy the decision framework worksheet and scenario set.
- Retrieval (interleaved): from Lessons 2–5, consent, conception, the four method
  groups and the condom key fact, and prevent–test–treat. Today: the synthesis.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). **Land the four-step frame:** (1)
**know the facts** — accurate information about contraception and infections (WHO,
S-453/S-454); (2) **know my values** — what matters to me, including the freedom to
delay or abstain; (3) **talk it through** — honest communication with a partner; (4)
**decide and revisit** — a decision can be changed. Risks to name honestly:
**pregnancy, infections, and emotional or relational outcomes.** Responsibilities to
name: **consent, communication, protection, and mutual care.** The frame produces a
*reasoned* decision, not one "right" answer — the point is the reasoning.

**Sensitive-topic guidance:** this is where personal disclosure is most likely — hold
the line: no learner is asked to share their own experience, and any who do should be
redirected to the fictional scenarios ("let's use the cards"). Normalize **delay and
abstinence** as complete, valid choices alongside being active; neither is ranked.
If a learner discloses risk or unsafe circumstances, respond privately and follow
your safeguarding procedure to a **trusted adult or health service**.

The **ethics** lens: a decision that protects and cares for everyone involved is a
good decision. The **egalitarian** lens: the freedom to choose — and the information
needed to choose well — is not equally available, and that is an injustice we name,
not a fact we ignore. The **global** lens: norms about when and how people become
sexually active differ across cultures, but the skills of weighing risk and
responsibility travel everywhere. The **social-emotional** lens: impulse and feeling
are real parts of decisions; naming them is part of deciding well.

## Procedure

1. **Retrieve, interleaved (5 min).** One line each: the five consent conditions;
   what must meet for pregnancy to begin; the four method groups; the condom key
   fact; the three words of infection care.
2. **Meet the four-step frame (10 min).** Study the diagram. Say each step in your
   own words. Notice the frame is a *loop* — you can return and change course.
3. **Worked example (10 min).** Follow a model: a fictional couple is deciding.
   *Know the facts* (accurate info on contraception and infections); *know your
   values* (one person wants to wait — that is honored); *talk it through* (they
   speak honestly); *decide and revisit* (they choose to wait and check in again).
4. **Apply the frame (15 min).** In small groups, take a scenario. Walk all four
   steps. Name at least one risk, one responsibility, and one option that honors
   both people's values. Write the reasoning, not just a verdict.
5. **Share reasoning (5 min).** Share one line from your group's thinking. Others
   listen without judging the conclusion.
6. **Reflect (5 min).** Write: "When I decide, I will make sure I have… (facts /
   values / a conversation / the right to change my mind)."
7. **Close (5 min).** This completes our first big question. Next, a new one: how do
   we know the information we're using is even true?

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use the worked example step-by-step; allow the frame to be spoken
  rather than written; provide sentence starters.
- **Extension:** Write a short "decision brief" that names risks, responsibilities,
  and two options, and explain which you would lean toward and why — without sharing
  anything personal.

## Assessment

- **Formative (peer + self):** Can the learner name the four steps, identify a risk
  and a responsibility in a scenario, and produce a reasoned option that honors both
  people's values?
- **Portfolio (unit):** The decision-frame worksheet becomes the first portfolio
  artifact for the unit's "risks-and-responsibilities analysis."

## Home connection

If it feels safe, ask a trusted adult how they weigh risks and responsibilities in a
big decision (not necessarily about sex). If not comfortable, reflect privately or
write a wonder-box question. No home conversation is required.
