# Lesson 2 — Consent: Freely Given, Informed, Reversible

## Summary

Learners define real consent as **freely given, informed, reversible, specific, and
enthusiastic**, practice checking for a genuine "yes" in everyday and relationship
scenarios, and learn that a "yes" can be withdrawn at any time — before and during.
This is the foundation of the unit's "responsibilities" thread.

## Objectives

- Analyze the risks and responsibilities of sexual activity using accurate
  information, beginning with consent as the non-negotiable first responsibility.
  _(D03.S4.10.01)_

## Connection

Consent is not only about sex. You already use it every day: "Can I borrow your
phone?" "Is it okay if I sit here?" "Want to try this food?" A real yes is freely
given — not pressured, not assumed from silence, not owed because of a gift or a
date. The same skill, sharpened, is what protects everyone when a relationship
becomes physical: a yes you can trust, given and received with care.

## Materials

- Consent conditions worksheet
- Scenario cards

## Preparation

- Copy the consent conditions worksheet and the scenario cards.
- Retrieval (spiral): from Lesson 1, the four commitments (dignity, accuracy,
  consent, care); from Grade 9, the basics of consent and body autonomy.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). **Land the five conditions:** consent
is **freely given** (no pressure, no threat, no guilt), **informed** (everyone knows
what they are agreeing to), **reversible** (a yes can become a no at any moment),
**specific** (yes to one thing is not yes to everything), and **enthusiastic** (a
genuine yes, not a resigned one). A widely used memory aid spells them **FRIES**
(freely given, reversible, informed, enthusiastic, specific) — the underlying
principles (freely given, informed, reversible) are the international framing in
UNESCO's guidance on sexuality education (S-412); "enthusiastic" and "specific" are
the additions of a popular mnemonic (Planned Parenthood). Silence, freezing, or
"sure, I guess" under pressure is **not** consent. These conditions hold in every
kind of relationship — between people of any gender, and whether a relationship is
same-sex or different-sex; consent is never tied to one kind of couple.

**Sensitive-topic guidance:** learners may **opt out of sharing** — use role-play
scenarios that are fictional and mostly non-sexual; no learner should ever be asked
to describe their own experiences. If a learner's question suggests coercion, abuse,
or being unsafe, do not promise secrecy; follow your safeguarding procedure and refer
to a **trusted adult or local service**.

The **ethics** lens: respecting another person's autonomy is the right thing to do,
in every relationship. The **egalitarian** lens: consent protects everyone equally
regardless of gender, and pressure can be exerted by anyone on anyone — in every
kind of relationship, including same-sex relationships and families of every form;
no relationship type is ranked above another. The **global**
lens: cultures express agreement and refusal differently, but the principle that a
person decides over their own body is a shared human-rights commitment. The
**social-emotional** lens: reading another person's yes or no is an emotional skill —
tone, hesitation, and body language all carry meaning.

## Procedure

1. **Retrieve (5 min).** What were the four commitments from Lesson 1? From Grade 9:
   what does "my body belongs to me" mean?
2. **Meet the five conditions (10 min).** Read the five conditions. For each, say a
   plain-language version aloud: freely given = no pressure; informed = we both know
   what we're agreeing to; reversible = I can change my mind; specific = yes to one
   thing isn't yes to all; enthusiastic = a real, wanted yes.
3. **Read the signals (10 min).** What does a real yes sound like and look like? What
   does a no look like — including silence, freezing, "maybe," "I don't know," or
   laughing it off? A no does not have to be loud to be real. Practice saying an
   ordinary no out loud with a partner ("No, thank you") and hearing it calmly.
4. **Work the scenarios (15 min).** In pairs or small groups, sort each scenario:
   is this a real yes, a clear no, or an "unsure — check again"? Use the worksheet.
5. **The reversible rule (5 min).** Say it together: **a yes can become a no at any
   time, and the other person stops.** Changing your mind is not a betrayal; it is
   the rule.
6. **Reflect (5 min).** Write one sentence: "The way I will check for a real yes
   is…" and one: "The way I will say no when I need to is…"
7. **Close (5 min).** Reminder: consent is the first responsibility, in every
   relationship and every step of one.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use only the everyday scenarios; offer the stoplight (green/yellow/
  red) as a visual for yes/unsure/no.
- **Extension:** Write a short dialogue in which one person withdraws a yes and the
  other responds with care, then read it aloud.

## Assessment

- **Formative (peer + self):** Can the learner name the five conditions and correctly
  sort a scenario as real yes, no, or unsure — with a reason?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The two reflection sentences, kept private or shared by
  choice.

## Home connection

If it feels safe, talk with a trusted adult about how your family asks for and gives
permission in daily life ("may I…", "do you mind…"). If that is not comfortable,
write the question for your wonder box instead. No one is required to discuss this at
home.
