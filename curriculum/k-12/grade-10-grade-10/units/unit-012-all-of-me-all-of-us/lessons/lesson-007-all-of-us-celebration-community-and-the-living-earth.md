# Lesson 7 — All of Us: Celebration, Community, and the Living Earth

## Summary

The capstone closes by gathering every artifact of the week — the whole-person map, the
carried-forward story, the Grade-11 learning plan, the local-to-global chain, and the
cooperative-and-mutual-aid pages — into one portfolio and one celebration. Learners
share, appreciate one another, and name a commitment they will carry into Grade 11,
celebrating the whole person, the caring community they have built, and the living
Earth. This is the unit's summative moment — warm, agentic, and hopeful, never a test.

## Objectives

- Gather and celebrate my considered position, my self-directed learning plan, my place
  in nested communities, and my commitment to cooperation and mutual aid as a co-author
  of my education, looking ahead to Grade 11. _(D01.S2.10.02, D01.S3.10.01,
  D02.S3.10.02, D02.S4.10.02)_

## Connection

A year is not a score; it is a story you have been writing, a web you have been woven
into, and a set of hands you have reached toward. When people finish a harvest, a build,
or a long journey, they do not just stop — they gather, name what was made, thank the
hands that helped, and look toward the next field. That is what this lesson is: a
gathering at the end of a year, to see the whole cloth you have made and to step,
together, toward the next one.

## Materials

- Celebration graphic (SVG)
- The closing portfolio (all unit artifacts)

## Preparation

- Gather each learner's five artifacts from Lessons 1–6. Arrange a circle for sharing.
- Retrieval/spacing: this lesson is a spaced retrieval pass across the whole unit (and
  the year) — recalling the artifacts now, once more, strengthens them for the future
  (Dunlosky et al., S-039).

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **the year's work
gathers into one whole — a person, a plan, a place in the web, and a commitment to
care — and that whole is worth celebrating.** This is the summative close, but the
tone is celebration, not grading. Assessment here is a portfolio and a shared
performance, in the spirit of the philosophy (assessment serves the learner; success is
collective — philosophy §§8–9).

Hold the four lenses visibly. **Ethics:** naming a commitment — a promise to act with
care — is an ethical act, and it is kept small and doable. **Egalitarianism:** the
celebration honors every learner's artifact as equally worthy; no one's story outranks
another's. **Global/anthropological:** gathering at a year's end to thank and look
ahead is a human pattern across cultures — let learners bring their own traditions of
marking endings and beginnings. **Technology/environment:** the living Earth is in the
circle itself — the tree, the growing thing, the weather that carried the year — and
stewardship is one of the commitments learners may name. Distinguish evidence from
values: "recalling and celebrating work strengthens memory and meaning" is descriptive
(Dunlosky et al., S-039); "every person's story is worth celebrating" is a value we
hold.

Keep it spacious and warm. Let quiet learners share in writing or not at all; presence
is enough.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (8 min).** Set your five artifacts out in a circle — whole-person map,
   carried-forward story, learning plan, community chain, and cooperative-and-mutual-aid
   pages. Walk the circle slowly and look at what you made this year.
2. **Recall and name (10 min).** For each artifact, say one line to yourself: one thing
   it taught you about yourself, your learning, your community, or how people care for
   one another. This final recall is what fixes the year in memory.
3. **Appreciate one another (12 min).** In a circle (or a gallery, or online), each
   person shares one artifact or one line — and leaves one true, specific appreciation
   for at least one other person's work. Appreciation, not ranking.
4. **Name a commitment (12 min).** Look at the celebration graphic: the circle around
   the living tree, the sun rising toward Grade 11. Write one small commitment you will
   carry into Grade 11 — to yourself, to a community, or to the Earth — and one first
   step. It can be tiny; a seed is enough.
5. **Celebrate and look ahead (8 min).** Read your commitment once, quietly or aloud.
   Then, together or alone, mark the year's end in a way that fits you — a shared meal,
   a song, a moment of quiet, a hand on a shoulder — and step toward Grade 11.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a sentence starter for the commitment ("In Grade 11 I will…, and
  my first step is…"); accept a spoken, drawn, or shown commitment (an object or
  gesture may stand in for words).
- **Access (blind / low vision):** Describe the celebration graphic aloud — a diverse
  circle around a living tree, a rising sun reaching toward "Grade 11" — and invite
  the learner to place a stone or leaf for each thing they are grateful for, as in the
  outdoor variant.
- **Extension:** Write a short letter to your future self, sealed or saved, to open at
  the start of Grade 11.

## Assessment

- **Summative (portfolio + performance):** The completed closing portfolio — five
  artifacts plus the commitment — shared and appreciated in the celebration circle.
- **Self-assessment:** A short reflection — "What I am proudest of this year, and what
  I will carry forward." Mastery is individual; the celebration is collective.

## Home connection

Bring your portfolio home and share it with your family or community in whatever way
fits — a reading, a quiet look together, a meal. Let them add one appreciation, too.
