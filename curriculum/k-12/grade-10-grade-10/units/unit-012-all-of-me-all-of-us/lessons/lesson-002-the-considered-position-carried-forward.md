# Lesson 2 — The Considered Position, Carried Forward

## Summary

Learners take the considered position from Lesson 1 and shape it into a **story that
continues** — a past, a present, and a next chapter for Grade 11. They practice
revising it, discovering that a considered position is a living document they hold
loosely, not a fixed verdict. The lesson deepens D01.S2.10.02 and gestures toward the
purpose work of Grade 11.

## Objectives

- Shape a considered position on what defines me into a revisable story of who I am
  becoming, with reasons for the next chapter. _(D01.S2.10.02)_

## Connection

A position written in pencil can be revised; a position carved in stone cannot. Think
of a map you drew of your neighborhood years ago — it was true then, but the
neighborhood has grown, and so have you. Your considered position is the same kind of
thing: not a wall you are trapped inside, but a map you keep redrawing as you walk. The
question this lesson asks is not "who have I permanently become?" but "who am I
becoming, and why do I believe that?"

## Materials

- Story-path graphic (SVG)
- Writing journal or portfolio

## Preparation

- Retrieval cue: from Lesson 1, the whole-person map; from earlier this year, the
  considered position (Unit 1, Lesson 9). Today we add the dimension of time — a story
  that continues.
- Prepare the three-part frame: **past** (what shaped me), **now** (my considered
  position), **next** (one chapter I want to write in Grade 11, and why).

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **a considered
position is not a verdict but a story — a narrative identity that links the past, the
present, and an imagined future, and that I hold loosely and revise.** Narrative
identity (McAdams, S-404) and "possible selves" (Markus & Nurius, S-399) are the
descriptive research here; identity is a story we tell, and it keeps being rewritten.
A sense of **purpose** grows by trying, reflecting, and being supported — not by
certainty at once; only a minority of young people report a clear purpose, and that is
ordinary, not a failure (Damon, S-400). This is the honest, non-pressuring bridge to
Grade 11's purpose work.

Hold the **ethics** lens: revising your position is an act of honesty toward yourself.
The **egalitarian** lens: everyone's next chapter looks different, and no one's is more
"correct" — purpose may be quiet or loud, near or far. The **global** lens: in many
traditions the self is told as a story that belongs to a people and a place, not only
to an individual — invite learners to tell it their way, solo or communal. The
**technology** lens can appear briefly: profiles freeze a story in one post; a living
position keeps moving. Distinguish evidence from values: "identity is a revisable
story" is descriptive; "you are allowed to revise, and 'I don't know yet' is a complete
answer" is a value we protect.

Model the three-part frame once (worked example, S-011), then let learners draft. Keep
it warm — this is the heart of the capstone.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (7 min).** From Lesson 1, what was the closing line of your whole-person
   map? Now add time: every position has a past (what shaped it), a now (where it
   stands), and a next (where it is going). Say which of the three feels easiest to
   name, and which hardest.
2. **See the path (8 min).** Look at the story-path graphic: past on the left, now in
   the middle, a dashed "next" on the right, and a sun rising beyond it. The dashed
   circle means the next chapter is open — you are allowed to step toward it without
   knowing every word.
3. **Draft your story (20 min).** Write three short paragraphs. **Past:** one thing —
   a person, a place, an experience — that shaped who you are. **Now:** your considered
   position, one or two sentences, in your own words. **Next:** one chapter you want to
   write in Grade 11, and one reason you believe it (your evidence).
4. **Revise it (8 min).** Read your story aloud once, quietly or to a partner. Find the
   line that felt least true, and change it. Notice that revising did not break the
   story — it made it more yours.
5. **Close (7 min).** Under your story write: "This position is mine and mine to
   revise." Add it to your closing portfolio.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide three sentence starters — "I was shaped by…", "I now stand
  for…", "Next I want to…" — and ask for one line each.
- **Access (blind / low vision):** Describe the story-path aloud — past on the left,
  now in the middle, a dashed next on the right with a rising sun — and offer a tactile
  path made of three stones placed left, middle, and right that the learner can walk.
- **Alternative expression (non-writing, non-oral):** Tell the past–now–next story by
  placing three found objects in a row and pointing to each, or by drawing three scenes;
  a partner may scribe only if the learner chooses.
- **Extension:** Add a counter-consideration — one way the next chapter might surprise
  you, and how you would respond if it did.

## Assessment

- **Formative (self + peer):** Can the learner link past, present, and a reasoned next
  chapter, and revise the story when a line does not feel true?
- **Portfolio artifact (unit):** The three-part story with the closing line, kept in
  the portfolio.

## Home connection

Tell one adult your "next chapter" for Grade 11 and the reason you believe it. Ask
them what they were becoming at your age — and whether their story, too, kept
revising.

- On "possible selves" as the bridge from present to future motivation: Markus &

- On purpose as something that grows by trying and reflecting, not certainty: Damon
