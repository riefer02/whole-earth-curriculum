# Lesson 1 — All of Me: The Whole Person I Am Becoming

## Summary

Learners retrieve the considered position they built earlier this year — what defines
them, independent of external labels — and expand it into a **whole-person map** of
six parts: values, story, choices, relationships, body, and dreams. The lesson is a
homecoming and a celebration, not a test. It opens the closing portfolio that will
gather the unit's artifacts.

## Objectives

- Restate and celebrate a considered position on what defines me, independent of
  external labels and expectations, as a whole person with many parts. _(D01.S2.10.02)_

## Connection

Think of the last time someone reduced you to one word — "the athlete," "the quiet
one," "the one from that family." A label is a single, flat card; a person is a whole
room of things at once. You already know this from your own life: the same person who
cooks a meal for others also plays music, worries about a friend, and dreams about
the sea. This lesson gathers that whole room back into one map, so we can look at you —
all of you — and celebrate it.

## Materials

- Whole-person map (SVG)
- Portfolio folder or journal

## Preparation

- Retrieval cue: from earlier this year (Unit 1), each learner wrote a **considered
  position** — a statement of what defines them, in their own words, independent of
  labels. Today we widen it, not rewrite it.
- Print or project the whole-person map; prepare the six part-names.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land, warmly and without
pressure: **a considered position is not a single label but a whole person made of
many parts — values, story, choices, relationships, body, and dreams — and none of
them is defined by an outside label.** Identity is a developing story, not a finished
fact (Erikson, S-155); thinking of it as parts that can shift by context is the
mature, accurate view (this is the spiral of D01.S2 across grades). Research on
"possible selves" (Markus & Nurius, S-399) reminds us the self includes imagined
futures, which is why "dreams" belongs on the map.

Hold the **ethics** lens: treating a person as a whole is a way of respecting them.
The **egalitarian** lens: labels flatten everyone, but they land hardest on people who
are already stereotyped — naming the parts labels cannot touch is an act of dignity.
The **global** lens: what counts as "the right parts" differs across cultures; let
learners choose their own six, and honor that difference. The **technology** lens can
appear as an optional reflection: feeds and profiles ask us to shrink into a single
image — the whole-person map pushes back. Distinguish evidence from values: identity
as a developing, many-parted story is descriptive; "every whole person deserves to be
seen" is a value we hold together.

Model once (worked example, S-011), then let learners build their own. Keep the tone
celebratory. This is the first artifact of the closing portfolio.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (7 min).** Earlier this year you wrote a considered position — a short
   statement of what defines you, in your own words, independent of labels. Without
   looking, say or jot two things you remember putting in it. Then check: what did you
   get right, and what would you add now?
2. **Meet the whole-person map (8 min).** Look at the graphic. Each part — values,
   story, choices, relationships, body, dreams — is a piece of the whole you. A label
   grabs only one piece and pretends it is all of you. Which part does a label most
   often miss?
3. **Build your map (20 min).** Draw a small figure in the middle and your own six
   parts around it (you may rename any part). For each part, write one true sentence —
   a value you hold, a chapter of your story, a choice you are proud of, a person who
   matters, a thing your body can do, a dream you are growing. Add a seventh, blank
   part and label it "still deciding" — because a whole person keeps becoming.
4. **Share one part (8 min).** With a partner, share the part you are proudest of, and
   listen to theirs. Notice: their map is not a copy of yours, and that is the point.
5. **Close (7 min).** In one sentence, finish: "I am more than any label because…"
   Write it at the top of your map. This is the first page of your closing portfolio.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer a pre-drawn map with the six part-names already placed; the
  learner writes one word in each.
- **Access (blind / low vision):** Describe the whole-person map aloud — a figure
  surrounded by six labeled parts, with empty expectation bubbles floating outside
  without touching — and offer a tactile version built from six found objects placed in
  a circle around a central stone.
- **Alternative expression (non-writing, non-oral):** Build the map with six found
  objects and point to each as a partner or pre-printed label names it; the closing
  line may be a chosen symbol or drawing instead of a written sentence.
- **Extension:** Add a "counter-consideration" note — one label you have been given,
  and a sentence on why it does not define the whole of you.

## Assessment

- **Formative (self + peer):** Can the learner name several parts of themselves and
  explain why a single label cannot contain the whole person?
- **Portfolio artifact (unit):** The whole-person map, with the closing sentence "I am
  more than any label because…", as the first page of the closing portfolio.

## Home connection

Show your whole-person map to someone who has known you a long time. Ask them to add
one part of you they have seen that you did not write down — then decide whether it
belongs on your map.
