# Unit 9 — Global History: Time, Culture & Atrocity

## Overview

This unit gives Grade 10 its full pass through the long, many-voiced human story,
deepening Grade 9's continuity and liberation into the hardest and most careful
questions. Learners compare how different societies measured and recorded time and
weigh how those systems shaped their sense of the past, and argue for a way to
divide a global era into periods and defend where its turning points should fall.
They evaluate the ethics of sharing, borrowing, and taking knowledge and culture
across peoples, and who has been credited or harmed, and analyze how global trade and
technology have spread and transformed cultural traditions in the modern world. They
analyze how colonization reshaped populations, environments, and borders across the
world and its effects on Indigenous peoples, and analyze modern migration and
displacement driven by conflict, economy, and climate, weighing their causes and
human consequences. They analyze a case of genocide or mass violence with care,
examining its causes, the responses of survivors, and how it is remembered, and
analyze how ordinary people have resisted oppression, sheltered others, and rebuilt
after atrocity, and what remembrance and justice require. History is told from many
perspectives, never as a single nation's triumph, and the voices of those too often
left out are centered with care and accuracy.

## Essential questions

- How have different societies measured and recorded time, and how do I argue for a periodization of a global era and defend its turning points?
- What are the ethics of sharing, borrowing, and taking knowledge and culture, and how have global trade and technology transformed cultural traditions?
- How did colonization reshape populations, environments, and borders, and how do I study atrocity and remembrance with care and accuracy?
