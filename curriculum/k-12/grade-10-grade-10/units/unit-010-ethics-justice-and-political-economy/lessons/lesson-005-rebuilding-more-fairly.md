# Lesson 5 — Rebuilding More Fairly

## Summary

Learners **rebuild a moral argument more fairly**, using the hidden assumptions and bias they found in
Lesson 4. They name who was left out, qualify sweeping claims, and rewrite the argument so it speaks
more honestly — then check their rebuild against a fairness list.

## Objectives

- Rebuild a moral argument more fairly, after surfacing its hidden assumptions and bias.
  _(D09.S1.10.02)_

## Connection

Finding an argument's hidden assumptions is only half the work. The other half is building it again
better — the way you would repair a tool rather than throw it away. If someone says "everyone should
switch to smartphones; they make life better for all of us," a fairer rebuild says something like:
"for people who can afford one and have a network, a smartphone can connect, pay, and teach — but we
should also build those same services for people who cannot, so the benefit is not just for some."
The rebuilt version is not weaker; it is *more true*, because it finally says what it means and
names who it left out. Rebuilding is how critique becomes repair.

## Materials

- Rebuild scaffold sheet
- Worked-example card

## Preparation

- Copy the rebuild scaffold sheet and worked-example card.
- Retrieval: from Lesson 4, recall the iceberg — a stated claim above the water, hidden assumptions
  below. Today we use what we found to build again.
- Prepare one worked example of rebuilding an argument fairly.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **a critique is not finished until
the argument is rebuilt — with the left-out people named, the sweeping claims qualified, and the
hidden assumptions made visible.** Teach with a worked example, then guided practice (S-011). The
**egalitarian** lens is the spine: rebuilding "more fairly" means asking who was left out and writing
them back in. The **ethics** lens: a fairer argument is one a person unlike the speaker could also
accept. The **global** lens: when we rebuild, we borrow the voice of someone from another place or
tradition — and notice how the argument changes. Emphasize the **steelman** discipline: rebuild the
strongest honest version of the argument, not a straw one — disagree with arguments at their best,
not their worst. Keep questions open (philosophy §11); whether a rebuilt argument is "fair enough" is
a values judgment, not a fact, and learners may disagree (philosophy §5). Preview: we leave the
micro of single arguments and turn to global questions — who bears responsibility for climate harm
and poverty.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Lesson 4: what is a hidden assumption? Name the iceberg's two halves.
2. **Meet the scaffold (10 min).** Look at the rebuild sheet — three columns: **original**, **what
   was left out**, **rebuilt**. Read the worked-example card and see how one argument was rebuilt.
3. **Worked example (10 min).** Watch the rebuild done step by step for "Everyone should switch to
   smartphones." (a) Name who was left out — people who cannot afford one, who have no network, who
   cannot use the interface. (b) Qualify the sweeping word — "everyone" becomes "many people." (c)
   Add what was missing — the same services should be built for those without. (d) Read the rebuilt
   version aloud and check: would a person unlike the speaker accept it?
4. **Guided practice (15 min).** In a pair, rebuild the argument from Lesson 4 you critiqued. Write
   the original, the left-out people, and the rebuilt version, then swap and improve each other's.
5. **Independent practice (10 min).** In your journal, rebuild one argument on your own and check it
   against the fairness list: (a) no sweeping word left unqualified; (b) the left-out are named; (c)
   the claim now says what it means.
6. **Close (5 min).** Read your before and after to a partner. Which sentence changed the most, and
   why?

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a half-built rebuild (one left-out group already added) to complete.
- **Extension:** Steelman an argument you disagree with — rebuild its strongest honest version — then
  say what it still leaves out.
- **Expression:** Learners who do not speak aloud can write the before/after, underline what changed,
  or have a partner read their written rebuild — no step requires the voice.

## Assessment

- **Formative (peer + self):** Can the learner name who was left out, qualify a sweeping claim, and
  produce a rebuilt version that a person unlike the speaker could accept?
- **Portfolio artifact (unit):** The before/after rebuild, added to the "critique" section of the
  portfolio.

## Home connection

Take a claim you heard at home this week ("everyone does X now"). Rebuild it fairly with someone
there, naming who it leaves out.
