# Lesson 10 — Designing Collective Action: Goals, Allies, Steps

## Summary

Learners **design a plan for collective action** on a real community or global issue, with clear
**goals, allies, and steps**. They study two documented movements, extract the pattern, and build
their own plan on a canvas, then check it for completeness.

## Objectives

- Design a plan for collective action on a real community or global issue, including goals, allies,
  and steps. _(D09.S3.10.01)_

## Connection

Almost nothing important changes because one person wanted it to. It changes because people who
wanted the same thing found each other, agreed on what they were after, and took steps together — a
cleanup, a petition, a march, a cooperative, a campaign. A plan for collective action has three
parts: a **goal** clear enough that you would know when you reached it, **allies** — the people and
groups who share the goal or can help, and **steps** — the concrete things you will actually do, in
order. Today you design one for something you care about.

## Materials

- Plan canvas
- Two movement case cards

## Preparation

- Copy the plan canvas and the case cards.
- Retrieval: from Grade 8 and 9, recall how ordinary people have worked together to change something
  unfair (a social movement's goals, strategies, and effects). Today we turn that into a *plan*.
- Prepare one worked example of filling the three boxes for a real issue.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **a collective-action plan is
concrete — a goal you could verify, allies who share it or can help, and steps you can actually take
— and it is built from real patterns, not invented from nothing.** Ground the pattern in two
documented movements: the Green Belt Movement, founded 1977 by Wangari Maathai, which organized
people — many of them women — to plant trees, countering deforestation and planting over thirty
million trees (S-059); and the 1930 Salt March, a nonviolent act of civil disobedience led by
Gandhi against the British salt monopoly, part of India's independence movement (S-390). Let learners
extract goals, allies, and steps from each rather than being told. The **community** lens is the
spine: action is done *with* others, not to them. The **egalitarian** lens: ask whose voices are in
the plan and whose are missing — a plan made only by the powerful is not collective. The **global**
lens: draw examples from many regions and causes, and let learners choose issues from their own
context. The **ethics** lens: the means should match the goal — a plan that harms people to help them
is not a plan worth having. Distinguish evidence (what these movements did) from values (what makes a
goal worth pursuing) (philosophy §5). Preview: next lesson we evaluate the plan's likely effects.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Grade 8/9: name one time ordinary people worked together to change
   something unfair. What did they want, and who helped?
2. **Meet the cases (10 min).** Read the two movement case cards. For each, underline the **goal**,
   circle the **allies**, and number the **steps**.
3. **Meet the canvas (10 min).** Look at the plan canvas — three boxes around a center: **issue**,
   **goals**, **allies**, **steps**. See how the movements' parts would drop into the boxes.
4. **Worked example (10 min).** Watch one plan built step by step for a real issue — e.g., a
   neighborhood without clean water: *goal* — a working tap or filter within reach of every family;
   *allies* — neighbors, a health worker, a local official, an aid group; *steps* — survey who needs
   water, gather signatures, meet the official, install and test. This is the pattern.
5. **Guided practice (15 min).** In a pair, choose an issue you care about and fill the canvas:
   write the goal so you could *verify* it, list at least three allies, and number at least four
   steps in order.
6. **Independent practice (5 min).** Check your plan against the completeness list: (a) a verifiable
   goal; (b) three or more allies; (c) four or more ordered steps. Mark "not yet" and fix anything
   missing.
7. **Close (5 min).** Share your goal and first step with another pair. What would you need to start
   tomorrow?

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a half-filled canvas (goal written) and a menu of possible allies and steps to
  choose from.
- **Extension:** Add a **coalition map** — a small diagram of who can influence whom — and a note on
  who holds the power to make your goal happen.
- **Expression:** Learners who do not speak aloud can fill the canvas on paper, point to their goal,
  or have a partner read their written plan — no step requires the voice.

## Assessment

- **Formative (peer + self):** Can the learner write a verifiable goal, name three or more allies,
  and order four or more steps?
- **Portfolio artifact (unit):** The completed plan canvas, as the first page of the "collective
  action" section.

## Home connection

Ask a family member about a time people in your community worked together on something. What was the
goal, and who were the allies?
