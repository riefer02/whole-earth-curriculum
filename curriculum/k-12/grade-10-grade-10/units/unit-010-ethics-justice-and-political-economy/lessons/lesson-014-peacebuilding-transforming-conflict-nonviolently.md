# Lesson 14 — Peacebuilding: Transforming Conflict Nonviolently

## Summary

Learners analyze **peacebuilding — preventing and transforming conflict nonviolently** — through
three strategies: **nonviolent resistance, dialogue and mediation, and truth and reconciliation**.
They study two documented cases, match strategies to settings, and write their own weighed judgment.

## Objectives

- Analyze peacebuilding — preventing and transforming conflict nonviolently — and weigh its
  strategies across different settings. _(D09.S4.10.02)_

## Connection

Peace is not just the absence of fighting; it is something that has to be *built* — a fair way to
share what is scarce, a way to hear each other without violence, and a way to face old hurts honestly
so they stop repeating. Some conflicts are best met by refusing to cooperate with injustice
nonviolently, the way the 1930 Salt March defied an unfair salt law without lifting a hand against
anyone (S-390). Others need truth-telling and repair, the way South Africa's Truth and Reconciliation
Commission invited public testimony and, in defined cases, amnesty in exchange for full disclosure
(S-392). Different settings call for different strategies — and weighing which one fits is the skill
of peacebuilding.

## Materials

- Peacebuilding strategies sheet
- Two conflict case cards

## Preparation

- Copy the strategies sheet and the case cards.
- Retrieval: from Grade 9, recall the roots of conflict (inequality, fear, scarcity) and the
  distinction between conflict and violence. Today we study how to transform conflict without
  violence.
- Prepare one worked example of matching a strategy to a setting.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"), and closes the unit. The idea to land: **peace is
built, not merely kept — through nonviolent resistance, dialogue and mediation, and truth and
reconciliation — and different settings call for different strategies, which we weigh rather than
rank.** Keep the history accurate and hedged: the 1930 Salt March was a nonviolent act of civil
disobedience led by Gandhi against the British salt monopoly, part of India's independence movement
(S-390); South Africa's Truth and Reconciliation Commission (main hearings 1996–1998) invited public
testimony about past abuses and, in defined cases, offered amnesty for full disclosure, alongside
reparations — a real, contested attempt to trade some punishment for truth and repair (S-392).
Present both as attempts with real strengths and limits, not as perfect models. Draw on Galtung's
distinction: peace is more than the absence of war — "positive peace" includes fairness, so
peacebuilding also means reducing the structural violence of inequality and exclusion (S-462). The
**community** lens is the spine: peace is made among people, in relationship. The **egalitarian**
lens: a peace that protects the powerful and silences the harmed is not peace. The **global** lens:
nonviolence, truth-telling, and reconciliation take different forms in every tradition — satyagraha,
hózhó, ubuntu, and others — none is the default (S-205, S-161, S-465). The **ethics** lens: the means
should match the end — a strategy that wins by humiliating others has already lost something. Close
the unit by returning to its opening: all of this has been *weighing* — benefits and harms, for people
and the planet — and questions held as questions, not answered as dogma (philosophy §11). Distinguish
evidence from values throughout (philosophy §5).

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Grade 9: what is the difference between conflict and violence? Name one
   root of conflict.
2. **Meet the cases (10 min).** Read the two case cards — the Salt March and the Truth and
   Reconciliation Commission. For each, underline the *strategy* and circle who was included.
3. **Meet the three strategies (10 min).** Read the strategies sheet: **nonviolent resistance**
   (refusing to cooperate with injustice, without harming people), **dialogue and mediation**
   (bringing sides to listen and agree), **truth and reconciliation** (facing old hurts honestly and
   repairing them). For each, name one setting where it fits best and one where it might not.
4. **Worked example (10 min).** Watch one weighing done step by step: for a conflict where an unfair
   law harms a whole community, *nonviolent resistance* fits — it names the injustice and withdraws
   cooperation — while *dialogue* may be premature if one side will not listen. Say why, and what
   would change the judgment.
5. **Guided practice (15 min).** In a pair, choose a conflict setting (a school dispute, a community
   over scarce water, a society after a war) and weigh two strategies for it. Which fits, which
   does not, and why? Swap settings with another pair and compare.
6. **Independent practice (10 min).** In your journal, write your weighed judgment in four sentences:
   the setting, the strategy you would lead with, why, and one limit. Mark "not yet" if you did not
   name a limit.
7. **Close (5 min).** In a small group, recall the unit's opening question: what does it mean to
   weigh benefits and harms for people and the planet? Say how peacebuilding is one more act of that
   same weighing.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a half-written weighing (setting and strategy filled) to complete with a
  reason and a limit.
- **Extension:** Combine the three strategies into one sequence for a single conflict, and argue for
  the order.
- **Expression:** Learners who do not speak aloud can write or draw the strategy-setting match, or
  have a partner read their written judgment — no step requires the voice.

## Assessment

- **Formative (peer + self):** Can the learner name three peacebuilding strategies, match one to a
  setting, and give a weighed judgment with a limit?
- **Portfolio artifact (unit):** The four-sentence judgment, as the closing page of the "peace and
  repair" section — and the final page of the unit portfolio.

## Home connection

Ask a family member: in a disagreement our community has had, what made peace possible — refusing,
talking, or facing the truth? Compare with the three strategies.
