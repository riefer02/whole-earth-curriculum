# Unit 10 — Ethics, Justice & Political Economy

## Overview

This unit deepens Grade 9's reasoning across frameworks into critique and action,
building on the analysis of moral questions, systemic injustice, power, and
reconciliation. Learners apply ethical reasoning to a question about technology or
the environment, weighing benefits and harms for people and the planet, and critique
a moral argument for hidden assumptions or bias, rebuilding it more fairly. They
analyze global justice questions, such as who bears responsibility for climate harm
or poverty, and weigh competing answers, and evaluate how technology and data can
expand or limit rights, such as privacy, and argue for protections. They design a
plan for collective action on a real community or global issue, including goals,
allies, and steps, and evaluate its likely effects, and analyze the ethics of civic
technology and digital citizenship, including misinformation, surveillance, and who
is included or excluded. They design a restorative process for a specific harm,
including who should be heard, how responsibility is taken, and how repair is made,
and analyze peacebuilding — preventing and transforming conflict nonviolently — and
weigh its strategies across different settings. Questions are held as questions,
never answered as dogma.

## Essential questions

- How do I apply ethical reasoning to technology and the environment, and critique a moral argument for hidden assumptions before rebuilding it fairly?
- Who bears responsibility for global harms such as climate change and poverty, and how do technology and data expand or limit rights such as privacy?
- How do I design collective action and a restorative process for real harm, and how does peacebuilding transform conflict nonviolently?
