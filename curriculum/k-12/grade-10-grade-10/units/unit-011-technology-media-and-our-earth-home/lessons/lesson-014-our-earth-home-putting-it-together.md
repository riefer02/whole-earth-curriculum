# Lesson 14 — Our Earth Home: Putting It Together

## Summary

Learners assemble the unit's portfolio and synthesize its four strands — technology, media, ecology, and
stewardship — into one stance: **neither worship nor fear**. They reflect on how the threads connect and
write a short personal statement of how they will weigh technology and care for the Earth, closing the
unit.

## Objectives

- Synthesize the unit's strands and evaluate a sustainability solution and the circular economy within a
  larger stance of stewardship. _(D10.S4.10.01, D10.S4.10.02, with all unit objectives revisited)_

## Connection

For three weeks you have asked four questions: *what does technology do to freedom and power? how do I
check what I am told? what does the living Earth give us, and where are its breaking points? and how do
we build something that lasts?* They look like separate subjects, but they are one question: **how do we
live well, together, on this one shared home, with tools we made and a planet we did not?** Putting the
threads together is not a summary exercise — it is how a way of seeing becomes a way of living.

## Materials

- Portfolio checklist
- Unit synthesis map

## Preparation

- Copy the checklist and the synthesis map.
- Retrieval: gather the unit's artifacts — the data-trail sheet, the asymmetry diagram, the gains/losses
  chart, the emerging-technology scale, the fact-check worksheet, the two-column fact/interpretation
  sheet, the ownership map, the frame/omission sheet, the ecosystem-services diagram, the biodiversity
  pyramid, the tipping-point diagram, the linear/circular diagram, and the sustainability evaluation.
- Prepare one worked example of connecting two strands in a single sentence.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **the unit's four strands are one stance
— neither worship nor fear — applied to technology and the Earth; synthesis is the moment the skills
become a way of living as a steward.** This is a summative and reflective lesson: the heavy lifting is
assembling, connecting, and writing, not new content. The **technology** and **environment** lenses are
the twin poles the learner learns to hold in tension. The **egalitarian** lens: a steward asks who gains
and who is left out, in every strand — privacy, news, services, solutions. The **global** lens: this one
home is lived in many ways, and the learner's way is one among them, not the default. The **ethics**
lens: synthesis lands in *choice* — what the learner will do. Use retrieval (the checklist) and spacing
(the artifacts span three weeks) deliberately; give feedback on the statement; treat "not yet" as
information, not a verdict. Hold questions open (philosophy §11): the stance is a practice, not a
finished answer. Celebrate the work as collective, not ranked.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Lay out your artifacts from the unit. What are the four strands, and which
   artifact belongs to each?
2. **Meet the synthesis (10 min).** Look at the synthesis map. The four strands meet in one stance:
   **neither worship nor fear** — the tools we made deserve critical judgment, and the Earth we did not
   make deserves care. A **synthesis** is not a list; it is the connection between the parts.
3. **Worked example (10 min).** Watch two strands connected in one sentence: "The same feed that shapes
   what I believe (media) is powered by the data I leave behind (technology), and a clear-eyed reader is
   as important as a careful steward." Note how it names *both* strands and the connection.
4. **Guided practice (15 min).** In a pair, write two more connecting sentences — one linking technology
   or media to ecology or stewardship, and one linking the fact-check method to the evaluation of
   solutions. Read them to each other and improve them.
5. **Independent practice (15 min).** Assemble your portfolio against the checklist. Then write a short
   **steward's statement** (five to eight sentences): how will you weigh technology — neither worshipping
   nor fearing it — and how will you care for the Earth home, starting with one concrete choice this
   month? Mark anything you are unsure of "not yet" and name one thing you want to revisit.
6. **Close (5 min).** Share one line from your statement. You are not finished learning this — you are
   beginning to live it.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide sentence starters for the connecting sentences and the statement; allow the
  statement to be spoken and transcribed.
- **Extension:** Write the steward's statement as a short letter to your future self, naming what you will
  do and what you will keep questioning.
- **Expression:** The statement may be written, spoken, drawn as a map, or recorded — the synthesis, not
  the format, is the goal.

## Assessment

- **Summative (portfolio):** Does the portfolio contain each artifact, and does the steward's statement
  connect the strands and name a concrete choice?
- **Peer + self:** Do the connecting sentences name both strands and a real connection? Does the statement
  show neither worship nor fear?
- **"Not yet" is information:** any gap is a thing to revisit, not a verdict.

## Home connection

Share your steward's statement with a family member, and ask them to name one thing *they* would weigh
carefully and one way *they* care for the Earth. Compare your two homes.
