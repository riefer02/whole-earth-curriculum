# Unit 11 — Technology, Media & Our Earth Home

## Overview

This unit gathers the outward year into critical judgment and stewardship — neither
worship nor fear — building on Grade 9's algorithmic power, echo chambers, climate,
and environmental justice. Learners analyze how data, surveillance, and automation
affect privacy, freedom, and who has power in a society, and evaluate an emerging
technology, such as AI or genetic tools, by weighing its promises and risks for
humanity and the environment. They apply a fact-checking method to a real claim,
tracing evidence and separating fact from interpretation, and analyze who owns the
media people consume and how concentrated ownership can shape what is reported and
left out. They explain what ecosystem services are — pollination, clean water, and
soil — and how biodiversity underpins them, and explain what a tipping point is and
why crossing one in an ecosystem or climate can be hard to reverse. They evaluate a
real sustainability solution, such as a circular economy or a restoration project,
for its benefits, limits, and fairness, and explain the idea of a circular economy —
designing out waste — and compare it with a take-make-waste linear economy.

## Essential questions

- How do data, surveillance, and automation affect privacy, freedom, and power, and how do I weigh an emerging technology's promises and risks?
- How do I fact-check a real claim, and who owns the media people consume and how does that shape what is reported?
- What are ecosystem services and tipping points, and how do I evaluate sustainability solutions and the circular economy?
