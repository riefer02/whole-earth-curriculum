# Lesson 10 — Power and Momentum: Rates, Motion, and Collisions

## Summary

Learners meet **power** as the rate of doing work (**P = W ÷ t**, measured in **watts**)
and **momentum** as mass times velocity (**p = m·v**), conserved in collisions. They
connect both to machines — why an engine is rated in watts — and to safety engineering
such as seatbelts and airbags, which stretch a collision out over time to soften the
force.

## Objectives

- Explain motion, force, and energy quantitatively in everyday and engineered systems
  (such as work, power, and momentum). _(D06.S3.10.01)_

## Connection

Two people lift the same bucket — one in 2 seconds, one in 10. Both do the same
**work**, but the first uses five times the **power**: power is how fast work happens,
which is why a strong motor is rated in watts. And when things collide — a ball into a
wall, a cart into another cart — the total **momentum** (mass × velocity) is conserved:
it is passed from one object to another, never lost. Understanding that is why cars
have crumple zones and airbags, which stretch the crash over a longer moment to soften
the force on the people inside.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **power = work ÷ time
(P = W/t, in watts) measures how fast work happens; momentum = mass × velocity
(p = m·v) is conserved in collisions; and stretching a collision over time (impulse)
reduces the force — which is why airbags and crumple zones save lives.** Teach both
with worked examples and unit discipline (S-011); the momentum-conservation step is the
hardest, so model the "total before = total after" setup explicitly.

The **intellectual** lens: conservation of momentum is a deep, testable law — it is why
collisions are predictable. The **technology** lens: every engine's rating and every
crash test is a power/momentum calculation in steel and plastic; safety engineering
turns this physics into saved lives. The **ethics** lens: safety features like airbags
were resisted as "extra cost" before they became standard — a reminder that physics
tells us *what is possible to protect*, but values decide whether we do. The
**egalitarian** lens: these safety technologies should protect everyone, not only those
who can pay for them — access to safe vehicles and safe roads is a fairness question,
not just an engineering one. Preview: Lesson 11 asks where the energy for all this work
comes from — energy generation.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Lesson 9, what is work, and what are its units? Name
   something you did today that took power — work done quickly.
2. **Meet power (10 min).** **Power = work ÷ time**, in watts (W):
   - Worked example: lifting the 200 J bucket in 2 s → P = 200 J ÷ 2 s = 100 W. In 10 s
     → P = 20 W. Same work, less power.
3. **Meet momentum (15 min).** **Momentum = mass × velocity** (p = m·v).
   - Worked example: cart A (2 kg, 3 m/s) hits stationary cart B (1 kg). Momentum
     before = 2 × 3 = 6 kg·m/s. After the collision, the total is still 6 kg·m/s,
     shared between the two carts — momentum is **conserved**.
   - Airbags: by stretching the stop over a longer time, the same change in momentum
     produces a smaller force on your body.
4. **Guided practice (15 min).** With a partner, solve: (a) P for 500 J in 5 s; (b) the
   momentum of a 1,200 kg car at 15 m/s; (c) a 3 kg ball at 2 m/s catches a 2 kg ball
   at rest — total momentum before? Compare and agree.
5. **Independent practice (10 min).** In your journal, explain in your own words why a
   crumple zone or airbag protects a passenger. Then write one sentence stating what is
   conserved in a collision.
6. **Close (5 min).** In one sentence each: what is power, what is momentum, and what
   is conserved when things collide?

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Act out the collision with two rolling objects first; give formulas with
  blanks to fill.
- **Extension:** Verify momentum conservation in a two-object collision with actual
  numbers, and explain how impulse (force × time) connects to the airbag.

## Assessment

- **Formative (peer + self):** Can the learner compute power and momentum with correct
  units, and explain conservation of momentum and why airbags reduce injury?
- **Portfolio artifact (unit):** The completed power-and-momentum worksheet with the
  airbag reflection, added to the physical-systems section.

## Home connection

Ask someone at home about a vehicle or machine they use. Together, wonder: what is its
power rating, and how does it protect people in a crash or sudden stop?
