# Lesson 8 — Craft in Rehearsal: Timing, Expression, Ensemble

## Summary

Learners **rehearse** and refine their devised piece using a feedback loop — rehearse,
watch, note, revise — to sharpen **timing**, **expression**, and **ensemble**, then
perform the revised piece. This completes the movement-and-drama objective begun in
Lesson 7.

## Objectives

- Devise and perform an original piece that develops a clear concept and demonstrates
  craft in movement and expression. _(D07.S3.10.01)_

## Connection

The first time you tell a story it is a sketch; the fifth time you know where to
pause. Rehearsal is not repetition for its own sake — it is the loop where you *watch,
notice, and fix*, turning a sketch into craft. Athletes, musicians, and cooks all do
this: do it, look at it, change one thing, do it again. Today your piece goes through
that loop, then meets an audience.

## Materials

- Rehearsal feedback sheet
- Open floor space
- Your storyboard from Lesson 7

## Preparation

- Retrieval: from Lesson 7, recall your piece and concept. Today you sharpen it.
- Prepare one worked example of *specific* feedback: "your turn came early, so the
  arrival felt rushed" versus vague "that was nice" — and show how the performer
  revises one moment.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **craft is a loop —
rehearse, watch (or record), note what wavered, revise one thing, repeat — and
feedback works only when it is specific and about the work, not the person.** Timing
(are the pauses and speeds deliberate?), expression (do face and body carry the
concept?), and ensemble (do the parts fit with others?) are the three observable
targets. The loop mirrors the assessment philosophy: observe what the learner *does*
and say back one concrete thing, never rank (S-011; S-189).

The **ethics** lens: honest feedback is a gift — specific, kind, and about the work —
and receiving it is how a maker grows without losing their own voice. The
**egalitarian** lens: feedback is not a judge's verdict but a peer's help; everyone
gives and receives, and a "not yet" is a next step, never a ranking. The **global**
lens: rehearsal traditions differ across cultures, but everywhere the watch-and-revise
loop is how craft is passed down — from the tinikling dancers who step between bamboo
poles in the Philippines (S-116) to the capoeira circle of Brazil (S-325), mastery is
made in repeated, watched practice. The **technology** lens: a camera lets a performer
see what a mirror cannot — the piece from the audience's eyes. The **environment**
lens: rehearsing outdoors teaches the performer to work *with* place and weather, not
against it. The **community** lens: a showing for an audience who wants the performer
to succeed is the natural home of a performance.

Keep the showing low-stakes and cooperative (assessment.md: performance for an
audience who wants the learner to succeed). Preview: Lesson 9 turns to the ethics of
engaging with *other* people's movement and drama traditions.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** You have a devised piece. Today it becomes *crafted* — and then
   *performed*.
2. **Meet the loop (10 min).** Watch a worked example: a performer runs a piece; a
   watcher says one specific thing ("the turn came early"); the performer revises one
   moment and runs it again. That is the loop: **rehearse → watch → note → revise**.
3. **Rehearse in a trio (20 min).** Take turns: one performs, one watches for timing,
   one watches for expression. Use the feedback sheet: what did you see clearly? Where
   did timing or expression waver? Then the performer revises *one* moment.
4. **Run it again (10 min).** Repeat the loop once more with the revision in place.
   Notice what changed. If you are working with others, check the *ensemble* — do your
   parts fit?
5. **Perform (10 min).** Show your piece once to a small audience — your trio, or the
   group. Afterward, say one thing you changed and why.
6. **Close (5 min).** Add your feedback sheet and any revised storyboard to your
   portfolio. Next: whose movement can I learn, and how?

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Focus on one target only (timing) and make one revision guided by a
  partner's single observation.
- **Extension:** Record the piece, watch it back, and write a short reflection naming
  one thing you would not have noticed without the recording.
- **Non-motor / adapted path:** A seated or adapted piece rehearses through the same
  loop — rehearse, watch, note, revise — with "timing" meaning pause and pace and
  "expression" carried by the face, voice, and available movement. A deviser who does
  not move can direct a partner and take the "note" role in the trio, so feedback and
  revision stay fully theirs.
- **Non-visual path:** A watcher who cannot see the performer can receive the piece as
  described sound and voice, and can give feedback on timing and expression from what
  is heard and spoken; the feedback sheet is the same.

## Assessment

- **Summative (performance + peer):** The learner performs the revised piece with
  observable craft — deliberate timing, clear expression matching the concept, and
  (where relevant) ensemble fit — and can name one revision made from feedback.
- **Self-assessment:** The learner self-places on the mastery checklist for
  _D07.S3.10.01_ and notes the next thing to sharpen.

## Home connection

Perform your piece once for someone at home. Ask them the one question on the sheet:
what did they see clearly?
