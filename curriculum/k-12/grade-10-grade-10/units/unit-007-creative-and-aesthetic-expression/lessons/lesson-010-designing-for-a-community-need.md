# Lesson 10 — Designing for a Community Need

## Summary

Learners identify a real **community need**, research it, and generate and document
ideas and a first **prototype**, learning the design process — find a need, research,
imagine, prototype, test — and the habit of recording every decision. This opens the
making-and-design strand; sustainability and fairness sharpen it in Lesson 11.

## Objectives

- Design and make something that serves a community need with attention to
  sustainability and fairness, and document the decisions. _(D07.S4.10.01)_

## Connection

Every useful thing around you began as someone noticing a *need* — a seat where people
wait, a handle that is easy to grip, a path where feet kept wearing the grass. Design
is not decorating an object; it is noticing a real problem a real group of people have,
and making something that helps. Today you become that noticer, and you write down
what you decide and why.

## Materials

- Design log
- Sketch paper and pencil
- Simple making materials (card, cloth, wire, tape, found objects)

## Preparation

- Retrieval: from Lessons 1, 4, and 7, recall building a body of work, a piece, and a
  sequence around one idea. Design is the same habit, pointed at a *need*.
- Prepare one worked example of the design process applied to a small need (e.g., a
  shade structure for a waiting spot) with each decision documented.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **design is a
process — find a need, research it, imagine, prototype, test — and documentation is
not paperwork but the thing that makes a decision defensible and shareable.** A
"community need" can be tiny (a shaded seat, a better path, a container that keeps
food dry) — small is fine; real is what matters. The design log is the through-line
that will let learners later defend their choices on sustainability and fairness
(Lesson 11) and judge responsible versus harmful design (Lesson 12).

The **ethics** lens: good design begins with serving people, not impressing a judge —
the need is the moral center. The **egalitarian** lens: ask *who* is served and *who
is left out* from the very first idea; a design that helps only the most able-bodied
or the richest has already failed a fairness test. The **global** lens: needs differ
by place and climate — a shade structure matters differently in a hot dry town than in
a cold wet one — so design is always local before it is general. The **technology**
lens: tools (digital or fabrication) can extend what a designer can make — but they
never replace the noticing. The **environment** lens: from the first sketch, ask what
the thing is made of and where it goes when it breaks (deepened in Lesson 11). The
**community** lens is the spine: design is done *with* and *for* a community, never to
it.

Model one worked example (worked example → guided practice → independent design;
S-011). Keep prototypes low-fidelity — the point is the thinking and the record, not a
finished object.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** You have made art, music, and movement around one idea. Today
   you make a *thing* around one *need*.
2. **Meet the process (10 min).** Watch a worked example: a shaded seat for a waiting
   spot. See the five stages — **find a need, research, imagine, prototype, test** —
   and see each decision written in the log.
3. **Find a need (10 min).** Think of your community — school, street, market, home,
   or a place you know. List three small needs. Choose one and write in your log:
   "The need is ______, and it matters to ______."
4. **Research and imagine (15 min).** Look around (or ask) how this need is met now —
   and where it is not. Sketch two or three ideas. Note the *constraints*: what
   materials, skills, and time are available?
5. **Prototype (10 min).** Build a rough first version of your best idea with the
   materials at hand. It does not need to work perfectly; it needs to show the idea.
6. **Close (5 min).** Write one decision you made and why, in your log. Keep the log
   and prototype in your portfolio. Next: sustainability and fairness.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a short list of small needs to choose from and ask for one idea
  with one documented reason.
- **Extension:** Ask one real person who would use the thing what they actually need,
  and revise the idea in response.
- **Non-motor / non-visual path:** The prototype can be described in words and sketched
  by a partner, or built by a partner under the designer's spoken direction; the design
  log is the document of thinking and can be spoken and transcribed rather than written
  by hand.
- **Language:** The design log may be kept in the learner's home language, with key
  decisions translated to a shared language for sharing; the five stages (find, research,
  imagine, prototype, test) carry cleanly across languages.

## Assessment

- **Formative (self):** Can the learner name a real need, state who it serves, produce
  one idea and a rough prototype, and document at least one decision with a reason?
- **Portfolio artifact (unit):** The design log's first page (need, who is served,
  idea, one documented decision) and the rough prototype, added to the portfolio.

## Home connection

Walk your home or street and find one small unmet need. Tell someone what you would
design for it and who it would help.
