# Lesson 5 — Recording Craft: Capturing and Shaping Sound

## Summary

Learners **record** their composed piece, capturing a clean take and then shaping the
sound through deliberate choices, logging each **technique** so they can explain how
the recording expresses their idea. This completes the music objective begun in
Lesson 4.

## Objectives

- Compose and record an original piece that expresses a clear idea or feeling, and
  explain the techniques used. _(D07.S2.10.01)_

## Connection

A story changes depending on *how* it is told — whispered or shouted, up close or from
across the room, with pauses or in one rush. Recording is the same craft applied to
sound: you do not just capture the music; you *choose* how it will be heard — how
near, how loud against the room, what stays and what fades. Today you make those
choices visible and name them.

## Materials

- A recording device (phone, recorder, or shared device)
- Techniques log
- Your graphic score from Lesson 4

## Preparation

- Retrieval: from Lesson 4, recall your piece and its idea. Today you capture it and
  shape how it is heard.
- Prepare one worked example: the same phrase recorded two ways — close and far, or
  one layer versus two — so learners hear that capture *changes* meaning.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **recording is a
craft, not a lucky tap of a button — the composer chooses capture, balance, and
revision, and can explain each choice.** The signal path (source → capture → balance →
playback, with a "listen and revise" loop) gives learners a concrete, observable frame
for their techniques log. The low-tech path is essential: with no device, a
*repeatable live performance* documented by a listener is the recording — the craft is
still in the choosing and the describing.

The **ethics** lens: a recording is a *document*; being able to say exactly what you
did and why is honesty about your own work (and, in Lesson 6, about others' work you
engage). The **egalitarian** lens: the craft of listening and choosing needs no
expensive gear — a phone or even a careful live rendition works; recording is not
gated by a studio. The **global** lens: recordings are how music travels and how
living traditions are documented — Smithsonian Folkways has preserved the world's
music since 1948 precisely so communities' own voices are heard (S-188). The
**technology** lens is the spine: the microphone and the DAW changed what music can be
— layered, edited, replayed — and understanding the path is understanding that power.
The **environment** lens: devices carry a material cost (rare minerals, e-waste), a
reminder to use and reuse them with care (S-397; previewed fully in Lesson 11).
Preview: Lesson 6 turns to the ethics of engaging with *other* people's music.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** You have a piece and a score. Today it becomes a *recording* —
   a document others can hear.
2. **Meet the path (10 min).** Watch a worked example: one phrase recorded two ways.
   Notice the four stages — **source**, **capture** (how and where you record),
   **balance** (what is loud, what is soft, how layers sit), and **playback** (how it
   is heard). Then notice the loop: you listen and revise.
3. **Set up (5 min).** Decide where and how to capture. Closer or farther? In a quiet
   room or with the world as a texture? Write the plan in your techniques log.
4. **Record takes (15 min).** Record at least two takes of your piece. Between takes,
   change one thing — distance, speed, a layer added or removed. Note each change and
   why.
5. **Listen and choose (10 min).** Listen back to both takes. Which serves your idea
   better? Write your final choice and the reason in the log.
6. **Close (5 min).** You now have a composed *and recorded* piece, with a log that
   explains it. Keep both in your portfolio. Next time: whose music can I use, and
   how?

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Make one clean take and log one technique (e.g., "I recorded close so
  the voice sounds near").
- **Extension:** Make a two-layer overdub and explain in a sentence how the layers
  serve the idea.
- **Non-hearing path (deaf or hard of hearing):** Replace "listen and choose" with
  *seeing and feeling* — use a visual waveform on the device if one is shown, place a
  hand on the speaker to feel the difference between takes, or have a peer describe in
  writing (or sign) what changed between takes. The techniques log is written/visual,
  so the craft of capture and balance is still fully documented.
- **No-device path:** The low-tech "performance recording" (a careful live rendition
  witnessed and described in writing by a listener) is the recording; the craft is in
  the choosing and the describing, not the device.

## Assessment

- **Summative (portfolio):** The learner presents a recorded (or documented
  live-performed) piece with a log naming at least two techniques — capture placement,
  balance, layering, or revision — each tied to the piece's idea.
- **Self-assessment:** The learner self-places on the mastery checklist for
  _D07.S2.10.01_ and notes what to improve on the next take.

## Home connection

Record a ten-second sound from your home — a kettle, a door, a voice — two ways (near
and far), and ask someone which feels different and why.
