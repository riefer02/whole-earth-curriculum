# Lesson 7 — Devising a Concept: Movement That Means Something

## Summary

Learners **devise** an original movement piece from a clear concept, choosing gesture,
levels, and timing deliberately and storyboarding the sequence so the concept is
visible in the movement. This is the first half of the movement-and-drama objective —
craft in rehearsal and performance comes in Lesson 8.

## Objectives

- Devise and perform an original piece that develops a clear concept and demonstrates
  craft in movement and expression. _(D07.S3.10.01)_

## Connection

You already speak a whole language without words: the shrug, the slow step of a person
who does not want to leave, the sudden straightening of a spine when someone has had
enough. Devising is taking that everyday language and making it *intentional* — a
concept, told through the body, one deliberate choice at a time. Today you turn
feeling into movement with a plan.

## Materials

- Movement storyboard sheet
- Open floor space

## Preparation

- Retrieval: from Lessons 1 and 4, recall building a body of work and a piece around
  one idea. Movement works the same way: one concept, developed through choices.
- Prepare one worked example: a four-moment sequence expressing "arrival" — low and
  closed, then rising, turning, opening — storyboarded frame by frame.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **devising means
starting from a concept and choosing movement to serve it — gesture, level (high/mid/
low), and timing (fast/slow, still/moving) — then storyboarding so the concept is
visible in the choices.** A storyboard (drawn frames with captions) is to movement
what the graphic score was to music in Lesson 4: a universal, low-tech way to document
an idea before it is polished. The unit's through-line holds again — one motif,
developed.

The **ethics** lens: devising your own concept is the honest starting point; it is the
opposite of copying another culture's dance and calling it yours (Lesson 9 makes this
explicit). The **egalitarian** lens: every body is an instrument and every body can
devise; the piece is built from *your* movement vocabulary, not a single imposed
ideal. The **global** lens: across cultures, movement carries meaning — gesture,
posture, and spacing communicate without words in performance and daily life alike
(S-189). The **technology** lens: a camera lets a deviser watch and revise — but the
storyboard does too, with no device. The **environment** lens: devising outdoors
reminds us the body is part of place — terrain becomes part of the concept. The
**physical** and **social-emotional** lenses: moving is how the body and feeling join;
name the feeling first, then let the body speak it.

Model one worked example (worked example → guided practice → independent devising;
S-011). Keep it short — four moments are enough to hold a concept.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** You have made a visual motif and a musical idea. Today you make
   a *movement* idea and develop it.
2. **Meet the choices (10 min).** Watch a worked example: a four-moment piece about
   "arrival." Notice the choices — **gesture** (what the hands and face do), **level**
   (high, middle, low), **timing** (fast or slow, still or moving). See each moment
   storyboarded with a caption.
3. **Choose your concept (5 min).** Pick one concept — leaving home, waiting for news,
   meeting an old friend, carrying something heavy. Write it: "This piece is about
   ______."
4. **Devise the sequence (15 min).** Build four moments: a beginning, a change, a
   turning point, an arrival. For each, decide the gesture, level, and timing that
   serve the concept.
5. **Storyboard it (10 min).** Draw each moment in a frame and write a caption under
   it. Your board should let a friend read the concept without hearing a word.
6. **Close (5 min).** Show your sequence once to a partner, who guesses the concept.
   Keep the storyboard in your portfolio. Next time: craft in rehearsal.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Devise a three-moment sequence (beginning, middle, end) using only
  level changes, and caption each.
- **Extension:** Add contrast — one fast moment against a slow one, or high against
  low — and write a sentence on how the contrast serves the concept.
- **Non-motor path (seated, limited mobility, or wheelchair):** The piece lives in the
  parts of the body that move freely — hands, face, shoulders, eyes, voice, breath.
  Levels become *close to the body or far from it*, high becomes *lifted hands or gaze*,
  low becomes *dropped head or closed posture*. Devising with a partner: the deviser
  directs with voice while a partner performs the shapes, so the concept is still
  authored by the learner.
- **Non-visual path (blind or low vision):** Storyboard in words and spoken captions
  rather than drawings, and have a partner read back each moment; the sequence can be
  devised and checked by touch and voice.
- **Neurodivergent support:** Give the concept as a short menu, keep the
  four-moment frame predictable, and allow stillness or a repeated calming gesture as a
  valid moment — pacing and quiet are choices, not failures.

## Assessment

- **Formative (self):** Can the learner state the concept in one sentence, perform a
  short sequence, and point to at least two deliberate choices (gesture, level, or
  timing) in their storyboard?
- **Portfolio artifact (unit):** The movement storyboard with its concept statement,
  added to the portfolio as the movement-piece plan.

## Home connection

Watch for one moment of "body language" at home — someone's posture when they are
tired, glad, or worried — and name the level and timing that carry the meaning.
