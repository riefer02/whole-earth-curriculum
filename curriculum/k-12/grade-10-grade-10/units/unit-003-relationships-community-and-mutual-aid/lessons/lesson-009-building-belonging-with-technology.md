# Lesson 9 — Building Belonging with Technology

## Summary

Learners **evaluate how technology and media create and transform communities and
belonging**. Digital tools build *new* kinds of community — online spaces, diaspora
connection, communities of shared need or interest — and transform *offline* belonging,
even as they decide who is welcomed and who is left out. They close by mapping their own
belonging and naming one inclusion and one exclusion they see.

## Objectives

- Evaluate how technology and media create and transform communities and belonging.
  _(D02.S3.10.01)_

## Connection

Think of a group that matters to you that could not exist without a screen: the faraway
family kept close by daily calls, the friends who share a rare interest no one in your town
has, the people who understand a struggle you thought was only yours. Technology does not
just move messages — it *builds rooms*. It lets a diaspora stay one people across oceans,
lets a person with a rare identity find others like them, lets a neighborhood organize a
cleanup in an afternoon. But the same rooms have doors, and doors keep some people out:
those without a device or a connection, those who do not speak the platform's language,
those who are shouted down or driven out. Whether your community is a village or a global
network, asking *who is welcomed, and who is left out* is how you read belonging itself —
and it is a question you can ask of every room you ever stand in.

## Materials

- Belonging circles diagram
- Writing journal

## Preparation

- Copy or draw the concentric belonging circles.
- Retrieval: from Grade 7, recall that online and offline communities differ and that
  people belong in each (D02.S3.07.01); from Grade 8, recall analyzing how power and
  resources are shared and who is left out (D02.S3.08.01). Today we put those together at
  Grade 10 depth.
- Prepare a worked example: one community built by technology and one transformed by it.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **technology both creates
new communities — online spaces, diaspora ties, communities of shared need — and transforms
offline belonging, while its design and its divides decide who is welcomed and who is left
out.** The balance is the honest center: online community is a real, ordinary part of
adolescent social life, not inherently harmful (S-226, S-311), yet access is unequal — cost,
connectivity, disability, and geography change who can join (S-281) — and the *design* of a
room (who moderates, who is amplified, who is banned) shapes belonging as much as the people
in it. Putnam's account of declining shared life is one lens on how connection can thin
(S-157); Anderson's "imagined communities" show how shared media can bind people who will
never meet (S-415). Present these as *lenses to evaluate with*, not verdicts.

Model one example once, then let learners work (worked example → guided practice →
independent work; S-011). The **technology** lens is the spine: read the room as a
*built* thing — its rules, moderation, and ranking decide belonging. The **community**
lens: ask what holds a community together and what sustains it over time. The
**egalitarian** lens is essential: belonging is not distributed fairly — the digital
divide, platform power, and harassment gate some people out, and that is a justice
question, not a personal one. The **ethics** lens: a welcoming room is one a person has
shaped to be fair; an exclusive room is one that has shaped the person to be cruel. The
**critical-thinking** lens: ask *who is missing from this room, and why?* The
**social-emotional** lens: belonging is deeply felt — being welcomed or shut out can shape
a whole year; invite, never force, and let learners share only what they choose. The
**global** lens: the same tools build different rooms in different places, each shaped by
its culture and its history. Preview: Lesson 10 zooms out — how a local community connects
to the regional, national, and global.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Grade 7: how do online and offline communities differ? From
   Grade 8: who gets left out when power and resources are shared unevenly? Today we ask
   what technology *does* to belonging.
2. **Meet the circles (10 min).** Look at the diagram. Your belonging is nested: family,
   class, neighborhood — then, threaded by screens, the online spaces, the diaspora ties,
   the global networks. Learn the two things technology does: it **creates** communities
   that could not exist before, and it **transforms** the ones that already do.
3. **Read two examples (10 min).** Watch a worked example. *Created:* a group of people with
   the same rare condition, or the same far-flung heritage, find each other online and
   become a community. *Transformed:* a neighborhood that used to meet at the market now
   also organizes on a group chat — faster, but with some neighbors left off it.
4. **Ask the belonging question (12 min).** For each room, ask: *who is welcomed, and who
   is left out?* Learn the gates: the **digital divide** (who has a device, a connection,
   the language), **moderation** (whose voice is protected, whose is silenced), and
   **harassment** (who is driven out). Name one gate you have seen.
5. **Map your own belonging (12 min).** Draw your own circles — offline and online. For
   each, write one thing that holds it together. Then mark one **inclusion** (who is
   welcomed) and one **exclusion** (who is left out) you can see.
6. **Close (5 min).** Remember: a community is not just the people in it — it is the door.
   You get to help decide, in every room you stand in, who the door lets in.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer two circles to map and a sentence starter ("This community welcomes
  ___, but it leaves out ___ because ___.").
- **Extension:** Analyze one online community's design and moderation and argue what change
  would make it more welcoming without losing what holds it together.

## Assessment

- **Formative (self):** Can the learner name one community technology created and one it
  transformed, identify one gate to belonging, and map one inclusion and one exclusion?
- **Portfolio artifact (summative for the unit):** The belonging-circles map, added to the
  unit portfolio as the "technology and belonging" analysis.

## Home connection

Ask an adult you trust: *what community do you belong to that a screen helps hold together —
and who gets left out of it?* Compare your maps.
