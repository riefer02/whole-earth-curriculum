# Lesson 3 — Movement Shapes Mood and Thinking

## Summary

Learners explore how **movement shapes mood and thinking**. They meet the mechanisms
behind the mood lift — endorphins, monoamines, and BDNF, among others (S-408) — and the
WHO picture of a balanced routine: **aerobic** activity plus **muscle-strengthening**
activity, with balance and flexibility across the lifespan (S-341). They feel the shift
in their own bodies and begin to see movement as **mental health**, not just fitness.

## Objectives

- Analyze how chronic stress, sleep, and movement shape mood, thinking, and long-term
  health — focusing on the movement side. _(D03.S1.10.01)_

## Connection

Think of a time you walked, ran, danced, swam, or played hard — and afterward felt
something lift: a steadier mood, a clearer head, an energy that had been missing. That
is not your imagination. Movement changes the chemistry of the brain and the body, and
it does it in ways that help mood and sharpens thinking. This matters everywhere — for
the child who walks far to school, the elder who gardens, the dancer in a festival, the
worker who cycles to a shift. The body was made to move, and when it does, the mind
moves with it.

## Materials

- Movement-and-brain diagram (movement → mood, focus, body)
- Writing journal

## Preparation

- Copy or draw the movement-and-brain diagram.
- Retrieval: from Lessons 1–2, recall the alarm/calm branches and sleep's night shift.
  Today we add the third force: **movement**, which is one of the body's most reliable
  ways to shift from alarm toward calm and to sharpen the mind.
- Plan a short, safe movement burst that fits your space and every body (a brisk walk,
  marching in place, a stretch-and-reach, a chair-based or adapted version).

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **movement is
evidence-backed medicine for the mind — it improves mood, reduces symptoms of
depression and anxiety, and supports focus — through mechanisms that include
endorphins, monoamines, and BDNF, among others** (S-408). Hold the mechanism claims
honestly and with hedging: endorphins are one piece, not the whole story; no single
chemical explains the mood lift (S-408). The WHO frames a balanced routine as aerobic
(endurance) activity plus muscle-strengthening activity, with balance and flexibility
also mattering across life (S-341). These are *descriptions*, never a prescription for
any particular body, and every body can move in ways that fit it.

Model once, then let learners practice (S-011). The **egalitarian** lens: the
opportunity to move is not shared equally — safe places to play, time, money, and
ability all gate access — so "move more" must never become a lecture aimed only at
those with the fewest choices. The **global** lens: movement traditions — dance, sport,
play, work — differ richly across cultures, and all of them count. The **ethics** lens:
movement is offered as joy and care, never as punishment or a way to "fix" a body; a
body is not a problem to be solved. The **technology & environment** lens: our
environments (walkable streets, green spaces, safe parks) shape whether movement is
easy or hard — that is a community question, not only a personal one. The
**social-emotional** lens: moving with others builds connection; invite, never force,
and keep the burst inclusive of every ability. Preview: Lesson 7 will turn this into a
training plan.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Lessons 1–2: the body has an alarm branch and a calm
   branch, and sleep is a deep calm. Notice that **movement** is a way to actively move
   your body from alarm toward calm — and it sharpens the mind too.
2. **Meet the mechanism (10 min).** Look at the diagram: a figure in motion, arrows to
   three outcomes — **better mood**, **sharper focus**, **stronger body**. Learn the
   "why": during movement the body and brain release chemicals — **endorphins** (the
   body's own opioids), **monoamines** (serotonin, dopamine, noradrenaline), and
   **BDNF** (a growth factor that supports brain cells) — that together lift mood and
   support learning (S-408). Remember: endorphins are one piece, not the whole story.
3. **Feel it (8 min).** Do the short movement burst — brisk walking or marching in
   place for two to three minutes, or a chair-based/adapted version that fits every
   body. Stop. Close your eyes for ten seconds. Notice: what changed in your energy,
   your breathing, your attention?
4. **Meet a balanced routine (10 min).** Learn the WHO picture: a balanced routine
   includes **aerobic** activity (endurance — walking, cycling, swimming, dancing) and
   **muscle-strengthening** activity (carrying, climbing, lifting, resistance), with
   balance and flexibility also mattering across the lifespan (S-341). Note that every
   one of these has versions that fit different bodies and abilities.
5. **Link it to your life (14 min).** In your journal, write: one movement you already
   enjoy or used to enjoy; what it does for your mood and focus; and one small way you
   could add a little more of it this week — in a form that fits your body and your
   life.
6. **Close (3 min).** Remember: movement is not about looking a certain way. It is a
   gift you give your own mind — a steadier mood and a clearer head, one step at a time.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer a one-box journal prompt ("when I move, I feel ___ and my mind
  feels ___") and model a seated movement option.
- **Extension:** Explain the endorphin/monoamine/BDNF mechanisms in your own words and
  state one honest limit — what the science *cannot* promise (no single chemical, no
  quick fix).

## Assessment

- **Formative (self):** Can the learner name one mechanism by which movement helps mood
  and one component of a balanced routine?
- **Portfolio artifact (summative for the unit):** The "movement and me" journal entry,
  added to the unit portfolio as the "movement shapes mood and thinking" reflection.

## Home connection

Ask an adult you trust: *what is one way you like to move, and how does it affect your
mood?* Then invite them to join you in the short movement burst this week.
