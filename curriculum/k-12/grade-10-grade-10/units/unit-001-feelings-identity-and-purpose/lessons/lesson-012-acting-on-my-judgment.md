# Lesson 12 — Acting on My Judgment

## Summary

Learners **turn their self-directed learning plan and help-seeking judgment into
action**. They take a first step, run a **progress check**, seek or withhold help **on
purpose**, and **gather the unit's artifacts into a portfolio** — closing the unit by
acting on their own judgment and seeing the whole arc: feeling, identity, and purpose as
one authored life. This completes D01.S3.10.01 and D01.S3.10.02.

## Objectives

- Direct their own learning by choosing goals, resources, and ways to check their own
  progress. _(D01.S3.10.01)_
- Balance independence with knowing when and how to seek help, and act on that judgment.
  _(D01.S3.10.02)_

## Connection

A plan is only a promise until the first step is taken. This lesson is the step. You
will do the thing you planned — even a small piece of it — then stop and look: *did it
work? what did the check show? do I keep going alone, or reach out?* That stop-and-look
moment, repeated, is what turns a learner into an *author*: not someone who follows a
path, but someone who walks, watches, and adjusts. And the work you gather along the way
is the proof — to yourself — that you have been writing your own story all along.

## Materials

- Unit portfolio checklist
- Action log (goal step, progress check, help sought or withheld, what I learned)
- Writing journal

## Preparation

- Copy or draw the checklist and action log.
- Retrieval: gather the unit's artifacts from Lessons 1–11 — the emotion-history map, the
  emotion-word chart, the comparison log, the empathy note, the contagion map, the
  identity map, the comparison chart, the labels worksheet, the considered position, the
  learning plan, and the help network. Today we *act* and *gather*.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **judgment is only real
when it becomes action — and the unit closes by acting (taking a step, checking progress,
seeking help on purpose) and by gathering the evidence into a portfolio.** This is the
unit's **summative** moment: a portfolio that shows growth through the learner's own
explanations and artifacts, not a score (philosophy §8; the scope's "no high-stakes
tests" commitment). The science-of-learning through-line: spaced retrieval — gathering
and re-reading one's own work across the unit — deepens learning (S-039).

Model the action-log entry once, then let learners practice (S-011). The **intellectual**
lens: this is metacognition closed into a loop — plan, act, check, adjust — now performed,
not just described. The **ethics** lens: acting on judgment includes honesty in the
check — reporting what really happened, not what you wished. The **egalitarian** lens: a
portfolio of your *own* work is the great equalizer — it measures growth, not rank, and
every learner's gathered work is worthy of celebration. The **global** lens: gathering
one's own work as evidence of learning is a practice with cousins everywhere — the
apprentice's finished piece, the healer's recorded cases, the storyteller's remembered
stories — all ways humans have long kept the proof of their becoming. The **technology &
environment** lens: a portfolio can live on paper or on screen; choose the medium that
serves *your* keeping and sharing, and notice that what we preserve — and what we let
go — is itself a judgment. The **social-emotional** lens: this is a moment of pride and
sometimes of uncertainty ("is this enough?"). The answer is yes: growth, not perfection,
is the measure, and every artifact here is evidence of a person authoring their own life.
Celebrate each learner, not by ranking, but by honoring the work.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** Look back over the unit: you traced the history under your
   feelings, named the words your culture gave you, saw through the mirror in your
   pocket, examined who told you who you are, wrote your considered position, and built
   your own learning plan. Today you *act* on it — and gather it all.
2. **Take the first step (15 min).** From your learning plan, do one real, small step
   *now* — read the first page, ask the first question, practice the first five minutes.
   If your step needs a person or place not here, write the exact step and the time you
   will take it.
3. **Run the check (12 min).** Use your action log. Record the step you took, then run
   the **progress check** you planned: what can you now do or show that you could not
   before? Be honest — a small real gain beats a large imagined one.
4. **Choose: alone or help (10 min).** Based on the check, make the judgment: *do I keep
   going alone, or seek help?* If help, write **who** and **what you will ask** (your
   three-part ask from Lesson 11). If alone, write the *next* step. Either way, you are
   acting on judgment — that is the skill.
5. **Gather the portfolio (10 min).** Use the checklist to assemble the unit's artifacts
   in order. On a cover page or first slide, write one sentence that threads them
   together: *this is the work of someone learning to author their own life.*
6. **Close (3 min).** Look at what you have gathered. This is not a pile of papers — it
   is the record of you, doing the work of becoming. You did that.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer a three-artifact checklist and a sentence starter for the thread
  ("This unit, I learned to ___ and ___.").
- **Extension:** Write the unit's thread in one paragraph — how *feeling*, *identity*,
  and *purpose* now connect for you — and name one thing you will carry into the next
  unit.

## Assessment

- **Summative (portfolio):** The assembled unit portfolio — all artifacts plus the action
  log and the one-sentence thread — as the unit's closing evidence of growth.
- **Formative (peer):** In a small circle, each learner shares one artifact and one next
  step, and receives one kind, useful comment.

## Home connection

Share your portfolio with a trusted adult — not for a grade, but for the telling. Tell
them the thread: what this unit taught you about being the author of your own life.
