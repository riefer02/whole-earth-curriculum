# Lesson 5 — When Feelings Go Viral

## Summary

Learners **evaluate how emotional contagion shapes emotional life**. They learn that
**feelings spread from person to person** — a smile, a panic, a burst of outrage — both
face to face and through feeds, which can carry a single mood to millions at once. They
trace a contagion chain, notice the moment a feeling "catches," and practice **keeping
their own mood** — choosing regulation over reaction (S-225).

## Objectives

- Evaluate how technology and media influence emotional life, such as emotional
  contagion. _(D01.S1.10.02)_

## Connection

Have you ever walked into a room and felt the mood change you before anyone said a word?
Or watched a thread of comments turn from curiosity to fury in minutes — and felt your
own pulse rise with it? That is **emotional contagion**: feelings passing from person to
person like a spark. It is how a crowd becomes brave, a team becomes hopeful, or a feed
becomes angry. It is not a flaw — it is a fact of being human. And once you can see it,
you can begin to choose it.

## Materials

- Contagion map (a small network diagram)
- Index cards (each with a mood or emotion)

## Preparation

- Copy or draw the contagion map and the mood cards.
- Retrieval: from Lessons 3–4, recall that feeds skew comparison and can flatten empathy.
  Today we ask how feelings themselves *spread* through people and platforms.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The ideas to land: **(1) emotions spread
between people — emotional contagion — through face, voice, body, and now through feeds
that carry a mood to many people at once; (2) contagion is neither good nor bad — it can
lift (courage, hope) or weigh (panic, outrage); (3) the skill is *noticing* the catch and
*choosing* — regulation over reaction.** The science is real but the framing must stay
honest: emotional contagion is well documented in face-to-face interaction (a widely
replicated phenomenon; see the emotion-regulation field, S-225), and online emotional
contagion is an active area of research with real but contested evidence (S-226, S-481) —
state it as "feelings *can* spread, and feeds *can* amplify the spread," not as settled
proof that a platform simply "controls" mood (philosophy §5, §11).

Model the skill once, then let learners practice (S-011). The **technology** lens: name
the mechanism — feeds rank high-engagement content, and strong emotion (outrage, delight,
fear) is highly engaging, so platforms tend to surface the most contagious moods
(S-481, S-482). The **global** lens: collective emotion is a real force in every society —
hope, grief, and fear rise and fall in groups (this lesson sets up D01.S1.11's "emotional
climate" next year); different cultures have different rituals for carrying and releasing
collective feeling. The **egalitarian** lens: when a mood sweeps a group, quieter or
different-feeling people can be steamrolled — noticing contagion is also noticing whom the
mood silences. The **ethics** lens: spreading a feeling is an act with consequences —
before amplifying a panic or an outrage, ask whether it is true and whether it helps; you
are responsible for the feeling you pass on. The **social-emotional** lens: learners may
have been caught in real viral panics or pile-ons; hold the topic gently, invite rather
than force sharing, and let "pass" stand.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** Think of a time a room's mood, or a thread's mood, changed *your*
   feeling. Notice how quickly it happened.
2. **Meet contagion (10 min).** Learn the word: **emotional contagion** is the way
   feelings spread from person to person — through face, voice, body, and now through
   screens. Watch a worked example on the contagion map: one person's worry moves to two
   friends, then to four, then to a whole room — each person catching it, not choosing
   it. Notice the difference between *catching* a feeling and *choosing* one.
3. **Trace a chain (15 min).** In a small group, use the mood cards. One person "holds"
   a mood; each next person names how it shifts them, tracing the spread. Then try it
   online: look at a short example comment thread and mark where the tone turns from
   neutral to hot. Where did it "catch"?
4. **Find the pause (12 min).** Ask: *where, in that chain, could someone have paused?*
   Learn that **regulation** — the ability to notice and steer a feeling (S-225) — is
   the tool that turns a caught feeling into a chosen one. Write one pause you could take
   next time: a breath, a question ("is this true?"), a step away from the screen.
5. **Draft (10 min).** Write or record two or three sentences: *feelings spread — I can
   choose what I catch and what I pass on. Here is my pause.*
6. **Close (3 min).** Remember: you are not a leaf in the wind of every passing mood.
   Noticing the catch is the first step to steering your own heart.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer a single-step trace and a starter ("When everyone got ___, I
  noticed I ___.").
- **Extension:** Distinguish emotional contagion from deliberate empathy (choosing to
  feel with someone) and explain when each is at work in a real online moment.

## Assessment

- **Formative (peer):** Can each learner trace one step of a contagion chain and name a
  pause point where someone could choose differently?
- **Portfolio artifact (summative for the unit):** The contagion map with the traced
  chain and the drafted pause reflection, added to the unit portfolio.

## Home connection

Ask someone at home: *have you ever caught a feeling from a room or a screen without
meaning to?* Share your pause — and ask what they do to keep their own mood their own.
