# Grade 3 Scope

## How to read this scope

Each `unit_id` in the frontmatter resolves to a unit file under `units/`.
`start_day`/`end_day` map the unit onto the 180-day school year; the list is a
continuous sequence from day 1 to day 180 with no gaps or overlaps. `domain` and
`pillar` name the primary domain(s) and pillar(s) each unit serves. `domain_weighting`
expresses the intended balance of instructional time across the ten domains, in
percent of the year. `lesson_count` counts the core lessons; the days between lessons
carry flex time, assessment, integration, and celebration. `assessment_plan`
describes how a child's growth is seen and recorded at this level.

## Year at a glance

The Grade 3 year is a spiral that deepens what Grade 2 began. It opens inward.
*Mixed Feelings, My Story & My Goals* builds on Grade 2's telling similar feelings
apart and noticing where a feeling lives in the body: now children describe how a
feeling changes over time and name when they feel more than one feeling at once, and
explain how the same situation can make different people feel differently — the first
real step of perspective-taking. They tell the story of their own life so far, in
order, with its important people and places, name the several groups that shape who
they are, set a simple goal and its first step, and practice managing a strong feeling
in a group without disrupting others. *My Body, My Senses & Movement* then reads the
body with new precision: how two senses work together to give a fuller picture, the
early signals of hunger, fullness, thirst, and tiredness and a helpful response to
each, movement sequences with rhythm and balance, and hand-eye coordination through
games of tossing, catching, and aiming.

*Caring, Perspective & Belonging* turns outward into the practice of care. Where Grade
2 learned to give encouragement and resolve a disagreement with words, Grade 3 learns
to take turns speaking and build on what a classmate said, to use "I" statements
without blame, to retell a story from another character's point of view and imagine
being someone else in a familiar place, to compare their own community with one far
away, to explain how rules and routines help a community work fairly, and to
contribute fairly to a group goal. The heart of the year is given to the two great
tools of school, now working with real independence. *Reading, Writing & Many
Languages* builds fluency and comprehension: reading grade-level texts with accuracy
and self-correction, finding the main idea and its supporting details, writing a
paragraph with a topic sentence, supporting details, and a closing, revising and
editing one's own work for spelling, punctuation, and clarity, and using simple
everyday phrases in another language — always in the learner's home language first,
with multilingualism held as wealth. *Numbers, Shapes, Patterns & Data* grows from
Grade 2's place value to 100 into place value to 1,000, multiplication and division
within 100 with their meaning, arithmetic patterns in tables and number grids,
two-step word problems using all four operations, the area and perimeter of
rectangles, sorting shapes by their shared attributes, measuring liquid volume and
mass with standard units, and drawing scaled picture and bar graphs — mathematics as
sense-making with real things, drawn from many traditions, for everyone.

From there the year looks out at the world. *Fair Tests & the Changing World* turns
wonder into a fair test: change one thing and keep the rest the same, record what the
data show, trace a food chain through producers, consumers, and decomposers, name the
parts of a plant and what each helps the plant do, describe the three states of matter
and how heating and cooling change them, feel how a push or pull changes motion and
what friction does, and explain how Earth's turning brings day and night, its orbit
the seasons, and wind, water, and ice the shaping of the land. *Making, Music &
Movement* gives the whole body and the imagination to pattern, rhythm, dance, and
design, and *Healthy, Safe & Strong* returns to the body with growing independence —
what a body needs to stay healthy and why each need matters, where food comes from and
how it reaches a table, first aid for minor hurts, and safe and unsafe touch, with the
correct names and the right to say no and tell an adult.

The final third steps into the wider human story. *People, Places & Long Ago* places
local events and landmarks on timelines, follows how a place changes over a long time
and what causes one change, compares how two peoples met basic needs for food,
shelter, and clothing, hears a story, song, or art form and what it shows a culture
values, traces why people move, and how rivers, mountains, and climate shaped where
people settled — and hears stories of people working together to change something
unfair. *Fairness & Our Shared Rules* weighs reasons for and against a choice, tells
needs from wants, notices when someone is left out and how to make it more fair,
proposes a rule with a reason, and practices talking through a conflict and repairing
harm rather than only punishing. *Tools, Media & Our Earth Home* meets simple machines
and how they make work easier, tells an advertisement from information and reads a
message's purpose and audience, traces a food chain and the way plants make their own
food from sunlight, and distinguishes resources that run out from those that come
back — with a first sense of what we can do. The year closes by gathering all of it
together in *All of Me, All of Us*, celebrating each whole person and the caring
community, and looking ahead to Grade 4.

Throughout, the four pillars are interwoven. Intellectual awareness (P3) carries the
most weight — reading and writing toward fluency and comprehension, multiplication and
division, and fair testing are the year's central work — but emotional and social
awareness (P1) and physical and somatic awareness (P2) remain strong, for a known,
cared-for, and capable body-and-self is still the ground all formal learning stands
on. Contextual and ecological awareness (P4) keeps growing: history moves from simple
timelines toward comparison and cause, civics toward reasons, rules, and repairing
harm, and ecology toward food chains, resources, and the first responsibilities of
stewardship. No high-stakes tests anywhere: a child's growth is seen through reading
aloud, writing, number work with real things, fair tests, and a portfolio of what they
make, say, and do.
