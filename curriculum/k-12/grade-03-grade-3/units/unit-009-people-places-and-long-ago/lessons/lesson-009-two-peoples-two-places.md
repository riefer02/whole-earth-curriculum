# Lesson 9 — Two Peoples, Two Places: A Comparison

## Summary

Learners bring two skills together into one **portfolio artifact**: a "two peoples, two
places" comparison that places the **Arctic** and the **desert** side by side and
explains how each **environment shaped** how people met their needs and where they
**settled**. It becomes the unit's recorded "two peoples, two places" comparison —
comparing with respect, never ranking.

## Objectives

- Compare how two peoples met basic needs for food, shelter, and clothing and explain
  how their environment shaped those choices. _(D08.S2.03.01)_
- Explain how rivers, mountains, or climate shaped where people chose to settle and
  live. _(D08.S3.03.02)_

## Connection

You have learned two big things: how **two peoples** meet the same needs in different
ways, and how the **land and climate** shape where and how people live. Today you will
do both at once, on one page — an **Arctic people** and a **desert people**, side by
side, with the place, food, shelter, and clothing of each, and a sentence for each that
says *how the place shaped it*. That page is a small, careful history of two peoples,
told with respect.

## Materials

- A "two peoples, two places" comparison sheet
- Crayons, pencils, paste

## Preparation

- Prepare the comparison sheet: two columns (Arctic people, desert people) and four
  rows (place, food, shelter, clothing), plus a line for "how the place shaped it" for
  each.
- Revisit the earlier lessons' ideas — the two peoples' needs (Lesson 3) and how the
  land shapes settlement (Lesson 6) — so the retrieval is fresh.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). **This is a retrieval-and-integration
lesson and a portfolio artifact — practice, not a test.** Learners are recalling two
skills they already practiced (comparing two peoples' needs; explaining how the land
shaped settlement) and combining them — spaced retrieval across the unit (Dunlosky et
al., 2013). Model one full column first (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006): fill the
"Arctic people" column and say the "shaped by" sentence aloud — "The frozen sea and the
cold shaped it: people fish and wear warm parkas and build snug homes." Then guide the
desert column. **Honor both peoples, and never rank:** each place asks something of its
people, and each people answers with deep knowledge. A learner may choose a different
pair of peoples or places if that is more meaningful to them — the structure matters,
not the specific example. Honor every body: a learner who is blind or has low vision
lays objects in two rows by touch and says or signs the comparison; a learner who does
not use a speaking voice draws or points. The artifact is evidence of growth, never a
ranking.

## Procedure

1. **Gather and remember (10 min).** You know two skills. Say them together:
   - **Two peoples** meet the same needs — food, shelter, clothing — and the place
     shapes how.
   - The **land and climate** shape where people settle and live.
2. **Watch one example (10 min).** Look at the model sheet. The "Arctic people" column
   is filled: place — cold and snowy; food — fish; shelter — a snow house; clothing — a
   warm parka. The "shaped by" line reads: "The cold and the frozen sea shaped it — warm
   clothes, snug homes, and food from the sea."
3. **Build your comparison (20 min).** On your sheet, fill both columns — Arctic people
   and desert people — for place, food, shelter, and clothing. For each column, write or
   tell the "shaped by" sentence.
4. **Explain it (10 min).** Point to each column and tell the comparison: "The Arctic
   is cold, so… the desert is hot and dry, so…" You may speak, sign, act, or show your
   sheet.
5. **Close (5 min).** You just told the story of two peoples, side by side, with
   respect. Comparing carefully is how we learn from one another.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Fill just the "place" and "food" rows for both peoples with heavy
  support, and say one "shaped by" sentence together.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Lay objects in two rows by touch (a warm wrap, a light
  cloth, a cup, a leaf) and say the comparison aloud while a partner records it.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Draw the cells and point to the "shaped by" line, or act
  each people's food, shelter, and clothing.
- **Support (Deaf/HH):** The sheet is visual — build it with pictures, and sign "cold,"
  "hot," "food," "shelter," and "clothing" as you go.
- **Support (motor-difference):** Use a large, stable sheet and easy-grip crayons; a
  partner places the drawings while the learner points, signs, or eye-gazes.
- **Extension:** Add a third people or place, and write a "shaped by" sentence that
  names *two* ways the environment shaped their lives.

## Assessment

- **Formative (portfolio):** Does the comparison show both peoples across place, food,
  shelter, and clothing, with at least one "shaped by" sentence that names how the
  environment shaped a choice?
- **Formative (oral/performance):** Does the learner explain the comparison in their own
  words, without ranking either people or place as better?

## Home connection

Take your comparison sheet home and share it. Ask someone: which two peoples or places
would *you* put side by side, and how did each place shape how they lived?
