# Lesson 5 — Why People Move

## Summary

Learners name the reasons people move from one place to another — **family**, **work**,
**safety**, and **environment** — and tell one story that shows a move and its reason.
Grade 2 told a story of moving and drew a place; Grade 3 names the *reasons* behind the
moving, holding every move as a real, worthy part of the long human story — people have
always moved, and moving is never a failure.

## Objectives

- Describe reasons people move from one place to another, such as family, work, safety,
  or environment, using a story or example. _(D08.S3.03.01)_

## Connection

People have always moved. A family moves to be near relatives. A parent moves for a new
job. A family moves to be safe from a flood or a fire. A herding family moves with the
seasons, following grass and water. Each move has a **reason**, and each reason is real.
Naming the reason — family, work, safety, or environment — is how we understand the
human story of moving, from long ago to today.

## Materials

- "Why people move" picture cards (four reason cards plus a "a family's move" story)
- A "reasons to move" four-part chart
- Pencils and crayons

## Preparation

- Prepare the four reason cards: **family** (joining relatives), **work** (a new job or
  seasonal work), **safety** (leaving a place that became unsafe), **environment**
  (drought, flood, or following pasture and water).
- Prepare a gentle story in pictures: a dry season arrives, a herding family packs,
  they journey, and they find a new home with grass and water — a move for
  **environment**, told warmly and blame-free.
- Prepare the four-part chart.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). **Hold every move with dignity, never
pity.** Moving is one of the oldest and most ordinary things people do — for family,
work, safety, or the environment (see Resources). Many families in the room may have
moved, or have relatives who moved; frame moving as a normal, strong thing people do,
not as loss or lack. **Keep the safety example gentle and age-appropriate:** "a place
became unsafe — a flood, a fire, a danger — so the family moved to be safe." Do not
dwell on frightening detail; the lesson's power is in naming reasons and honoring
journeys. **Model one full example first** (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006): tell the
herding-family story and think aloud — "The grass dried up and the water shrank, so
they moved to where the grass and water were. The reason was the **environment**." Then
guide the other three reasons. Distinguish evidence from values: *that* people move for
these reasons is a factual, cross-cultural claim (see Resources); *that* moving is
worthy of respect is a value we hold together. Honor every body: describe the cards in
words for a learner who cannot see them; a learner who does not use a speaking voice
points or acts. A learner may tell their own family's move — invite, never require.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of a time you or someone you know moved, or imagine moving.
   What might make a family pack up and go to a new place?
2. **Meet the four reasons (10 min).** Say them together:
   - **Family** — to be near relatives.
   - **Work** — a new job, or seasonal work.
   - **Safety** — a place became unsafe, so the family moved to be safe.
   - **Environment** — drought, flood, or following grass and water.
3. **Hear one story (10 min).** Listen to the story of a herding family. The dry season
   came, the grass dried, and the water shrank. So they packed and journeyed to a place
   with grass and water, and made a new home there. Which reason is this? **Environment.**
4. **Sort the reasons (15 min).** Take the reason cards. For each, tell a short example:
   "A family moved to live near a grandmother — that is **family**." Draw or write one
   example of each reason on your chart.
5. **Tell your own story (10 min).** Make up or retell a short "why people move" story
   — yours, a friend's, or an imagined one. Name the reason. You may speak, act, draw,
   or sign.
6. **Close (5 min).** Moving is one of the oldest things people do. Every move has a
   reason, and every reason is real.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Meet only two reasons (family and environment) with heavy picture
  support, and tell one short story together.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Hear the story told clearly and name the reason
  aloud; act out packing and arriving.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Sort the reason cards and point to the reason for each
  story beat instead of speaking.
- **Support (Deaf/HH):** The cards are visual — read them and sign "family," "work,"
  "safety," and "environment" as you place each one.
- **Support (motor-difference):** Use larger cards and a stable chart; direct a partner
  to place each card while you point, sign, or eye-gaze.
- **Extension:** Tell a story with *two* reasons for one move (work *and* family), and
  say which reason you think came first.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name at least three of the four reasons
  people move?
- **Formative (portfolio/oral):** Does the learner tell or draw one story of a move and
  name its reason, in their own words, with dignity and without pity?

## Home connection

Ask someone at home if your family — or a family they know — ever moved, and why. Tell
them which of the four reasons it was: family, work, safety, or environment.
