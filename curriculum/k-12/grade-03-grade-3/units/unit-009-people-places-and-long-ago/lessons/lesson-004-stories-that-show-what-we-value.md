# Lesson 4 — Stories, Songs, and Art That Show What People Value

## Summary

Learners meet a **story**, a **song**, and an **art form** from different cultures and
explain what each shows about what that culture **values**. They meet **kente cloth** of
the Akan peoples of Ghana, whose patterns carry meanings like wisdom and unity; a
**Dreaming story** and dot painting of Aboriginal Australia, which carry the value of
caring for Country and family; and a **song** — a treasure that passes on joy and
memory. Every culture keeps treasures like these, and all are living and real.

## Objectives

- Describe a story, song, or art form from a culture and explain what it shows about
  what that culture values. _(D08.S2.03.02)_

## Connection

Think of something your family or people keeps — a song, a recipe, a way of making
something. It is not just a thing; it carries a meaning, something your people care
about. Every culture has such treasures: a woven cloth whose patterns mean wisdom, a
painting whose dots tell a story of caring for the land, a song that passes joy from
elder to child. Looking closely at a treasure — and naming what it says its people
**value** — is how we come to know and respect one another.

## Materials

- Picture cards of three cultural treasures (kente cloth, a dot painting, a drum and
  song notes)
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Prepare the cards and short tellings. **Kente cloth (Akan peoples, Ghana):** the
  colors and patterns each carry a meaning; kente is worn at important moments and is
  held as a treasure of wisdom, unity, and honor. **Dreaming story and dot painting
  (Aboriginal Australia):** art and story pass on the value of caring for Country and
  for family and kinship. **A song:** songs pass on joy, memory, and belonging. (See
  Resources.)
- Be ready to clap a simple rhythm or hum a short tune as the "song" example.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). **Honor every treasure as living, not
"folk art" or "ancient."** Kente is woven today; Aboriginal artists paint today; songs
are sung today. Never call any of it "simple" or "primitive" — each is deep, careful
knowledge passed from one generation to the next. **The skill is reading meaning:** the
lesson's job is to move from "this is pretty" to "this shows what a people care about"
— a pattern that means wisdom, a story that teaches caring for the land, a song that
keeps memory alive. Model one full example first (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006):
"Kente's patterns are not random; each one has a name and a meaning, like wisdom or
unity. So kente shows that its people value wisdom and togetherness." Then guide the
second treasure. **Use accurate names and hedges** (see Resources): say "the Akan
peoples of Ghana," "Aboriginal peoples of Australia," and note that there are many
Aboriginal nations, each with its own stories and art. Honor every body: describe the
cards in words for a learner who cannot see them; a learner who does not use a
speaking voice points or acts the value. A learner may also name a treasure from their
own culture — every culture's treasures belong in this lesson.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of a song, story, or thing your family keeps. What do you
   think it says about what your family cares about?
2. **Meet a treasure: kente cloth (15 min).** Look at the woven cloth. Its **patterns**
   are not random — each one has a name and a meaning: wisdom, unity, honor. People
   wear kente at important times to carry those meanings with them. So kente shows that
   its people **value wisdom and togetherness**.
3. **Meet a second treasure: a Dreaming story and dot painting (10 min).** The dots
   tell a **story** — of how the land was shaped and of the people's duty to care for
   **Country** and for family. So the painting and story show that this culture
   **values caring for the land and for one another**.
4. **Meet a third treasure: a song (10 min).** Listen or clap a rhythm. Songs pass on
   joy, memory, and belonging from elder to child. A song shows a culture **values
   remembering and belonging together**.
5. **Draw and tell (10 min).** Choose one treasure. Draw it and write or tell one
   sentence: "This shows that the culture values…" You may choose a treasure from your
   own culture too.
6. **Close (5 min).** A story, a song, an art form — each is a window into what a people
   hold dear. Every culture's treasures are real, and all are worthy of respect.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Meet only kente, and finish one sentence together — "Kente shows its
  people value…" — with picture support.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Feel a woven sample or a raised dot pattern, hear the
  story and song clearly, and describe the value aloud.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Point to the treasure and to a "value" picture (a heart,
  a circle of people, a tree) instead of speaking.
- **Support (Deaf/HH):** The cards are visual — read the patterns with your eyes, and
  sign "value," "story," "song," and "art" as you go.
- **Support (motor-difference):** Direct a partner to point to the treasure and the
  value while you sign or eye-gaze.
- **Extension:** Name a second treasure — from another culture or your own — and tell
  one value it shows, and who taught it to whom.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name one story, song, or art form and
  the culture it comes from?
- **Formative (oral/performance):** Does the learner explain, in a finished sentence,
  one value the treasure shows about its culture — without calling any culture's
  treasure better or simpler?

## Resources

- Smithsonian National Museum of African Art, "Kente cloth" — describes kente cloth of
  the Akan peoples of Ghana and Côte d'Ivoire, noting that its patterns and colors
  carry meanings and that it is worn on important occasions.
  https://africa.si.edu/
- AIATSIS (Australian Institute of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Studies) —
  notes that Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander art carries knowledge, law, and
  stories, including the value of caring for Country, and that there are many distinct
  nations, each with its own traditions.
  https://aiatsis.gov.au/

## Home connection

Ask someone at home about a song, story, or thing your family keeps. What does it mean,
and what does it show your family values? Who taught it to whom?
