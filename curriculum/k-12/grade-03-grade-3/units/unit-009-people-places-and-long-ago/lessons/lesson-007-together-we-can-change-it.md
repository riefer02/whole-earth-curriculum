# Lesson 7 — Working Together to Change Something Unfair

## Summary

Learners hear the gentle, hopeful story of the **1930 Salt March**, when Mohandas
Gandhi and thousands of ordinary people walked peacefully together to the sea to make
their own salt and change an unfair rule. They describe the **steps** — notice the
unfairness, **work together**, act **peacefully**, and **change it** — a first,
age-appropriate story of collective, nonviolent change, ending in hope, never blame.

## Objectives

- Describe a story, real or told, of people working together to change something
  unfair, and the steps they took. _(D08.S4.03.01)_

## Connection

Sometimes people notice something that is not right — an unfair rule, someone being
left out — and instead of looking away, they **work together** to change it. One such
story comes from India in 1930: there was an unfair rule that said people had to buy
salt only from certain sellers and pay a heavy tax, and they were not allowed to gather
salt from the sea themselves. Thousands of ordinary people decided to change it — by
walking together, peacefully, to the sea to make their own salt.

## Materials

- A picture set of the Salt March story (notice, walk together, make salt)
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Prepare the story accurately and gently (see Resources): In 1930, Mohandas Gandhi led
  a 240-mile (about 385-km) march to the sea at Dandi, India, to protest an unfair salt
  law; he and thousands of others peacefully gathered and made their own salt in
  defiance of the rule, part of the larger movement for India's independence. Keep the
  telling warm and nonviolent — no frightening content.
- Prepare or draw the picture cards (an unfair rule noticed, a long line of people
  walking, making salt at the shore).

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). **Tell the story gently and hopefully.**
The Salt March is real and well-documented (see Resources), and its shape is right for
this age: notice an unfair rule, gather, walk peacefully, act, and change it — without
fighting. **Keep it nonviolent and blame-free:** do not dwell on punishment or
suffering; the lesson's power is in ordinary people, working together, and the hope of
change. **The "steps" are the learning:** notice → work together → act peacefully →
change it. Model the steps once (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006), then let learners
retell them. Distinguish evidence from values: *that* the march happened and the rule
was unfair is fact (see Resources); *that* changing unfairness together and peacefully
is good is a value we hold together. Offer Wangari Maathai (Grade 2 retrieval) and the
Montgomery bus boycott as other examples of the same shape, so learners see this
pattern across many peoples. **No child is ever the problem, and no one must share a
personal unfairness.** Honor every body: a learner who cannot see the cards hears the
story told clearly; a learner who does not use a speaking voice acts or points.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Have you ever seen something unfair and wished it could change?
   What would it take to change it?
2. **Hear the story (15 min).** Listen. Long ago in India, there was an unfair rule
   about salt: people had to buy it from certain sellers and pay a heavy tax, and they
   could not gather salt from the sea themselves. Gandhi and thousands of ordinary
   people **noticed** this was unfair. They **walked together**, peacefully, a very long
   way to the sea. At the shore, they gently **made their own salt** from the sea water,
   showing the rule was unfair. Many people joined, and over time the unfair rule was
   changed.
3. **Name the steps (10 min).** Say them in order, pointing to the cards: **notice**
   the unfair rule; **work together**; **act peacefully** (walk, make salt); **change
   it** — and hope grows.
4. **Draw and tell (10 min).** Draw the steps — or one step — and write or tell one
   sentence about why it mattered.
5. **Close (5 min).** Anyone can help change something unfair — by noticing, and by
   working together, peacefully. That is how change begins.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Tell just three beats (notice, walk together, change) and ask one
  question: "What did they do to change it?"
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Hear the story told clearly and describe the steps
  aloud; feel a bowl of water and salt as the story's image.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Act out noticing, walking, and making salt, and point to
  the story cards in order.
- **Support (Deaf/HH):** Tell the story with the picture sequence and signs — the
  pictures carry it; sign "unfair," "together," and "change" as you go.
- **Support (motor-difference):** Direct a partner to act or point while you sign or
  eye-gaze; use a large, stable picture set.
- **Extension:** Think of one small unfair thing near you and one peaceful first step
  someone could take — tell why it might help.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner retell at least three steps the people
  took (notice, work together, act peacefully, change it)?
- **Formative (portfolio/oral):** Does the learner say why it mattered, in their own
  words, with hope rather than blame?

## Home connection

At home, tell someone the story of the Salt March and the steps the people took. Ask
them: what is one small unfair thing, and one peaceful first step someone could take
together to change it?
