# Lesson 1 — Placing Our Local Landmarks on a Timeline

## Summary

Learners build a **timeline** — a line that orders events from the past toward today —
and place three or four **local landmarks** on it in order. The new skill this year is
the *why*: learners name the **clue** (evidence) that tells them the order is correct —
an old photo, an elder's memory, a dated plaque, a story. This is the first real
historian's question at Grade 3: not just *what came first*, but *how do we know?*

## Objectives

- Place local events or landmarks on a timeline in order and explain how they know the
  order is correct. _(D08.S1.03.01)_

## Connection

You already know a day has an order — wake, eat, play, rest — and that a timeline lines
up bigger events from before today. Your town or village has its own order of
**landmarks**: a tree someone planted, a bridge that was built, a school that opened.
Each one carries a clue about when it happened — a photo, a date carved on a plaque, a
grandparent's memory. Placing them in order *and naming the clue* is how people begin
to tell the true story of a place.

## Materials

- A long timeline strip (one per learner)
- A set of "local landmarks" cards for the worked example, each with a clue card
- Pencils and crayons

## Preparation

- Draw or gather simple landmark cards: a young tree being planted, a bridge being
  built, a school opening. Give each a clue card: an old photo (the tree was small and
  new), an elder's memory ("I remember crossing the river on a boat before the
  bridge"), a dated plaque ("School, built 1987").
- Give each learner a timeline strip with a "long ago" end and a "now" end.
- Decide whether the line reads left-to-right or top-to-bottom, and keep it the same
  for everyone.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). **Ordering is a foundational skill, so
teach it with a worked example first** (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006): model
placing three landmarks on the line, and for each one say the clue — "the photo shows
the tree when it was small, so the tree was planted first; the plaque says the school
was built in 1987, so it came later." Then guide the same two moves for a second set
before learners work independently. **The evidence is the point of this grade:** Grade
2 ordered events; Grade 3 adds "how do you know?" — a photo, a date, a memory, a
building style. Keep landmarks local, real, and gentle (a tree, a bridge, a school, a
market, a well), never frightening or private. Honor every body and every family
shape: a learner who is blind or has low vision sequences real objects by touch and
says or signs the order and the clue; a learner who does not use a speaking voice
points or acts. Every place's order of landmarks is real and worthy — a town, a
village, a farm, or a settlement all have their own. Remember that different peoples
mark time in different ways (many calendars, many ways of naming years) — a timeline
is one tool among many, not the only truth about time.

## Procedure

1. **Gather and remember (5 min).** Think of one thing in your town or village that has
   been there a long time, and one thing that is newer. Which came first? How do you
   know?
2. **Meet the timeline and the clue (10 min).** Look at the worked example: a line with
   a "long ago" end and a "now" end. Three landmarks — a tree planted, a bridge built,
   a school opened. For each one, name its **clue**: the old photo shows the tree when
   it was small; the elder remembers crossing by boat before the bridge; the plaque
   reads "built 1987."
3. **Guided practice (10 min).** Mix the cards. Together, put them back in order, and
   for each say the clue that proves it: "The tree came first because… the school came
   after the bridge because…"
4. **Build your own timeline (15 min).** Choose three or four real landmarks from your
   place. Draw or show each one on its spot along your line, from "long ago" to "now,"
   and write or draw the **clue** that tells you the order.
5. **Explain your order and your clue (5 min).** Point to each landmark and say, "This
   came first because…" and "I know because…" You may point, act, draw, or sign.
6. **Close (5 min).** You just did what historians do: you ordered landmarks *and*
   explained how you know. A timeline with evidence is a true story of time.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use only three landmarks, and place them together with clear gestures
  toward each clue.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Sequence real objects by touch and say or sign the
  order and the clue aloud; a partner reads the labels.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Order picture cards or objects and point to the clue
  cards instead of speaking.
- **Support (Deaf/HH):** The cards are already pictures — read the order with your
  eyes and hands, and sign "before," "after," and "clue" as you place each card.
- **Support (motor-difference):** Use a large, stable line and bigger cards; point or
  eye-gaze to each spot while a partner moves the card.
- **Extension:** Add a fifth landmark and name *two* different kinds of clue (a memory
  and a date), and tell which is more trustworthy for that landmark and why.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner place three or four local landmarks on
  the timeline in a sensible order, from furthest back to most recent?
- **Formative (oral/performance):** Does the learner name a **clue** (photo, memory,
  date, story) that shows how they know the order — by speaking, signing, pointing, or
  acting?

## Home connection

At home, ask someone to tell you about two or three landmarks in your place and *how
they know* which is older — a photo, a memory, a date. Put them in order together and
say the clue for each.
