# Lesson 2 — How Our Place Changed Over Time

## Summary

Learners follow one place — a **river**, a **market**, or a **school** — across a long
time, and name both what **changed** and what **caused** one of those changes. Grade 2
noticed that things change and stay the same; Grade 3 asks the next, harder question:
*why?* The answer is a **cause** — a bridge being built, a road arriving, more people
coming to trade — and finding one cause is how historians explain the past.

## Objectives

- Describe how a place or community changed over a long time, such as a river, market,
  or school, and what caused one of those changes. _(D08.S1.03.02)_

## Connection

Think of a place you know well — a river, a market, a road, or your school. It was not
always the way it is now. Once the river had no bridge; once the market was only a few
stalls; once the school was one small room. Something made it change — a bridge was
built, a road arrived, more people came. Noticing the change is the first step; finding
the **cause** is the second. That is how the story of any place is told.

## Materials

- "How a place changed" picture cards (river, market, school — three times each)
- A "then &#8594; now &#8594; why" chart
- Pencils and crayons

## Preparation

- Prepare the three picture sets. River: a quiet crossing, a bridge being built, a
  busy port. Market: a few stalls, a growing market, a big market. School: one room,
  a bigger school, a large school.
- Prepare the "then → now → why" chart, and be ready to model the cause aloud: "The
  bridge was built, so more people could cross, so the little market grew."

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). **Cause-and-effect is a new and harder
skill, so model it step-by-step before independent work** (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark,
2006): hold up the three river cards, and think aloud — "Long ago, a quiet crossing.
Then, a bridge was built. Now, a busy port. The **cause** of the change was the
bridge: it let more people and goods cross, so the place grew." Then guide the same
move for the market or school before learners choose their own. **Keep causes real,
gentle, and non-judgmental:** do not say the old way was worse. A quiet crossing, a few
stalls, or a one-room school are not "backward" — they are earlier chapters of the same
story, and many places still live in those chapters today. Many great cities and
markets grew at river crossings and ports (see Resources); use this as a true, global
pattern, not a story of one region. Honor every body: describe the cards in words for
a learner who cannot see them; a learner who does not use a speaking voice points or
acts the three times and the cause. A learner's own place may be the best example of
all — invite, never require, its telling.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of a place you know. What is one way it is different from
   how it was long ago? What do you think made it change?
2. **Meet "change" and "cause" (10 min).** **Change** is the difference between then
   and now. A **cause** is the reason for the change. Look at the river: long ago, a
   quiet crossing; then, a bridge was built; now, a busy port. Say it together: "The
   **cause** was the bridge — it let more people cross, so the place grew."
3. **Guided practice (10 min).** Take the market cards. Together, place them in order,
   then name the change and the cause: "The market grew because a road brought more
   people to buy and sell."
4. **Choose your own place (15 min).** Pick a river, a market, or a school. On your
   chart, draw it **then**, draw it **now**, and write or tell one **cause** of the
   change.
5. **Share (5 min).** Tell a partner: "Long ago it was… now it is… one cause was…"
   Point, act, draw, or sign.
6. **Close (5 min).** Places change for reasons. Finding a cause — and telling it — is
   how we explain the past, not just remember it.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use just one place (the river) and one change and cause, with heavy
  picture support.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Hear the three times described clearly and name the
  cause aloud; feel objects (a stone, a stick bridge, pebbles) to mark before, cause,
  and after.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Place the three cards in order and point to the "why"
  spot, or act the change and the cause.
- **Support (Deaf/HH):** The picture cards are visual — order them and sign "then,"
  "now," and "cause" as you go.
- **Support (motor-difference):** Use larger cards and a stable chart; direct a partner
  to place each card while you point, sign, or eye-gaze.
- **Extension:** Name *two* causes for one change, and tell which one you think mattered
  more and why.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner describe one place at two times and name
  at least one change?
- **Formative (oral/performance):** Does the learner name a **cause** — a reason the
  change happened — in their own words, without calling the old way worse?

## Home connection

Ask someone at home how one place in your area has changed over the years, and what
made it change. Tell them one change and one cause you found.
