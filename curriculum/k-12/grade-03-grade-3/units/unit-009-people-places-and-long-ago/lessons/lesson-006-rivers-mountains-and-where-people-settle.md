# Lesson 6 — Rivers, Mountains, and Where People Settle

## Summary

Learners explain how **rivers**, **mountains**, and **climate** shaped where people
chose to **settle** and live: rivers give water, food, and a way to travel; mountains
give terraced slopes to farm; a dry climate draws people to **oases** and valleys where
water is. Grade 2 met environments and adaptations; Grade 3 asks the next question —
*why here?* — and finds the answer in the land itself.

## Objectives

- Explain how rivers, mountains, or climate shaped where people chose to settle and
  live. _(D08.S3.03.02)_

## Connection

Why do people live *here* and not *there*? Look at the land for the answer. People
settle beside a **river** because it gives water to drink, fish to eat, and a road to
travel on. People settle on **mountain** slopes, cutting flat steps called terraces to
grow food. And where the **climate** is dry, people settle beside an **oasis** or a
valley where water is. The land shapes where we live — and people have read the land
with care for thousands of years.

## Materials

- "Land shapes where we live" picture cards (riverside village, mountain terraces,
  desert oasis) with matching reason cards
- A three-part chart (river, mountain, climate)
- Pencils and crayons

## Preparation

- Prepare the cards. **Rivers:** a village beside a river — reason: water, travel, and
  good soil. **Mountains:** terraces cut in steps on a slope — reason: flat steps to
  grow food on steep land. **Climate:** homes beside an oasis in the desert — reason:
  water where it is dry. (See Resources.)
- Prepare the three-part chart, and be ready to model: "People settle by the river
  because it gives water and a way to travel."

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). **The skill is explaining "why here," so
model one full example first** (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006): "Look at the river.
People settle beside it because it gives water to drink, fish to eat, and a road to
travel. The river *shaped* where the settlement is." Then guide the mountain and
climate examples. **Keep it global and true:** many of the world's great cities grew on
rivers, and people have terraced mountain slopes and settled oases across every
continent (see Resources) — this is a pattern of all peoples, not one region. **Never
rank:** a mountain terrace farm is not "worse" than a river plain; each is a wise,
fitting answer to its land. Honor every body: describe the cards in words for a learner
who cannot see them; a learner who does not use a speaking voice points or acts. Where
possible, connect to the learner's own place — the river, slope, or climate near home is
the truest example of all.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Look around your own place in your mind. Is there a river, a
   hill, a mountain, a dry or rainy season? How do you think it shaped where people
   built their homes?
2. **Meet the land (10 min).** A **river** gives water, food, and a way to travel. A
   **mountain** is high and steep, but its slopes can be farmed in steps. **Climate** is
   the weather of a place over time — hot or cold, wet or dry.
3. **See how each shapes settlement (15 min).** Match each settlement to its reason:
   - **Rivers:** people settle beside the river — water, travel, and good soil to grow.
   - **Mountains:** people cut **terraces** — flat steps — to grow food on the slopes.
   - **Climate:** where it is dry, people settle near an **oasis** or a valley with
     water.
4. **Explain one "why here" (10 min).** Choose one. Say it in your own words: "People
   settled here because the river gives…" or "People farmed the mountain by cutting…"
   Point, act, draw, or sign.
5. **Close (5 min).** The land shapes where we live, and people have read the land with
   knowledge for thousands of years. Reading the land is a human gift.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Meet only rivers, and finish one sentence together — "People settle by
  rivers because…" — with picture support.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Feel objects — a cup of water (river), a raised step
  (terrace), shade (oasis) — and describe why each place draws people.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Match the settlement cards to the reason cards and point
  to the "why here" spot instead of speaking.
- **Support (Deaf/HH):** The cards are visual — match them and sign "river," "mountain,"
  "water," and "settle" as you go.
- **Support (motor-difference):** Use larger cards and a stable chart; direct a partner
  to place each card while you point, sign, or eye-gaze.
- **Extension:** Choose a fourth land shape — a coast, a forest, a grassland — and tell
  one way it shaped where people settled.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner match each land shape — river, mountain,
  climate — with one way it shaped settlement?
- **Formative (oral/performance):** Does the learner explain, for one place, *why* the
  land shaped where people settled — in their own words, without ranking any settlement
  as better?

## Resources

- National Geographic Education, "River" — describes how rivers provide water, food,
  and transport, and notes that river deltas and floodplains (valleys) are highly
  fertile zones that attract settlement, so many towns and cities grew beside rivers.
  https://education.nationalgeographic.org/resource/river/
- International Potato Center (CIP), "Potato" — notes the potato was domesticated in
  the Andes, where people built terraces to farm the steep mountain slopes, supporting
  the mountain-terrace example.
  https://cipotato.org/potato/

## Home connection

Look at your own place with a grown-up: is there a river, a slope, a dry or rainy
season? Ask where people first settled nearby and why — and tell them how you think the
land shaped that choice.
