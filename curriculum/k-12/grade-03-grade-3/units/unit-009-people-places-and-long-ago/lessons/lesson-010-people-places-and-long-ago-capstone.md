# Lesson 10 — Our People, Places & Long Ago Story

## Summary

The capstone: learners gather the whole unit into one **"my story" page** — a timeline
of landmarks with their clues, a place that changed and one cause, two peoples side by
side, a treasure that shows values, why people move, how the land shapes settlement,
and working together for a say. It becomes the unit's portfolio artifact and a first
telling of each learner's own place in the long, many-voiced human story.

## Objectives

- Place local events or landmarks on a timeline in order and explain how they know the
  order is correct. _(D08.S1.03.01)_
- Describe how a place or community changed over a long time and what caused one of
  those changes. _(D08.S1.03.02)_
- Compare how two peoples met basic needs for food, shelter, and clothing and explain
  how their environment shaped those choices. _(D08.S2.03.01)_
- Describe a story, song, or art form from a culture and explain what it shows about
  what that culture values. _(D08.S2.03.02)_
- Describe reasons people move from one place to another, using a story or example.
  _(D08.S3.03.01)_
- Explain how rivers, mountains, or climate shaped where people chose to settle and
  live. _(D08.S3.03.02)_
- Describe a story of people working together to change something unfair, and the steps
  they took. _(D08.S4.03.01)_
- Explain what it means to have power or a say in a group, and what can happen when some
  people are left out. _(D08.S4.03.02)_

## Connection

All this time you have been gathering pieces of a bigger picture: how time unfolds on a
timeline of landmarks, how places change and what causes it, how two peoples meet the
same needs in different places, what a story or song or art form shows a culture
values, why people move, how the land shapes where people settle, and how working
together can change something unfair and give everyone a say. Today you will put the
pieces together into one story — *your* story of people, places, and long ago. Every
learner's story is different, and every one belongs.

## Materials

- A "my story" page divided into sections (my timeline, a place that changed, two
  peoples, a treasure that shows values, why people move, the land shapes us, working
  together for a say)
- Crayons, pencils, paste

## Preparation

- Prepare the "my story" page template, or plan to divide a sheet into five to seven
  labeled sections.
- Gather learners' earlier work from the unit (timelines, then-now-why charts, two
  peoples comparisons, value drawings, why-people-move charts, land-and-settlement
  charts, the Salt March story, the "say for all" circles) to revisit.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). **This is the unit's portfolio artifact
and summative moment — but it is a celebration, not a test.** Retrieval practice across
the whole unit is the engine: learners are recalling and *using* everything they
learned, spaced across the unit (Dunlosky et al., 2013). The page has five to seven
sections, one per idea; a learner may complete all, or tell some sections orally, by
acting, or with a scribe. Honor every body and every family shape: no one must disclose
their own timeline events, their own place, or a private unfairness — sections can be
filled with *any* example met this unit. "No correct family," "no correct place," "no
child is ever the problem." Gather the pages into a class mural so the whole becomes a
shared, many-voiced story — history told from many perspectives, never a single one.

## Procedure

1. **Gather and remember (10 min).** You have learned seven things. Say them together:
   - A **timeline** orders landmarks, and clues tell us the order is right.
   - A place **changes** over time, and we can name a **cause**.
   - **Two peoples** meet the same needs, and the place shapes how.
   - A **story, song, or art form** shows what a culture values.
   - People **move** for family, work, safety, or environment.
   - The **land and climate** shape where people settle.
   - Working **together** can change something unfair, and everyone deserves a **say**.
2. **Make your story page (25 min).** Fill each section — drawing, or telling an older
   learner who scribes for you:
   - **My timeline:** two or three local landmarks in order, with the clue for each.
   - **A place that changed:** one place, then and now, and one cause.
   - **Two peoples:** Arctic and desert (or another pair), with how the place shaped
     food, shelter, and clothing.
   - **A treasure:** one story, song, or art form, and the value it shows.
   - **Why people move:** one move and its reason.
   - **The land shapes us:** one way a river, mountain, or climate shaped settlement.
   - **Working together for a say:** one story of changing something unfair, and why
     every voice matters.
3. **Share your story (15 min).** Tell your story to a partner or the group — point to
   each section as you go. You may speak, sign, act, or show your page.
4. **Gather the mural (10 min).** Add your page to the class mural of people, places,
   and long ago. Stand back and see how many different stories now live side by side —
   each one true, each one worthy.
5. **Close (5 min).** Your story is part of the long human story — told from many
   perspectives, never just one. You are the author of your own page in it.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Complete two or three sections with heavy support, and tell the rest
  orally.
- **Non-speaking:** Build the story with picture cards, objects, or acting, with a peer
  or older learner scribing captions.
- **Blind/low-vision:** Tell the story aloud by section while an older learner or the
  facilitator records it; use textured markers to place each part.
- **Deaf/HH:** Build the story with pictures, objects, and a signed telling — the page
  itself is visual; sign each section as you point to it.
- **Motor difference:** Tell each section aloud or by pointing while a partner or older
  learner scribes or assembles the page; use larger sections and easy-grip crayons.
- **Extension:** Add an eighth section — "a question I still wonder about" — and write
  or tell one question the unit left you asking.

## Assessment

- **Summative (portfolio):** Does the "my story" page (or its told/scribed version)
  show evidence across the four strands — a timeline with a clue, a change with a cause,
  two peoples and values, movement and settlement, and working together for a say?
- **Performance (oral):** Can the learner tell their story, moving through the sections,
  using the unit's ideas in their own words?

## Home connection

Take your story page home and share it with someone. Ask them: what is one thing they
would add to their own people, places, and long ago story?
