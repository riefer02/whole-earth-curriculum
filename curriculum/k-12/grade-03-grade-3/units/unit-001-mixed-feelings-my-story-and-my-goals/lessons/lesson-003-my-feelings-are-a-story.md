# Lesson 3 — My Feelings Are a Story

## Summary

Learners put the first two ideas together: feelings **change over time** *and* more
than one feeling can be **true at once**. They tell one real event as a three-part
"feeling story" — **Beginning, Middle, End** — noticing where a feeling changed and
where two feelings mixed. This consolidates their mixed-feeling vocabulary before the
unit turns outward to other people's feelings.

## Objectives

- Describe how a feeling can change over time and name when they feel more than one
  feeling at once. _(D01.S1.03.01)_

## Connection

Every event in your life has a beginning, a middle, and an end — and your feelings ride
along the whole way. At a harvest festival: at the **beginning** you are **excited**; in
the **middle** you are **hot and tired *and* happy** all at once; at the **end** you are
**calm and glad**. Feelings do not just arrive and stay — they move and mix, like
threads woven through a story. Reading your own feeling story helps you understand
yourself.

## Materials

- Three-panel feeling story strip
- Worked example strip
- Crayons and pencils

## Preparation

- Prepare the worked example strip (festival day: excited → hot/tired AND happy → calm
  and glad).
- Copy a blank three-panel strip for each learner.
- Arrange space for drawing and sharing.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It is a consolidation lesson: the goal
is that learners *use* the two earlier ideas together, not learn a new fact. Model the
worked example aloud, then release learners to their own events. Some learners will mix
feelings in the middle, some in the ending — there is no single correct shape. Keep the
order (beginning, middle, end) as the one thing to check, because sequencing is the
skill being practiced. Honor a learner's right to keep a private event private; invite
them to choose any event, including a small or everyday one.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** You have two new ideas: a feeling can **change over time**, and
   you can feel **more than one feeling at once**. Today you use both at the same time to
   tell your feelings as a story.
2. **Meet a worked example (10 min).** Look at the festival story. **Beginning:** I felt
   excited. **Middle:** I felt hot and tired *and* happy — two feelings at once. **End:**
   I felt calm and glad. See how the feeling changed from panel to panel, and how two
   feelings shared the middle? Your feelings can do that too.
3. **Choose your event (5 min).** Pick one real event — a celebration, a journey, a
   game, a hard day. It can be big or small.
4. **Tell your feeling story (15 min).** On your strip, draw or write three panels in
   order: how you felt at the **beginning**, how it **changed** in the **middle** (and
   whether two feelings mixed), and how you felt at the **end**. Use your exact feeling
   words.
5. **Share and check the order (5 min).** Tell a partner your three-part story. Then
   check together: are beginning, middle, and end in the right order? If not, you can
   fix it — that is how writers work.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a word bank of feeling words and three feeling faces to arrange
  in order before drawing; scribe the words the learner says.
- **Extension:** Write one "I feel ___ and ___" sentence for the middle panel to name the
  mixed feeling exactly.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner tell a feeling story with a beginning,
  middle, and end in order, and name at least one change or one mixed feeling?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The three-panel feeling story strip.

## Home connection

Tell your three-part feeling story to someone at home, and ask them to tell one of
theirs.
