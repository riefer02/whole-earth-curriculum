# Lesson 5 — My Life Story, In Order

## Summary

Learners tell the story of their own life so far **in time order**. Using a "life line"
with numbered stops — **born, a first memory, something I learned, a big change, now** —
they practice sequencing their own history. This builds self-concept and the
chronological thinking that later grades deepen into fuller personal narratives.

## Objectives

- Tell a simple story of their own life so far, in order. _(D01.S2.03.01)_

## Connection

Your life is a story, and like any story it has an order: a beginning, a middle, and a
now. You were **born** — that is the first page. Then came **first memories**, then
**things you learned** (to walk, to speak, to ride, to read), then **a big change** (a
move, a new sibling, a new school), and then **now**. Telling it in order — first, next,
then — is how you make sense of where you have been. Every person's story is different,
and every story matters.

## Materials

- Life-line chart
- Blank life-line page
- Crayons and pencils

## Preparation

- Print or draw the life-line chart (five numbered stops).
- Copy a blank life-line page for each learner.
- Arrange a circle and a quiet space for drawing.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The skill is *sequencing* — putting
events in order — not producing a "complete" or dramatic biography. Some learners' lives
have hard chapters (a move, a loss, a separation); honor them gently and let the learner
choose what to share (see `docs/facilitation.md`). Use the worked example (your own or a
shared one) before releasing learners to their own. Every family's shape and every
child's pace is normal here — there is no "correct" set of five stops. If a learner
cannot remember or does not know an early stop, that is fine: they may say "I don't
know yet" or ask someone at home.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** So far you have been reading your feelings. Now you become the
   author of your own story. Every story is told in order — and so is yours.
2. **Meet the life line (10 min).** Look at the road with five stops. **1 — I was born.**
   **2 — a first memory.** **3 — something I learned.** **4 — a big change.** **5 — now.**
   These go in order, like a story read from the first page to the last.
3. **Hear a worked example (10 min).** Listen to a life line told in order: "First I was
   born in a small town. Next, my first memory is holding my grandmother's hand. Then I
   learned to ride a bicycle. Then a big change — we moved near the sea. Now I am here,
   telling you." Notice the order words: *first, next, then, now*.
4. **Make your own life line (15 min).** On your page, draw or write one thing at each
   of the five stops, in order. You may use the stops as they are, or change them to fit
   your life. If a stop is hard, say "I don't know yet."
5. **Share one stop (5 min).** Tell a partner your story from stop 1 to stop 5, or share
   just one stop you are proud of. Listen to theirs — every life line is different, and
   that is exactly right.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use three stops instead of five; offer picture prompts (a baby, a school,
  a house, a bicycle) to place in order.
- **Extension:** Add a sixth stop and say what you hope comes next.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner tell their life story with events in time
  order, using order words (first, next, then, now)?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The blank life-line page.

## Home connection

Ask someone at home to help you fill in an early stop you are not sure about, and add it
to your life line.
