# Lesson 9 — My Goal and Its First Step

## Summary

Learners set a simple, reachable personal goal of their own choosing and name its
**first, small step**. Using a staircase image, they see that a tall goal is reached one
step at a time. They practice the feedback loop of making a first step *small and
doable* — the seed of the planning skills later grades grow.

## Objectives

- Set a simple personal goal and name the first step toward it. _(D01.S3.03.01)_

## Connection

Think of something you want to learn or do that you cannot do *yet* — tie a knot, read a
whole chapter, swim across the pool, be kind to a new classmate, learn a song. That is a
**goal**. A goal is not reached all at once; it is climbed like a staircase, one step at
a time. The **first step** is the smallest thing you can do today. A fisher learns to
mend nets one knot at a time; a runner trains one lap at a time. Everyone, everywhere,
reaches goals the same way: first step first.

## Materials

- Goal staircase chart
- My goal card
- Crayons and pencils

## Preparation

- Print or draw the goal staircase chart.
- Copy a "my goal" card for each learner.
- Arrange space for sharing and the "small and doable" check.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The goal must be the *learner's own*, not
assigned, and there is no ranking of goals — a goal to be kinder is as real as a goal to
read faster. The one skill to coach is the **first step**: it should be small and doable
today or tomorrow ("I will ask for one knot lesson" not "I will learn every knot").
Model your own goal and first step first (worked example), then let learners write
theirs, then use a quick peer/self check to make the first step smaller if needed. Keep
it light — this is the beginning of agency, not a contract.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** You have read your feelings and told your story. Now you look
   ahead: what do you want to reach, and how do you begin?
2. **Meet the staircase (10 min).** Look at the chart. At the top is a flag — **my
   goal**. At the bottom is one highlighted step — **the first step**. You do not jump to
   the top; you climb one step at a time. A goal is something you want to do that you
   cannot do *yet*; the first step is the smallest thing you can do today.
3. **Hear a worked example (10 min).** "My goal is to read a whole chapter book. My
   first step is to read three pages tonight." Is that first step small and doable? Yes —
   three pages, tonight. Now test your own: if a first step feels too big, make it
   smaller until you can say "I could do that today."
4. **Set your goal (10 min).** On your card, draw or write **your goal** and **your
   first step**. Choose something you really want, not something others want for you.
   Then check it with a partner: is the first step small and doable? Help each other make
   it smaller if needed.
5. **Close (5 min).** You are the author of your own next step. Hold your card, say your
   goal and first step, and know: you can begin today.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer goal idea cards (a game skill, a kindness, a school skill, a
  household help) and scribe the learner's goal and step.
- **Extension:** Add a second step after the first, and one way you will know when you
  have reached your goal.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner state a personal goal and a first step
  that is small and doable?
- **Self-check:** The learner uses the "small and doable" test on their own first step.
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The "my goal" card.

## Home connection

Share your goal and first step with someone at home, and try the first step today.
