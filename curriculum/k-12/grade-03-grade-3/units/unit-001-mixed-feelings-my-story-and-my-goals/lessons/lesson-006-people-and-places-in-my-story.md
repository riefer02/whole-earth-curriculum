# Lesson 6 — The People and Places in My Story

## Summary

Learners add the "characters" and "settings" to their life story. They return to their
life line and place the **important people** and **important places** at the correct
stops, in order. This completes the objective of telling their own story with its people
and places, and it honors the many shapes a family and a home can take.

## Objectives

- Tell a simple story of their own life so far, in order, including important people and
  places. _(D01.S2.03.01)_

## Connection

Every story has characters and settings. The "characters" of your story are the people
who have helped raise and shape you — a parent, a grandparent, a sibling, a teacher, a
friend, a neighbor. The "settings" are the places you have lived, played, and learned —
a house, a yard, a market, a riverbank, a school, a high-rise, a village. Placing them
on your life line shows *where* and *with whom* your story happened. Families come in
many shapes, and homes come in many kinds — all of them are real.

## Materials

- Story map chart
- Blank story map page
- Crayons and pencils

## Preparation

- Print or draw the story map chart (timeline with who/where boxes).
- Copy a blank story map page for each learner.
- Have learners bring or recall their life line from the last lesson.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The skill is placing people and places at
the correct *stop* in the story — the ordering still matters, even as the content
becomes richer. Honor every family structure and living situation: a learner may name a
grandmother, two mothers, an aunt, a guardian, or no one at a stop — all are valid, and
none is ranked. Keep the focus on *who was there for you* and *where you felt at home*,
not on any one "correct" family model. If a learner's story includes a place that no
longer exists or a person who is gone, hold it gently and let them choose what to share.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Last time you told your life story in order. Today you fill it in:
   the **people** and **places** that make it yours.
2. **Meet the story map (10 min).** Look at the timeline. At each stop there are two
   boxes: **"who was there"** and **"where I was."** A story's *characters* are its
   people; its *settings* are its places. Both matter, and both sit in order along the
   line.
3. **Add people and places to the example (10 min).** Go back to the worked example life
   line. At stop 2 ("a first memory"), the person is *my grandmother*, and the place is
   *her kitchen*. At stop 4 ("a big change"), the people are *my whole family*, and the
   place is *our new home by the sea*. See how each person and place fits its own stop?
4. **Build your own story map (15 min).** Take your life line. At each stop, draw or
   write who was there and where you were. If a stop has many people, choose the ones
   that matter most to you. Keep them in order.
5. **Share one stop (5 min).** Tell a partner one stop — who and where — and why it
   matters to you. Listen to theirs; notice how many different kinds of people and homes
   are in one room.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use one stop only (the "now" stop) and name one person and one place;
  offer picture choices of people and places.
- **Extension:** Describe one place with your senses — what it looked, sounded, and
  smelled like.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner place important people and places at the
  correct stops of their life story, in order?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The story map page.

## Home connection

Ask someone at home to tell you about one important person or place from *their* story,
and where it fits in their life.
