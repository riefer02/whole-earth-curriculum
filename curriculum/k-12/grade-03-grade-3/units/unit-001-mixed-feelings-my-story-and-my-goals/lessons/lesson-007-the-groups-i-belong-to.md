# Lesson 7 — The Groups I Belong To

## Summary

Learners name the **several groups** they belong to at once — family, class, community,
a club or team, a faith or cultural community, a language group — and see themselves at
the center of a web of belonging. This is the first half of understanding how group
membership shapes who they are.

## Objectives

- Name several groups they belong to. _(D01.S2.03.02)_

## Connection

Right now you belong to more than one group at the same time. You are in a **family**,
a **class**, a **neighborhood or village**, maybe a **team or club**, maybe a **faith or
cultural community**, and you speak at least one **language** with others who speak it
too. Being in a group means you share something with the other people in it. In a big
city apartment block, a child belongs to a family, a class, a football team, and a
grandmother's village far away — all at once. Belonging is like a web with you in the
middle.

## Materials

- My Groups web
- Group idea chart
- Crayons and pencils

## Preparation

- Copy a "My Groups" web page for each learner.
- Prepare the group idea chart (kinds of groups).
- Arrange a circle for sharing.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The goal is breadth — that a learner can
name *several* groups, not just one or two. Normalize that everyone belongs to many
groups, and that some groups are chosen (a club, a team) while others are given (a
family, a birthplace, a language). Honor every kind of group without ranking: a faith
community is not "better" than a sports team, and a child with no formal club still
belongs to their family, class, and community. Where natural, invite learners to name a
group that meets far away or online — belonging can cross distance. Keep the web open
and welcoming; there is no "correct" number of groups.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** You have been telling the story of your life. Now look at the
   groups you belong to — the circles you stand inside every day.
2. **Meet the web (10 min).** Look at the page: **you** are in the middle, and empty
   circles wait around you. Each circle is a group you belong to. A group is people you
   share something with — a home, a class, a place, a game, a belief, a language.
3. **Brainstorm kinds of groups (5 min).** Read the idea chart: **family, class,
   community, a club or team, a faith or culture, a language.** Can you think of more? A
   choir, a farm, a group of cousins, a group that meets online.
4. **Fill your web (15 min).** Draw or write one group in each circle — your own real
   groups. Try to name **several**, not just one. If you run out of circles, draw more.
5. **Share and count (5 min).** With a partner, name your groups and count them. Notice
   how many groups one person can belong to — and how your list and theirs are different.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide picture cards of group kinds (a house, a school, a ball, a
  place of gathering, a speech bubble) to place in the circles; scribe the names.
- **Extension:** Sort your groups into kinds — family, place, interest, language, faith
  or culture — and label each circle.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name several (three or more) groups they
  belong to?

## Home connection

Ask someone at home what groups *they* belong to, and add one new idea to your own web.
