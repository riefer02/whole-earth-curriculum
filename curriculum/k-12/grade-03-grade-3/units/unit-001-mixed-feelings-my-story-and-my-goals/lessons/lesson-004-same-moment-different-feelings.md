# Lesson 4 — The Same Moment, Different Feelings

## Summary

Learners take the first real step of **perspective-taking**: the same situation can make
different people feel differently, because people have different lives, wants, and
memories. They practice explaining why three different people might react three
different ways to one shared moment, and they learn that no feeling is the "right" one.

## Objectives

- Explain how the same situation can make different people feel differently.
  _(D01.S1.03.02)_

## Connection

Rain falls on the village. The child who wanted to play outside feels **disappointed**.
The farmer whose crops were thirsty feels **relieved**. The little one who loves to
splash feels **delighted**. Same rain, three feelings. Or think of a long trip: one
person is **excited** to see a new place, another is **sad** to leave home, another is
**nervous** about the unfamiliar road. The moment is shared, but the feeling is each
person's own — because each person brings a different life to the same moment.

## Materials

- Same-moment poster
- Same-moment scenario cards
- Chart paper and markers

## Preparation

- Print or draw the same-moment poster (rain: disappointed / relieved / delighted).
- Prepare scenario cards with one situation and three different people's reactions.
- Write the thinking question large: "Why might this person feel this way?"

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It plants the seed of perspective-taking
that grows in later units ("Caring, Perspective & Belonging"). The important rule: no
feeling is the "correct" one. When a learner says why someone feels a certain way, accept
their reasoning even if it differs from yours, and wonder together. Gently separate
evidence from guessing ("we are imagining why — we cannot know for sure"). Keep the
examples respectful of every child's life (a move, rain, a new rule, sharing) and avoid
stereotyping who feels what. This is also an egalitarian moment: every feeling gets to
be heard, not just the loudest one.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** You know your own feelings can change and mix. Now look outward:
   does everyone feel the same thing you do, in the same moment? Often, no — and that is
   worth understanding.
2. **Meet the rain moment (10 min).** Look at the poster. Three people, one rain. One is
   **disappointed** — they wanted to play. One is **relieved** — the crops needed it. One
   is **delighted** — puddles! Same rain, three feelings. Why? Because each person wants
   something different and lives a different life. Their feelings are *all* real; none is
   wrong.
3. **Explain the "why" (15 min).** Take a scenario card: one situation, three people.
   For each person, name the feeling and then **explain why**: "They feel ___ because
   ___." Try another card. The thinking question is: *why might this person feel this
   way?*
4. **Pair-share (10 min).** Think of a real shared moment — a rainstorm, a rule change, a
   shared treat, a move. Tell a partner how *you* felt, then guess how someone *else*
   might have felt and why. Listen without saying "you're wrong."
5. **Close (5 min).** The same moment can hold many different feelings. Understanding
   that is how you begin to understand other people — and to be kind to them.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Work with two reactions instead of three; offer feeling word cards to
  place on the poster while explaining.
- **Extension:** Name one person who might feel *two* things at once about the same
  moment, using "and."

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner explain why two different people might
  feel differently about the same situation?
- **Peer:** During pair-share, partners check each other's "why" by asking "why might
  they feel that way?"

## Home connection

Ask someone at home about a shared moment you both remember, and compare how each of
you felt — then wonder together why it differed.
