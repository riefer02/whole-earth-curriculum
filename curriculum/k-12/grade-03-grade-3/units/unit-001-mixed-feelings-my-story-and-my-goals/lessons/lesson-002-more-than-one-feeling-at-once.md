# Lesson 2 — More Than One Feeling at Once

## Summary

Learners discover that two feelings can live inside them **at the same time** — happy
*and* sad, excited *and* nervous. Both are true. Using two overlapping circles and a
"both/and" frame, they practice naming two feelings at once, growing their feeling
vocabulary past one word at a time.

## Objectives

- Name when they feel more than one feeling at once. _(D01.S1.03.01)_

## Connection

A family moves to a new town. On the last night in the old home, a child feels
**excited** for the new adventure *and* **sad** to leave their friends. Both at the
same time. A child holds a brand-new baby sibling: **proud** to be the big one *and* a
little **left out** now that everyone fusses over the baby. A festival day ends: **happy**
from the music and sweets *and* a bit **tired and let-down** that it is over. Feelings
are not always one at a time — sometimes two (or more) sit inside you together, and
both are true.

## Materials

- Two-overlapping-circles chart
- Both/and page
- Scenario cards

## Preparation

- Print or draw the overlapping-circles chart (happy / sad / "both" in the middle).
- Copy a both/and page for each learner.
- Prepare scenario cards with mixed-feeling moments.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The key move is the little word **and**:
"I feel happy *and* sad." Normalize mixed feelings as ordinary and healthy — this is
what the unit is named for. Never tell a learner that a mixed feeling is "confusing" or
that they should "pick one." Model the overlapping circles with your own body and voice
first, then let learners build their own. Some learners will feel only one thing about a
scenario; that is fine — invite, never force, a second feeling. Honor every family's
moments of change (a move, a new sibling, a goodbye) as real, not as "right" examples.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Yesterday you saw that a feeling can change over time. Today you
   learn something even more surprising: you can feel **more than one feeling at once**.
2. **Meet the overlapping circles (10 min).** Look at the chart. One circle is **happy**,
   one circle is **sad** — and where they overlap, both are true at the same time:
   **happy AND sad**. That is a *mixed feeling*. You can feel excited AND nervous before
   a big day, proud AND a little shy when everyone looks at you, glad AND sad on the last
   day of a visit.
3. **Try the word "and" (10 min).** Hear a moment: "Your best friend is moving away, but
   you are happy they get to see the sea." Say it both ways: "I feel sad *and* happy."
   The word **and** holds two feelings side by side, and neither one is wrong.
4. **Build your own both/and (10 min).** On your page, write or draw two feelings in the
   two circles. In the middle, write the moment where both are true. Try more than one
   pair if you like.
5. **Close (5 min).** You are big enough to hold more than one feeling. When two
   feelings come at once, you do not have to choose — you can name them both. That is a
   superpower.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use picture cards of single feelings and let the learner pick two to place
  in the overlap; scribe the sentence "I feel ___ and ___."
- **Extension:** Name three feelings at once and the moment that held all three.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name two feelings that were true at once,
  using the frame "I feel ___ and ___"?

## Home connection

At home, share a moment when you felt two things at once, and ask someone to share one
of theirs.
