# Lesson 8 — How My Groups Shape Me

## Summary

Learners move from *naming* their groups to *explaining* how each one shapes them.
Groups hand them words, foods, songs, stories, games, and skills that become part of who
they are. They complete the objective — naming groups and how each shapes their identity
— while holding that no group is ranked above another and every group's gifts are real.

## Objectives

- Name several groups they belong to and how each one shapes who they are.
  _(D01.S2.03.02)_

## Connection

The groups you belong to do not just surround you — they **shape** you, a little every
day. Your family teaches you a **language** and how to **greet** people. Your community
teaches you a **food** and how it is made. A club teaches you a **game or skill**. A
faith or cultural community teaches you a **song or story**. Over time, these become part
of you — the way you speak, the food you love, the games you know, the stories you carry.
A group far away, reached by letter or message or screen, can shape you too. Everyone is
shaped by several groups, and no one's shaping is better than another's.

## Materials

- How My Groups Shape Me chart
- My group gift page
- Crayons and pencils

## Preparation

- Print or draw the "How My Groups Shape Me" chart (figure + gift boxes).
- Copy a "my group gift" page for each learner.
- Have learners bring their "My Groups" web from last lesson.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The key is the *how*: learners must
explain a connection ("my family gave me this word"), not just list. Model the worked
example first, then release. Guard against ranking groups: no group's gifts are "better"
or "more important" — a game is as much a gift as a sacred song. This is also an
egalitarian and global moment: every culture's gifts are honored side by side. Where
natural, acknowledge that a group may be online or far away, reached through technology,
and still shape the learner. Honor every family's and community's practices without
stereotyping.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Last time you named your groups. Today you ask: what has each
   group **given** you? How has it **shaped** you?
2. **Meet the gift chart (10 min).** Look at the figure in the middle and the gift boxes
   around it: **a word or language, a food or dish, a song or story, a game or skill.**
   Each group hands you gifts like these, and they become part of you.
3. **Hear a worked example (10 min).** "My *family* gave me my first language — the
   words I think in. My *community* taught me to make a flat bread on the fire. My
   *football team* taught me to pass and to take turns. My *grandmother's village* gave
   me a song I still hum." Each gift has a **group** it came from and a **way** it shaped
   me.
4. **Find your own gifts (10 min).** Take your groups web. Pick two or three groups and,
   for each, name one thing it gave you and how it became part of you. Draw or write them
   on your page: "My ___ gave me ___, and now I ___."
5. **Share and listen (5 min).** Tell a partner one group and its gift. Listen to theirs.
   See how a room full of different groups makes a rich, strong community.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer a sentence frame ("My ___ taught me ___.") and picture cards of
  gifts (a speech bubble, a bowl, a ball, a song note); scribe the words.
- **Extension:** Name two gifts from the same group and how each shaped you in a
  different way.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name a group and explain at least one way
  it shaped who they are?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The "my group gift" page.

## Home connection

Ask someone at home what one of their groups taught them, and share one of your own
gifts.
