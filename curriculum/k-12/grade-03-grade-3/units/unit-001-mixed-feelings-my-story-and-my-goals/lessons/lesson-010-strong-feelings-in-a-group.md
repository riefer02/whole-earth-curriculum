# Lesson 10 — Strong Feelings in a Group

## Summary

The capstone lesson. Learners put the whole unit together into one practical skill:
noticing a **strong feeling** rising in a group and using a **quiet signal** — a calm
breath, asking for a turn, or stepping to a calm spot — to manage it without disrupting
others. They revisit the unit's feeling vocabulary (mixed and changing feelings) and
their goal-setting to choose the tool that fits them.

## Objectives

- Use a strategy to manage a strong feeling in a group setting without disrupting
  others. _(D01.S3.03.02)_
- Describe how a feeling can change over time and name when they feel more than one
  feeling at once. _(D01.S1.03.01)_

## Connection

A strong feeling can rise in the middle of a group — a game is lost, a turn is skipped,
a shared task gets hard, a long wait gets long. The feeling is real and allowed. The
question is what you do with it *while the group is still going*. You can notice the
feeling and use a quiet signal to get what you need — a calm breath, a turn, a little
space — so the group keeps working *and* you keep being okay. Managing a feeling in a
group is a kindness to yourself and to everyone else in the circle.

## Materials

- Quiet signals poster
- Group scenario cards
- A "my signal" card

## Preparation

- Print or draw the quiet signals poster.
- Prepare group scenario cards.
- Arrange an open circle for role-play.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The capstone is a *skill*, not a test, and
never a punishment. Frame the quiet signals as help, exactly like the Grade 2 calm tools:
a learner uses them to care for themselves *and* the group — not because they are "in
trouble." Model each signal with your own body, then guide role-play, then let learners
choose their own. Some learners will need a movement option (stepping to the side) rather
than a stillness one (a calm breath) — both are equally good. A learner with motor
differences may signal any way that works — a nod, a point, a glance, holding up a printed
signal card, or a partner voicing the need for them; any safe signal counts as the skill.
Never shame a learner whose
first instinct is to burst out; the win is that they notice and try a tool, not that they
are silent. A learner may choose any safe signal; there is no single "right" one.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** This is your last lesson in "Mixed Feelings, My Story & My
   Goals." Look back: you learned that feelings change and mix, that the same moment can
   feel different to different people, that your life is a story with people, places, and
   groups, and that you can set a goal and take a first step. Today you put it to work in
   a group.
2. **Meet the quiet signals (10 min).** Look at the poster. **Hand on heart** = I need a
   calm breath. **Two fingers up** = I need a turn. **Step to the side** = I need some
   space. A signal is a quiet way to say what you need without stopping the whole group.
   It is help, never a punishment.
3. **Practice together (15 min).** Hear a scenario: "Your team is one point behind and
   the ball goes out. Your chest is hot." Choose a signal and use it: hand on heart, one
   slow breath, then two fingers up — "I need a turn." Try it with two more scenarios (a
   skipped turn, a long wait, a task that keeps failing).
4. **Role-play your own (10 min).** With a partner or small group, act out a real group
   moment where a strong feeling rises. Use your chosen signal quietly, and let the group
   keep going. Then switch roles.
5. **Close (5 min).** You are a person who can feel big things, name them, and choose
   what to do — in a group and on your own. That is your strength, and you are still
   growing it, and that is exactly right. Choose your signal, and carry it with you.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Practice one signal only (hand on heart) until it is solid, then add
  another; provide the feeling word as a choice ("is it mad or frustrated?").
- **Extension:** For each signal, name how it helps *the group* too, not just you.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner use a quiet signal to manage a strong
  feeling in a group role-play without disrupting the group?
- **Performance:** The learner demonstrates a chosen signal in a role-play.
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The "my signal" card, added to the unit portfolio alongside
  the feeling story, story map, and goal card.

## Home connection

Teach your quiet signal to someone at home, and use it together the next time a group
moment (a meal, a game, a long wait) feels big.
