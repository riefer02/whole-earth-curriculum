# Lesson 2 — Eyes and Ears Together

## Summary

Learners meet a second sense team: sight and hearing. They listen to a sound with eyes
closed, then watch it with ears covered, then use both — and discover that each sense
adds a clue the other misses. When eyes and ears team up, the picture is fuller.

## Objectives

- Describe how two senses can work together, such as taste and smell, to give a fuller
  picture of something. _(D03.S1.03.01)_

## Connection

Have you ever heard a clap from across the room, or watched a bird land without hearing
it over the wind? Your eyes tell you where something is; your ears tell you what sound
it makes. Alone, each sense only gets half the story. Together, they fill in the whole
picture — that is how you catch a tossed ball, or know a drum is being hit even while
you watch it.

## Materials

- A small sound-making object (shaker, hand clap, or drum)
- A soft ball or beanbag to drop or bounce
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Choose a few clear sounds to use — a clap, a shaker, a dropped ball.
- Clear a small space where learners can sit and cover or close their eyes safely.
- Prepare paper and crayons for the closing drawing.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Prepare the sound sources and space, then guide the three-step
listen/watch/both activity. This spirals from Lesson 1's taste-and-smell team to a
second pair of senses. Honor every body and every ability: a learner who is deaf or
hard of hearing gets far more from eyes (and may read lips or use sign — that is sight
doing extra work), and a learner with low vision gets far more from ears — and can gather
the "sight" clue through touch instead, feeling the ball's bounce through the floor
with a hand or foot, or having a friend guide their hand toward the sound. Frame
this as a strength of teamwork, never as a lack: when one sense has less to work
with, the others carry more. Let learners choose how to "cover" a sense — closing
eyes or covering ears — in the way that is comfortable for them (never cover a
hearing aid or cochlear implant; a learner who uses one may prefer to simply not
listen for a moment, or watch a friend's silent motion instead).

Where a tablet or camera records the sound, notice what the tool can do — save and
replay a sound — and what it cannot: it does not feel the clap through the floor, and
it is made somewhere and needs power, while your own ears and hands sense with nothing
but themselves. A tool is a helper, never a replacement for the senses.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Last time you found that taste and smell team up. Today you
   will meet another team: your eyes and your ears.
2. **Hear only (10 min).** Close your eyes (or cover them gently). Listen as a friend
   shakes a shaker, claps, or drops a soft ball. What can your ears tell you? Maybe the
   sound is loud or soft, near or far. What can your ears NOT tell you? They cannot show
   you exactly where the ball is or what it looks like.
3. **See only (10 min).** Now cover your ears (or have your friend make a silent
   motion). Watch the shaker move or the ball drop. What can your eyes tell you? Maybe
   where it is and how it moves. What can your eyes NOT tell you? They cannot hear the
   clap or the thud.
4. **Both together (10 min).** Now use both — watch AND listen. Notice how the two
   clues join. You know where it is, how it moves, AND what sound it makes. That is a
   fuller picture.
5. **Draw the team (10 min).** Draw an eye and an ear with an arrow meeting in the
   middle, and label them "sight" and "hearing." Write one sentence about a clue each
   one gives you.
6. **Close (5 min).** Say it together: "eyes and ears team up." Two senses together
   fill in the whole picture.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use one clear sound at a time and guide each step aloud; accept spoken
  or drawn answers.
- **Extension:** Ask learners to name a third sense that could add a clue to the same
  sound (for example, feeling the ball's bounce through the floor with their feet).

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation/peer):** Can the learner say, in their own words, one clue
  the eyes give and one clue the ears give, and that together they make a fuller
  picture?
