# Lesson 3 — A Fuller Picture

## Summary

Learners build a "senses working together" chart. They explore one object one sense at
a time — touch with eyes closed, then smell, then sight, then taste or sound — and
record one clue each sense adds. The chart shows, in their own hand, that more senses
make a fuller picture.

## Objectives

- Describe how two senses can work together, such as taste and smell, to give a fuller
  picture of something. _(D03.S1.03.01)_

## Connection

Think of a ripe mango sitting in a basket. Your eyes see its orange color. Your fingers
feel its smooth skin. Your nose smells its sweetness. Your tongue tastes the juice.
Each sense gives you one clue — but only all of them together give you the whole mango.
Today you will gather the clues one by one and put them on a chart.

## Materials

- One real object to explore (a fruit, a leaf, a flower, or a shell)
- A cloth or box to hide the object
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Choose safe, familiar objects for each group — nothing sharp, nothing that could be
  an allergen if tasted. Tasting is optional; smelling and touching are enough.
- Fold or draw a chart with a space in the middle for the object and one bubble for
  each sense.
- Prepare a cloth or box to hide each object.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Prepare the objects and charts, then guide the one-sense-at-a-
time exploration. This is the unit's first portfolio artifact — the "senses working
together" chart named in the unit's assessment plan. The sequence matters: touch and
smell come before sight so learners feel how much each hidden clue matters before the
eyes fill in the picture. Honor every body: a learner with low vision gathers richer
clues from touch, smell, and sound; a learner who cannot smell gathers theirs from
sight and touch — every chart is a true and complete picture for that learner. Tasting
is optional and never required, and only safe, familiar foods are used. Celebrate that
no two charts are identical, just as no two bodies sense identically.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** You have met two sense teams — taste with smell, and eyes with
   ears. Today you will gather clues from even more senses at once.
2. **Touch, eyes closed (10 min).** Your group hides an object under a cloth. Close
   your eyes and feel it. What clues does your skin give — smooth or rough, hard or
   soft, cool or warm? Write or draw one clue on your chart.
3. **Smell (5 min).** Still hidden, smell it. What does your nose add — sweet, fresh,
   grassy? Add one clue.
4. **Sight (10 min).** Now lift the cloth. What do your eyes add — color, shape, size?
   Add one clue. Notice how the picture got fuller with each sense.
5. **Taste or sound (5 min).** If it is safe and you want to, taste a small piece (or
   tap it to hear its sound). Add that last clue. Your chart now shows many senses
   working together on one thing.
6. **Share and close (5 min).** Trade charts with a friend. How are your clues alike,
   and how are they different? One thing, many senses, one fuller picture.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use just three senses (touch, smell, sight) and have a partner scribe or
  draw the clues.
- **Extension:** Ask learners to write which single sense told them the MOST about the
  object, and whether it is always the same sense or different for different things.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name one clue from at least two
  different senses and say that together they give a fuller picture?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The completed "senses working together" chart, kept in the
  learner's portfolio.

## Home connection

Choose one object at home and, with a family member, gather one clue from each sense —
touch, smell, sight, sound, and (if safe) taste. Tell them how each sense added to the
picture.
