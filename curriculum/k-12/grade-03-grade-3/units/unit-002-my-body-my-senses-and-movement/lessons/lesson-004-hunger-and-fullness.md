# Lesson 4 — Hunger and Fullness

## Summary

Learners meet two of the body's signals: hunger and fullness. They learn the early
signs of each — a rumbling, empty feeling and low energy for hunger; a comfortable,
satisfied feeling and slowing down for fullness — and name one helpful response to
each. They close by drawing the two signals.

## Objectives

- Recognize early body signals of hunger, fullness, thirst, and tiredness and say one
  helpful response to each. _(D03.S1.03.02)_

## Connection

Have you ever been playing or working and suddenly noticed your tummy feels empty and
rumbling, and you keep thinking about food? That is your body sending a signal:
hunger. Later, after a meal, your tummy feels comfortable and satisfied — that is the
signal of fullness. Your body talks to you. Learning its signals is how you take good
care of it.

## Materials

- Signal cards (rumbling tummy, low energy, comfortable tummy, slowing down)
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Print or draw the four signal cards.
- Prepare paper and crayons for the closing drawing.
- Arrange a circle for the signal-matching game.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Prepare the cards, then guide the matching and drawing. This
spirals up from Grade 1, where learners first noticed hunger and thirst as body
signals — now they add fullness and name a helpful response to each. Hold this topic
with great care: food and bodies are sensitive. Never comment on how much or how
little a learner eats, never praise or shame a body or an amount, and never make
hunger a problem to be "fixed" by willpower. Bodies differ — in size, in hunger, in
how often they need food — and different families eat on different schedules and in
different ways; every pattern is to be respected. Some learners may not have enough
food at home; frame hunger as a body signal to answer when we can, and respond with
care if this comes up. The goal is noticing, never judgment. Learners with certain
conditions may have different hunger signals; all observations are welcome.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Put a hand on your tummy. How does it feel right now? Today you
   will learn two of its signals: hunger and fullness.
2. **Meet hunger (10 min).** Hunger is your body asking for food. Its early signals:
   your tummy feels empty and may **rumble**, and your energy dips so you feel tired or
   find it hard to focus. What is a helpful response? **Eat a meal or a snack.** Notice
   the signal early, and answer it.
3. **Meet fullness (10 min).** Fullness is your body saying it has had enough. Its
   signals: your tummy feels **comfortable and satisfied**, not stuffed, and you may
   slow down and stop thinking about food. What is a helpful response? **Stop eating
   and notice you are satisfied.** Fullness is a good, gentle feeling, not a rule about
   how much to eat.
4. **Match the signals (10 min).** Sort the four cards into two groups — hunger and
   fullness — and match each to its helpful response: hunger → eat; fullness → stop.
   Point to your own tummy for each one.
5. **Draw your two signals (10 min).** Draw your tummy empty and rumbling, and your
   tummy comfortable and satisfied. Write one helpful response under each.
6. **Close (5 min).** Say it together: "when I am hungry, I eat; when I am full, I
   stop." Your body knows — you just have to listen.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Introduce one signal at a time and act it out together; accept spoken
  or drawn answers.
- **Extension:** Ask learners to name a time they noticed a hunger or fullness signal
  early, and what happened when they answered it.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation/self):** Can the learner name one signal of hunger and one
  signal of fullness, and a helpful response to each, without any judgment of amounts
  or bodies?

## Home connection

At your next meal, notice together with your family what your tummy feels like before
you eat and when you begin to feel satisfied. Name the signal aloud — "I feel full" —
in your own words.
