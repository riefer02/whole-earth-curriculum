# Lesson 6 — Reading My Body's Signals

## Summary

Learners gather all four body signals into one place: a "body-signal map." On a body
outline they mark where hunger, fullness, thirst, and tiredness each show up, and write
one helpful response for each. The map is their own guide to reading and answering
their body — and a portfolio artifact.

## Objectives

- Recognize early body signals of hunger, fullness, thirst, and tiredness and say one
  helpful response to each. _(D03.S1.03.02)_

## Connection

Your body has been sending you signals all along — a rumbling tummy, a dry mouth,
heavy eyelids. Today you will draw them all on one map, the way a traveler marks the
roads on a journey. When you know the map, you can read your body's signals and answer
them before they get loud.

## Materials

- A body outline sheet
- Crayons or markers
- Signal cards (reused from Lessons 4–5)

## Preparation

- Print or draw a simple body outline for each learner.
- Set out crayons and the four signal cards from Lessons 4–5.
- Prepare a space to share finished maps.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Prepare the body outlines and signal cards, then guide the
mapping. This is the unit's second portfolio artifact — the body-signal drawing named
in the assessment plan — and it closes the body-signals strand of the unit. Keep every
response gentle and non-judging: there is no "correct" amount to eat, drink, or sleep,
and no body is praised or shamed. A learner's map is a record of their noticing, never
a grade. Some learners may show signals in different spots than the cards — that is
fine; their own body is the authority. If a learner shares that their body feels
hungry, thirsty, or tired right now, honor it: offer water, food, or a rest as the
situation allows. Maps may be shared only if the learner chooses.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** You have learned four signals in the last two lessons. Say them:
   **hunger, fullness, thirst, tiredness.** Today you will put them all on one map.
2. **Recall the signals (10 min).** Look at the signal cards. For each one, say the
   signal, where it shows up, and its helpful response: hunger → empty rumbling tummy →
   eat; fullness → comfortable satisfied tummy → stop; thirst → dry mouth → drink
   water; tiredness → heavy eyelids and slow body → rest.
3. **Map the signals (15 min).** On your body outline, mark each signal where it shows
   up: draw a rumbling tummy for hunger and fullness, a dry mouth for thirst, and heavy
   eyelids for tiredness. Label each spot.
4. **Add the responses (10 min).** Beside each signal, draw or write one helpful
   response. Your map now shows four signals and four kind answers.
5. **Share and close (10 min).** Share your map with a partner if you want. How are
   your maps alike? How are they different? Every body is a little different, and every
   map is right for its own body.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Map two signals at a time, or have a partner scribe while the learner
  points and colors.
- **Extension:** Ask learners to add a fifth signal their body sends them that the
  cards do not show (such as feeling cold, or needing to move), and its response.

## Assessment

- **Summative (portfolio/observation):** Can the learner place all four signals on a
  body map and name one helpful response to each, in their own body's way?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The completed body-signal map, kept in the learner's
  portfolio.

## Home connection

Show your body-signal map to someone at home and read it to them — all four signals and
their kind answers. Ask them if their body sends any signals in a different spot.
