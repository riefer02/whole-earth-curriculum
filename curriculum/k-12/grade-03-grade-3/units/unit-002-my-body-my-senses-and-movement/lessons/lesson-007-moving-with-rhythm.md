# Lesson 7 — Moving with Rhythm

## Summary

Learners meet rhythm. They make a steady beat with their hands, then build and perform
a short four-move sequence — such as stomp, stomp, clap, turn — in time with the beat.
Rhythm is the "heartbeat" of movement, and every body can move to it in its own way.

## Objectives

- Perform a short movement sequence, such as a dance or obstacle course, with rhythm
  and balance. _(D03.S2.03.01)_

## Connection

Have you ever clapped along to a song, or tapped your foot without thinking about it?
That is rhythm — a steady beat your body can feel and join. People everywhere make
rhythm: djembe drumming in West Africa, taiko drumming in Japan, bhangra dance from the
Punjab, and the clapping games children play in every land — from a city street to a
village square. Today you will move your own body to a beat of your own.

## Materials

- A beat source (clapping, a drum, a shaker, or a steady-beat song)
- A clear floor space
- Sequence cards (step, stomp, clap, pat, turn)

## Preparation

- Clear a safe floor space.
- Choose a beat source, or plan to clap a steady beat together.
- Prepare sequence cards showing step, stomp, clap, pat, and turn.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Prepare the space and beat source, then lead the steady beat
before learners build their own sequences. This begins the movement strand, which
builds on Grade 2's movement sequences — now with rhythm added. Model a steady beat
and a four-move sequence slowly before learners try (explicit modeling, then guided
practice). Honor every body: rhythm can be made by clapping, tapping, blinking,
rocking, or rolling — and a sequence can be performed standing, seated, or with
wheels. Every version counts fully. Keep it joyful, never a competition: there is no
"best" rhythm, only a steady one. Learners who are deaf or hard of hearing can feel
the beat through the floor or a drum they touch; learners with low vision can follow a
hand-held beat — rhythm is felt, not only heard.

Where a music player is used, notice the difference: a player can bring a beat from
anywhere in the world, but it needs a battery or a wall and was made in a factory far
away — while your own body makes a steady beat with nothing but itself. Both are
welcome; only your body can feel the beat the way you do.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Listen — can you feel a beat anywhere right now? Your own
   heartbeat is a quiet rhythm. Today you will make rhythm and move to it.
2. **Make a steady beat (10 min).** Clap a steady beat together: clap, clap, clap,
   clap — even and the same, like a heartbeat. Try it slowly, then a little faster.
   Pat your knees, then stomp your feet — the beat stays steady.
3. **Learn a rhythm sequence (10 min).** Watch and follow a four-move sequence in time
   with the beat: **stomp, stomp, clap, turn.** Each move lands on one beat. Say the
   words as you move.
4. **Build your own sequence (10 min).** Choose four moves from the cards and put them
   in order — maybe clap, pat, stomp, turn, or step, step, clap, spin. Practice it in
   time with the beat, slowly at first.
5. **Perform (10 min).** Perform your rhythm sequence for a partner, a mirror, or the
   group. Watch your friends and keep the beat with them. Every sequence is good.
6. **Close (5 min).** Say it together: "my body can move with rhythm." How did it feel
   to move in time with the beat?

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use a two-move sequence (stomp, clap) repeated, with an adult or
  partner keeping the beat; accept seated or rocking versions.
- **Extension:** Ask learners to perform their sequence once slowly and once faster,
  keeping the same steady beat, or to lead the beat for a partner.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation/performance):** Can the learner perform a short movement
  sequence in time with a steady beat, in their own body's way?

Make a beat at home — clap or tap a table — and teach your rhythm sequence to someone.
See if you can keep the beat steady together.
