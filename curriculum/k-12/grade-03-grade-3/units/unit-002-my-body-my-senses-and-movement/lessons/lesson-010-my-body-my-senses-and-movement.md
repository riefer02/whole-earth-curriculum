# Lesson 10 — My Body, My Senses & Movement

## Summary

The capstone gathers the whole unit: learners name two senses that team up, read a body
signal and its helpful response, perform a movement sequence with rhythm and balance,
and aim at a target. They close by celebrating everything their one body can sense,
signal, and do.

## Objectives

- Describe how two senses can work together to give a fuller picture of something.
  _(D03.S1.03.01)_
- Recognize early body signals of hunger, fullness, thirst, and tiredness and say one
  helpful response to each. _(D03.S1.03.02)_
- Perform a short movement sequence, such as a dance or obstacle course, with rhythm
  and balance. _(D03.S2.03.01)_
- Improve hand-eye coordination through games and tasks such as tossing, catching, and
  aiming at a target. _(D03.S2.03.02)_

## Connection

You have spent this whole unit getting to know the body that is yours — how its senses
team up, the signals it sends, the way it moves with rhythm and balance, and how its
eyes and hands work together. Today you put it all together. Every body is different,
and every body can do wonderful things in its own way. This is a day to notice and
celebrate yours.

## Materials

- Sense cards and body-signal cards
- Soft balls or beanbags and a target
- A beat source
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Set up four stations: **Senses** (sense cards), **Signals** (body-signal cards),
  **Move** (a clear space and a beat source), **Aim** (soft balls and a target).
- Prepare paper and crayons for the closing celebration drawing.
- Plan the rotation so every learner visits every station.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Set up the four stations, run the rotation, then lead the
closing celebration. The capstone is a celebration of growth, not a test: observe what
each learner can now do and note it for the portfolio without ranking anyone. Honor
every body in its own way — adapted movement, adapted aiming, and spoken or drawn
answers all count fully. Keep body-signal responses gentle and private, as in the
earlier lessons: a learner shares only what they choose, and no body or amount of food,
water, or sleep is ever judged. If a learner shows they are hungry, thirsty, or tired,
honor it. Close with joy: the measure of this unit is that each learner leaves feeling
their body is known, capable, and good. Where a camera or tablet records the
celebration for a class book, notice what the tool can do — keep a picture for later —
and what it cannot: it does not feel the beat, smell the air, or know when a body needs
water or rest. Tools help us remember; the body does the sensing.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Look back over the unit. What did you learn about your body?
   Name one thing you are proud of.
2. **Senses station (10 min).** Name two senses that team up — taste and smell, or
   eyes and ears. Say one clue each sense adds. How do they make a fuller picture
   together?
3. **Signals station (10 min).** Look at the signal cards. Pick one — hunger, fullness,
   thirst, or tiredness. Point to where it shows up in your body and say one helpful
   response. Share only if you want.
4. **Move station (10 min).** Perform a short sequence with rhythm and balance — a
   stomp-stomp-clap-turn, then a slow balanced walk along a line. Do it in your own
   body's way, with wheels, seated, or with support if that is you.
5. **Aim station (10 min).** With a friend, toss and catch a soft ball, then take turns
   aiming it at the target. Use kind words: "Nice aim!" "Your turn!"
6. **Celebrate (10 min).** Draw or show one sense team, one body signal and its
   response, and one thing your body can do with rhythm and balance. Share it if you
   want. Your body is yours, and it can do wonderful things.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Shorten the rotation to two stations (senses and move), with an adult or
  partner helper at each.
- **Extension:** Ask learners to combine everything into a short "all about my body"
  share — naming a sense team, a signal, and a movement in one flow.

## Assessment

- **Summative (performance/observation):** Can the learner name a sense team, read a
  body signal and its response, perform a movement sequence with rhythm and balance,
  and aim at a target, in their own body's way?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The "my body celebration" drawing, kept in the learner's
  portfolio.

## Home connection

Show someone at home your "my body celebration" drawing and tell them one sense team,
one body signal and its response, and one thing your body can do with rhythm and
balance.
