# Lesson 1 — Senses Team Up

## Summary

Learners meet the unit's big idea: senses do not work alone. They taste a familiar food
twice — first with the nose gently pinched closed, then with the nose open — and notice
that flavor grows fuller when smell joins taste. They close by drawing the nose-and-
tongue team.

## Objectives

- Describe how two senses can work together, such as taste and smell, to give a fuller
  picture of something. _(D03.S1.03.01)_

## Connection

Think of biting into a sweet, juicy mango or a warm piece of bread. You might say "this
tastes good" — but your tongue is not working alone. Your nose is smelling the mango at
the same time, and the two are talking to your brain together. Taste and smell are
partners: together they make the fuller picture we call flavor.

## Materials

- Small pieces of a familiar safe food (orange, bread, or a food your family already
  eats)
- A clean napkin or towel to gently pinch the nose
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Check for any food allergies or food needs in the group first, and choose only foods
  that are safe and familiar for every learner.
- Cut or portion small pieces of the food, one per learner, and set out napkins.
- Prepare paper and crayons for the closing drawing.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Prepare the food and napkins, then lead the pinch-and-taste
before learners draw. This spirals up from Grade 2, where learners matched each sense
to its body part — today two senses join forces. Safety first: never require a learner
to taste anything, and never use a food that could be an allergen or is unfamiliar to a
family; plain water or plain bread works for everyone. Honor every body: some people
smell or taste more or less strongly than others, and some cannot smell or taste at all
— every answer about what they notice is true for them, and "I notice nothing
different" is a real and welcome observation. The factual idea here — that much of what
we call flavor comes from smell and taste together — is well established in sensory
science (see Resources).

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Last year you matched each sense to its body part: tongue for
   taste, nose for smell. Today you will find out what happens when two senses work as
   a team.
2. **Predict (5 min).** Look at your piece of food. Guess: will it taste the same if
   you pinch your nose closed? Say or write your guess.
3. **Taste with the nose pinched (10 min).** Gently pinch your nose closed with your
   napkin and put a small piece of food on your tongue. Chew slowly and notice the
   taste. What do you get? Maybe sweet, salty, or a little bland. Then let go of your
   nose. What changes? Most people find the flavor suddenly gets fuller and stronger.
4. **Taste with the nose open (10 min).** Now taste a second small piece with your nose
   open and free. Notice how the smell and the taste arrive together. That fuller
   picture is called **flavor** — taste and smell teamed up.
5. **Draw the team (15 min).** Draw a nose and a tongue with an arrow meeting in the
   middle, and label them "smell" and "taste" joining to make "flavor." Write one
   sentence about what you noticed.
6. **Close (5 min).** Say it together: "taste and smell team up to make flavor." Two
   senses together give a fuller picture than one alone.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use only one familiar food and guide the pinch-and-taste step by step;
  accept spoken or drawn answers instead of written ones.
- **Extension:** Ask learners to compare two foods and guess which smell goes with
  which taste, or to name another pair of senses that might team up.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation/self):** Can the learner describe, in their own words or
  drawing, that smell and taste work together to give a fuller picture of a food?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The nose-and-tongue "team" drawing with its one sentence,
  kept in the learner's portfolio.

## Home connection

At your next meal, ask someone at home to pinch their nose and taste one bite, then let
go — and tell them what you learned about taste and smell teaming up.
