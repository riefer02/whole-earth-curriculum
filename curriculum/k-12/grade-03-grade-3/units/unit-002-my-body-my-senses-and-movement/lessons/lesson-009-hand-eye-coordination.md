# Lesson 9 — Hand–Eye Coordination

## Summary

Learners build hand-eye coordination — the teamwork of their eyes and hands. They
practice tossing and catching a soft ball, then aiming it at a target from a distance
that fits them. Eyes guide the hands, and practice makes the team stronger.

## Objectives

- Improve hand-eye coordination through games and tasks such as tossing, catching, and
  aiming at a target. _(D03.S2.03.02)_

## Connection

Have you ever tossed a ball into a basket, or caught one a friend threw to you? Your
eyes and hands are working as a team: your eyes tell your hands where the ball is, and
your hands move to meet it. This is hand-eye coordination, and it is how people play
tossing and aiming games in every corner of the world — bocce in Italy, pétanque in
France, and the hoop-and-stick and stone games children play across Africa, the
Americas, and Asia — in a courtyard, a beach, a field, or a schoolyard.

## Materials

- Soft balls or beanbags
- A target (bucket, hoop, or chalk circle)
- Floor markers for a throwing line

## Preparation

- Set up a throwing line and one or more targets at a reachable distance.
- Set out a range of soft balls and beanbags so each learner can choose what fits.
- Clear the throwing lane so no one is in the path of a throw.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Prepare the targets and line, then model a gentle toss-and-
catch and an aimed throw slowly before learners try (explicit modeling, then guided
practice). This builds on Grade 2's throw-and-catch and now adds aiming — the eyes and
hands working together, which also returns to the unit's senses-teaming idea. The
skill, not the score, is the goal: celebrate control and growing accuracy, never
distance or speed, and never rank learners. Honor every body: a learner who uses a
wheelchair throws and catches seated, a learner who needs a larger or softer ball or a
scoop uses one, and every version counts fully. Offer adapted versions as real
choices. Supervise throws so everyone faces the same direction and no one is hit; pair
learners so a confident partner can toss gently to one still learning.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** You have learned that senses team up. Today your eyes and hands
   will team up in a game of toss, catch, and aim.
2. **Warm up (5 min).** Roll a ball between your hands, then toss it a little up and
   catch it with both hands. Keep your eyes on the ball all the way — your eyes are the
   guide.
3. **Toss and catch (10 min).** Toss the ball up and catch it with both hands. Watch it
   all the way in, and catch it softly. Try a little higher, then a little lower. With
   a partner, toss gently back and forth, eyes on the ball every time.
4. **Aim at a target (15 min).** Stand at the line and look at the target. Hold the
   ball with both hands, look, and throw it toward the target. Your eyes tell your
   hands where to send it. Try again — each try, your eyes and hands get better at
   working together. If you want, step back a little and try from farther.
5. **Check your aim (10 min).** Count your hits in a friendly way — not to win, but to
   notice your own growing accuracy. What helped your aim: looking first, throwing
   softly, trying again?
6. **Close (5 min).** Shake out your hands. Say it together: "my eyes and hands are a
   team." With every try, the team gets stronger.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Stand close to the target, use a large soft ball or beanbag, and catch
  with a scoop or a basket before bare hands.
- **Extension:** Ask learners to aim at a smaller target or from farther back, or to
  toss with one hand and then the other.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation/self):** Can the learner toss and catch a soft ball and aim
  it at a target with growing control, in their own body's way?

## Home connection

At home, toss a rolled-up pair of socks into a basket or box with someone, and tell
them how your eyes and hands are working together as a team.
