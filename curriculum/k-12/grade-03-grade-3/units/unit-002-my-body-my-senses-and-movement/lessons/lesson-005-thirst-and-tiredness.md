# Lesson 5 — Thirst and Tiredness

## Summary

Learners meet the last two body signals of the unit: thirst and tiredness. They learn
the early signs of each — a dry mouth and lips for thirst, heavy eyelids, yawning, and
a slow body for tiredness — and name one helpful response to each. They close by
drawing the two signals.

## Objectives

- Recognize early body signals of hunger, fullness, thirst, and tiredness and say one
  helpful response to each. _(D03.S1.03.02)_

## Connection

Have you ever been playing hard on a warm day and noticed your mouth feels dry and you
really want a drink? That is thirst. Or have you felt your eyelids getting heavy and
your body slow at the end of a long day? That is tiredness. Just like hunger and
fullness, these are your body's signals — and each one has a kind answer.

## Materials

- Signal cards (dry mouth, heavy eyelids and yawning, a water glass, a resting child)
- A cup of clean water per learner
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Print or draw the four signal cards.
- Set out cups of clean water.
- Prepare paper and crayons for the closing drawing.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Prepare the cards and water, then guide the matching and
drawing. This completes the four body signals the unit teaches (hunger and fullness
came in Lesson 4). Model the response to thirst by making water freely available — a
learner may drink whenever their body asks, with no need to ask permission. Keep
tiredness gentle: tiredness is a normal body signal, not laziness, and rest is a real
need, not a reward to be earned. Some learners do not sleep well at home; frame rest
with kindness, never blame. Bodies differ — some need more water or more sleep than
others — and every amount is respected. Drinking water when thirsty is a healthy habit
the World Health Organization and health authorities around the world encourage for
everyone.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think back: last time you learned hunger and fullness. Today
   you will learn two more signals your body sends.
2. **Meet thirst (10 min).** Thirst is your body asking for water. Its early signals:
   your **mouth and lips feel dry**, and you may find it hard to swallow. What is a
   helpful response? **Drink clean water.** Notice the signal early, and answer it.
3. **Meet tiredness (10 min).** Tiredness is your body asking for rest. Its signals:
   your **eyelids feel heavy**, you **yawn**, and your body feels slow. What is a
   helpful response? **Rest or sleep.** Rest is not lazy — it is how your body
   recharges.
4. **Match the signals (10 min).** Sort the four cards into thirst and tiredness, and
   match each to its response: thirst → drink water; tiredness → rest. Point to your
   own mouth for thirst and your own eyes for tiredness.
5. **Draw your two signals (10 min).** Draw your dry mouth asking for water, and your
   heavy eyelids asking for rest. Write one helpful response under each.
6. **Close (5 min).** Say it together: "when I am thirsty, I drink water; when I am
   tired, I rest." Listening to your body is a way of being kind to it.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Introduce one signal at a time and act it out together; accept spoken
  or drawn answers.
- **Extension:** Ask learners to notice how a tired body and a hungry body can feel a
  little alike (low energy), and to name which signal they think they are feeling and
  why.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation/self):** Can the learner name one signal of thirst and one
  signal of tiredness, and a helpful response to each?

## Home connection

At home, notice your mouth and your eyes through the day. When you feel thirsty, drink
water; when you feel tired, rest — and tell someone which signal you noticed.
