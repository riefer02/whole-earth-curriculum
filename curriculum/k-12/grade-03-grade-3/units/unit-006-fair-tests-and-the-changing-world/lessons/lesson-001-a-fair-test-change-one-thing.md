# Lesson 1 — A Fair Test: Change One Thing

## Summary

Last year learners made a prediction and tested it by doing. This year their testing
grows a crucial step: a **fair test**. A fair test changes exactly **one thing** and
keeps **everything else the same**, so that when something different happens, we know
what caused it. Learners plan a fair test together, name the one thing they will
change and the many things they will keep the same, and offer a prediction. They see
that "fair" here means *honest* — not winning.

## Objectives

- Plan and carry out a simple fair test by changing one thing and keeping the rest the
  same. _(D06.S1.03.01)_

## Connection

You want to know: does my toy car roll farther on a smooth floor or a rough mat? If you
change the surface *and* give a harder push *and* use a different car, you will never
know what made the difference. A **fair test** changes only **one thing** — the
surface — and keeps the car, the ramp, and the release the same. Then, if the car rolls
farther on the smooth floor, you can trust that the surface is what changed it. Fair
means honest, the same idea as a fair game where everyone follows the same rules.

## Materials

- One toy car or ball and a ramp (a board or a book to prop up)
- A smooth surface and a rough surface (a floor and a mat, towel, or rug)
- A class "fair test" chart or large paper
- Paper and pencils

## Preparation

- Set up the ramp on the smooth surface and place the rough surface beside it.
- Prepare the chart with two columns: "What stays the same" and "What changes."
- Have the toy car (or ball) and the rough surface ready for a quick worked example.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"), so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. The core skill — *change one thing, keep the rest the same* — is
a foundational inquiry skill, so teach it with a **worked example and guided practice**
before independent planning (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006): you model the ramp
test once, then pairs plan their own. Keep it joyful and honest: a fair test is about
trust, not trickery, and it connects naturally to the fairness learners already care
about in games and sharing (the egalitarian lens). Where a "right" answer is not known
ahead of time — and sometimes we cannot test a thing at all — that is not a failure;
it is the edge of what we can find out. This lesson sets up Lesson 2, where the same
test is carried out and recorded; the two lessons deliberately space the planning and
the doing.

## Procedure

1. **Gather and remember (5 min).** Last year you predicted and tested. What is one
   thing you predicted and tested, and what happened? Today your testing grows up: you
   will plan a **fair test**.
2. **Meet a fair test (10 min).** Watch the worked example: "Does the car roll farther
   on the smooth floor or the rough mat?" A **fair test** changes only **one thing** —
   the surface — and keeps the car, the ramp, and the release the same. Say together:
   what stays the same? What changes?
3. **Plan a fair test together (15 min).** Pick a question you can test: "Does a stone
   slide farther on smooth rock or sand?" "Does warm water melt ice faster than cold?"
   On the chart, list everything you will **keep the same**, and the **one thing** you
   will **change**. Say your **prediction**: what do you think will happen, and why?
4. **Plan your own (15 min).** On your paper, draw three boxes: **My question**, **The
   one thing I change**, and **What I keep the same**. Add a **prediction**. A fair
   test changes one thing and keeps the rest the same.
5. **Close (5 min).** Fair means honest: one thing changes, the rest stay the same. Keep
   your plan — tomorrow you will carry it out and read what the data show.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Plan together on the shared chart; the learner names one thing to change
  and one to keep the same with a partner.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Point to picture cards for "same" and "change," or move
  two cards to show the plan.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Plan a test by touch and sound — a ball rolled on two
  surfaces you can feel — and describe the plan aloud.
- **Support (motor):** Direct a partner to roll or slide the object while you name the
  one change and the things you keep the same.
- **Extension:** Write a second fair test question and explain which thing is hardest to
  keep the same and why.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name the one thing they will change and
  at least two things they will keep the same?
- **Self:** The learner checks their own plan: "Did I change only one thing?"

## Home connection

At home, plan a tiny fair test with a grown-up: "Which cup keeps water warmer — the
covered one or the open one?" Agree on the one thing you will change and what you will
keep the same.
