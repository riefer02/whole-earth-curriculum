# Lesson 6 — Heating and Cooling Change States

## Summary

Water is the perfect teacher for **change of state**. Learners watch the same matter
move between its three states: **heating** melts a solid into a liquid and evaporates a
liquid into a gas, while **cooling** freezes a liquid into a solid and condenses a gas
back into a liquid. They hold ice as it melts, watch a wet mark dry, and draw the circle
that water travels — the same circle happening in kitchens, rivers, and skies everywhere.

## Objectives

- Describe the three states of matter (solid, liquid, and gas) and how heating and
  cooling change them. _(D06.S3.03.01)_

## Connection

You have seen this a hundred times: an ice cube **melts** into water in your warm hand;
a puddle **dries** in the Sun; steam **condenses** into little drops on a cool window.
That is one substance — water — moving between **solid**, **liquid**, and **gas**. Heat
pushes it one way; cold pulls it the other. The same circle is making the rain and the
snow and the clouds above your head, wherever you live.

## Materials

- Ice cubes and a warm spot
- A small cup of water and a cold place or freezer
- A warm, wet mark on a surface
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Have ice cubes, a warm spot, and (if possible) a freezer or very cold place ready.
- Make a wet mark on a smooth surface to watch it dry over the lesson.
- If using a kettle or pan for steam, an adult operates it and keeps hands clear.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Anchor the four words — **melt**,
**freeze**, **evaporate**, **condense** — with concrete, watched examples before the
circle is drawn (concrete before abstract). Model once (worked example: "heat melts
ice"), then let pairs test one change themselves. Note the four changes are reversible
— matter is not created or destroyed here, just rearranged (a first seed of the
conservation idea, deepened in Grade 4). Keep the global lens: the same freezing,
melting, and evaporating waters a rice field, carves a river, and clouds a sky anywhere
on Earth. If boiling water is shown, an adult handles the heat, and learners watch at a
safe distance — no fear, just care. Distinguish the evidence (what we watched happen)
from the words we use to name it; the names are tools.

## Procedure

1. **Gather and remember (5 min).** Last lesson you met the three states: solid, liquid,
   gas. Today: what makes matter change from one to another? The answer is **heat** and
   **cold**.
2. **Heat melts and evaporates (15 min).** Hold an ice cube and watch it **melt** — a
   solid becomes a liquid. Watch a wet mark slowly dry in the warm air — the liquid
   **evaporates** into a gas you cannot see. Say the two heat words: **melt**,
   **evaporate**.
3. **Cold freezes and condenses (10 min).** Put a small cup of water somewhere cold and
   predict what will happen: it will **freeze** into a solid. If you have steam or a
   cold window, watch a gas **condense** back into liquid drops. Say the two cold words:
   **freeze**, **condense**.
4. **Draw the circle (15 min).** Draw water in its three states — ice, water, steam — in
   a circle. Draw arrows with the right words: heating goes melt → evaporate; cooling
   goes freeze ← condense.
5. **Close (5 min).** Heat and cold are the hands that move matter between its states.
   The ice in your cup, the drying puddle, and the clouds in the sky are all the same
   water, changing.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Match two changes (ice melts, water freezes) to their names with a
  partner.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Point to the ice melting or the mark drying; a partner
  says the word.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Feel ice melt in your hand and feel the cool of your
  wet hand drying; name each change by telling.
- **Support (motor):** Direct a partner to hold the ice and wet the mark while you watch
  and name each change of state.
- **Extension:** Trace one water drop's full journey through all four changes and tell
  where each might happen in the world.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name at least two changes of state and
  say whether heat or cold causes each?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The labeled change-of-state circle drawing.

## Home connection

At home, watch a change of state with a grown-up: an ice cube melting in a cup, a wet
cloth drying on a line, or breath fogging a cold window. Name the change together.
