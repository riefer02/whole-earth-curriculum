# Lesson 4 — A Plant's Helping Parts

## Summary

Every plant is built from parts that help it **survive and grow**. Learners meet the
three big ones: **roots** reach down to drink **water** and hold the plant in the
ground; the **stem** carries water up and holds the **leaves** toward the light; and
the **leaves** catch **sunlight** to make the plant's food. They draw and label a plant
and connect each part to plants people grow and eat around the world.

## Objectives

- Describe how the parts of a plant (roots, stem, and leaves) help it survive and grow.
  _(D06.S2.03.02)_

## Connection

Look at any plant you know — a bean in a cup, a tree by the road, rice in a field. It
stands there quietly doing three jobs at once. Its **roots** drink water from the soil
and hold it steady. Its **stem** is like a straw and a tower: it carries water up and
holds the **leaves** up to the light. And the **leaves** catch sunlight to make the
plant's food. Pull up a weed and you can see all three with your own eyes.

## Materials

- A real plant to look at (a potted plant or a pulled-up weed with roots)
- A large plant-parts drawing or poster
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Have a real plant (with visible roots, stem, and leaves) ready for close looking.
- Prepare or draw the labeled plant poster.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Teach the three parts with a **worked
example** — point to the roots, stem, and leaves on a real plant and say each job once —
then let learners point and say them with a partner (guided practice) before drawing
alone. Keep the global lens wide: many staple foods are plant parts — carrots and sweet
potatoes are **roots**, sugarcane and asparagus are **stems** (celery stalks are leaf
stalks, not true stems), spinach and cabbage are **leaves**, and rice and maize are
seeds. Naming foods from many places makes no single
diet the default and honors the way plants feed the whole world. Handle the pulled-up
plant with care and return it or compost it — a small ethical step of respect for a
living thing. Scientific claims (roots take up water and minerals, leaves make food in
sunlight) are well-supported; state them plainly and let learners wonder beyond them.

## Procedure

1. **Gather and remember (5 min).** In the last lesson you traced a food chain, and the
   **producer** was a plant. Today: how does a plant stay alive and grow?
2. **Meet the parts (10 min).** Look at a real plant. **Roots** reach down into the
   soil to drink **water** and hold the plant steady. The **stem** carries water up and
   holds the **leaves** high. The **leaves** catch **sunlight** and make the plant's
   food. Point to each part and say its job.
3. **Act it out (10 min).** Use your body: be the roots reaching down, the stem standing
   tall, the leaves reaching out for the light. Say each job as you do it.
4. **Draw and label (15 min).** Draw a plant with roots, a stem, and leaves. Label each
   part and write or draw one thing it does. "Roots — drink water." "Stem — carries
   water up." "Leaves — catch sunlight."
5. **Close (5 min).** Roots drink, the stem carries, the leaves catch the light. Together
   the parts keep the plant alive — and plants keep us and the whole food chain alive.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Match three cards (roots, stem, leaves) to three jobs with a partner.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Point to each part on the real plant; a partner says its
  job.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Feel a real plant — its roots, stem, and leaves — and
  describe each by touch; a partner labels the drawing.
- **Extension:** Name one food you eat that is a root, one that is a stem, and one that
  is a leaf, and draw all three.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner point to each part and say (or match) one
  thing it does?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The labeled plant-parts drawing.

## Home connection

At home, look at a vegetable or plant with a grown-up. Which part are you eating — a
root, a stem, or a leaf? Say what that part does for the plant.
