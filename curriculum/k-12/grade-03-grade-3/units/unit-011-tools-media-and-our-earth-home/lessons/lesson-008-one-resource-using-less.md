# Lesson 8 — One Resource: Where It Comes From, and Using Less

## Summary

Learners trace one resource people use every day — **water**, **wood**, or **electricity** —
from **where it comes from** to how it reaches us, and name one way to **use less** of it:
turning off the tap or the light, or reusing what we have. They learn that knowing where a
resource comes from is the first step to using it with care.

## Objectives

- Name one resource people use every day, where it comes from, and one way to use less of
  it. _(D10.S4.03.02)_

## Connection

Turn on a tap and water comes out. But where was that water before? It fell as **rain**,
gathered in a **river** or a **well**, and traveled to your tap. Switch on a light, and the
power came from somewhere too — maybe the sun, wind, water, or fuel. Every resource you use
has a **where it comes from**. And every one of them can be used a little less — turn off
the tap, switch off the light, reuse what you have. Knowing where a thing comes from is the
first step to caring for it.

## Materials

- "Where it comes from" picture cards (a resource traced from source to use, with a "use less" action)
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Prepare the picture cards: water (rain → river or well → tap), wood (tree → wood →
  table), electricity (power source → wire → light), each with a "use less" action (turn
  off the tap, reuse the wood, switch off the light).
- Set out paper and crayons.
- Have ready a cup of water or a real leaf to trace by feel.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). This is the Grade 3 upgrade of Grade 2's
"name one way to save water or energy": now learners also name **where the resource comes
from**. The three-part frame — *name the resource, where it comes from, one way to use
less* — is a foundational skill, so model it once and guide practice before learners work
independently (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006). Keep the tone gentle and empowering: this
is agency, not guilt. A learner uses less *because they understand* the resource, not
because they are told to obey. Use examples with no wealth assumption and no blame: a
village well and a city tap both trace back to rain; a family with little water already
saves carefully, and the lesson honors that. Distinguish evidence from values: "water comes
from rain, rivers, and wells" is a fact; "we should turn off the tap" is a value of care —
we name both. Honor every body: describe each card and let a learner who is blind or has
low vision feel a cup of water or a leaf and name where it comes from; a non-speaking
learner points to the source and to the "use less" action; acting out turning off a tap or
a light works for learners who learn best by moving.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Everything you use comes from somewhere. Today you pick one resource
   and trace it back to where it comes from.
2. **Meet the three-part frame (10 min).** Watch the worked example: **water** comes from
   **rain, rivers, and wells**; it reaches us through a **tap**; one way to use less is to
   **turn off the tap** when we are done. Say the frame: "___ comes from ___. One way to
   use less is ___."
3. **Trace more resources (10 min).** For each card, name the resource, where it comes
   from, and one way to use less: **wood** comes from **trees** — reuse it; **electricity**
   comes from power sources like sun, wind, water, or fuel — **switch off the light**.
4. **Choose and draw (15 min).** Choose one resource you use every day. Draw where it comes
   from and one way to use less of it. Write or say the frame: "___ comes from ___. One way
   to use less is ___."
5. **Share (5 min).** Tell a partner your three-part answer — the resource, where it comes
   from, and how to use less. Listen to theirs.
6. **Close (5 min).** Knowing where a resource comes from is the first step to caring for
   it. You are not just a user of resources — you are their steward.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use the frame "___ comes from ___. One way to use less is ___," completing it
  together aloud for one resource.
- **Blind/low vision:** Feel a cup of water or a leaf, and say where it comes from and one
  way to use less; a partner scribes the drawing.
- **Deaf/hard of hearing / non-speaking:** Point to the source and to the "use less" action
  on the cards; the learner's own drawing may use pictures instead of words.
- **Motor differences:** Direct a partner's hands to draw while the learner says or points
  to the resource, its source, and the "use less" action.
- **Extension:** Name a second resource and a second way to use less, or explain why using
  less of what runs out matters most.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name one resource people use every day,
  where it comes from, and one way to use less of it?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The "resource, where it comes from, use less" drawing with the
  completed frame.

## Home connection

At home, choose one resource your family uses — water, wood, or electricity — and ask a
grown-up where it comes from. Together, name one way your family can use a little less of
it this week, and try it.
