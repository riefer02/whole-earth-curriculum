# Lesson 1 — Simple Machines: Lever, Wheel, and Ramp

## Summary

Learners meet three **simple machines** — the **lever**, the **wheel**, and the **ramp** —
and tell how each makes a job easier. A **lever** (a stick over a stone, a seesaw) lifts a
heavy thing with less effort; a **wheel** lets a load **roll** instead of slide; a **ramp**
lets a load move up a slope instead of being lifted straight up. They learn that a simple
machine is not magic and not scary: it is a small thing, made by people, that changes how
force is used so a job feels easier.

## Objectives

- Name a simple machine, such as a lever, wheel, or ramp, and tell how it makes a job
  easier. _(D10.S1.03.01)_

## Connection

Think of a time something felt too heavy or too hard: a rock too heavy to lift, a box too
heavy to drag across the ground, a load too heavy to carry up steps. People long ago found
three small helpers. A **lever** — a stick over a stone — lifts the rock. A **wheel** lets
the box roll. A **ramp** lets the load roll up a slope. You have seen all three: a seesaw in
a park, a cart in a market, a slope up to a door. Each one turns a hard job into an easier
one.

## Materials

- Simple-machine picture cards (a lever, a wheel, a ramp — each with a job it makes easier)
- A real lever, wheel, and ramp to try (a ruler on a pencil, a toy cart, a book propped as a ramp)
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Prepare or draw the three simple-machine cards: a lever lifting a rock (and a seesaw), a
  wheel on a cart or wheelbarrow, and a ramp up to a step or a lorry.
- Set out one real model of each: a ruler balanced on a pencil (lever), a small cart or a
  rolling cylinder (wheel), a book propped on a block (ramp).
- Clear space to act out lifting, rolling, and sliding.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Keep the message gentle and true: a simple
machine is a **wonderful thing people make**, but it is neither magic nor anything to fear.
It does not create strength out of nothing — it **changes how force is used** so a job
feels easier (a lever changes where you push; a wheel changes sliding into rolling; a ramp
spreads the lift over a longer, gentler path). This is a foundational idea, so **model each
machine, then guide practice** before learners work independently (Kirschner, Sweller &
Clark, 2006). Honor every tool and every people equally: a lever, a wheel, and a ramp are
used by people everywhere, and none is "more modern" or "better" than another — a hand cart
in one market and a lorry ramp in another solve the same kind of problem. Honor every body:
a learner who is blind or has low vision can feel each machine (the ruler tipping on a
pencil, the wheel rolling, the book's slope) and name the job it makes easier by feel; a
non-speaking learner points to the machine and to the job; acting out lifting, rolling, and
sliding works for learners who learn best by moving. Supervise all handling. Note for later:
we are not ranking machines — the wheel is not "better" than the lever; each is the right
tool for its own job.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of something that felt too heavy or too hard to move. Today you
   meet three small helpers people invented to make such jobs easier.
2. **Meet the lever (8 min).** A **lever** is a stiff bar that tips on a point. Watch or try
   one: a stick over a stone, or a ruler on a pencil. Push down on the long end and the
   short end lifts the heavy thing. Say it: "A lever lifts a heavy thing more easily."
3. **Meet the wheel (8 min).** A **wheel** is a circle that rolls on an axle. Watch or try a
   cart. A load on wheels **rolls** instead of dragging. Say it: "A wheel lets a heavy load
   roll, so it is easier to move."
4. **Meet the ramp (8 min).** A **ramp** is a slope. Watch or try a book propped as a ramp.
   Instead of lifting a load straight up, you roll or slide it up the slope. Say it: "A
   ramp lets a load move up a slope instead of being lifted straight up."
5. **Match machine to job (8 min).** With the cards, match each simple machine to the job it
   makes easier. Say it in a full sentence: "The lever makes lifting the rock easier."
6. **Draw one machine (6 min).** Draw one simple machine — lever, wheel, or ramp — and the
   job it makes easier. Write or say one sentence: "The ___ makes ___ easier."
7. **Close (2 min).** A lever, a wheel, and a ramp are simple machines. They are not magic
   and not scary — they change how you push or pull so a hard job feels easier. People
   everywhere use them.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Name one machine and its job together aloud, using the frame "The ___ makes
  ___ easier," while holding the real model.
- **Blind/low vision:** Feel each machine (the tipping lever, the rolling wheel, the slope of
  the ramp) and name the job it makes easier by shape, motion, and weight.
- **Deaf/hard of hearing:** Share each machine with sign, pictures, and acting; the learner
  signs or points to the machine and to the job it makes easier.
- **Non-speaking:** Point to the machine and to the job it makes easier, while a partner
  says the full sentence aloud.
- **Motor differences:** Choose the machine and direct a partner's hands to move it and draw
  it, while the learner says or points to the job; use big, light models placed within reach.
- **Extension:** Name a second job the same simple machine helps, or find a lever, a wheel,
  and a ramp working together in one real tool (a wheelbarrow is a wheel plus a lever).

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name a simple machine (lever, wheel, or ramp)
  and tell how it makes a job easier?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The "simple machine and its job" drawing with a sentence.

## Home connection

At home, find one simple machine — a lever, a wheel, or a ramp — in a room or outside, and
tell a grown-up the job it makes easier. Ask the grown-up which simple machine they use the
most and why.
