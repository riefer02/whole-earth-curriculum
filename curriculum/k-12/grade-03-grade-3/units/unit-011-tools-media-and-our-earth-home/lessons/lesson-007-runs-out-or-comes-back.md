# Lesson 7 — Runs Out or Comes Back

## Summary

Learners tell a natural resource that can **run out** from one that **comes back**. Coal,
oil, and metal are made over millions of years and can be used up; sunlight, wind, rain
(water), and plants that regrow keep coming back. They sort resources into two columns and
learn that what runs out needs careful use — a first step of stewardship.

## Objectives

- Tell the difference between a natural resource that can run out and one that comes back,
  such as sunlight, with an example of each. _(D10.S4.03.01)_

## Connection

Think of the things you use every day — water to drink, food to eat, things made of metal
or plastic, light and warmth. Where do they come from? All of them come from the Earth. Some
of the Earth's gifts **come back** on their own: the sun rises every morning, the wind
blows, the rain falls, and a tree can grow again. Others **run out**: coal, oil, and metal
were made over millions of years, and once we use them up, they are gone for a very long
time. Knowing the difference is the first step of caring for our home.

## Materials

- Resource cards to sort ("runs out" and "comes back")
- A two-column "runs out / comes back" chart
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Prepare resource cards: *runs out* — coal, oil, metal; *comes back* — sunlight, wind,
  rain (water), a tree that regrows.
- Prepare the two-column chart labeled "runs out" and "comes back."
- Set out paper and crayons.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Keep the tone hopeful, not frightening:
this is the first step of **stewardship**, not a warning of doom. The core idea is two
kinds of natural resource: one that **can run out** (coal, oil, gas, metal — made over
millions of years, not replaced in a human lifetime) and one that **comes back** (sunlight,
wind, rain/water, plants that regrow). Use the standard's own example — **sunlight** — as
the anchor for "comes back." Be careful and honest at the edges: some "comes back" resources
(clean water, trees) only come back if we use them slowly enough — but at Grade 3 we keep
the two kinds clear and add that gentle note, saving "use it so it lasts" for Grade 4. Model
the sort once, then guide practice (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006). Use examples from
many places — a sunny field, a windy coast, a forest, a mine — so no single place is the
default, and never imply one place wastes while another saves. Distinguish evidence from
values: "coal is made over millions of years" is a fact; "we should use less" is a value of
care — both are welcome, and we name which is which. Honor every body: describe each
resource for a learner who is blind or has low vision (feel a stone for metal, a leaf for a
regrowing plant, sunlight and wind on the skin); a non-speaking learner points to the
"runs out" or "comes back" column; acting out the sun rising and coal being used up works
for learners who learn best by moving.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Everything you use comes from the Earth. Today you learn that some
   of the Earth's gifts run out, and some come back.
2. **Meet the two kinds (10 min).** A resource that **comes back** keeps returning: the
   **sun** rises every day, the **wind** blows, the **rain** falls, and a **tree** can grow
   again. A resource that **runs out** is used up and not replaced in a human lifetime:
   **coal**, **oil**, and **metal** were made over millions of years.
3. **Sort the resources (12 min).** With the cards, place each resource in the "runs out"
   or "comes back" column. Say it in a sentence: "Sunlight comes back — it rises every
   morning." "Coal runs out — it was made over millions of years."
4. **Make the gentle note (5 min).** Some things that come back — like clean water and
   trees — only come back if we use them slowly and carefully. We will practice that next
   lesson.
5. **Draw one of each (10 min).** Draw one resource that runs out and one that comes back.
   Label each: *runs out* or *comes back*.
6. **Close (3 min).** Knowing what runs out and what comes back is the first step of caring
   for our Earth home. What runs out needs our care most of all.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Sort two resources together aloud — one "runs out" and one "comes back" —
  using the frame "___ runs out because ___; ___ comes back because ___."
- **Blind/low vision:** Feel real samples (a stone for metal, a leaf for a regrowing plant)
  and feel sunlight and wind on the skin; name which runs out and which comes back.
- **Deaf/hard of hearing / non-speaking:** Sort cards by pointing and sign or point to
  "runs out" or "comes back" for each resource.
- **Motor differences:** Direct a partner's hands to sort and draw while the learner says
  or points to each resource and its column; use big, light cards.
- **Extension:** Explain why a resource that runs out needs careful use, and name one
  thing that comes back that we still must use slowly (water, trees).

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner tell a natural resource that can run out
  from one that comes back, and give an example of each (such as coal and sunlight)?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The "runs out / comes back" drawing with labeled examples.

## Home connection

At home, find one thing made of metal or plastic (a resource that runs out) and one thing
that comes back (sunlight through a window, rain, a plant growing). Tell a grown-up which
is which.
