# Lesson 2 — One Job, Then and Now

## Summary

Learners compare how one job was done **long ago** and how it is done **today** and name
what **changed**. The job of getting water was once done by carrying a jug by hand and is
now often done by turning a tap; the job of grinding grain was once done with a hand stone
and is now often done by a mill. They notice that what changed is the **tool or machine**,
the **speed**, and often the need for **electricity** or running water — and that the old
way still works in places without power. New is not better; it is just different.

## Objectives

- Describe how one tool or machine changed the way a job was done long ago compared with
  today. _(D10.S1.03.02)_

## Connection

How did your family get water today — from a tap, a well, a pump, or a river? Long ago,
almost everyone carried water by hand in a jug or a bucket. The job is the same — getting
water — but the tool changed the way it is done: a tap and pipes bring water right to the
house, so the job is faster and easier. That is what it means to compare a job then and
now.

## Materials

- Then-and-now picture cards (pairs for the same job)
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Prepare or draw then-and-now pairs for one job each: water carried by hand → a tap;
  grain ground by hand → a mill; clothes washed by hand → a washing machine; grain cut by
  hand → a cutting machine.
- Prepare to say, for each pair, the shared job and what changed.
- Clear space to act out a job the old way and the new way.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Hold the unit's stance carefully: teach
technology with **neither worship nor fear**. A new machine is not "better" just because it
is new — it often makes a job faster or easier, and it often needs electricity, running
water, or fuel that not everyone has. An old tool is not "worse" just because it is old — it
works anywhere and never runs out of power. Both are real tools made by people to solve a
problem (retrieval: link back to Lesson 1's simple machines). Use examples from many places
so no one home is the default: a hand pump in a village, a water jug carried from a river, a
washing machine in a city flat. Distinguish evidence from values gently: "the machine is
faster" is a fact; "the machine is better" is a value judgment — and at this age we hold
them apart. Honor every body: a learner who is blind or has low vision can feel the old
tool and hear the new one described; a non-speaking learner can point to "then" or "now"
and to a picture for "faster" or "needs electricity." Keep the change concrete, not scary —
a job made easier, not a world lost.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Remember Lesson 1: tools make jobs easier. Sometimes there is an
   **old way** and a **new way** to do the same job.
2. **Meet then and now (15 min).** For each pair, name the shared job first: "Both the jug
   and the tap bring water." Then say what changed: "The tap is faster and easier, and it
   needs pipes and power. The jug works by hand, anywhere."
3. **Sort then and now (10 min).** With the cards, put each picture in the "long ago" pile
   or the "today" pile. For one pair, say the job and what changed.
4. **Say it in a sentence (5 min).** Finish the frame together: "Long ago people ___.
   Today people ___. What changed is ___."
5. **Draw one job, then and now (8 min).** Draw one job done the old way and the new way,
   and write or say what changed.
6. **Close (2 min).** A new tool changes the way a job is done — often faster and easier,
   often needing power. New is not better and old is not worse. Both ways matter.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Compare one pair only, saying the job and one change together aloud with the
  frame.
- **Blind/low vision:** Feel the old tool and hear the new one described; tell what changed
  (faster, needs electricity) by voice.
- **Deaf/hard of hearing / non-speaking:** Sort cards by pointing, and signal "faster,"
  "easier," or "needs electricity" with a sign or picture to say what changed.
- **Motor differences:** Direct a partner's hands to sort and draw while the learner says
  or points to the job and the change; use big, light cards.
- **Extension:** Tell why the old way still matters where there is no electricity, and name
  one job your family does by hand that machines could also do.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner describe how one tool or machine changed the
  way a job was done long ago compared with today, and name what changed?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The "then and now" drawing with the "what changed" sentence.

## Home connection

At home, ask a grown-up how one job was done long ago — getting water, cooking, washing,
traveling — and how it is done now. Tell them what changed and which way your family uses.
