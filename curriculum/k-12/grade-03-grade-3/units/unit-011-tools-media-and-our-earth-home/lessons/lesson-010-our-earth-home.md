# Lesson 10 — Our Earth Home: Tools, Media, Living Things, and Care

## Summary

This capstone brings the whole unit together. Learners build a four-part "Earth home"
picture: a **simple machine** and the job it makes easier, with a then-and-now change; a
**message** with a purpose and audience, told as advertisement or information; a **food
chain** with the direction energy moves, and plants making their own food; and a **resource**
with where it comes from and one way to use less. They celebrate that tools, media, living
things, and care all belong to one shared home.

## Objectives

- Name a simple machine, such as a lever, wheel, or ramp, and tell how it makes a job
  easier. _(D10.S1.03.01)_
- Describe how one tool or machine changed the way a job was done long ago compared with
  today. _(D10.S1.03.02)_
- Tell the difference between an advertisement and information, and give one clue that
  something is an ad. _(D10.S2.03.01)_
- Explain that a message has a purpose and an audience, and name both for a simple
  message. _(D10.S2.03.02)_
- Build or draw a simple food chain with a plant and an animal and show the direction
  energy moves. _(D10.S3.03.01)_
- Explain that green plants make their own food using sunlight, and that all living things
  depend on plants or on animals that eat them. _(D10.S3.03.02)_
- Tell the difference between a natural resource that can run out and one that comes back,
  such as sunlight, with an example of each. _(D10.S4.03.01)_
- Name one resource people use every day, where it comes from, and one way to use less of
  it. _(D10.S4.03.02)_

## Connection

You have spent this unit learning four big things: **tools** are simple machines that make
jobs easier and change over time; **media** carries advertisements or information, and every
message has a purpose and an audience; **living things** are linked in food chains, with
plants making their own food from sunlight; and we can **care** by knowing what runs out,
what comes back, and using less. Today we put them all together, because they all live in
the same home — the Earth we share.

## Materials

- Large paper for a class "Earth home" picture (a big circle divided into four parts)
- Paper, crayons, glue, and found natural materials

## Preparation

- Draw or mark a big circle — the Earth — divided into four parts, labeled: Tools, Media,
  Living, Care.
- Gather paper, crayons, glue, and found natural materials for adding to each part.
- Prepare to recall one thing from each strand with each learner.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). This is a **celebration, not a test.** Help
each learner recall one thing from each of the four strands and add it to the shared Earth
home picture. Keep the tone joyful, hopeful, and inclusive — every machine, every message,
every living thing, and every act of care is welcome, and none is ranked. Hold the unit's
whole stance gently: technology met with neither worship nor fear, media met with judgment
not suspicion, the Earth met as home not storehouse, and learners met as helpers not
saviors. Let retrieval do its quiet work: each learner naming one thing from each strand is
spaced practice of the whole unit. Honor every body: a learner who is blind or has low
vision may add a found object by feel to each part while a partner describes the part; a
non-speaking learner adds marks and points while a partner names them; a learner with motor
differences directs a partner's hands. Every body is honored, and every body's contribution
counts.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Look at the big Earth circle. This is our shared home. Today we fill
   it with everything we learned.
2. **Part one — Tools (10 min).** Name one simple machine — a lever, a wheel, or a ramp —
   and the job it makes easier; then say one way a tool changed a job long ago compared
   with today. Draw or place a mark in the "Tools" part.
3. **Part two — Media (10 min).** Name one message, its purpose and audience, and say how
   you know it is an advertisement or information (give one clue). Draw a mark in the
   "Media" part.
4. **Part three — Living (10 min).** Draw a food chain with a plant and an animal and
   arrows showing the direction energy moves; then say how a green plant makes its own food
   from sunlight, and who depends on it. Draw them in the "Living" part.
5. **Part four — Care (10 min).** Name one resource that runs out and one that comes back,
   then name one everyday resource, where it comes from, and one way to use less of it. Add
   a mark to the "Care" part.
6. **Celebrate (5 min).** Step back and look at the full Earth home. Tools, media, living
   things, and care — all together, in one home, cared for by many hands.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Work on one part of the Earth at a time, or let the learner add just one mark
  to a shared class picture with a partner's help.
- **Motor differences:** Direct a partner's hands to add each mark while the learner says
  what it stands for; the choosing and the saying are the contribution.
- **Blind/low vision:** Add found objects by feel to each part while a partner describes the
  part and names what the learner chose.
- **Deaf/hard of hearing / non-speaking:** Share each part with sign, pictures, and acting;
  the learner signs or points to the mark they add and to the part it belongs to.
- **Extension:** Make a small four-page "Earth home" book, one page for each part, and add a
  fifth page about a new way the learner wants to help.

## Assessment

- **Portfolio:** The four-part "Earth home" picture or book becomes a portfolio artifact
  capturing the whole unit.
- **Observation (formative):** Can the learner recall one thing from each of the four
  strands — a simple machine and its job (with a then-and-now change), a message with a
  purpose and audience (ad vs information), a food chain with the direction energy moves
  (and plants making food), and a resource with where it comes from and one way to use
  less?

## Home connection

Show your "Earth home" picture to a grown-up and tell them the four things you learned: a
simple machine and its job, a message with a purpose and audience, a food chain with the
direction energy moves, and one resource with a way to use less. Ask them what small act of
care they like to do for the Earth.
