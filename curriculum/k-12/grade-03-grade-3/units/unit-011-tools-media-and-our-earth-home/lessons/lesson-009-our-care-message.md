# Lesson 9 — Our Care Message: Purpose, Audience, and Help

## Summary

Learners bring the unit's two halves together by **making a message that cares**. Each
writes a "care message" that names **one resource**, **where it comes from**, and **one way
to use less** of it — with a clear **purpose** (to persuade people to help) and a real
**audience** to share it with. Making a message teaches the message, and sharing it makes
care real.

## Objectives

- Explain that a message has a purpose and an audience, and name both for a simple
  message. _(D10.S2.03.02)_
- Name one resource people use every day, where it comes from, and one way to use less of
  it. _(D10.S4.03.02)_

## Connection

You have learned two big things this unit: how to see a message's **purpose and audience**,
and how to **care** for a resource by knowing where it comes from and using less. Today you
put them together. You will make a small message that helps people care — naming a resource,
where it comes from, and one way to use less, made for a real audience. A message that
cares tells the truth and asks people to help, not to buy.

## Materials

- Care-message template (space for one resource, where it comes from, one way to use less, the purpose, and the audience)
- Crayons and pencils

## Preparation

- Prepare or fold the care-message template, one per learner.
- Model one example aloud: "Water comes from rain, rivers, and wells. One way to use less is
  to turn off the tap. My purpose is to persuade, and my audience is my family."
- Set out crayons and pencils.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). This is a **making and sharing** day, not a
test. The heart of it is that a message can *care*: it can name a resource, its source, and
one way to use less, made for a real audience. Retrieval: learners recall Lesson 4 (purpose
and audience), Lesson 7 (runs out vs comes back), and Lesson 8 (one resource, where it comes
from, use less). Keep the media stance warm — making a truthful, helpful message is the
joyful opposite of fear, and it puts technology to work *caring* rather than *selling*.
Honor all resources and all homes equally: a message about a village well is as worthy as
one about a city tap; a message told aloud and acted out counts just as much as a printed
card. Distinguish evidence from values gently: "water comes from rain" is evidence; wanting
to share the message is a value of care — both are welcome. No wealth assumption: any scrap
of paper, or a message spoken and acted, is enough. Honor every body: a non-speaking learner
can draw and point while a partner writes, or share in sign; a learner who is blind or has
low vision can say the message aloud and have a partner scribe it, and feel the card's
shape; a learner with motor differences can direct a partner's hands to write. Peer check:
partners check each other's message for a clear purpose (persuade) and a real audience.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** You can name a message's purpose and audience, and you can care for
   a resource. Today you make a message that does both at once.
2. **Model one care message (10 min).** Watch or make one together: one resource ("water"),
   where it comes from ("rain, rivers, and wells"), one way to use less ("turn off the
   tap"), the purpose ("to persuade people to help"), and the audience ("my family").
3. **Choose your resource (5 min).** Pick one resource from Lesson 7 or 8 — water, wood, or
   electricity. Say where it comes from and one way to use less.
4. **Make your message (15 min).** Write and draw your care message: the resource, where it
   comes from, one way to use less, the purpose (persuade), and your audience.
5. **Peer check (5 min).** Trade messages with a partner. Does it name a resource, where it
   comes from, one way to use less, and an audience? Give one kind word.
6. **Share (8 min).** Read or act your care message to someone — a partner, a small group,
   or the class. Tell them the resource, where it comes from, one way to use less, and who
   it is for.
7. **Close (2 min).** A message that cares tells the truth, names its purpose, and helps
   real people. You are now a maker of caring messages.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use the frame "___ comes from ___. One way to use less is ___. My purpose is
  to persuade, and my audience is ___," with a partner's help.
- **Blind/low vision:** Say the message aloud and have a partner scribe it; feel the card
  and share it by voice.
- **Deaf/hard of hearing / non-speaking:** Draw the message and share it in sign or by
  pointing while a partner reads the words aloud.
- **Motor differences:** Direct a partner's hands to write and draw while the learner says
  or points to the resource, its source, the "use less" action, and the audience.
- **Extension:** Add a second way to use less, or check a partner's message and explain why
  its purpose and audience are clear.

## Assessment

- **Formative (peer):** Does the partner's message name a resource, where it comes from, one
  way to use less, and an audience, with a clear purpose?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The finished "care message" becomes a portfolio artifact,
  capturing the media and stewardship threads together.

## Home connection

Show your care message to a grown-up and share the resource, where it comes from, and one
way to use less. Ask the grown-up: what one message of care would they like to send about
the Earth, and who would they send it to?
