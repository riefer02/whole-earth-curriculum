# Lesson 5 — A Food Chain: Where Energy Moves

## Summary

Learners **build or draw** a simple food chain with a **plant** and an **animal** and show
the **direction energy moves** with arrows. The **sun** gives the grass its start, the
**rabbit** gets energy from eating the grass, and the **fox** gets energy from eating the
rabbit. They learn that the arrows point the way energy travels — always from the sun to
the plant, and from the plant to the animals that eat it.

## Objectives

- Build or draw a simple food chain with a plant and an animal and show the direction
  energy moves. _(D10.S3.03.01)_

## Connection

Where does your energy come from? From the food you eat. And where does that food get its
energy? Trace it back: the bread came from wheat, a plant; the wheat grew using sunlight.
Every meal is the end of a **food chain** — a path of energy that starts with the sun, moves
into a plant, and then into the animals (and people) that eat. When you draw a food chain,
the **arrows** show which way the energy is moving.

## Materials

- Food-chain picture cards (sun, grass, a rabbit, a fox)
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Prepare the food-chain cards: sun → grass → rabbit → fox, plus arrow cards showing the
  direction energy moves.
- Prepare examples of plant-eaters (a cow, a rabbit, an elephant) and animal-eaters (a
  lion, an owl, a shark).
- Set out paper and crayons.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). This is the Grade 3 upgrade of Grade 2's
"energy from eating": now learners **build or draw** the chain themselves and are explicit
about **the direction the energy moves**, shown by the arrow. The arrow is the key new idea
and the place beginners make mistakes — it points *from the eaten to the eater* (from grass
to rabbit, from rabbit to fox), the direction the energy travels. Model the arrow once,
then guide practice before learners build their own (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006).
Keep it **concrete and gentle**: energy is what lets a body move, grow, and live; a food
chain is a *story* of energy moving, not a scary story of eating — say "the fox gets energy
from the rabbit," not gory detail. Offer chains from many biomes — grass → rabbit → fox;
sea plants → fish → shark; grass → zebra → lion — so no single place is the default. A
light ethical thread: every living thing takes energy to live, and all of it belongs to one
connected living world (this returns in Lessons 6 and 9). Honor every body: describe each
card in words for a learner who is blind or has low vision; a non-speaking learner places
the cards in order and points the arrow to show the direction energy moves; acting out each
part works for learners who learn best by moving.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** What gives *you* energy to run and play? (Food.) Every animal gets
   energy the same way — by eating. Today you follow that energy along a chain.
2. **Meet the chain (10 min).** Lay out the cards. The **sun** gives the **grass** its
   start. The **rabbit** eats the grass and gets energy from the plant. The **fox** eats
   the rabbit and gets energy from the animal.
3. **Point the arrows (8 min).** The **arrow** shows the direction energy moves. Place the
   arrows so they point *from the eaten to the eater*: grass → rabbit → fox. Say it: "The
   arrow points from the plant to the animal that eats it."
4. **Build your own chain (12 min).** Build or draw a food chain of your own — sun, a
   plant, a plant-eater, an animal-eater — and draw the **arrows** showing which way the
   energy moves. Label each part.
5. **Check the direction (5 min).** Trade with a partner. Do their arrows point the way the
   energy moves — from the plant to the animal that eats it? Give one kind word.
6. **Close (5 min).** A food chain is a path of energy, and the arrow shows its direction:
   from the sun, into the plant, and into the animals that eat. All of it is one connected,
   living world.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Order the given chain (sun → grass → rabbit → fox) and point the arrows
  together, saying "the arrow points from the grass to the rabbit."
- **Blind/low vision:** Order the chain by feel (raised cards or named objects) and say each
  part and which way the energy moves.
- **Deaf/hard of hearing / non-speaking:** Place the cards in order and sign or point the
  arrow to show the direction energy moves; a partner says the sentence.
- **Motor differences:** Direct a partner's hands to lay out the chain, place the arrows,
  and draw, while the learner says or points to the direction energy moves.
- **Extension:** Build a second chain of your own choosing and add a second animal-eater,
  pointing the arrows the whole way.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner build or draw a simple food chain with a
  plant and an animal, and show the direction energy moves with arrows?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The drawn or built food chain with labeled parts and arrows
  showing the direction of energy.

## Home connection

At home, name one food you ate today and trace it back along a food chain: did it come from
a plant, or from an animal that ate a plant? Draw the chain with arrows showing which way
the energy moved.
