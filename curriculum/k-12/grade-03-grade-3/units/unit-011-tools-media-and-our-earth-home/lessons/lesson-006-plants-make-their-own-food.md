# Lesson 6 — Plants Make Their Own Food

## Summary

Learners discover that **green plants make their own food using sunlight** — with water and
air — and that this makes plants the **producers** every animal depends on. Because a
plant's food is where every animal's energy starts, **all living things depend on plants,
or on animals that eat them**. A plant is a producer; every animal, including people, is a
consumer that depends on producers.

## Objectives

- Explain that green plants make their own food using sunlight, and that all living things
  depend on plants or on animals that eat them. _(D10.S3.03.02)_

## Connection

Think of the last thing you ate. If it was bread or rice or fruit, it came from a plant. If
it was meat or milk or an egg, it came from an animal — an animal that ate plants. However
you trace it back, the food always begins with a **green plant** using **sunlight** to make
its own food. Plants do something no animal can do: they make food out of sunlight, water,
and air. That is why every living thing, deep down, depends on plants.

## Materials

- "How a plant makes food" picture cards (sun, a green leaf, water, air, and who depends on the plant)
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Prepare the picture cards: the sun shining on a green leaf, water from the soil, air, and
  the words "the plant makes its own food"; plus a plant-eater (a rabbit) and an
  animal-eater (a fox) that depend on plants.
- Set out a real green leaf or plant to hold in the light.
- Set out paper and crayons.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). This is a **new foundational idea** — the
Grade 3 upgrade of Grade 2's "animals get energy from eating" — so teach it with a worked
example and guided practice before learners draw their own (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark,
2006). Keep the science simple and true: green plants use **sunlight**, **water**, and
**air** to make their own food (this is photosynthesis, but at this age the words "plants
make their own food using sunlight" are enough — do not demand the word *photosynthesis*).
The big claim is that **all living things depend on plants, or on animals that eat them**:
plant-eaters depend directly, animal-eaters depend indirectly (they eat an animal that ate a
plant), and people depend on both. Use examples from many places and biomes — rice fields,
grasslands, sea plants, forests — so no single place is the default; note that sea plants
and tiny green plants in water make food the same way. Honor every body: describe each card
in words for a learner who is blind or has low vision and let them feel a real leaf; a
non-speaking learner points to sun, water, air, and leaf to tell the story; acting out a
plant reaching for light and an animal eating works for learners who learn best by moving.
Hold the ethics gently: plants feed the whole living world, so caring for plants is caring
for every animal — this returns in Lessons 9–10.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Remember Lesson 5: animals get energy from eating. But where does a
   *plant's* energy come from? Plants do not eat. Today you learn what they do instead.
2. **Meet the plant's secret (10 min).** A **green plant makes its own food using
   sunlight**. It takes in **water** from the soil and **air** from around it, and the
   **sunlight** helps it turn these into food. Watch or model it with the cards: sun +
   water + air → the plant's own food.
3. **See who depends on plants (10 min).** The **rabbit** eats the plant and gets energy.
   The **fox** eats the rabbit. Even the fox depends on plants — through the rabbit. Say it:
   "All living things depend on plants, or on animals that eat them."
4. **Find plants in your own food (8 min).** Think of a food you ate. Trace it back: bread
   → wheat (a plant); milk → a cow → grass (a plant). Say the chain: "This food depends on
   plants."
5. **Draw it (10 min).** Draw a green plant making its own food from sunlight (sun + water
   + air → food), and one animal that depends on it. Label the plant "makes its own food."
6. **Close (2 min).** Plants make their own food using sunlight — the only food that does
   not start by eating something else. Every animal, including you, depends on plants. That
   is why green things matter everywhere on Earth.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Tell the story together aloud with the cards — sun + water + air → food — and
  name one animal that depends on the plant.
- **Blind/low vision:** Feel a real leaf and hear the story; say it back: "The plant makes
  its own food using sunlight, and the rabbit depends on it."
- **Deaf/hard of hearing / non-speaking:** Point to sun, water, air, and leaf in order to
  tell the story, and point to the animal that depends on the plant.
- **Motor differences:** Direct a partner's hands to arrange the cards and draw, while the
  learner says or points to sun, water, air, leaf, and the animal.
- **Extension:** Explain that an animal-eater (a fox) still depends on plants, by tracing
  the chain: fox → rabbit → plant.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner explain that green plants make their own food
  using sunlight, and that all living things depend on plants or on animals that eat them?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The drawing of a plant making its own food and an animal that
  depends on it.

## Home connection

At home, look at one food on your table and trace it back to a green plant. Tell a grown-up:
"Even the meat, milk, or eggs we eat begin with a plant making food from sunlight."
