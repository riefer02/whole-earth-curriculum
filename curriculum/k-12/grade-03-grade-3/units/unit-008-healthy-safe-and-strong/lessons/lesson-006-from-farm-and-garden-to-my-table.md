# Lesson 6 — From Farm and Garden to My Table

## Summary

Learners follow food's journey from seed to table — plant, grow, harvest, transport,
sell, cook — and meet the many workers who help it along the way. They learn that every
meal is the work of many hands, and that food travels far or near, with local food
connecting people to the land around them.

## Objectives

- Identify where common foods come from, such as plants, animals, farms, and gardens, and
  how they reach a table. _(D03.S3.03.02)_

## Connection

A seed goes into the soil. Sun and rain help it grow. Someone waters it, tends it, and
later **harvests** it. Someone carries it to a **market** or a shop. Someone buys it and
cooks it. Then it reaches your table. For other foods the journey starts differently — a
fish is caught from a river or the sea, milk comes from herded animals, and wild fruit is
gathered from a tree — but every food still reaches a table by many hands. Every meal is a
long journey made by many hands — and today you will follow that journey from start to
finish.

## Materials

- "Farm to table" picture sequence cards (seed, growing plant, harvest, transport,
  market, table)
- A "food journey" chart (a long arrow with six steps)
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Prepare the sequence cards and the food journey chart.
- Think of one or two local examples of foods grown nearby, so learners can see that some
  food travels a short way and some a long way.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). **Every meal is many people's work.** Lift
up the farmers, gardeners, harvesters, transporters, millers, market sellers, and cooks —
often underpaid and too often invisible — so learners meet food with gratitude and a sense
of justice, not guilt. Teach that food can travel far (by truck, ship, or plane) or near
(from a nearby farm or garden), and that **local** food connects people to the land around
them and often uses less fuel. Honor that food reaches tables in many ways — grown on
farms and gardens, gathered from wild places, fished from water, or herded with animals —
and that each journey is real and worthy, not only the planted-field story. This is where **technology** enters honestly: tools,
irrigation, mills, trucks, and refrigeration help move food to many tables, and they also
use energy and affect the Earth — a seed for later grades, held lightly here. A learner
who is blind or has low vision arranges the cards by feel or retells the journey aloud; a
learner who does not use a speaking voice points or arranges the cards in order; a learner
who is deaf or hard of hearing follows the cards and the acted-out journey from the front.
This lesson completes D03.S3.03.02 (how food reaches a table) and the food-journey chart
is a unit portfolio artifact.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Last lesson you learned where food begins. Now follow one food all
   the way to your table.
2. **Meet the journey (15 min).** Arrange the cards in order on the arrow. **Seed** — a
   tiny beginning in the soil. **Grow** — sun, water, and care help it rise. **Harvest** —
   people gather what grew. **Transport** — trucks, carts, boats, or trains carry it.
   **Market** — it is sold in a market or shop. **Table** — someone cooks it and you eat.
   Some foods start their journey differently — a fish is caught, milk is milked from
   herded animals, wild fruit is gathered from a tree — but each still travels to a table
   by many hands. Point to each step.
3. **Who helps along the way? (10 min).** A farmer plants and tends. A harvester gathers.
   A driver or carrier moves it. A miller grinds grain into flour. A seller offers it. A
   cook prepares it. Every meal is the work of many hands — people who deserve our thanks
   and fair pay.
4. **Far or near (10 min).** Some food travels a long way — across seas and mountains. Some
   comes from nearby — a **local** farm or garden, just down the road. Local food connects
   you to the land and people around you, and it often uses less fuel. Both kinds feed us.
5. **Draw one journey (5 min).** Choose one food and draw its journey from origin to
   table, with the people who help.
6. **Close (5 min).** Say it together: "My food travels by many hands, from seed to table.
   I thank the land and the people who feed me."

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Arrange just three steps (seed, grow, table) first; accept spoken, drawn, or
  gestured retellings.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Arrange the cards in order and point to each worker.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Retell the journey aloud as a story, with the learner
  naming each step in order.
- **Extension:** Trace a real food from your home through all six steps, and name one thing
  that would change if the food were grown nearby.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner put the food journey in order and name at
  least one person who helps food reach a table?
- **Portfolio:** The "food journey" drawing becomes a unit portfolio artifact.

## Home connection

At home, choose one food and ask a grown-up where it came from — a nearby farm or garden,
or a faraway place. Trace its journey from origin to your table and thank the people who
grew and carried it.
