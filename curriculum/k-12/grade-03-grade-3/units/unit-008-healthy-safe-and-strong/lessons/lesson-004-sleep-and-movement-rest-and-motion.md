# Lesson 4 — Sleep and Movement — Rest and Motion

## Summary

Learners complete the four body needs by learning why sleep and movement matter: sleep
restores and mends the body, and movement strengthens the heart, muscles, and bones and
lifts the mood. They feel both in their own bodies — moving, then resting. No body or pace
is judged, because every body moves and rests in its own way.

## Objectives

- Describe what a body needs to stay healthy, such as food, water, sleep, and movement,
  and why each matters. _(D03.S3.03.01)_

## Connection

Think of the last time you were very tired and then slept deeply — you woke up ready
again. And think of running or dancing until your heart beat fast and your skin felt
warm, then how good it felt afterward. **Sleep** and **movement** are two more needs your
body has, and they work like a team: sleep restores you, and movement makes you strong.
Today you will feel both in your own body.

## Materials

- "Rest and motion" picture cards (bedtime, a nap, running, dancing, walking, stretching)
- A "rest and motion" chart with two columns
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Prepare the picture cards and the two-column chart ("sleep restores," "movement
  strengthens").
- Clear a safe space for a short movement moment, adapted to every body.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). **No body or pace is judged.** Every body
moves and rests in its own way, and some bodies move with wheels, aids, or help — all
movement counts. Do not turn movement into a contest or tie it to body size; the goal is
the felt sense of moving and resting. Sleep needs vary, too — most children aged 6–12
need about 9–12 hours a night (American Academy of Sleep Medicine), but families and
bodies differ, and rest is never a rule to be shamed over. A learner who uses a
wheelchair or has motor differences moves in their own way — celebrate it; a learner who
does not use a speaking voice points or acts out rest and motion; a learner who is blind
or has low vision feels the heartbeat after moving and the body slowing at rest; a
learner who is deaf or hard of hearing follows the cards and the demonstration, and can
feel the fast-then-slow heartbeat by touching their chest. This lesson finishes the four
needs and sets up the unit's later food-origins and safety lessons. Health facts: the
World Health Organization recommends regular physical activity for children's health,
and the American Academy of Sleep Medicine recommends about 9–12 hours of sleep for
children aged 6–12.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** You have learned two needs — food and water. Today you meet the
   last two: **sleep** and **movement**.
2. **Why sleep matters (10 min).** When you sleep, your body **restores** itself — it
   mends, grows, and stores what you learned that day. A body that does not sleep enough
   feels tired, cross, and fuzzy. Point to the sleep cards: bedtime, a nap, a quiet rest.
3. **Why movement matters (10 min).** When you move, you make your **heart**, **muscles**,
   and **bones** strong, and you lift your mood. Moving is not a contest and not about how
   a body looks — it is about feeling good and staying strong. Point to the movement
   cards: running, dancing, walking, jumping, stretching.
4. **Feel it in your own body (10 min).** Move for a short time — run in place, jump,
   stretch, or move in your own way. Stop and put a hand on your chest: your heart is
   beating fast and you feel warm. Now sit or lie still and rest. Feel your heart slow and
   your breath soften. That is the team of **motion** and **rest**.
5. **Sort rest and motion (5 min).** Sort the cards into the two columns: sleep restores,
   movement strengthens.
6. **Close (5 min).** Say it together: "Sleep restores me, and movement makes me strong.
   I rest and I move, and I care for my body."

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Do one movement and one rest, then feel the heartbeat together; accept
  spoken, drawn, or gestured answers.
- **Support (motor-difference):** Movement is any movement — rolling, reaching, swaying,
  or moving with a peer; celebrate it and feel the heartbeat at rest.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Point to the cards and act out rest and motion with the
  body.
- **Extension:** Name one more way movement helps your body or mood, and one thing you do
  to get a good sleep.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner say, in their own way, that sleep restores
  the body and movement strengthens it — without any judgment of bodies or pace?
- **Self:** The learner notices their own heartbeat fast after moving and slow after rest,
  and names which they need more of today.

## Home connection

With a grown-up, notice together how your body feels after moving and after resting
today. Name one way you rest well and one way you like to move.
