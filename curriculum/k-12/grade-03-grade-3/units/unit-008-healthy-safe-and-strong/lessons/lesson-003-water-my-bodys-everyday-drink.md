# Lesson 3 — Water — My Body's Everyday Drink

## Summary

Learners deepen Grade 2's water lesson by learning why water is essential: it fills every
cell, carries what the body needs, and cools it down. They notice their own thirst and
drink when thirsty, and they learn, with care and honesty, that clean water is precious
and not available to everyone — so water is honored and never wasted.

## Objectives

- Describe what a body needs to stay healthy, such as food, water, sleep, and movement,
  and why each matters. _(D03.S3.03.01)_

## Connection

Your body is mostly water. Water is in your blood, your tears, your sweat, and every
cell. It carries what your body needs to each part of you, and it cools you when you are
hot. When your mouth feels dry and your body says "drink," that is **thirst** — your
body's way of asking for water. Today you will learn why water matters so much and how to
answer your own thirst.

## Materials

- A clear cup or bottle of water for each learner
- A "where water is in my body" picture
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Ensure there is safe drinking water for learners to drink.
- Prepare the "where water is in my body" picture (water in blood, tears, sweat, every cell).
- Plan for any learner who cannot easily drink on their own — a caregiver, older peer, or a
  cup with a straw may help; the goal is water when thirsty, not drinking alone.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). **Water is a human need and a question of
justice, not a personal failing.** Teach why water matters and how to answer thirst, then
hold the global truth gently: many people do not have clean water to drink, and that is a
fairness problem we can work on — not something to make any learner feel ashamed of their
own home. Never shame a learner for being thirsty or for having little water at home. How
much water a body needs varies with weather, activity, and the person, so "drink when
thirsty" is the rule, not a fixed amount. A learner who cannot drink on their own is
helped without comment; a learner who does not use a speaking voice signs or points
"drink" and shows thirst; a learner who is blind or has low vision feels the cup and the
water's coolness and hears thirst named by body signs; a learner who is deaf or hard of
hearing follows the picture and demonstrations from the front, since every idea is shown,
not only told. The health facts are from the World Health Organization: water is
essential to life, and safe drinking-water keeps bodies well. This lesson deepens Grade
2's water objective (U.02.008) into the unit's "why water matters" look.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Put a hand on your chest and breathe. Your body is mostly water —
   and it needs water every single day to work well.
2. **Why does my body need water? (10 min).** Water fills every cell, carries what your
   body needs to each part of you, helps your body stay cool when it is hot, and washes
   out what your body is done with. Without water, a body quickly feels bad. Look at the
   picture: water is in your blood, your tears, and your sweat.
3. **What is thirst? (10 min).** A dry mouth, a tired feeling, wanting a drink — that is
   **thirst**, your body's way of asking for water. You may be thirsty after playing, in
   hot weather, when you wake up, and during a meal. Point to each thirsty time on the
   picture.
4. **Drink when thirsty (10 min).** Notice your own body right now. Are you thirsty? If
   you are, drink some water. Drinking when thirsty keeps you **hydrated** — your body
   full of the water it needs. If you are not thirsty now, remember the feeling so you
   will know it later.
5. **Water is precious (10 min).** In some places clean water comes from a tap; in others
   from a well or a boiled kettle; in others people must walk far to carry water, and the
   water may not be clean. That is not fair. We honor water by using just enough and never
   wasting it, and we share when there is enough. Draw one way to save water.
6. **Close (5 min).** Say it together: "Water keeps my body well. I drink when I am
   thirsty, and I honor water by not wasting it."

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use the thirsty-body picture to practice one "I'm thirsty" signal; drink
  together, then alone.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Practice a sign or a point for "drink" and "thirsty"; use
  the picture to show thirst.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Name thirst by body feel — a dry mouth, a warm tired
  feeling — and let the learner feel the cup and the water; name the thirsty times aloud.
- **Support (motor-difference):** A caregiver or peer helps hold the cup or offers a
  straw; the learner leads by showing thirst and drinking what they can.
- **Extension:** Draw where water comes from at home and explain one more way to avoid
  wasting it.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner say or show why the body needs water and
  notice or name their own thirst?
- **Self:** The learner drinks when thirsty and names one time their body asks for water.

## Home connection

At home, notice one time your body feels thirsty today and drink water when it does. Ask
a grown-up where your family's water comes from and one way to save it.
