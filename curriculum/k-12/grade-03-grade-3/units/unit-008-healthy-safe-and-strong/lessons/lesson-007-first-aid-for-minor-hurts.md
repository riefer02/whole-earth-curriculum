# Lesson 7 — First Aid for Minor Hurts

## Summary

Learners learn a basic first-aid response for common minor injuries: for a cut or scrape,
tell an adult, wash with clean water, and cover it; for a bruise, cool it and rest. They
practice the steps on a doll or in the air — never on a real body — and learn that telling
an adult always comes first, because some hurts need grown-up or medical help.

## Objectives

- Demonstrate a basic first-aid response for common minor injuries, such as telling an
  adult about a cut, scrape, or bruise. _(D03.S4.03.01)_

## Connection

You are running and you fall. Your knee stings, and there is a small red **scrape**. Or
you bump your arm and later a **bruise** appears. These small hurts happen to everyone,
and there is a calm, careful way to respond: **first aid**. The very first step is always
the same — **tell an adult**. Today you will learn what to do next.

## Materials

- First-aid step cards (cut, scrape, bruise)
- A small first-aid kit to show (clean water, soap, a clean cloth or bandage, a cool
  cloth)
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Prepare the first-aid step cards and gather a small kit to *show* (not to use on real
  bodies).
- Read the facilitator note fully before teaching; keep the tone calm and reassuring, not
  frightening.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). **Reassure, never frighten.** The lesson
covers minor, everyday hurts only — cuts, scrapes, and bruises — and its core message is
empowering: *tell an adult, and let the adult help.* Practice is done on a doll, a stuffed
animal, or in the air, **never on a real body or a real wound**, so no learner is hurt or
embarrassed. Teach the steps plainly: for a cut or scrape — tell an adult, wash with clean
water (soap if available), cover with a clean cloth or bandage; for a bruise — tell an
adult, cool it with a cool cloth, and rest. Always stress that if a hurt is deep, big, or
will not stop bleeding, the adult will decide if more help is needed. A learner who is
blind or has low vision practices the motions hand-over-hand and says the steps aloud; a
learner who does not use a speaking voice signs or points to the step cards; a learner who
is deaf or hard of hearing follows the cards and the demonstration from the front, since
every step is shown, not only told. The first-aid facts follow the guidance of the
International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies (IFRC): clean a minor
wound with clean water and cover it, and cool a bruise. This lesson opens the unit's
safety strand (D03.S4.03.01) and leads into the capstone on safe touch.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Small hurts — a **cut**, a **scrape**, a **bruise** — happen to
   everyone. The calm, careful response to a hurt is called **first aid**.
2. **The first step is always the same (5 min).** For every hurt, the very first thing is
   to **tell an adult**. The adult looks, decides, and helps. You never have to handle a
   hurt all alone.
3. **A cut or a scrape (15 min).** A **cut** is a break in the skin, like from something
   sharp. A **scrape** is skin rubbed off, like from a fall. For both: tell an adult, wash
   the hurt with **clean water** (soap if there is any), and **cover** it with a clean
   cloth or bandage to keep dirt out. Practice the motions in the air or on a doll, in
   order.
4. **A bruise (10 min).** A **bruise** is a dark, tender spot under the skin, from a bump.
   For a bruise: tell an adult, cool it with a cool cloth, and **rest** the hurt part.
   Practice the motions.
5. **When a hurt is bigger (5 min).** If a cut is deep or big, or will not stop bleeding,
   the adult decides and may get a nurse or doctor. That is why telling an adult always
   comes first.
6. **Close (5 min).** Say it together: "For a cut, scrape, or bruise, I tell an adult. I
   wash, cover, cool, and rest. First aid keeps me safe."

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Practice one hurt at a time, with the adult modeling each step first;
  accept spoken, drawn, or gestured answers.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Order the step cards and point to each step for each hurt.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Say the steps aloud in order and practice the motions
  hand-over-hand with a doll.
- **Extension:** Teach the steps to someone younger, and name one hurt that would need a
  nurse or doctor instead of a bandage.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner say, in order, the first-aid steps for one
  minor hurt — with "tell an adult" first — in their own way?
- **Self:** The learner names the hurt they practiced and the very first step for every
  hurt.

## Home connection

With a grown-up, name what you would do for a small cut or scrape at home — tell, wash,
cover — and where your family keeps clean water and a cloth.
