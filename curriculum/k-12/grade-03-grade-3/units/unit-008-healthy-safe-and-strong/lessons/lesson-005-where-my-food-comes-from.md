# Lesson 5 — Where My Food Comes From

## Summary

Learners trace common foods to their origins: plants, animals, farms, and gardens. They
sort foods such as rice, mango, eggs, milk, beans, fish, and honey by where they come
from, and they learn that every food has an origin in the living world — a first step
toward understanding how food reaches a table.

## Objectives

- Identify where common foods come from, such as plants, animals, farms, and gardens, and
  how they reach a table. _(D03.S3.03.02)_

## Connection

The rice in your bowl, the mango in your hand, the egg on your plate — none of them
appeared from nowhere. Every food begins somewhere in the living world: on a **plant**,
from an **animal**, in a **farm**, or in a **garden**. Knowing where food comes from
connects you to the Earth and to the people who grow your food. Today you will trace your
foods to their origins.

## Materials

- Food origin cards (rice, mango, egg, milk, beans, fish, honey, and more)
- Four origin mats labeled "from plants," "from animals," "from a farm," and "from a
  garden"
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Prepare the food origin cards and the four origin mats.
- Note that many foods fit more than one origin (a mango comes from a plant *and* can be
  grown in a garden or on a farm); sorting is for noticing, not for being right.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). **Every food has an origin in the living
world, and no origin is better than another.** The four mats are *one* way to sort, not
the only right one — a mango comes from a plant and can be grown in a garden or on a
farm, and a hen's egg comes from an animal and can be raised on a farm or in a yard.
Celebrate the wide, global range of foods: rice, maize, millet, wheat, beans, lentils,
roots, fruits, fish, eggs, milk, and honey all feed the world. Honor that food reaches
families in more ways than planted farms and gardens — many people gather wild fruits,
greens, and honey, fish rivers, lakes, and seas, or herd animals that move with the
seasons, and these ways of living are as real and worthy as farming. Avoid ranking any
food as more or less important. A learner who is blind or has low vision sorts real or textured
foods by touch; a learner who does not use a speaking voice points or signs; a learner
who is deaf or hard of hearing follows the mats and demonstration from the front, since
every idea is shown, not only told. This lesson opens the unit's D03.S3.03.02
"where food comes from" path and leads into the farm-to-table journey. The framing that
foods originate from plants, animals, farms, and gardens reflects the FAO's account of
food coming from plants and animals raised by people.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of one food you ate today. Where did it begin? Today you
   will find out.
2. **Meet the four origins (10 min).** Some food comes from **plants** — fruits,
   vegetables, grains, seeds, and roots. Some comes from **animals** — eggs, milk, fish,
   and meat. A **farm** is a place where people grow plants and raise animals to feed
   people,    and a **garden** is a smaller place, sometimes at home, where food is grown. And not
   every family's food begins in a planted field — some food is **gathered** from wild
   places (wild fruits, greens, and honey), some is **fished** from rivers and seas, and
   some comes from **herded** animals. These are all real ways food comes to a table.
   Point to each mat as we name it.
3. **Sort foods to their origins (15 min).** Take a card. Which mat does it go on? Say
   why: "Rice comes from a plant — it is a grain." "An egg comes from an animal — a hen."
   "Honey comes from bees." If a food fits more than one mat, put it where it makes sense
   and say why — sorting is for noticing, not for being right.
4. **Every food has a living origin (10 min).** Look at the picture: a plant, an animal,
   a farm, and a garden all point to a plate. Every single food began in the living world.
   There is no food that appeared from nothing.
5. **Draw one origin (5 min).** Draw one food and where it comes from — a plant, an
   animal, a farm, or a garden.
6. **Close (5 min).** Say it together: "Every food comes from the living world — plants,
   animals, farms, and gardens. I am connected to the Earth through the food I eat."

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Sort just four familiar foods first; use real foods from the learner's own
  kitchen. Learners may point, sign, or gesture instead of speaking.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Use real or textured foods and sort by touch, naming
  each food and its origin as it is felt.
- **Extension:** Name one food from each origin (plant, animal, farm, garden) that their
  family eats, and draw a "food origins" map of their own meals.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner place a food into one of the four origins
  and, in their own way, show that every food begins in the living world?
- **Self:** The learner names one food they already knew and one new origin they learned.

## Home connection

At your next meal, point to one food and name where it comes from — a plant, an animal,
a farm, or a garden. Tell a grown-up which origin your food began in.
