# Lesson 2 — Food — Energy to Grow, Play, and Learn

## Summary

Learners recall the food groups from Grade 2 and learn why food matters: it is the body's
fuel, giving energy to grow, play, and learn. Every family's way of eating is honored, and
no food, meal, or body is ranked — the point is noticing that food powers the whole body's
work, whatever a family eats.

## Objectives

- Describe what a body needs to stay healthy, such as food, water, sleep, and movement,
  and why each matters. _(D03.S3.03.01)_

## Connection

Have you ever felt tired and slow, then eaten something and felt ready to go again? That
is food doing its job. Food is your body's **fuel** — like wood for a fire or petrol for a
car, food is what your body burns for **energy** to grow, play, and learn. Today you will
see how food powers every part of your day.

## Materials

- Food group reminder cards (fruits, vegetables, grains, proteins from many places)
- A "food is fuel" picture
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Prepare the food group reminder cards and the "food is fuel" picture.
- Hold the line on no food shaming: food is fuel, and every family's fuel is welcome and
  worthy — there is no "good" or "bad" food here, only food that powers a body.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). **No food moralizing, no "good" or "bad"
foods, and no talk of body size or weight.** Food's job is to give energy, and different
families get that energy from different foods — rice, maize, wheat, millet, beans, roots,
fish, eggs, fruits, and greens all fuel bodies well. A food that is everyday for one
family may be rare for another, and neither is wrong. This lesson retrieves Grade 2's
food groups (U.02.008) and deepens it with the *why*: energy for growth, play, and
learning. A learner who does not use a speaking voice points, signs, or acts out the
energy a food gives; a learner who is blind or has low vision feels real or textured
foods and names the energy each gives; a learner who is deaf or hard of hearing follows
the cards and demonstrations from the front, since every idea is shown, not only told.
Some learners may not have enough food at home — frame food as energy every body
deserves, and respond with care if a learner shares something hard. The health facts are
from the World Health Organization: food provides the energy and nutrients the body needs
to grow and stay healthy.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Remember last year: you sorted foods into **fruits**,
   **vegetables**, **grains**, and **proteins**. This year we learn what all food is for.
2. **Food is fuel (10 min).** Look at the "food is fuel" picture. Just as a fire needs
   wood and a car needs fuel, your body needs food. Food gives your body **energy** — the
   power to move, think, and grow. Without food, a body quickly runs low and feels weak.
3. **What does energy do? (10 min).** Your body uses food energy to **grow** (taller,
   stronger bones and muscles), to **play** (run, jump, laugh), and to **learn** (your
   brain burns energy when you think and read). Point to each one on the picture.
4. **Many fuels, one job (10 min).** Sort the food cards back into the four groups. A
   rice-and-beans meal, a flatbread-and-lentils meal, a fish-and-roots meal — different
   fuels, same job: energy for your body. No meal is better than another; there are many
   ways to fuel a body well.
5. **Draw your fuel (5 min).** Draw one meal your family eats, and under it draw what the
   energy helps you do — grow, play, or learn.
6. **Close (5 min).** Say it together: "Food is my fuel. It gives me energy to grow,
   play, and learn."

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Match just a few foods to "grow, play, or learn" with a reminder card;
   accept spoken, drawn, or gestured answers.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Point to foods and act out the energy each one gives.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Feel real foods and name the energy each gives; describe
   the "fuel" idea aloud as a story.
- **Extension:** Name one food from each of the four groups your family eats, and explain
   what kind of energy (grow, play, or learn) it most helps you with.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner say, in their own way, that food gives the
   body energy to grow, play, and learn — without any ranking of foods or bodies?
- **Self:** The learner names one food they ate and what it helped their body do.

## Home connection

At your next meal, point to the food on your plate and tell a grown-up how it fuels you —
to grow, to play, or to learn.
