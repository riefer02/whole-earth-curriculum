# Lesson 1 — What My Body Needs

## Summary

Learners open the unit by naming the four things every body needs to stay healthy: food,
water, sleep, and movement. They begin to say why each one matters, and they start a
"what my body needs" chart that the unit will keep filling in. The four needs are shown
as equals — none is a choice, and none ranks above another.

## Objectives

- Describe what a body needs to stay healthy, such as food, water, sleep, and movement,
  and why each matters. _(D03.S3.03.01)_

## Connection

Every day, without thinking much about it, you eat food, drink water, sleep, and move.
These are not extras and not treats — they are **needs**. A body that misses any one of
them for long quickly feels bad. Today you will name your body's four needs and why each
one matters, so you can care for the body that carries you through every day.

## Materials

- "Four body needs" picture cards (eating, drinking water, sleeping, moving)
- A large "my body needs" chart with four columns
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Prepare the four "body needs" picture cards and the four-column chart.
- Set a warm, non-judging tone: every body needs these four things, and every body is
  different in how and how much it needs them.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). **The four needs are equals.** Do not rank
them, and do not turn any of them into a rule about amounts, schedules, or body size.
Food, water, sleep, and movement each matter, and different bodies need different amounts
at different times — that range is normal and is never a measure of a person's worth. This
lesson retrieves Grade 2's balanced-meal and water work (U.02.008) and opens the spiral
for this unit's deeper looks at food, water, sleep, movement, and safety. A learner who
does not use a speaking voice acts out or points to each need; a learner who is blind or
has low vision feels a cup, a blanket, and their own moving body to anchor each need; a
learner who is deaf or hard of hearing follows the picture cards and demonstrations from
the front, since every idea is shown, not only told. Some learners may not have steady
access to food, water, or safe sleep at home — frame needs as what every body deserves,
without making any learner feel singled out, and respond with care if a learner shares
something hard. The health facts are from the World Health Organization: health is
supported by adequate food, safe water, sleep, and regular physical activity.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think about everything you did for your body today — you ate
   something, you drank something, you slept last night, and you moved. Those four things
   are your body's needs.
2. **Meet the four needs (10 min).** A **need** is something a body must have to stay
   well. Your body needs **food** for energy, **water** to work, **sleep** to rest and
   restore, and **movement** to stay strong. Point to each picture card as we name it.
3. **Why does each need matter? (15 min).** Food gives your body **energy** to grow,
   play, and learn. Water keeps every part of your body working. Sleep lets your body
   rest, mend, and store what it learned. Movement keeps your heart, muscles, and bones
   strong and lifts your mood. Tell a partner one need and why it matters.
4. **Sort actions into needs (10 min).** Which need is each action? Walking to school is
   **movement**. Eating lunch is **food**. Drinking water is **water**. Going to bed is
   **sleep**. Stand in the circle or point to the card that matches each action.
5. **Start your chart (5 min).** On the big chart, add one thing under each need — one
   food you eat, one time you drink water, one bedtime habit, one way you move. We will
   keep adding to this chart all unit long.
6. **Close (5 min).** Say it together: "My body needs food, water, sleep, and movement.
   I can give my body what it needs."

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Introduce one need at a time and act it out together; accept spoken, drawn,
  or gestured answers.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Point to the need cards and act out each need with the body.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Anchor each need by feel — a cup for water, a blanket
  for sleep, the body in motion for movement — and name the need aloud together.
- **Extension:** Name one more way your family meets each need at home, and add it to the
  chart.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name the four body needs and, in their own
  way, say why one of them matters?
- **Self:** The learner names the need they think about most and one way they meet it.

## Home connection

With a grown-up, name the four things your body needs and point out one way your family
meets each one today — food, water, sleep, and movement.
