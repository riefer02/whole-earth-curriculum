# Lesson 15 — Measuring & Estimating Mass

## Summary

Learners **estimate** and then **measure** how heavy things are, using **grams** and
**kilograms**, and discover that one kilogram equals one thousand grams. The balance
makes **mass** something you can see and compare before you read a number.

## Objectives

- Measure and estimate liquid volume and mass using standard units and solve real
  problems with them. _(D05.S4.03.01)_

## Connection

A market seller weighs a kilogram of rice; a baker weighs grams of yeast; a doctor
checks how much a baby has grown; a cook weighs out spices so the dish is just right.
**Mass** is how much matter a thing holds — how heavy it is — and we measure it in
**grams** and **kilograms**. People have weighed goods at markets since the first
scales were carved, because a fair trade depends on a fair measure. Today you weigh
like a market-keeper: estimate first, then balance to check.

## Materials

- A balance scale or hanger balance
- Five objects to weigh (fruit, stone, cup of seeds, pencil, small bottle)
- A 1-kilogram reference and gram weights

## Preparation

- Give each group a balance, objects, a 1-kg reference, and gram weights.
- Clear a stable surface for balancing.
- Plan a worked example (an apple ≈ 100 g).

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It introduces **mass** with the **gram**
and **kilogram** — the new skill is *estimating* mass by feel and comparison, then
*measuring* with a balance and standard weights. Teach with a **worked example**
(philosophy §13): hold the 1-kg bag, feel an apple against it, then balance the apple
with gram weights and read ≈ 100 g. Note the distinction between mass (how much matter)
and weight (the pull of gravity) — at this age the practical word is "mass," and we
keep it concrete (Van de Walle et al., 2019). **No one's accuracy at estimating
measures their smartness; estimation grows with practice, and "not yet" is
information, never a verdict.** The gram and kilogram are shared international units
that make trade fair everywhere. Measure in the home language. For learners who are
Deaf or hard of hearing, sign "heavier" and "lighter" and point to the tipping pan; for
learners who are blind or low-vision, feel the balance tip and the weights in the
hands. Learners with limited movement direct a partner to add weights. Mastery arrives
at its own pace.

## Procedure

1. **Gather — how heavy? (5 min).** Hold a stone in one hand and a leaf in the other.
   Which is heavier? **Mass** is how much matter a thing holds, and we measure it in
   **grams** and **kilograms**.
2. **Worked example — the 1-kg feel (10 min).** Lift the 1-kilogram bag. That is how
   one kilogram feels. Now hold the apple — is it more or less than a kilogram? Much
   less. Balance the apple against gram weights and read the total: about **100 grams**.
   So **1 kilogram = 1,000 grams**, and an apple is one tenth of that.
3. **Estimate, then measure (15 min).** For each object: **estimate** its mass first —
   about how many grams? Write your estimate. Then **balance** it with gram weights and
   read the measure. How close were you? Record both numbers.
4. **Order by mass (8 min).** Put your objects in order from lightest to heaviest by
   feel, then check with the balance. Were you right?
5. **Check with a friend (5 min).** Trade estimates and measurements. Do you agree?
   Balance again together, kindly, if you differ.
6. **Close (2 min).** Remember: mass is measured in grams and kilograms — 1,000 grams
   make a kilogram — and a fair measure is an honest measure.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Compare "heavier and lighter" against the 1-kg bag only today; add exact
  grams another day.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Feel the balance tip and the weight of each object in
  the hands.
- **Extension:** Find something in the room that is about 1 kilogram, and explain how
  you know by balancing it.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner estimate an object's mass, then measure
  it with a balance and gram weights and read the result?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The learner's estimate-and-measure record, kept as a record
  of measurement.

## Home connection

At home, find something that weighs about 1 kilogram (a bag of rice, beans, or flour)
and compare other things to it — then tell a grown-up which are heavier and lighter.
