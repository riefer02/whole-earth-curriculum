# Lesson 4 — My Goal and My First Step

## Summary

Learners turn a wish into a plan. They set one simple, personal goal — something real
and reachable — and then name the **very first step** toward it, the one small thing
they can do first. Each learner makes a "goal and first step" staircase page, which
joins the year-end portfolio and carries the year's self-regulation work forward.

## Objectives

- Set a simple personal goal and name the first step toward it. _(D01.S3.03.01)_

## Connection

Everyone, everywhere, has wanted something they did not yet have — to read a hard book,
to ride without help, to learn a new game, to be a better friend. A wish becomes a
**goal** when you decide to reach it, and a goal becomes reachable when you name the
**first step**: not the whole climb, just the first small thing. A mountain is climbed
one step at a time, and every step begins with the very first one. Today you set a goal
of your own and name its first step.

## Materials

- A "goal and first step" staircase page
- Crayons and pencils

## Preparation

- Prepare the staircase page: a goal flag at the top, a "my first step" step near the
  bottom, and room to fill in.
- Have the year's earlier goal and calm-tools work ready to look back on (optional).
- Arrange a circle for sharing goals and first steps.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It spirals up from Grade 2's "choose a
calm tool on my own," now into **choosing a direction and its first move**. The
commitments: **a goal is the learner's own, not the adult's**, and **the first step is
small enough to actually take** — a goal too big or too adult-chosen fails the lesson.
Coach learners from "I want to be the best" toward something concrete and near (read
one new book; learn to tie one knot; practice ten kind greetings; hold a pencil the new
way for one week). Honor every goal as equal in worth — a goal about kindness is as
real as a goal about reading. Some learners live with scarcity or instability; keep
goals free of things that cost money. For a learner who is blind or has low vision,
describe the staircase aloud and build it tactually; the goal and step live in words,
not pictures. For a learner who does not use a speaking voice, accept drawing, pointing,
signing, or a partner voicing. The first step is the lesson: naming it is the power.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of one thing you want to be able to do or be better at —
   not a wish for later, but something you could begin soon. That is your **goal**.
2. **Set your goal (10 min).** Write or draw your goal at the top of the staircase. Make
   it small and real: "read one new book," "learn to tie my own knot," "say one kind
   thing to a new person each day this week." It should be *your* goal, not someone
   else's.
3. **Name the first step (10 min).** Now look at the bottom step. What is the very
   first small thing you would do? "Find a book." "Ask for one piece of rope." "Say
   hello to one new person tomorrow." Write or draw that first step. Not the whole
   climb — just the first step.
4. **Check your plan (10 min).** Ask yourself: *Can I really do this first step soon?*
   If it is still too big, make it smaller. A good first step is one you could do today
   or tomorrow.
5. **Share and promise (10 min).** Tell a partner your goal and your first step, and
   listen to theirs. Then make a small promise to yourself: *I will take my first step.*
6. **Close (5 min).** A goal feels big, but the first step is small enough for you.
   Naming it is a grown-up power. This year you learned to set a direction — next year
   you will climb.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer a menu of small, concrete goals and first steps to choose from;
  scribe the words a learner says.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Point to or act out the goal and the first step; a
  partner writes or voices them.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Set the goal and step by voice; build the staircase
  tactually so the "first step" can be felt.
- **Extension:** Add a *second* step after the first, and say when you will take it.

## Assessment

- **Formative (self + portfolio + observation):** Can the learner state one simple,
  concrete goal and name a first step small enough to actually take?

## Home connection

Tell someone at home your goal and your very first step — then take the first step and
tell them how it went.
