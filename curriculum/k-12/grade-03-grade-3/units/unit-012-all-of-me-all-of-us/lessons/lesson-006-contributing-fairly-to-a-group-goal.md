# Lesson 6 — Contributing Fairly to a Group Goal

## Summary

Learners take on a real group goal — a garden bed, a mural, a shared structure, a class
book — and discover what **fair** cooperation means. It is not one person doing
everything, and it is not one person doing nothing: it is **each person giving a real
part**. They plan a fair share, do the job together, and check afterward that the load
was shared. This gathers the year's teamwork for the celebration to come.

## Objectives

- Practice cooperation by contributing fairly to a group goal. _(D02.S4.03.01)_

## Connection

Think of a job that many hands must do: planting a field, raising a wall, cooking a
feast, carrying a harvest in from the fields. Such jobs fail two ways — if **one person
tries to do it all**, or if **one person does nothing** while others carry them. Fair
cooperation is the middle path: **everyone gives a real part**, sized to what they can
do, so the whole job gets done and no one is drained or left idle. All over the world,
communities work this way. Today your class tries it.

## Materials

- A real group goal (a garden bed, a mural, a shared structure, or a class book)
- Chart paper and markers

## Preparation

- Choose a group goal that genuinely needs every person's part (plant a bed, paint a
  mural, build a tower, assemble a class book of pages).
- Set out chart paper for the "who does what — a fair share" plan.
- Arrange space to work together safely, with every learner able to reach their part.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It spirals up from Grade 2's "many
hands, one big job" (a job no one could do alone) into **fairness *within* the job** —
the harder, more mature idea. Two commitments carry it: **every part is real and
needed** (the person who clears the path is as needed as the one who plants), and **a
fair share is sized to the person, not identical for all** — fairness is not "everyone
does exactly the same," but "everyone gives a real, reachable part and no one is
drained or left out." A learner who uses a wheelchair or has limited strength can
direct, count, fetch, steady, or cheer — all real parts; name them as such. Watch for
the two unfair patterns and name them kindly if they appear: one child grabbing all the
work, or one child fading out of it. The check afterward is where the learning lands:
*was the work shared fairly, and would we change anything?* For a learner who is blind
or has low vision, give a hands-on part with a partner describing the shared goal. For
a learner who does not use a speaking voice, accept pointing, signing, or a pictured
plan.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Here is our group goal. It cannot be done well by one person
   alone — and it also cannot be done fairly if one person does it all. We need a fair
   share.
2. **Plan the fair share (10 min).** Look at the goal and list every part it needs:
   digging, planting, watering, carrying — or drawing, cutting, pasting, arranging.
   Then decide together: *who takes which part, so everyone gives a real one?* Write or
   draw the plan. No one does everything; no one does nothing.
3. **Do the job (15 min).** Work together, each at your part. If someone finishes
   early, they help a neighbor rather than sit idle. If someone is stuck, another
   lends a hand — that is cooperation, not competition.
4. **Check the fairness (10 min).** Stop and look. Did everyone give a real part? Was
   any one person doing too much, or too little? Say each person's part out loud. If a
   part was uneven, say how you would balance it next time.
5. **Close (10 min).** Fair cooperation means everyone gives a real part, and everyone
   shares the finished work. At our celebration, we will gather the whole class's work —
   and now you know exactly how to make that fair.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Give each learner one clear, reachable part and a partner; use a
  pictured plan.
- **Support (mobility/strength):** Offer non-physical parts — directing, counting,
  fetching, steadying, cheering — and name them as real parts.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Use pointing, signing, or a pictured plan to choose and
  show a part.
- **Extension:** After the job, name one part that was uneven and one change that would
  make the share fairer.

## Assessment

- **Formative (performance + peer + observation):** Did the learner give a real part
  toward a group goal, and can they name their own part and how the work was shared
  fairly (or one way it could be fairer)?

## Home connection

Do one shared job at home with a fair share — each person with a real part — and tell
someone how sharing the work made it fair.
