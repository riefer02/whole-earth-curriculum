# Lesson 2 — The Story of My Life So Far

## Summary

Learners become storytellers of their own life. They sequence their life so far in
order — **first**, **next**, **then**, **now** — and mark the important people and
places that belong in the story. Each learner makes a "my life path" page, a personal
timeline that shows one coherent, growing self and joins the year-end portfolio.

## Objectives

- Tell a simple story of their own life so far, in order, including important people
  and places. _(D01.S2.03.01)_

## Connection

Every person's life is a story with an order: you were a baby, then you learned to
walk and talk, then you started school, and now you are here. All over the world,
people tell their life stories to remember who they are and where they come from —
around a fire, in a family album, in a song, or in a letter. The important people and
places in your story — the home you grew in, the person who cared for you, the friend
who played with you — are part of what made you *you*. Today you tell your story so
far, in order.

## Materials

- A "my life path" page
- Crayons and pencils
- Family photos or drawings (optional)

## Preparation

- Prepare the "my life path" page with a winding path and four or five empty stops
  labeled "first," "next," "then," and "now."
- Gather crayons, pencils, and any photos or drawings learners may want to use.
- Arrange learners so they can share their stories in pairs.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It spirals up from Grade 2's work on
alike/different and strengths, now into **a coherent story of one whole life**. Two
commitments carry it: **every family and home shape is honored**, and **the story has
no "right" length or shape**. A learner's important people may be parents, one parent,
grandparents, extended kin, guardians, foster or group care, a neighbor, or a trusted
adult — all belong in a life story. A learner who was adopted, or who has moved homes,
or who has lost someone, may need the story held gently: invite, never demand, and let
them choose what to include. Honor a learner's *own* home-word for a person (abuela,
bà, bibi, auntie) and their own place-names. For a learner who is blind or has low
vision, tell the story by voice and touch — objects in order — rather than pictures.
For a learner who does not use a speaking voice, accept drawing, pointing, signing, or
a partner voicing the story. The point is a story that is *theirs*, in order, told in
their own way.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Close your eyes — or lower your gaze, or just look down and
   think quietly — and think back. What is the very first thing you can remember? Who
   was there? Where were you? That is the beginning of your story.
2. **Put it in order (10 min).** A story goes in order: **first**, **next**, **then**,
   **now**. On your path, mark four or five stops: *I was born*; *I learned to walk
   and talk*; *I started school*; and anything else big that happened in between —
   a move, a new sibling, a first trip. Put them in the order they happened.
3. **Add the important people (10 min).** At each stop, add the people who were there —
   the person who cared for you, the grandparent, the friend, the teacher. They are
   part of your story.
4. **Add the important places (10 min).** Now add the places: the home, the school,
   the market, the field, the sea or mountain you know. Draw or write each one beside
   its stop.
5. **Tell your story (10 min).** Tell your life story so far to a partner, in order,
   using your path. Then listen to your partner's story. Notice how each story is
   different — and each one is worth telling.
6. **Close (5 min).** Your life so far is a real story, with a beginning, a middle,
   and a now — and it is still being written. You are the author.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer a three-stop path and picture choices; scribe the words a learner
  says.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Point to pictures or objects in order; a partner voices
  the story.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Build the path tactually — objects in a row, in
  order — and tell it by voice.
- **Support (motor differences):** Paste pre-made pieces or dictate while a partner
  places them.
- **Extension:** Add *why* one person and one place matter to who you are now.

## Assessment

- **Formative (portfolio + peer + observation):** Can the learner sequence three or
  more life events in order, and name at least one important person and one important
  place in their story?

## Home connection

Tell someone at home the story of your life so far, in order, and ask them to tell you
one part you did not know — then add it to your path.
