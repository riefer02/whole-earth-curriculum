# Lesson 7 — Our Care, Our Celebration

## Summary

The year ends as it began — with who we are and who we are to each other. Learners
display their year of work equally, share one example of how people in a community
help one another, and set one goal (with its first step) for the year ahead. Then they
celebrate who they have become, together. A celebration, never a contest.

## Objectives

- Give an example of how people in a community help one another. _(D02.S4.03.02)_
- Tell a simple story of their own life so far, in order, including important people
  and places. _(D01.S2.03.01)_
- Name several groups they belong to and how each one shapes who they are.
  _(D01.S2.03.02)_
- Set a simple personal goal and name the first step toward it. _(D01.S3.03.01)_

## Connection

All over the world, people gather to celebrate — a harvest, a season, a graduation, a
year's work — and at the heart of every good celebration is **care**: someone cooked,
someone set a place, someone helped a smaller one reach the table. A community is made
of people helping one another, and when you help, you do not just fill a need — you
grow warmer and more connected too. Today you remember the care you gave and received
this year, you show who you have become, and you look ahead to one goal. Then you
celebrate it all.

## Materials

- Every learner's year work (writing, number work, fair tests, made things) plus this
  unit's pages
- A space to display the work
- A shared way to celebrate (song, clap, movement)

## Preparation

- Gather each learner's chosen work and this unit's pages ("The Story of My Life So
  Far," "The Groups That Shape Me," and "My Goal and My First Step").
- Set up the display so every learner's work is shown equally — side by side, none
  higher or more special than another.
- Arrange a circle for the help-sharing, the goal-ahead, and a simple shared
  celebration.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It is the year's **capstone**, and its
two commitments are **celebration, not competition** and **a community is made of
people helping one another**. No best-work awards, no winners, no ranking — every
learner's work is displayed equally, and every child belongs. Honor every family shape:
a child's caring circle may be parents, one parent, grandparents, extended kin,
guardians, foster or group care, or a trusted adult — and a learner may share their
celebration with any of these, or pass. In the sharing circle, sharing is by choice; a
pass is a full share. Help learners name *concrete* examples of community help (a
neighbor watching the baby, a market seller keeping fair change, a friend sharing a
book, a classmate tying a shoe) so the standard is met in the everyday, not the
grand. Keep the goal-ahead small and real, with a first step — the habit from Lesson 4.
For a learner who is Deaf or hard of hearing, make the celebration visual — clapping,
signing, a flag wave, a visual "bravo." For a learner who is blind or has low vision,
offer a spoken or tactile tour of the gallery. Keep the tone warm and generous: the
class has grown together, and everyone belongs.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Today is a celebration of all of you — all of me, all of us.
   There are no winners and no rankings, just everyone, together.
2. **Set up the gallery (10 min).** Lay out or hang every learner's year of work, plus
   this unit's pages: your life story, your groups, your goal. Every piece is shown
   equally — no one's is higher or more special. If a learner chooses not to show a
   piece, that choice is honored.
3. **Remember how a community helps (10 min).** Think of one example of people helping
   one another in your community — a neighbor, a seller, a friend, a family member, a
   classmate. Hold that memory. It is how communities are built.
4. **Circle of care (15 min).** Sit in a circle. Each learner may share their example
   of community help — or pass. Then think together: *why does helping help everyone,
   including the helper?* Helping fills a need, and it also connects and warms us.
   When we help, the whole circle grows stronger — and so do we.
5. **Look ahead (10 min).** From your "goal and first step" page, name one goal for
   the year ahead, and its very first step. Say it to the circle — or hold it in your
   heart. You are not finished growing; you are just beginning Grade 4.
6. **Celebrate together (10 min).** Make a shared celebration — a song, a clap, a
   movement the class creates together. You have grown all year, and you did it by
   caring for each other.
7. **Close (5 min).** Look around. You are a whole person — feelings, story, groups,
   and goals. And you belong to a caring community, all of you, together. That is
   something worth celebrating.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Allow a learner to celebrate by pointing to their work or holding up a
  partner's hand; a pass counts as sharing.
- **Support (Deaf/hard-of-hearing):** Make the celebration visual — clapping, signing,
  a flag wave, or a visual "bravo."
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Describe work aloud or offer a spoken or tactile
  tour of the gallery.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Point, sign, or draw the example of help and the goal;
  a partner voices them.
- **Extension:** Say, in your own words, how people in a community help one another,
  and name one goal and its first step for Grade 4.

## Assessment

- **Summative (portfolio + performance + observation):** The learner shares their year
  of work (displaying it or passing), gives one example of how people in a community
  help one another, and names one goal ahead with its first step — by word, drawing,
  sign, or a partner voicing.
- **Observation:** Does the learner show, in their own way, that they see themselves
  as a whole person and their class as a caring community they belong to?

## Home connection

Bring your year's work to the people who care for you — your family, whatever shape it
takes — and tell them one example of community help you remember, and the one goal and
first step you have set for the year ahead.
