# Lesson 3 — The Groups That Shape Me

## Summary

Learners name the groups they belong to — family, class, neighborhood, a team or club,
a language, a place — and, for each, how that group shapes a part of who they are. Each
learner makes a "rings" page with themselves at the center, gathering many belongings
into one whole, growing person. This page joins the year-end portfolio.

## Objectives

- Name several groups they belong to and how each one shapes who they are.
  _(D01.S2.03.02)_

## Connection

You are not one thing — you belong to many groups at once. You belong to a **family**
(whatever shape yours takes), a **class**, a **neighborhood** or village, maybe a
**team** or **club**, a **language** you speak, and a **place** you know — a city, a
farm, a coast, a mountain. Each group shapes you a little: your family teaches you love
and your story, your class teaches you to learn with others, your language gives you
the words you think and sing in. All over the world, people belong to many such groups,
and each one leaves its mark. Today you name yours.

## Materials

- A "groups that shape me" rings page
- Crayons and pencils

## Preparation

- Prepare the rings page: a small "me" in the center with three to six empty rings
  around it.
- Have the year's earlier "communities I belong to" work ready to look back on
  (optional).
- Arrange learners to share their rings in pairs or small groups.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It spirals up from Grade 2's "alike and
different" and the "welcoming circle," now into **the groups that make one whole self**.
Two commitments carry it: **every group shape is honored** (a learner's family may be
parents, one parent, grandparents, kin, guardians, foster or group care, or a trusted
adult; a "team" may be a play group, a faith community, a club, or none at all), and
**no group outranks another** — a neighborhood shapes a child as truly as a language
does. Invite learners to share their home-words for a group or place; names carry
belonging. Some learners live between places or have moved; honor that a learner can
belong to a place they no longer live and a place they live now. For a learner who is
blind or has low vision, name and feel the rings — count them aloud, and let the
learner say each group by voice rather than by sight. For a learner who does not use a
speaking voice, accept drawing, pointing, signing, or a partner voicing. The goal is
that every learner can say, in their own way: *I belong to these groups, and each one
shapes me.*

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** You belong to many groups at once. Say a few aloud as they come
   to you: a family, a class, a neighborhood, a team or club, a language, a place.
2. **Name your groups (10 min).** On your rings page, write or draw yourself in the
   middle. Then, in each ring, write or draw one group you belong to. Try for three or
   more: family, class, neighborhood, team, language, place — or others of your own.
3. **Name how each shapes you (15 min).** For each group, add one small thing it gives
   you: my family teaches me love and my story; my class shows me how to learn with
   others; my language gives me the words I think and sing in; my place gives me the
   land and sky I know. Write or draw it beside the ring.
4. **Share and listen (15 min).** Show your rings to a partner and say how one group
   shapes you. Then listen to theirs. Notice: some groups you share, and some are only
   theirs — and both are real.
5. **Close (5 min).** You are a whole person made of many belongings. Each group shapes
   a part of you, and all of them together are the whole, growing you.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer a three-ring page with picture choices for groups; scribe the
  words a learner says.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Point to pictures or act out a group; a partner names
  it and how it shapes them.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Count and feel the rings; name groups by voice, and
  build the page tactually.
- **Extension:** Explain how two of your groups shape you *differently*, with a real
  example for each.

## Assessment

- **Formative (portfolio + observation):** Can the learner name three or more groups
  they belong to, and say how at least two of them shape who they are?

## Home connection

Ask someone at home to name the groups *they* belong to and how one of them shaped
them — then tell them your own rings and how a group shapes you.
