# Lesson 1 — Feelings That Change and Mix

## Summary

Learners name two new truths about feelings that they are now old enough to hold: a
single feeling can **change over time**, and **two feelings can be true at once**. They
track one feeling across a day (nervous → calm → proud), and then find a "mixed"
feeling — happy *and* sad, excited *and* scared — and give a real example from their
own life. This opens the year-end unit by gathering the year's emotional literacy at
its new depth.

## Objectives

- Describe how a feeling can change over time and name when they feel more than one
  feeling at once. _(D01.S1.03.01)_

## Connection

Think of one ordinary day. In the morning you might feel **nervous** about a thing you
have to do; by midday you feel **calm**, because it went fine; by evening you feel
**proud**. One day, one you — and the feeling kept changing. Now think of a goodbye: a
friend moves away, or a season ends, or you leave a place you love. You might feel
**sad** to leave and **excited** for what is next — both at the very same time. Feelings
are not stuck, and they are not always one at a time. Today you practice naming both
of those true things.

## Materials

- A "feelings over time" chart (morning–midday–evening path)
- Crayons and paper
- The class "feelings wall" (optional)

## Preparation

- Draw or prepare the "feelings over time" path with three stops: morning, midday,
  evening.
- Have the year's feeling-word bank ready to look back on.
- Arrange a circle for acting out and sharing.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. It is the **spiral-up** from Grade 2, where learners told two
alike feelings apart; now they hold that a feeling can *move* and *mix*. Two
commitments carry it: **no feeling is wrong**, and **two feelings can both be true — a
child does not have to choose one.** A learner may say a goodbye felt "only sad" or
"only excited"; honor that too — mixed feelings are *one* truth about feelings, not a
rule every moment must follow. Feeling words live differently in every language and
culture; invite a learner to share a home-word for a mixed or changing feeling, and
notice that some languages have a single word where others need two. For a learner who
is blind or has low vision, describe the faces and the "body-sense" of each stop aloud
(nervous = fluttery stomach; calm = loose shoulders; proud = lifted chest) so the
change can be *felt* rather than seen. A learner who does not use a speaking voice can
draw, point, sign, or show the change with face and body while a partner names it.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of one ordinary day this week. What did you feel when you
   woke up? At the middle of the day? At the end? Did the feeling stay the same, or
   did it change?
2. **Watch a feeling change (15 min).** Look at the "feelings over time" path. Here is
   one day: **nervous** in the morning, **calm** at midday, **proud** by evening. Say
   each word and make each face. Then act out the change together — nervous flutter,
   calm breath, proud chest. A feeling can *change* as the day goes on.
3. **Meet a mixed feeling (10 min).** Now look at the second picture: one face that is
   half-smiling and half-sad — **happy and sad at once**. That is called a *mixed*
   feeling. A goodbye can feel sad (leaving) and exciting (a new place) together. Both
   are true at the same time.
4. **Find your own (15 min).** On your paper, draw or write one feeling that changed
   for you this year (from what, to what), and one time you felt two feelings at once
   (which two?). Share with a partner if you like.
5. **Close (5 min).** Feelings move, and feelings can mix. Naming both — the change and
   the mix — helps you understand yourself, and helps a friend feel truly understood.
   That is a grown-up power you now have.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer a two-stop path (just "before" and "after") and picture choices
  for feelings; scribe the words a learner says.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Point to faces or act out the change and the mix; a
  partner names them.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Describe each face and its body-sense aloud; build
  the path tactually with textured markers.
- **Extension:** Name a situation where a feeling changed *and* a second feeling
  appeared at the same time, and tell how you knew both were true.

## Assessment

- **Formative (self + observation):** Can the learner describe one feeling that
  changed over time (from what to what), and name one time they felt more than one
  feeling at once?

## Home connection

Tell someone at home about one feeling that changed for you today — or one time you
felt two feelings at once — and ask them about a time a feeling of theirs changed or
mixed.
