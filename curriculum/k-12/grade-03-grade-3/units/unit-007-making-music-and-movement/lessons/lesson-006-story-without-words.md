# Lesson 6 — Telling a Story Without Words

## Summary

Learners **retell** a short story or a feeling using only **movement and gesture** — no
words — and explain why they chose each gesture. They discover that the body is a
language of its own, and that people everywhere tell stories with hands, faces, and
movement when words are not enough.

## Objectives

- Retell a story or feeling using only movement and gesture, and explain their choices.
  _(D07.S3.03.02)_

## Connection

Think of a wave hello, a shrug, a nod, a hand over the heart, a finger to the lips for
"shh." People everywhere use **gesture** — movement of the hands, face, and body — to
say things without words. A mime, a dancer, a person using sign language, a child
acting out "the big fish got away" — all tell stories with movement. Today you will
tell a whole story or feeling using only your body, and explain your choices.

## Materials

- A short story or feeling everyone knows (a seed growing, a storm passing, a balloon
  floating away)
- Open space to move

## Preparation

- Choose a short story or feeling to retell, or let learners choose their own.
- Clear a safe space to move.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Retelling with movement and explaining choices is a **skill**, so
model it once — "I grow slowly from a crouch to tall to show the seed growing; I chose
slow because seeds grow slowly" — before learners try (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark,
2006). The heart is the **explanation of choices**, not the polish of the movement. Any
story or feeling can be retold seated, with hands and face alone, or with the whole
body. A learner with limited mobility retells fully with face, hands, and shoulders, or
directs a partner's movement and explains the choice — directing is retelling. A
learner who does not speak explains by pointing to a "choice" word card (big, slow,
small, fast) or by showing the gesture twice with contrast. There is no wrong retelling
as long as the learner can say (or show) why they moved that way.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Choose a small story or feeling to retell — a seed growing, a
   storm passing, a balloon floating away, or a feeling like "joy" or "worry." You will
   tell it with no words at all.
2. **Find the moments (10 min).** Think of the story in three moments: the beginning,
   the middle, the end. For "a seed grows": it is small and still, then it pushes up,
   then it stands tall in the sun.
3. **Make a gesture for each moment (10 min).** Use your hands, face, and body — not
   your voice. Crouch small, push up slowly, stretch tall. Each moment needs one clear
   **gesture**.
4. **Perform and explain (15 min).** Show your retelling to a partner or the group.
   Then explain your **choices**: "I moved slowly because seeds grow slowly; I reached
   high because the plant reaches for the sun."
5. **Share and close (5 min).** Remember: your body is a language. People everywhere
   tell stories with hands, faces, and movement — and your movement told a story with
   no words at all.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Retell one moment with one clear gesture (the sun rises: arms circle up)
  and say why, then build a second moment if ready.
- **Support (limited mobility, seated):** Retell with face, hands, and shoulders, or
  direct a partner's movement and explain the choice — directing is retelling.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Point to a choice word card (big, slow, small, fast) or
  show the gesture twice with a contrast to explain.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to retell a whole story with a beginning, middle, and
  end and to explain two or three choices, then have a partner guess the story.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Does the learner retell a story or feeling using only
  movement and gesture, and explain their choices?
- **Performance:** The learner's movement-only retelling, performed live.

## Resources

- No external resources required; the claims in this lesson are about the learner's own
  movement, which they practice directly.

## Home connection

Tell a grown-up a story with no words — only hands, face, and body — and let them
guess. Then explain why you chose each movement.
