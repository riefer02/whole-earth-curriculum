# Lesson 3 — Long and Short Sounds

## Summary

Learners tell **long sounds** from **short sounds**, build a **rhythm pattern** out of
them, and **perform** it. They discover that rhythm is made of long and short sounds,
and that long-and-short patterns — a heartbeat, a dripping tap, a drum — are how rhythm
works the world over.

## Objectives

- Create a short rhythm pattern using long and short sounds and perform it.
  _(D07.S2.03.01)_

## Connection

Listen to the sounds that keep time around you — a heartbeat, a dripping tap, a drum,
the tick of a clock, the long call and short answer of birds. Some sounds **hold**
(an "ahhh"), and some sounds **pop** (a "ta"). Rhythm is built from these two: **long
sounds** and **short sounds**. Today you will build a rhythm pattern out of long and
short sounds and perform it.

## Materials

- Voices, hands, and feet
- A small set of sound makers (a drum or pot, a shaker, a stick)

## Preparation

- Gather a few sound makers that can make a clear long sound (a ring, a hum) or a
  short sound (a tap, a clap).
- Arrange the group in a circle so everyone can hear and see.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Telling long from short and building a pattern is a **skill**,
so model it with explicit instruction (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006): hold an
"ahhh" for a long sound, tap a "ta" for a short sound, then play a pattern and have
learners echo. Keep patterns short at first (two to four sounds). "Long" and "short"
are about **time held**, not about loudness. A learner who is Deaf or hard of hearing
reads long and short by **sight and vibration**: a long sound vibrates for a long time
(a big slow drum stroke), a short sound stops quickly (a quick tap), and they can play
the pattern by making long and short movements or taps. A learner who does not sing or
speak performs with claps, taps, and hums. A learner who is blind or has low vision
leads and repeats by ear and touch. Every performance belongs.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Make a **long sound** — hum or ring an "ahhh" and let it hold.
   Now make a **short sound** — a quick "ta" or a tap. One holds, one pops.
2. **Tell them apart (10 min).** With a partner, take turns making sounds. Your partner
   says (or points to) "long" or "short." A long sound keeps going; a short sound stops
   quickly.
3. **Build a pattern (10 min).** Put long and short sounds in a row — maybe
   **long, long, short, short, long**. Say it, tap it, or play it on a sound maker.
   That is your **rhythm pattern**.
4. **Perform it (10 min).** Perform your pattern for a friend or the group, the same
   way a second time. Does it come back the same? If it drifts, try again — a pattern
   holds its shape when the longs stay long and the shorts stay short.
5. **Share and close (5 min).** Remember: rhythm is made of long and short sounds. When
   you perform a pattern, you carry time with your voice and hands — just as people
   everywhere have kept the beat.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use a two-sound pattern ("long, short") and echo it slowly together, one
  sound at a time.
- **Support (Deaf/HH):** Read long and short by vibration and movement — a slow drum
  stroke for long, a quick tap for short — and perform with long and short movements.
- **Support (non-speaking/non-singing):** Perform with claps, taps, and hums.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Lead and repeat by ear and touch.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to build a four-sound pattern and perform it twice in a
  row, checking the longs stay long and the shorts stay short.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Does the learner build a pattern of long and short
  sounds and perform it?
- **Performance:** The learner's rhythm pattern, performed live.

## Resources

- No external resources required; the claims in this lesson are about the learner's own
  listening and performing, which they practice directly.

## Home connection

Teach someone at home your long-and-short pattern ("long, long, short, short, long")
and have them echo it back. Then let them make one for you to repeat.
