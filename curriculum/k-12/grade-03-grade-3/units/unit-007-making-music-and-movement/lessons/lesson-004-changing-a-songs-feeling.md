# Lesson 4 — Changing a Song's Feeling

## Summary

Learners sing or speak the same short song several ways — **faster**, **slower**,
**louder**, **softer**, **higher**, **lower** — and describe how each change changes
the song's feeling. They discover that the same words can be a lullaby or a march, and
that **tempo**, **dynamics**, and **pitch** are the dials of feeling in music.

## Objectives

- Describe how changing tempo, dynamics, or pitch changes the feeling of a song.
  _(D07.S2.03.02)_

## Connection

Think of a lullaby and a celebration song. The lullaby is slow and soft; the
celebration is fast and loud. The **same words** can feel completely different when you
change how you sing them — how fast (**tempo**), how loud or soft (**dynamics**), and
how high or low (**pitch**). Today you will sing one song several ways and watch its
feeling change in your hands.

## Materials

- Voices and hands
- A short familiar song or chant (a lullaby, greeting song, or counting chant)

## Preparation

- Choose one short, familiar song or chant everyone can sing or speak.
- Be ready to lead the song at different speeds, volumes, and pitches.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Changing tempo, dynamics, and pitch and naming the feeling is a
**skill**, so model each change one at a time — "watch: now I sing it slower... the
feeling turns calm" — before learners take turns leading (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark,
2006). Use **one song** throughout so the only thing changing is tempo, dynamics, or
pitch (this is the arts version of a fair test: change one thing, keep the rest the
same). Define the three words simply: tempo is speed, dynamics is loud and soft, pitch
is high and low. A learner who does not sing changes pitch by pointing high or low,
dynamics by big or small movements, and tempo by tapping fast or slow — the feeling
can be shown as well as sung. A learner who is Deaf or hard of hearing feels tempo as
fast or slow vibration and sees dynamics as big or small gestures. Every feeling named
is welcome; there is no wrong mood.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Sing or speak your song the usual way. How does it feel? Now get
   ready to change it.
2. **Change the tempo (10 min).** Sing the song **faster**, then **slower**. Tempo is
   speed. How did the feeling change? Fast often feels excited or rushed; slow often
   feels calm or sad.
3. **Change the dynamics (10 min).** Sing the song **louder**, then **softer**.
   Dynamics is loud and soft. How did the feeling change? Loud often feels strong or
   joyful; soft often feels gentle or secret.
4. **Change the pitch (10 min).** Sing the song **higher**, then **lower**. Pitch is
   high and low. How did the feeling change? High often feels light or bright; low
   often feels heavy or deep.
5. **Share and close (5 min).** Tell (or point to) one change you made and how it
   changed the feeling. Remember: the same song can be many songs — tempo, dynamics,
   and pitch are the dials of feeling, and you turn them.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Change one thing at a time and offer a choice ("did fast feel excited or
  calm?"), then change together.
- **Support (non-singing):** Show tempo by tapping fast or slow, dynamics by big or
  small arm movements, pitch by pointing high or low.
- **Support (Deaf/HH):** Feel tempo as fast or slow vibration and see dynamics as big or
  small gestures; a partner can describe the pitch.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to change two things at once (slow AND soft) and name
  the new feeling, or to lead a change for the group.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Does the learner describe how changing tempo, dynamics,
  or pitch changes the feeling of the song?
- **Performance:** The learner sings or shows the song with one change and names the
  new feeling.

## Resources

- No external resources required; the claims in this lesson are about the learner's own
  singing and listening, which they practice directly.

## Home connection

Sing a favorite song with a grown-up three ways — fast, soft, and high — and talk about
how the feeling changes each time.
