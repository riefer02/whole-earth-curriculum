# Lesson 10 — The Gallery of Making

## Summary

This capstone gathers everything the unit explored. Learners lay out their work like a
gallery and share one thing they made — a patterned artwork, a mood they found in a
picture, a rhythm pattern, a movement retelling, or a designed build. They see, all at
once, that pattern, sound, movement, and design are all **making** — and that every
piece has value and a story.

## Objectives

- Use a simple pattern or repetition of shape, line, or color in an artwork.
  _(D07.S1.03.01)_
- Describe the mood of an artwork and point to what in the picture creates it.
  _(D07.S1.03.02)_
- Create a short rhythm pattern using long and short sounds and perform it.
  _(D07.S2.03.01)_
- Describe how changing tempo, dynamics, or pitch changes the feeling of a song.
  _(D07.S2.03.02)_
- Perform a short dance or movement piece that uses high and low levels, speeds, and
  shapes. _(D07.S3.03.01)_
- Retell a story or feeling using only movement and gesture, and explain their choices.
  _(D07.S3.03.02)_
- Follow a simple design process of imagine, plan, make, and improve to build something
  that meets a need. _(D07.S4.03.01)_
- Explain how the materials available shape what can be made, using an example from
  their own making. _(D07.S4.03.02)_

## Connection

All through this unit, you have made things with your hands, your voice, your body, and
your ideas — a pattern you drew, a mood you read in a picture, a rhythm of long and
short sounds, a story told with movement, a thing you designed and improved. Today you
get to look at it all together, the way a family or a village shows its work at a fair
or a festival. Making is how you share who you are — and every piece you made has a
story worth hearing.

## Materials

- Each learner's favorite piece from the unit
- A simple display space (table, wall, or cloth)

## Preparation

- Help learners choose one favorite piece (a patterned artwork, a mood picture, a
  rhythm pattern, a movement retelling, or a designed build).
- Arrange a display space and a circle for sharing.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Make this a true celebration, not a contest. There are no "best"
pieces — the point is that every learner shares and every learner is heard. Let
learners share in whatever form suits them: talk, point, sing, move, or show. Sharing
is a choice, never a duty — a learner may pass, and watching and clapping for others is
full participation. Never single out a shy learner to share alone. Honor every
culture's arts as living heritage, and every learner's work as belonging. Close by
naming the unit's big idea together: making is a voice, and every voice matters.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Bring your favorite thing from this unit. Look around at what
   everyone brought. It is a gallery of making.
2. **Set up the gallery (10 min).** Lay out the pictures and builds. Remember the
   rhythm patterns and movement pieces are part of the gallery too — they live in your
   voice and body.
3. **Walk the gallery (10 min).** Move quietly around and look. Find one thing that
   makes you curious. Ask its maker, "Tell me about this" — or point to it and smile.
4. **Share and celebrate (20 min).** One at a time, share your piece if you want to —
   show your pattern, name a mood, play your rhythm, move your story, or explain your
   designed build and the improvement you made. You may also pass, or let a friend help
   you share. Everyone claps for everyone, because every piece has a story.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: your hands, your voice, your body, and your ideas can
   all make something new. That is making, music, and movement — and it is yours
   forever.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer a gentle prompt to share ("show us your pattern," or "play your
  rhythm for us") and let the learner point or gesture rather than speak.
- **Support (seated/adapted, non-speaking):** Share from where the learner is — point,
  show, sign, move a hand, or have a friend help present the piece; that is full
  participation.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to name one thing they want to make next, and how they
  would improve it.

## Assessment

- **Summative (portfolio):** The learner's favorite piece, kept in the portfolio with a
  note of its story.
- **Performance (observation):** The learner shares one piece from the unit, in any
  form.

## Resources

- No external resources required; this lesson gathers the learner's own work from the
  unit.

## Home connection

Make a tiny gallery at home: lay out a few things you made this unit and give a
grown-up a "tour," telling the story of each one.
