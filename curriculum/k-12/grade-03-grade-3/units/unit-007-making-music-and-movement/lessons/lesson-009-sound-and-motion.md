# Lesson 9 — Sound and Motion Together

## Summary

Learners combine the **rhythm pattern** from earlier in the unit with a **movement
pattern** of levels, speeds, and shapes, and perform them together as one piece. They
discover that music and movement are two halves of one language, and that drummers and
dancers everywhere move together.

## Objectives

- Create a short rhythm pattern using long and short sounds and perform it.
  _(D07.S2.03.01)_
- Perform a short dance or movement piece that uses high and low levels, speeds, and
  shapes. _(D07.S3.03.01)_

## Connection

Think of a drummer and a dancer moving together — the drum's long boom matches a slow,
high reach; its short tap matches a quick, low step. Sound and motion are two halves of
one language. The beat tells the body when to move, and the body tells the beat what it
feels. Today you will put your long-and-short rhythm and your high-and-low movement
together into one piece.

## Materials

- Voices, hands, and bodies
- A small set of sound makers (a drum or pot, a shaker, a stick)

## Preparation

- Have learners recall their rhythm pattern (long and short sounds) and their movement
  (high and low levels, speeds, shapes).
- Arrange the group so pairs have room to sound and move together.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Combining sound and motion is a **skill**, so model it once —
"long boom, I reach high and slow; short tap, I drop low and quick" — before learners
try (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006). Keep the pattern short (two to four sounds and
two to four moves) so the match stays clear. Sound and motion can live in the same body
(clap while moving) or split across partners (one sounds, one moves) — both are
combining. A learner with limited mobility pairs sound with the movement they can make
(a high hand, a low head) or directs a partner and matches the sound — directing and
sounding is full participation. A learner who does not sing sounds with claps, taps, or
a drum. A learner who is Deaf or hard of hearing matches movement to the **feel** of the
beat's vibration. Every piece belongs.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Warm up your rhythm: make a **long** sound ("ahhh" or a boom)
   and a **short** sound ("ta" or a tap). Then warm up your movement: reach **high**,
   sink **low**, move **slow**, move **fast**.
2. **Make a rhythm pattern (10 min).** Put long and short sounds in a row — maybe long,
   short, short, long. Practice it until it comes back the same each time.
3. **Make a movement pattern (10 min).** Put movements in a row to go with it — a slow
   high reach on the long, a quick low step on each short. Your movement **matches**
   your sound.
4. **Put them together (15 min).** Perform them at the same time: sound the rhythm
   while you move the pattern, or let a partner sound while you move, then trade. Make
   the sound and the motion line up.
5. **Share and close (5 min).** Show your piece. Remember: music and movement are two
   halves of one language — drummers and dancers everywhere move as one, and now you
   have too.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Pair one sound with one move (long boom = slow high reach) and do it
  together, then add a second pair if ready.
- **Support (limited mobility, seated):** Match sound with the movement you can make (a
  high hand, a low head), or direct a partner's movement while you sound — directing and
  sounding is full participation.
- **Support (non-singing):** Sound with claps, taps, or a drum.
- **Support (Deaf/HH):** Match movement to the feel of the beat's vibration; a partner
  can sound while you move.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to make a piece with three sound-motion pairs and to
  perform it with a partner, one sounding and one moving, then trading.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Does the learner combine a long-and-short rhythm pattern
  with a movement pattern of levels and speeds, and perform them together?
- **Performance:** The learner's sound-and-motion piece, performed live.

## Resources

- No external resources required; the claims in this lesson are about the learner's own
  sounding and moving, which they practice directly.

## Home connection

Teach a grown-up your sound-and-motion piece — a long sound with a slow high reach, a
short sound with a quick low step — and perform it together.
