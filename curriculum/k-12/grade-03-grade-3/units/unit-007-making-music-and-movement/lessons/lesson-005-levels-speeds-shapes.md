# Lesson 5 — Levels, Speeds & Shapes

## Summary

Learners make body **shapes** at **high**, **middle**, and **low** levels, move them at
**fast** and **slow** speeds, and put them together into a short **dance** with a clear
beginning, middle, and end. They discover that every body can dance, and that level,
speed, and shape are a movement language.

## Objectives

- Perform a short dance or movement piece that uses high and low levels, speeds, and
  shapes. _(D07.S3.03.01)_

## Connection

Your body can be tall as a tree (that is a **high** level), low as a stone (a **low**
level), and everywhere in between. It can move fast as rain or slow as a cloud, and it
can make round shapes and sharp shapes. Dancers everywhere use **level**, **speed**,
and **shape** to speak without words. Today you will make your body speak in levels,
speeds, and shapes.

## Materials

- Open space to move
- A simple beat or song (optional)

## Preparation

- Clear a safe space so every learner can move without bumping into anything or anyone.
- Have a simple beat ready (a drum, clapping, or a recording) if you want music.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. This is movement, so the heart is safety and welcome, not
perfection. Honor every body: a learner who uses a wheelchair, walker, or other aid
dances with the parts that move — reaching high with arms or chin, sinking low by
bending or tilting, making shapes with hands and shoulders. A seated learner dances
fully. A learner with limited mobility can be the "director" who chooses the level,
speed, and shape while a partner or the group moves it. There is no "best" movement;
the only test is whether the body made the level, speed, or shape it meant to. Keep
movements gentle and give everyone room to move at their own range. Celebrate each
dancer's choices.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Stand (or sit) where you have room. Reach up high — that is a
   **high** level. Sink down low — that is a **low** level. Stand in the middle — that
   is a **middle** level.
2. **Make shapes (10 min).** Make a **shape** with your body — round like a ball, or
   sharp like a star. Try a shape at a high level, a middle level, and a low level.
   Freeze each one like a statue.
3. **Change speed (10 min).** Move from your high shape to your low shape **slowly**,
   like honey. Now move **fast**, like a spark. Speed is how fast or slow you travel
   between shapes.
4. **Put it together (15 min).** Make a short dance: a beginning shape (high and
   sharp), a middle move (slowly down to low and round), and an ending shape (low and
   still). Do it a few times so it feels like yours.
5. **Share and close (5 min).** Show your dance to a friend or the group. Remember:
   your body speaks through level, speed, and shape — and every body's dance belongs.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer one clear sequence ("high shape, slowly sink low, freeze") and do
  it together first.
- **Support (seated/adapted, wheelchair, limited mobility):** Dance with the moving
  parts — arms high and low, chin up and down, hands and shoulders making shapes — or
  direct a partner who moves your chosen level, speed, and shape.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to make a dance with three levels and two speeds, and
  to teach their sequence to a partner.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Does the learner perform a short movement piece using
  high and low levels, speeds, and shapes?
- **Performance:** The learner's short dance, performed live.

## Resources

- No external resources required; the claims in this lesson are about the learner's own
  moving body, which they experience directly.

## Home connection

With a grown-up, make a "level dance" at home — a high shape, a low shape, and a slow
move between them — and teach it to each other.
