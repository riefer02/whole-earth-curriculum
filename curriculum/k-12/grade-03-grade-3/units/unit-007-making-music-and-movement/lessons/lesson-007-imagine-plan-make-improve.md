# Lesson 7 — Imagine, Plan, Make, Improve

## Summary

Learners follow a four-step design process — **imagine**, **plan**, **make**, and
**improve** — to build something that meets a simple **need**, and test it to see how
it works. They discover that making is a loop, not a straight line, and that this loop
is how everything people build gets better.

## Objectives

- Follow a simple design process of imagine, plan, make, and improve to build something
  that meets a need. _(D07.S4.03.01)_

## Connection

Everything around you that works well was once an idea that got tested and fixed and
tested again — a shoe, a basket, a bridge, a pot. The maker first **imagined** it,
then **planned** it, then **made** it, then **improved** it after trying it out. That
four-step loop is called a **design process**. Today you will run the loop yourself to
build something that meets a need.

## Materials

- A small pile of making materials (paper, cloth, string, tape, boxes, sticks, cups,
  clay)
- A four-step design chart (imagine, plan, make, improve)

## Preparation

- Gather a small pile of varied materials so learners have real choices.
- Display or draw the four-step design chart where everyone can see it.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. The design loop is a **skill**, so model it once — "we need a
pencil holder; I imagine a cup; I plan paper folded around a box; I make it; I test it
and it tips over, so I improve it by adding a wider base" — before learners run their
own loop (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006). The key idea is the **loop**: improve
comes from testing, not from getting it right the first time. "Improve" can be one
small change. The plan may change as the learner builds — that is the loop working. A
learner with limited fine motor directs a partner who handles the materials — imagining,
planning, and deciding the improvement are the heart of the task. A learner who does
not speak points to the four boxes and to the change they made. A learner who is blind
or has low vision plans by feel and names the steps aloud, and tests the build with
their hands. Every loop belongs.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Look at the materials. Today you will **design** — build
   something that meets a **need**. First, pick a need: a holder for pencils, a bridge
   for a toy to cross, a stand to keep a book open, a cup to hold water. Say or point
   to your need.
2. **Imagine (5 min).** In your mind (or aloud), picture what you could make to meet
   the need. That is step one: **imagine**.
3. **Plan (10 min).** Before you build, say or draw your plan — what it will look like,
   what materials you will use, what you will do first. That is step two: **plan**.
4. **Make (15 min).** Build your plan with the materials. It does not have to be
   perfect; it has to exist so you can test it. That is step three: **make**.
5. **Improve (10 min).** Test it. Does it meet the need? If it tips, breaks, or falls
   short, change one thing to make it work better — a wider base, a stronger join, an
   extra fold. That is step four: **improve**. Test again.
6. **Share and close (5 min).** Show what you made and tell (or point to) the need it
   meets and the improvement you made. Remember: imagine, plan, make, improve — that
   loop is how everything good gets built.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer one clear need ("make a place for the pencils") and a two-step
  loop (make, then improve), and let the learner choose one material.
- **Support (fine motor):** The learner imagines, plans, and directs while a partner
  handles the materials.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Point to the four boxes and to the change made to
  improve.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Plan by feel, name the steps aloud, and test the
  build with the hands.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to run the loop twice, making a second improvement, and
  to tell how the second version is better.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Does the learner follow imagine, plan, make, and improve
  to build something that meets a need, and identify the improvement they made?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The built thing (or a photo) and the plan, kept in the
  portfolio.

## Resources

- No external resources required; the design-loop claims here are about the learner's
  own process, which they practice directly.

## Home connection

At home, find a small need (a place for keys, a stand for a spoon) and run the loop
with a grown-up — imagine, plan, make, improve — then test it together.
