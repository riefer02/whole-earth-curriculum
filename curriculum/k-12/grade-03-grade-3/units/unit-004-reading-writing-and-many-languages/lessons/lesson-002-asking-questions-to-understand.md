# Lesson 2 — Asking Questions to Understand

## Summary

Learners practice asking and answering the five questions — who, what, where, when,
and why — about a short talk or explanation. They discover that a question is not a
test of the speaker but a tool the listener uses to reach the speaker's meaning.

## Objectives

- Ask and answer questions to understand a speaker's meaning. _(D04.S1.03.02)_

## Connection

Think of a friend describing a game you have never played, or a grown-up explaining a
recipe you want to cook. If you only nod, you may miss the point. But if you ask,
"Where do I put it?" or "When do I add the water?" or "Why do I stir?" — suddenly the
meaning comes clear. Questions are how a listener reaches inside a speaker's words
and finds the idea they are really trying to share.

## Materials

- Question word cards (who, what, where, when, why, each with a picture cue)
- A short talk or explanation to hear

## Preparation

- Prepare the five question word cards.
- Choose a short topic or happening for partners to talk about.
- Arrange partners so each pair can face one another.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Teach the five question words as explicit, worked instruction:
name each word, model the question it makes, then guide practice (Kirschner, Sweller
& Clark, 2006). The key idea to protect: a question is an act of attention, not a
challenge. Model asking with a warm, curious voice, and model answering with patience.
Questions may be spoken, signed, written, or shown by pointing to a card — all count
fully. Keep the talk friendly and small; no learner's private life is put on display.
This is an egalitarian moment: every question is worth an answer, and no question is
"foolish."

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of a time you really wanted to understand something
   someone said. What did you do? Chances are, you asked.
2. **Meet the five questions (10 min).** Learn the five question words: **who** (a
   person), **what** (a thing or happening), **where** (a place), **when** (a time),
   **why** (a reason). Say them in your home language. Put up one finger for each.
3. **Watch a worked example (10 min).** The facilitator (or a volunteer) tells one
   short thing — a happening or an idea. Then the group asks the five questions, one
   at a time, and the teller answers. Notice how each answer adds a piece to the
   meaning.
4. **Ask and answer with a partner (15 min).** Your partner tells a short happening
   or idea. Ask the five questions, one by one, and listen for each answer. Then
   trade roles: you tell, your partner asks, and you answer.
5. **Close (5 min).** Who, what, where, when, why — five keys that open a speaker's
   meaning. Asking is caring: it says, "I want to understand you."

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Start with just who and what, then add where, when, and why one at a
  time; use the cards as a guide.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Point to the question cards, sign, or write a question;
  the asking is what matters, not the voice.
- **Extension:** After a question is answered, ask a follow-up — "and why is that?"
  — to dig one step deeper into the meaning.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner ask and answer the five questions
  about a short talk, and does the asking lead them closer to the speaker's meaning?

## Home connection

Tonight, when someone at home tells you about their day, ask one of the five
questions — who, what, where, when, or why — and notice how much more you understand.
