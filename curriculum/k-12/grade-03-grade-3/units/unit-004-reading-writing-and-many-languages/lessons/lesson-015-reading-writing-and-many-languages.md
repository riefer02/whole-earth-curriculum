# Lesson 15 — Reading, Writing & Many Languages

## Summary

Learners close the unit with a celebration and review: they share their retellings,
their questions, their revised and edited paragraphs, and their language treasures —
and see reading, writing, and many languages as one connected whole in their lives.
This is the unit capstone.

## Objectives

- Review and celebrate the whole unit: retelling in sequence, asking and answering
  questions, reading accurately and fluently with self-correction, finding the main
  idea and details, writing a complete paragraph, revising and editing, and learning
  phrases in another language.
  _(D04.S1.03.01, D04.S1.03.02, D04.S2.03.01, D04.S2.03.02, D04.S3.03.01,
  D04.S3.03.02, D04.S4.03.01, D04.S4.03.02)_

## Connection

Think of three tools that work together: a lamp to see, a hand to write, and a voice
to speak. Reading, writing, and many languages are your tools like that — they do not
live apart; they work as one. You read to understand, you write to be understood, and
you reach across languages to meet other people. The whole human family has always
done this, in every land, in every script. Today we gather it all and see the treasure
we now hold.

## Materials

- The unit's collected work (each learner's portfolio)
- A multilingual celebration chart or word wall

## Preparation

- Gather learners' portfolios and arrange a sharing space.
- Prepare the celebration chart or word wall in the classroom's languages, each
  greeting the same size, none above another.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. The capstone celebrates, reviews, and cements the unit's
deepest value: reading and writing are tools, and multilingualism is wealth — no
language, spoken or signed, is better than another (UNESCO holds that valuing every
child's language strengthens learning and dignity). Keep it a celebration, never a
contest: no ranking, no "best" paragraph, no high-stakes test. Review is light and
joyful — a glance back at the portfolio, one paragraph read aloud, one greeting said
in many languages — not an exam. Every voice, accent, and way of speaking is honored
equally. A learner who is Deaf or hard of hearing shares in sign, and a signed
greeting stands on the wall exactly as high as any spoken one. Growth is seen against
each learner's own beginning, never against a peer.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Look back at your portfolio from this unit. You have
   retold, questioned, read, written, revised, edited, and learned new phrases.
   Today we celebrate it all.
2. **Share your reading and writing (15 min).** Read your revised and edited
   paragraph aloud to the group, or retell a story in sequence with its key details.
   Listen with full attention; this is each learner's own work, in their own
   language.
3. **Greet in many languages (10 min).** Around the circle, each person says or
   signs a greeting or an everyday phrase they learned. Everyone says it back.
   Notice: the greetings sit side by side, all the same size, none above another.
4. **Remember the journey (10 min).** Together, name what we learned: retell in
   sequence with key details; ask the five questions; read accurately and fluently
   and self-correct; find the main idea and its details; write a paragraph; revise
   and edit; learn phrases in another language. Point to each in your own work.
5. **Close (5 min).** Reading, writing, and many languages — your treasures, all
   working together. Many languages, one circle, none better.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** A learner may share by pointing to their paragraph and having the
  group read it together, or by sharing one greeting; passing is always allowed.
- **Support (sign language):** Share a signed greeting or paragraph; the whole circle
  signs it back, honoring it exactly as a spoken one.
- **Extension:** Write one sentence about what reading, writing, or many languages
  means to you, and add it to the class word wall.

## Assessment

- **Formative (portfolio/performance):** Can the learner share one piece of the
  unit's work — a retell, a paragraph, or a greeting — and say in their own words one
  thing they learned about reading, writing, or many languages?

## Home connection

Teach someone at home one greeting you learned this unit, and tell them the big idea:
reading, writing, and many languages are treasures that work together.
