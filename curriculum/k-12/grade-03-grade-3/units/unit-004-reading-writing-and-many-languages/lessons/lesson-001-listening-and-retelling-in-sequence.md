# Lesson 1 — Listening and Retelling in Sequence

## Summary

Learners listen to a partner tell a short, four-step happening or explanation, then
retell it back in the correct sequence, including its key details. Sequence is the
goal — not exact words — and every language has its own words for "first," "next,"
and "last."

## Objectives

- Retell a spoken story or explanation in sequence, including key details.
  _(D04.S1.03.01)_

## Connection

All day people tell you things that happen in steps: how to make a meal, the way to
the market, what happened at the river or on the farm. If the steps come out of
order, the story breaks — like putting the roof on a house before the walls. Listening
for the sequence and retelling it back with its key details is how people everywhere
pass along know-how, one generation to the next.

## Materials

- A spoken story or explanation to share (four clear steps, in the home language)
- Sequence cards (first, next, then, last)
- Four small objects, such as stones or seeds (low-tech)

## Preparation

- Choose or remember a short happening or explanation with four clear steps.
- Prepare the sequence cards or four small objects.
- Arrange partners so each pair can sit facing one another.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Retelling honors the speaker's meaning, not their exact words —
celebrate a learner's own wording, in their own language and their own way of
speaking; there is no single "correct" accent. Model one retelling in four clear
moves before partners work, then guide the practice. Oral traditions everywhere carry
stories by retelling; this is an ancient and universal human skill. A learner who is
Deaf or hard of hearing retells in sign, in pictures, or with objects moved in order;
a learner who does not use a speaking voice retells by pointing, signing, drawing, or
using a communication device — holding the sequence and the key details is what
counts.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Sit facing your partner. Remember what listening feels like:
   your full attention on the speaker — with your eyes, your ears, or both. If you
   listen by watching faces and hands in sign, that is listening too.
2. **Meet the sequence words (5 min).** Learn the words that hold a story in order:
   **first**, **next**, **then**, **last**. Say them in your home language. Every
   language has its own words for these steps.
3. **Listen once (5 min).** Your partner tells a short happening or explanation with
   four steps. Listen all the way through, without talking, with your whole body.
4. **Listen again and mark the sequence (10 min).** Hear it a second time. As you
   hear each step, move one object or touch one card: **first**… **next**… **then**…
   **last**. Notice the key details — the ones the story would not make sense without.
5. **Retell it back (15 min).** Now retell the happening to your partner in order,
   using your own words and the sequence words, and keeping the key details. Then
   trade roles: your partner tells, you listen, and you retell.
6. **Close (5 min).** Sequence is what keeps a story whole, and key details are what
   make it true. First, next, then, last — the shape of every telling, in every
   language.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Retell with just three steps first (first, next, last), then add the
  fourth; use objects instead of words if helpful.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Retell by moving four objects in order, signing, or
  pointing to four pictures — the sequence and the key details are the retelling.
- **Extension:** Retell a five-step happening and notice where each extra step
  belongs in the order.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner retell a partner's four-step happening
  or explanation in the correct sequence, in their own words and in their own
  language, including its key details?

## Home connection

Ask someone at home to tell you how to do one small task in four steps, then retell
it back to them in order and ask, "Did I keep the order and the important details?"
