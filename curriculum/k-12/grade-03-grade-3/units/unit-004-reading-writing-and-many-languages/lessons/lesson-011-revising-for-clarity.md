# Lesson 11 — Revising for Clarity

## Summary

Learners revise their own paragraph for clarity, using three moves — **add**,
**remove**, and **rearrange** — to make their meaning come through clearer. Revision
is about meaning: it asks "is this what I really meant, and can the reader see it?"

## Objectives

- Revise and edit their own writing for spelling, punctuation, and clarity, with
  support. _(D04.S3.03.02)_

## Connection

Think of telling a friend the way to your home. The first time, you might say it in a
tangle, and they look confused. So you try again — you add a landmark, you take out
the part that confused them, you put the steps in a better order. That trying-again is
revising. Every good writer, in every language, does it: writing is not finished when
the words first land; it is finished when the meaning comes clear.

## Materials

- A paragraph the learner wrote (from Lesson 10)
- A revise bookmark (add, remove, rearrange)

## Preparation

- Have learners bring their paragraphs from Lesson 10.
- Prepare the revise bookmark.
- Have pencils and space to rewrite ready.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Revision is a taught skill: model it explicitly with a worked
example — read an unclear sentence aloud, find the confusing part, then add, remove,
or rearrange — before guided practice (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006). The key
shift to protect: revision is not about handwriting or spelling; it is about
*meaning*. Keep the focus on "is this clear?" — in the learner's home language and
writing system, script-agnostic. Revision may be done by hand, aloud, in sign, or on
a device. Honor the learner's own voice and accent in their writing; there is no
"correct" way to sound. Celebrate a revision that makes meaning clearer, whatever the
wording. A learner who is Deaf or hard of hearing revises in sign or writing the same
way. A learner who is blind or has low vision revises by dictation, on a braille
writer, or by listening to the text read back and naming what to change.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Remember giving directions that tangled. What did you do? You
   tried again, and it came clear. That is revising.
2. **Meet the three moves (5 min).** Learn the three revise moves: **add** — put in
   what is missing; **remove** — take out what does not help; **rearrange** — put the
   parts in a better order. All three make meaning clearer.
3. **Watch a worked example (10 min).** The facilitator reads an unclear sentence
   aloud, says "that part confuses me," and tries the moves: adds a word, removes a
   tangle, rearranges the order. Watch the meaning come clear.
4. **Revise your paragraph (15 min).** Read your paragraph aloud. Find the part that
   is not clear. Try the three moves: add what is missing, remove what does not help,
   rearrange what is out of order. Write your new, clearer version.
5. **Read it back (5 min).** Read your revised paragraph. Does the meaning come
   clearer now? If a part is still fuzzy, do one more move.
6. **Close (5 min).** Revision is not fixing mistakes — it is making meaning. Add,
   remove, rearrange: the three moves of every clear writer, in every language.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Revise a single sentence first, using one move at a time; a partner
  may read the paragraph aloud while the learner listens for the unclear part.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Revise by signing or pointing to the parts to change;
  a scribe or device records the new version.
- **Support (blind/low vision):** Revise by dictation or on a braille writer, listening
  back to hear where the meaning is unclear.
- **Extension:** Revise a paragraph to say the same idea two ways — once shorter,
  once fuller — and notice what each is good for.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation/self):** Can the learner find an unclear part of their own
  paragraph and use add, remove, or rearrange to make the meaning clearer?

## Home connection

Take a note you wrote at home — a message, a list, a story — and make it clearer with
one of the three moves: add, remove, or rearrange.
