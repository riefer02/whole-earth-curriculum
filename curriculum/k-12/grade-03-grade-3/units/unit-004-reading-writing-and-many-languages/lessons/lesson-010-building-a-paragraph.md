# Lesson 10 — Building a Paragraph with Details and a Closing

## Summary

Learners grow their topic sentence into a complete paragraph, adding at least two
supporting details and a closing sentence. The sandwich image — topic bun, detail
fillings, closing bun — holds the three parts together in the learner's mind.

## Objectives

- Write a paragraph with a topic sentence, at least two supporting details, and a
  closing sentence. _(D04.S3.03.01)_

## Connection

Think of a sandwich: the top bun starts it, the fillings are the heart of it, and the
bottom bun finishes it. A paragraph is a sandwich made of words. The topic sentence
is the top bun, the supporting details are the fillings, and the closing sentence is
the bottom bun that holds it all together. Take away any part and it falls apart —
in every language, on every table.

## Materials

- A topic sentence from last time
- A paragraph sandwich frame

## Preparation

- Have learners bring their topic sentences from Lesson 9.
- Prepare the sandwich frame (top bun, fillings, bottom bun).
- Have paper and pencils ready.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Building a paragraph is a foundational skill, so teach it
explicitly with a worked example — model a complete three-part paragraph, then guide
learners to build their own (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006). The structure to
protect: topic sentence, at least two supporting details, closing sentence. Learners
write in their home language and their own writing system — script-agnostic, no Latin
alphabet assumed. Details may be facts, examples, or small reasons; the closing may
restate the idea or give a final feeling. A learner who is Deaf or hard of hearing
writes, signs, or dictates the paragraph; the thinking is the writing. A learner who
is blind or has low vision builds the paragraph by dictation, on a braille writer, or
by typing and listening back — the thinking is the writing. Celebrate a complete,
honest paragraph in any wording; there is no single "correct" paragraph.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Remember your topic sentence — your top bun. Today we add the
   fillings and the bottom bun to make a whole paragraph.
2. **Meet the sandwich (5 min).** Learn the three parts of a paragraph: **top bun** —
   the topic sentence you already wrote; **fillings** — at least two supporting
   details that tell more; **bottom bun** — a closing sentence that finishes the idea.
3. **Watch a worked example (10 min).** The facilitator builds a paragraph aloud and
   on the board: topic sentence, then two details, then a closing. Watch how the
   sandwich comes together and stays together.
4. **Build your paragraph (20 min).** Start with your topic sentence. Add two
   supporting details — facts or examples that tell more about your topic. Finish
   with a closing sentence that wraps it up. Write it all in order.
5. **Read it back (5 min).** Read your whole paragraph aloud. Does it have all three
   parts? Does it make sense in order?
6. **Close (5 min).** A paragraph is a sandwich of words — top bun, fillings, bottom
   bun. You built it, and it holds together.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide detail starters — "One thing I know is…", "Another thing is…"
  — and a closing starter — "That is why…" — so the learner fills in the sandwich.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Sign or dictate the details and closing; the paragraph
  may be typed or written by a scribe.
- **Support (blind/low vision):** Build the paragraph by dictation, on a braille
  writer, or by typing and listening back; a scribe or device records it.
- **Extension:** Add a third supporting detail and notice how the extra filling makes
  the paragraph richer.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner write a complete paragraph with a
  topic sentence, at least two supporting details, and a closing sentence, in order,
  in their own language and writing system?

## Home connection

Build a word sandwich at home: write a paragraph about something you did today, with a
topic sentence, two details, and a closing, and read it to someone.
