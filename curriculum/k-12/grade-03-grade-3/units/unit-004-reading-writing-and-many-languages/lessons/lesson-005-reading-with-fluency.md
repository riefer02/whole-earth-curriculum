# Lesson 5 — Reading with Fluency

## Summary

Learners practice reading fluently — smoothly, at a talking pace, and with
expression — by re-reading a familiar grade-level text and tracking how their reading
eases. Fluency grows through repetition, not through racing or ranking, and
punctuation is the signpost for voice.

## Objectives

- Read grade-level texts with accuracy and fluency, self-correcting when meaning
  breaks down. _(D04.S2.03.01)_

## Connection

When you first walk a path, you go slowly and watch your feet. After a few trips, you
walk it without thinking. Reading is the same: the first time through a sentence is
bumpy, but after you re-read it, the words flow — like talking. A fluent voice lets
the meaning come through, so a story sounds the way it would if the teller were
speaking it to you. Punctuation is the map: a period says stop, a comma says pause, a
question mark says rise.

## Materials

- A familiar grade-level text (in the home writing system)
- A fluency chart to mark re-readings

## Preparation

- Choose a short passage the learner already knows and has read once.
- Prepare a simple fluency chart (three or four rows to mark re-readings).

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Fluency means accuracy plus a smooth, expressive pace — the
goal is meaning, not speed, and there is no competition and no "correct" accent.
Reading should sound like the learner's own natural way of speaking, in their own
language. Re-reading is the engine of fluency; the National Reading Panel's
evidence base points to re-reading and guided oral reading as the core practice, so
keep the text easy enough to be enjoyed, not struggled with. Teach punctuation as
explicit signposts: period, comma, question mark. A learner who is Deaf or hard of
hearing builds fluency in sign — a smooth, expressive signing of the text is fluent
reading. A learner who is blind or has low vision builds fluency in braille, in large
print, or by listening — a smooth, expressive re-reading by touch or ear is fluent
reading too. Celebrate each learner's growth against their own yesterday, never
against a peer. Where a timer or a recording is used, notice what the tool does — a
timer counts words but does not race you, and a recording lets you hear your own
voice like a patient friend — and what it cannot do: it cannot feel whether the
reading made sense; your own mind does that. A tool is a helper, never a judge.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of a path or a song you know so well you can do it
   without thinking. Reading gets like that with practice.
2. **Meet fluency (5 min).** **Fluency** means reading **smoothly**, at a talking
   **pace**, with **expression** — so the meaning comes through. Meet the punctuation
   signposts: a **period** says stop, a **comma** says pause, a **question mark** says
   rise.
3. **Read it once (5 min).** Read the familiar text once. It is okay if it is
   bumpy — that is the first walk on the path.
4. **Re-read and smooth it out (15 min).** Read it again, a little smoother, letting
   the punctuation guide your voice. Then a third time. Slide a finger or pointer
   along the line to keep a steady pace. Mark each re-reading on your fluency chart.
5. **Listen to yourself (10 min).** If you can, record your reading or read to a
   partner. Notice: where is it still bumpy? Where does it already flow like talking?
   Re-read the bumpy part once more.
6. **Close (5 min).** You did not race anyone — you grew against your own first try.
   Smooth reading lets the meaning sing.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Re-read a single short, very familiar line several times; smoothness
  is the only goal.
- **Support (Deaf/Hard of Hearing):** Practice a smooth, expressive signed reading
  of the text; re-signing builds fluency the same way.
- **Support (blind/low vision):** Re-read in braille, large print, or by listening;
  smooth, expressive re-reading by touch or ear builds fluency the same way.
- **Extension:** Read with a partner, taking turns by sentence, and match each
  other's smooth, expressive pace.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation/self):** Does the learner's reading grow smoother and
  more expressive across re-readings, sounding like natural talk in their own
  language, with punctuation guiding the voice?

## Home connection

Read the same short favorite text aloud at home twice tonight, and notice how much
smoother the second reading feels.
