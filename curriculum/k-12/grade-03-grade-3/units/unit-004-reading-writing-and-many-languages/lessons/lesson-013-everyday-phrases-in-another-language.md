# Lesson 13 — Everyday Phrases in Another Language

## Summary

Learners learn and use simple everyday phrases in another language — a greeting, a
number, and a color — practicing them by ear and in writing. Every script is honored
equally, and multilingualism is treated as wealth.

## Objectives

- Learn and use simple everyday phrases in another language, such as greetings,
  numbers, and colors. _(D04.S4.03.01)_

## Connection

Imagine arriving somewhere new and being able to say just three things: "hello," a
number, and a color. Suddenly you are not a stranger — you are a guest who tried.
Everyday phrases are small keys that open real doors between people. And you already
carry the biggest treasure: your home language, which no one has to lose in order to
gain another. Multilingualism is wealth — each language adds, none takes away.

## Materials

- Phrase cards (a greeting, a number, a color)
- A phrase partner

## Preparation

- Prepare phrase cards in the classroom's languages, each script equal in size.
- Pair learners for practice.
- If possible, invite or record a fluent speaker of the new language.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Learning phrases in another language is a skill: model each
phrase clearly, have learners echo it, then guide practice in pairs (Kirschner,
Sweller & Clark, 2006). The value to protect: there is no "correct" accent — an
accent is a sign that a learner already speaks another language, and that is wealth,
not a flaw. Honor the learner's home language first; the goal is to *add* a language,
never to replace one. UNESCO holds that valuing a child's own language strengthens
learning and dignity. Phrases may be spoken, signed, or both — a signed greeting is a
greeting, and a sign language counts fully as a language. Script-agnostic: a phrase
may be written in any script, or not written at all. Celebrate every attempt; mastery
grows through patient repetition, not through ranking.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of a greeting, a number, and a color you know in your
   home language. You already carry a whole language — a treasure.
2. **Meet the phrases (10 min).** Learn three everyday phrases in another language: a
   **greeting**, a **number**, and a **color**. Listen to each one, then say or sign
   it back. Try it a few times.
3. **Practice with a partner (15 min).** With your partner, take turns: one says or
   signs a phrase, the other repeats it. Try the greeting, the number, and the color
   until they feel comfortable. Help each other gently.
4. **Write or draw them (10 min).** Write each phrase in the new script if you can,
   and next to it in your own. Or draw a picture for each. Notice: the same meaning,
   in two ways.
5. **Close (5 min).** Three small keys — hello, a number, a color — and a door opens.
   You did not lose your language to gain another; you added a treasure.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Learn just one phrase well — the greeting — before adding the number
  and color; repetition and a patient partner are the tools.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Learn the phrases in sign or by pointing to the cards;
  a signed greeting counts fully.
- **Extension:** Learn one more everyday phrase — "please," "thank you," or "my name
  is…" — and teach it to a partner.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation/performance):** Can the learner say or sign a greeting, a
  number, and a color in another language, and use at least one of them in a simple
  exchange with a partner?

## Home connection

Teach someone at home the greeting, number, and color you learned, and ask them to
teach you one phrase from a language they know.
