# Lesson 12 — Editing for Spelling and Punctuation

## Summary

Learners edit their revised paragraph for **spelling** and **punctuation**, using a
simple checklist, so their writing is correct and ready to share. Editing is the last
polish — the small things that help a reader follow the words.

## Objectives

- Revise and edit their own writing for spelling, punctuation, and clarity, with
  support. _(D04.S3.03.02)_

## Connection

Think of getting ready to meet people: you wash your face and smooth your clothes —
the small last touches that show care. Editing is the small last touch for writing.
You have already made the meaning clear; now you fix the spelling and the punctuation
so the reader can follow without stumbling. A period says "one thought ends here." A
question mark says "someone is asking." These small marks carry big meaning, in every
writing system.

## Materials

- A revised paragraph (from Lesson 11)
- An editing checklist (check spelling, check periods and question marks, check
  capitals)

## Preparation

- Have learners bring their revised paragraphs.
- Prepare the editing checklist.
- Have pencils ready.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Editing is a taught skill: model it explicitly with a worked
example — fix a misspelled word, add a missing period, add a capital — then guide
practice (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006). Keep editing distinct from revising:
revising makes meaning clearer; editing makes the marks correct. The punctuation
marks to teach at this level are the period, question mark, and exclamation mark, plus
a capital to start a sentence — in whatever writing system the learner uses, since
some scripts have no capitals and different end marks. Script-agnostic: honor the
learner's own system's conventions. A learner who is Deaf or hard of hearing edits in
writing or sign the same way. A learner who is blind or has low vision edits by
dictation, on a braille writer, or by listening to the text read back and naming the
fixes. Never make editing a hunt for "wrongness" to shame; it is care for the reader,
and it is celebrated. Where a spelling check is used, notice what it does — it
catches some slips and saves time — and what it cannot do: it does not always know
which word you meant, so the writer's own eyes and care finish the job. A tool is a
helper, never a judge.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Remember: revising made your meaning clear. Now for the small
   last touches — editing — so the reader can follow without stumbling.
2. **Meet the checklist (5 min).** Learn the editing checklist: **one, check
   spelling** — is each word spelled right? **two, check punctuation** — does each
   sentence end with a period, question mark, or exclamation mark? **three, check
   capitals** — does each sentence start with a capital (where your writing system
   uses them)?
3. **Watch a worked example (10 min).** The facilitator writes a sentence with a
   misspelled word and no end mark, then edits it: fixes the spelling, adds the
   period, adds the capital. Watch the sentence become ready to share.
4. **Edit your paragraph (15 min).** Read your paragraph slowly with the checklist.
   Fix the spelling. Add the missing end marks. Check the capitals. Read it once more
   to be sure.
5. **Read it ready (5 min).** Read your edited paragraph aloud. Is it ready to share?
   If a small thing is still off, fix it now.
6. **Close (5 min).** Editing is care for the reader. Spelling and punctuation are
   the small marks that let your words travel, in any language, to any reader.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Edit a single sentence first, using one checklist item at a time; a
  partner may point to a missing mark.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Edit by pointing, signing, or using a device; the
  corrections may be recorded by a scribe.
- **Support (blind/low vision):** Edit by dictation or on a braille writer, listening
  back to catch what needs a fix.
- **Extension:** Trade paragraphs with a partner and gently find one spelling or
  punctuation fix for each other, then check it together.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation/self):** Can the learner edit their own paragraph for
  spelling and punctuation, using the checklist, so the writing is correct and ready
  to share?

## Home connection

At home, take one message you wrote — a note, a text, a list — and check it with the
checklist: spelling, punctuation, capitals. Fix what needs it.
