# Lesson 3 — Explaining One Idea to a Group

## Summary

Learners practice explaining **one** idea to a small group: naming the single point,
putting it in order, and giving an example. The group listens and then shows they
understood, so explaining and understanding grow together.

## Objectives

- Explain one idea to a small group. _(D04.S1.03.02)_

## Connection

Think of explaining the rules of a game to someone who has never played, or telling
a friend how to reach your home. If you try to explain everything at once, the idea
scatters and nobody can follow. But if you pick **one** idea, put it in order, and
give an example, the group can see it — the way one clear lamp lights a whole room.
Everywhere in the world, people teach each other by explaining one idea at a time.

## Materials

- Idea cards (a game rule, a favorite place, how to do one small task)
- A small-group circle (3–5 learners)

## Preparation

- Prepare the idea cards or a list of friendly ideas.
- Arrange small circles of three to five learners.
- Choose one idea to model with the whole group first.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Explaining is a skill, so teach it explicitly: model one clear
explanation with a single point, an order (first, next, last), and an example, then
guide learners to try it (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006). Keep ideas friendly and
small — no learner is put on display, and passing is always allowed. Explanations may
be spoken, signed, written, drawn, or shown with objects; all count fully. Encourage
the group to listen and then show understanding — by retelling, asking a question, or
nodding — rather than judging. This is a cooperative moment, not a contest: the goal
is that the group *understands*, and the group's understanding is the speaker's win.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Sit in your small group. Think: when did someone explain
   something so clearly that you got it right away? What made it clear?
2. **Meet the recipe (10 min).** Learn the recipe for a clear explanation: **one,
   one idea** — stick to a single point; **two, in order** — first, next, last;
   **three, an example** — something the group can picture. Say the recipe together.
3. **Watch a worked example (10 min).** The facilitator explains one small thing —
   how to wash hands, how to plant a seed, how to share a game — using the recipe.
   Notice: one idea, in order, with an example.
4. **Explain to your group (15 min).** Choose an idea card, or pick your own idea.
   Plan it quietly: what is my one point? What is my order? What is my example? Then
   explain it to your group. The group listens, then shows they understood by
   retelling it or asking one question.
5. **Close (5 min).** One idea, in order, with an example — that is how we pass
   know-how from one mind to another, in every language on Earth.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer the exact frame "First…, next…, last…" and model with a very
  familiar idea; a non-speaking learner signs or shows a picture sequence.
- **Extension:** Explain the same idea twice — once with words only, once with a
  drawing — and notice what the picture adds.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation/peer):** Can the learner explain one idea to the group,
  keeping to a single point with an order and an example, and does the group show
  they understood?

## Home connection

At home, explain one thing you know how to do — a game, a chore, a recipe — using the
recipe: one idea, in order, with an example. Ask, "Did that make sense?"
