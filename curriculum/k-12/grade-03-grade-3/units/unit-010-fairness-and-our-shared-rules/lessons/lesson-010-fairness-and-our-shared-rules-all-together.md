# Lesson 10 — Fairness & Our Shared Rules, All Together

## Summary

Learners gather everything from the unit into one six-part "fairness" page: reasons
for and against, the same act in different situations, needs and wants, left out made
more fair, a rule and its reason, and calm-down-talk-through-and-repair. They share
their page and build a class "Fairness & Our Shared Rules" book.

## Objectives

- Give reasons for and against a simple choice and say what they would do and why.
  _(D09.S1.03.01)_
- Explain why the same act can be good in one situation and not in another.
  _(D09.S1.03.02)_
- Tell the difference between a need and a want and explain why meeting needs fairly
  matters. _(D09.S2.03.01)_
- Identify a situation where someone was left out or treated unequally and describe
  how to make it more fair. _(D09.S2.03.02)_
- Explain how rules keep a group safe and fair and propose a rule for a shared space
  with a reason. _(D09.S3.03.01)_
- Explain what it means to have a voice in a group and what can happen when someone's
  voice is not heard. _(D09.S3.03.02)_
- Use a simple step-by-step way to calm down and talk through a conflict without
  hurting anyone. _(D09.S4.03.01)_
- Explain what it means to repair harm rather than only punish, using an example.
  _(D09.S4.03.02)_

## Connection

Fairness is not one big thing — it is many small things you can do every day: weighing
reasons for and against, remembering that the same act can be good or not depending on
the situation, meeting needs before wants, noticing who is left out and changing it,
proposing a rule with a reason, hearing every voice, and going from conflict to calm
talk to repair. When you put them together, you are practicing how to live fairly with
other people — at home, in class, and anywhere on Earth people are together. Today you
will put them all together.

## Materials

- A six-part "fairness" page per learner
- Crayons and drawing tools
- Stapler, string, or clips for a class book

## Preparation

- Prepare a six-box page for each learner (or one large shared sheet): "reasons for
  and against," "the same act, different situations," "needs and wants," "left out —
  made more fair," "my rule and its reason," and "calm down, talk it through, repair."
- Set up a gathering place and a spot to assemble the class book.

## Facilitator note

Written to the learner ("you"). This is the unit's capstone: each learner authors
their own small statement of fairness and shared rules. Keep all six boxes optional in
depth — a word, a mark, or a drawing counts. This is a celebration of what learners can
*do*, not a test of what they know; there is no "correct" page. Celebrate each page as
a real contribution, and never rank pages against one another. A learner who does not
speak aloud shares by pointing to a box and to a picture, or by signing; a learner who
is blind or low-vision describes their page aloud and the boxes are read back to them;
a learner with a motor difference can have a partner draw while they choose and
direct. Every voice matters — not just the loudest or fastest — and a learner may
always pass. The proposed rule, the "left out" change, and the "calm down, talk it
through, repair" strip stay live after this lesson, so the unit's habits continue
beyond the page. Questions stay questions: fairness is practiced, not preached.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Remember the unit: reasons for and against, the same act in
   different situations, needs and wants, left out made more fair, a rule and its
   reason, having a voice, and calm-talk-repair. You have practiced all of it.
2. **Fill the six boxes (20 min).** On your page, draw or write one thing in each box:
   a choice weighed with reasons for and against; the same act good in one situation
   and not in another; a need and a want, and why needs come first; a "left out" moment
   and its fair change; a rule for a shared space and its reason; and a calm-talk-repair
   path for a small conflict.
3. **Share (10 min).** With a partner or small group, show your page and tell about one
   box you are proud of. Listen to theirs.
4. **Make the class book (5 min).** Add your page to the "Fairness & Our Shared Rules"
   book or wall. Now everyone's fairness is together.
5. **Close (5 min).** Fairness is many small things you can do every day. Your choices
   matter — to your friends, your community, and you.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Complete one box at a time with a grown-up scribe; a single word or
  mark in each box counts. A non-speaking learner points to pictures for each box.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to add a seventh box: "tomorrow" — one fair or caring
  thing they will do next.

## Assessment

- **Summative (portfolio):** The six-part "fairness" page shows a weighed choice, the
  same act in two situations, a need and a want, a "left out" change, a rule and its
  reason, and a calm-talk-repair path.
- **Observation:** Does the learner connect each box to the right idea from the unit
  and share it in their own words?

## Home connection

Take your "fairness" page home and share one box with your family. Ask them what
fairness means to them, and listen.
