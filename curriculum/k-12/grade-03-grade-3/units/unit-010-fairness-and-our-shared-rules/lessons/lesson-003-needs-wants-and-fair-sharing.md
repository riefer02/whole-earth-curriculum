# Lesson 3 — Needs, Wants, and Fair Sharing

## Summary

Learners tell the difference between a **need** and a **want**, and explain why
meeting needs fairly matters. A need is something people must have to live and grow —
food, water, shelter, safety, health, learning, rest. A want is something nice but not
needed to live. Fairness begins here: before sharing extras, a group makes sure
everyone's *needs* are met.

## Objectives

- Tell the difference between a need and a want and explain why meeting needs fairly
  matters. _(D09.S2.03.01)_

## Connection

Think of a hot day. Water is a **need** — without it, a body cannot stay well. A
sweet drink is a **want** — nice, but you can live without it. When water is scarce, a
fair group makes sure *everyone* gets water before anyone gets the sweet drink. That
is why telling needs from wants matters to fairness: needs keep people alive and able
to take part, so they come first. People everywhere do this — a village shares the
well's water before anything else; a family feeds every child before any treat; a
ship shares its fresh water at sea. Today you will sort needs from wants and say why
it matters.

## Materials

- Needs-and-wants picture cards (food, water, shelter, safety, health, learning, rest;
  a new toy, sweets, a fancy gadget)
- Two sorting baskets or circles, labeled "need" and "want"
- Crayons and paper

## Preparation

- Prepare the needs-and-wants cards, each with a simple icon and a label.
- Set up the two sorting circles or baskets; arrange a circle for sharing.

## Facilitator note

Written to the learner ("you"). This lesson teaches the **need/want distinction** as
the ground of fair sharing. Keep it concrete and universal: a need is something a
person must have to live, grow, and take part; a want is something nice but not
needed. Do not shame any want — wants are human and fine; the point is only *order* of
fairness (needs before extras). This is an egalitarian, global claim: every person
everywhere has the same basic needs, and a group is fair when it meets everyone's
needs before some people's wants. Hold it as a question ("which of these would life be
harder without?"), and welcome learners who notice that not every child's needs are
met — that noticing is the beginning of caring, connected to agency and hope ("we can
help make it true"). A learner who does not speak aloud places a card in a circle or
points; a learner who is deaf or hard of hearing follows each card through sign and
picture; a learner who is blind or low-vision hears each card described and names
"need" or "want" aloud; a learner with a motor difference can direct a partner to
place the card. A learner may always pass. The goal is the learner's own explanation —
"food is a need because without it…" — not a memorized list.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of something you *must* have every day and something you
   *would like* but could live without. Which is which?
2. **Meet "need" and "want" (10 min).** A **need** is something people must have to
   live and grow: food, water, shelter, safety, health, learning, rest. A **want** is
   something nice but not needed to live: a toy, sweets, a gadget. Both can be good —
   but needs come first when there is not enough.
3. **Sort them (15 min).** Pick a card. Is it a need or a want? Place it in the right
   circle (or point, sign, or say it). For each need, finish the sentence: "This is a
   need because without it…" Wonder together: what would a day be like without one of
   the needs?
4. **Why fairness starts with needs (10 min).** Draw or write a picture of a small
   group sharing a limited need (a jug of water, a plate of food). Show everyone
   getting their need before anyone gets a want. Tell a partner why needs come first. If
   drawing is not your way, act it out or have a partner scribe while you direct.
5. **Close (5 min).** Fair begins with needs: a group is fair when everyone's needs are
   met before anyone's extras.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use a few clear cards (water, food, toy, sweets) and model "water is a
  need because…"; a non-speaking learner points to the circle; a learner with limited
  movement directs a partner.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to think of one thing that could be a need for one
  person and a want for another (a warm coat in a cold place) and wonder why.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner tell a need from a want and explain why
  meeting needs fairly matters?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The learner's "everyone's needs first" sharing picture (or
  a built, acted, or partner-scribed version).

## Home connection

At home, sort a few things into "need" and "want" with a grown-up. Wonder together:
when there is not enough, what should the family share first, and why?
