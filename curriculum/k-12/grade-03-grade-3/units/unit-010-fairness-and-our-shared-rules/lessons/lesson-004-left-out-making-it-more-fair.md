# Lesson 4 — Left Out: Making It More Fair

## Summary

Learners practice the two-step move at the heart of fairness: **notice** when someone
is left out or treated unequally, then **change** the situation to make it more fair —
invite them in, add a support, or change a rule. Fairness is something we can *do*,
not only something we can name.

## Objectives

- Identify a situation where someone was left out or treated unequally and describe
  how to make it more fair. _(D09.S2.03.02)_

## Connection

Think of a game where three children play and a fourth stands at the edge, not
invited — or a sign written in only one language that some families cannot read, or a
table too high for a friend who uses a wheelchair. Someone is **left out** or treated
**unequally**. The first step is to *notice*; the second is to *change* it: invite the
fourth child in, write the sign in more than one language, lower the table or add a
ramp. People everywhere do this — a village widens a path, a class adds a seat, a
family teaches a game to the youngest. Today you will practice noticing and then
making it more fair.

## Materials

- "Left out" picture cards (a child not invited to a game, a sign in only one
  language, a table too high for a wheelchair, a child always going last)
- Crayons and paper

## Preparation

- Prepare the "left out" picture cards.
- Arrange a circle for acting out and sharing.

## Facilitator note

Written to the learner ("you"). This lesson teaches **inclusion as an act**: fairness
is not only seeing inequality but *doing* something to change it. Keep examples
gentle, everyday, and blame-free — the point is never to shame anyone but to notice
systems and situations ("a sign in one language," "a table too high") as well as
choices ("not invited"). Include ability naturally: a ramp, a lowered table, a
translated sign are just ordinary ways everyone takes part, not "special" cases. This
is an egalitarian, global claim: everywhere, people who are left out can be brought in
by a change we can describe and make. Connect any gap to agency and hope ("we can
change this"), not fear. A learner who does not speak aloud points to who is left out
and to the change; a learner who is deaf or hard of hearing follows each card through
sign and picture; a learner who is blind or low-vision hears each situation described
and names the change aloud; a learner with a motor difference can direct a partner to
act the change. A learner may always pass. The goal is the learner's own description —
"this person was left out because…, and it would be more fair if…" — not a memorized
phrase.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of a time you saw someone left out — at a game, a meal, a
   task. Did anyone notice? Did anything change?
2. **Meet "notice, then change" (10 min).** Being **left out** or treated **unequally**
   means someone does not get to take part the way others do. Making it **more fair**
   has two steps: **notice** who is left out and why, then **change** the situation so
   they can join.
3. **Notice and change (15 min).** Pick a card. Name who is left out and why. Then
   name one change that makes it more fair (say, sign, point, or act it). Wonder
   together: could there be more than one fair change?
4. **Draw your own change (10 min).** Draw or write a "left out" moment you have seen
   or imagined, then the change that made (or would make) it more fair. If drawing is
   not your way, act it out, build it with found objects, or have a partner scribe
   while you direct.
5. **Close (5 min).** Fairness has two steps: notice who is left out, then change it so
   everyone can take part. You can do both.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use two clear cards and model "this person was left out because…, and
  it would be fair if…"; a non-speaking learner points to the change; a learner with
  limited movement directs a partner.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to propose two different changes for the same "left
  out" situation and wonder which is fairer and why.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner identify a situation where someone was
  left out or treated unequally and describe how to make it more fair?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The learner's "notice, then change" drawing (or a built,
  acted, or partner-scribed version).

## Home connection

At home or in your neighborhood, notice one place where someone could be left out (a
step, a game, a sign). Wonder with a grown-up: what one change would make it more
fair?
