# Lesson 5 — Rules That Keep Us Safe and Fair

## Summary

Learners explain how rules keep a group **safe** and **fair**. A rule is not just an
order to obey; it is a **reason in shared form** — wait your turn so everyone gets
one, wash hands so germs don't spread, walk inside so no one gets bumped, one voice at
a time so everyone is heard. Rules protect everyone, not only the loud or the strong.

## Objectives

- Explain how rules keep a group safe and fair and propose a rule for a shared space
  with a reason. _(D09.S3.03.01)_ — this lesson focuses on *explaining* how rules keep
  a group safe and fair; Lesson 6 focuses on *proposing* a rule.

## Connection

Think of a place where people share one thing — a water pump, a ball, a path, a
classroom floor. Without rules, it becomes a scramble: the fastest gets the water, the
loudest gets heard, someone gets bumped. A **rule** changes that: wait your turn, one
voice at a time, walk inside. Every rule has a **reason** — a "so that…" — and that
reason is what makes it fair instead of just bossy. People everywhere make rules this
way: a fishing crew agrees who hauls when, a market sets where stalls may stand, a
class agrees how to share the crayons. Today you will find the reason behind each rule.

## Materials

- Rule picture cards (wait your turn, wash hands before eating, walk inside, share the
  tools, one voice at a time)
- A "rule and its reason" chart
- Crayons and paper

## Preparation

- Prepare the rule cards, each with its reason on the back or a matching card.
- Prepare the "rule → reason" chart; arrange a circle.

## Facilitator note

Written to the learner ("you"). This lesson reframes **rules** as shared reasons, not
commands from above — a key egalitarian move. A rule is fair when it protects everyone
and its reason can be explained to anyone. Do not present rules as obedience ("because
I said so"); invite learners to find the "so that…" behind each one, and hold open the
question of whether a rule that cannot give a reason is really fair. Keep examples
safe, everyday, and global — rules appear in every community, and everywhere a good
rule has a reason. A learner who does not speak aloud matches a rule to its reason by
pointing or signing; a learner who is deaf or hard of hearing follows each rule through
sign and picture; a learner who is blind or low-vision hears each rule and reason
described and pairs them aloud; a learner with a motor difference can direct a partner
to match the cards. A learner may always pass. The goal is the learner's own
explanation — "this rule keeps us safe/fair because…" — not a memorized list.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of a rule you follow every day — at home, in class, on a
   road. Why do you think it is there?
2. **Meet "a rule is a reason in shared form" (10 min).** A **rule** tells everyone in
   a group what to do, so the group can be **safe** (no one is hurt) and **fair**
   (everyone gets a chance). Every good rule has a **reason** — a "so that…" we can
   explain.
3. **Match rule to reason (15 min).** Pick a rule card. Say what it is for: safety,
   fairness, or both — and finish the reason: "…so that…" (say, sign, point, or act
   it). Wonder together: what would happen without this rule?
4. **Trace a rule's reason (10 min).** Draw or write one rule from your own life and an
   arrow to its reason. Tell a partner what the rule is for. If drawing is not your
   way, act out the rule and its reason, or have a partner scribe while you direct.
5. **Close (5 min).** Rules are not just orders. They are reasons we share — so a group
   can be safe and fair for everyone.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use two rules and model "this rule is so that…"; a non-speaking learner
  points to the reason; a learner with limited movement directs a partner.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to find a rule in the room whose reason is hard to
  explain and wonder: is it still a fair rule? Why or why not?

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner explain how a rule keeps a group safe
  and fair, with a reason?
- **Performance:** The learner's "rule → reason" match and explanation, shared aloud.

## Home connection

At home, ask a grown-up the reason behind one family rule. Wonder together whether the
reason keeps everyone safe and fair — and whether any rule could be improved.
