# Lesson 8 — Calm Down and Talk It Through

## Summary

Learners practice a simple **step-by-step** way to calm down and talk through a
conflict without hurting anyone: stop and breathe, name the feeling, say what you
need, listen to the other, and find a fair solve. This is the unit's core peace skill,
taught as guided practice — the learner has seen each piece, and now walks the whole
path.

## Objectives

- Use a simple step-by-step way to calm down and talk through a conflict without
  hurting anyone. _(D09.S4.03.01)_

## Connection

Think of a time your body got hot and your voice got loud in a conflict — you wanted
to grab, yell, or cry. In that moment, words alone don't work; the body has to calm
down first. There is a simple path that works everywhere: **stop and breathe**, name
the feeling, say what you need, listen to the other person, and find a fair solve.
People everywhere do some form of this — a deep breath before answering, two neighbors
talking it out instead of shouting, friends saying "I felt… I need…" until they find a
way. Today you will practice the whole path, step by step.

## Materials

- A "calm down and talk" step strip (stop and breathe, name the feeling, say what you
  need, listen to the other, find a fair solve)
- Two puppets or soft toys
- Crayons and paper

## Preparation

- Prepare the five-step strip, each step with a simple icon.
- Set up a small space for acting out; have the calm anchor (a stone or leaf) ready.

## Facilitator note

Written to the learner ("you"). This lesson teaches **self-regulation + conflict
talk** as one skill, not two. The order matters and is the point: the body must calm
before the words can land, so Step 1 is always *stop and breathe* — never "just calm
down" as a scolding, but a real, practiced breath (slow in through the nose, slower
out through the mouth). Breathing is one way, not the only way: any slow calm that
works counts — pressing feet to the floor, holding the calm anchor, or counting slowly
— so a learner with breathing, sensory, or trauma-related difficulty never has to force
a breath. Model the full five steps as a worked example, then let
learners rehearse in pairs with a small, invented, blame-free conflict. The "I
feel… I need…" words carry the blame-free move from Grade 2 forward — no one is "bad";
each side names a feeling and a need, and the pair finds a solve that works for both.
A learner who does not speak aloud points to each step on the strip or signs; a
learner who is deaf or hard of hearing follows the steps through sign and the strip; a
learner who is blind or low-vision hears each step described and names what comes
next; a learner with a motor difference can direct a partner or hold the calm anchor.
A learner may always pass. The aim is that every learner can say, in their own way, "I
can calm down and talk it through."

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of a conflict where your body got hot first. What did you
   do — and what could you have done instead?
2. **Meet the five steps (10 min).** Learn the path: (1) **stop and breathe** — slow in,
   slower out; (2) **name the feeling** — "I feel…"; (3) **say what you need** — "I
   need…"; (4) **listen to the other** — hear their feeling and need; (5) **find a fair
   solve** — a way that works for both.
3. **Watch it modeled (10 min).** Watch (or act) one whole path: two puppets want the
   same ball. They breathe, name feelings, say needs, listen, and find a fair solve
   (take turns). Name each step as it happens.
4. **Rehearse it (15 min).** In a pair, choose a small acted conflict and walk the five
   steps. Take turns being each side. A learner who does not speak aloud points to the
   steps or directs a partner.
5. **Close (5 min).** You can calm your body and talk it through — step by step — so no
   one gets hurt and both sides are heard.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Practice just the first two steps (stop and breathe, name the feeling)
  with the calm anchor; a non-speaking learner points to each step as the pair does
  it; a learner with limited movement directs a partner.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to try a second conflict where the first fair solve
  does not work and the pair must loop back to listening to find a new one — and wonder
  why listening is the step that unlocks the solve.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner use the step-by-step way to calm down
  and talk through a conflict without hurting anyone?
- **Performance (portfolio):** The role-played path through the five steps, with the
  steps noted (or drawn or photographed), kept as a portfolio artifact.

## Home connection

At home, teach someone the five steps: breathe, name the feeling, say the need,
listen, find a fair solve. Try them the next time a small conflict comes up.
