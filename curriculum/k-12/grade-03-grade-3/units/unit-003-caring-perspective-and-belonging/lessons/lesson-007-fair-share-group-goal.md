# Lesson 7 — Fair Share, Group Goal

## Summary

Learners practice **cooperation by contributing fairly to a group goal**. They choose
one real shared goal, divide its jobs so everyone has a part, do the work together, and
reflect on whether the contribution was fair — everyone contributes, no one does
everything, and no one does nothing.

## Objectives

- Practice cooperation by contributing fairly to a group goal. _(D02.S4.03.01)_

## Connection

One person cannot lift a heavy beam, but four people can — *if* the work is shared. If
one person does everything while the others watch, that is not cooperation; it is
carrying someone else's load. If everyone takes a part that fits what they can do, the
job gets done and everyone is part of it. This is how a group reaches a goal fairly:
**everyone contributes.** Everywhere people build, harvest, and celebrate together,
this is the pattern.

## Materials

- A "group goal" sign
- A "jobs" chart
- Building or cleaning supplies

## Preparation

- Choose a real, reachable group goal (build a block structure, tidy a shared space, or
  plan a class celebration).
- Hang the jobs chart with a column for each person's name and part.

## Facilitator note

This lesson puts cooperation into practice — the skill CASEL places in relationship
skills and the spirit the curriculum names as mutual aid over competition. The key
principle to protect is **fair contribution**: everyone contributes, no one does
everything, and no one does nothing. Match each learner to a part that fits what they
can do well — a child who builds stacks, a child who plans and checks, a child who
carries — so every body and every way of helping counts fully. Watch for the learner
who quietly takes on too much and the learner who drifts to the edge; gently rebalance
the jobs chart so the load is shared. The goal is the shared one; there is no "best
helper" and no ranking. Reflect afterward on *fairness*, not speed or neatness — did
everyone get to contribute? Read the procedure aloud and model dividing jobs once
before groups do it themselves (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006).

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of a job too big for one person. How does it get done?
   Many hands — each with a part.
2. **Choose the goal (5 min).** Look at the group goal. Say what "done" looks like:
   "Our structure will stand taller than our knees," or "Our shelf will be clear."
3. **Divide the jobs fairly (10 min).** On the jobs chart, give each person a part
   that fits what they do well. The rule: **everyone contributes; no one does
   everything; no one does nothing.**
4. **Do the work (15 min).** Work together on the goal, each doing your part and
   helping when a part needs another pair of hands. Use kind words: "Need a hand?"
   "Nice job steadying that!"
5. **Reflect and close (10 min).** Look at what you made or did together. Ask: did
   everyone get to contribute? Was the sharing fair? Remember: a group reaches its goal
   when everyone carries a fair part.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Assign parts ahead of time with picture labels; pair a learner who needs
  support with a patient peer who shares the part.
- **Extension:** Invite the group to plan a bigger shared goal (a class celebration) and
  divide its jobs across several days, checking each day that the sharing stays fair.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Does the learner take a part, do it, and help others,
  so that the contribution across the group is fairly shared?
- **Peer (check):** After the work, does the group agree that everyone contributed and
  no one did everything or nothing?

## Home connection

At home, pick one shared family job — setting the meal, tidying, watering plants — and
divide its parts fairly with everyone, then notice how it feels when everyone carries a
part.
