# Lesson 10 — Caring, Perspective & Belonging

## Summary

The capstone gathers the whole unit. Learners name and show each of the eight skills of
**caring, perspective, and belonging** — taking turns and building on, an I statement,
retelling from another's view, a day in someone else's shoes, comparing communities, a
fair rule, a fair group goal, and helping one another — then fill a "caring community"
page and celebrate that care is how a community holds everyone.

## Objectives

- Take turns speaking in a group discussion and build on what a classmate said.
  _(D02.S1.03.01)_
- Use "I" statements to say how they feel without blaming during a conflict.
  _(D02.S1.03.02)_
- Retell a story from another character's point of view. _(D02.S2.03.01)_
- Imagine and describe what it is like to be someone else in a familiar place.
  _(D02.S2.03.02)_
- Compare their own community with a community in a different place or culture.
  _(D02.S3.03.01)_
- Explain how rules and routines help a community work fairly for everyone.
  _(D02.S3.03.02)_
- Practice cooperation by contributing fairly to a group goal. _(D02.S4.03.01)_
- Give an example of how people in a community help one another. _(D02.S4.03.02)_

## Connection

You have spent this unit turning outward — learning to take turns and build on, to say
how you feel without blaming, to see through another's eyes, to compare communities
fairly, to explain fair rules, and to contribute and help. Today you put it all
together. Caring is not one big act; it is these small, real skills, practiced
together. And when everyone practices them, a community becomes a place where every
person belongs. That is worth celebrating.

## Materials

- A "caring community" page (eight frames)
- Paper and crayons
- A soft turn marker

## Preparation

- Print the caring-community page with eight frames, one per skill.
- Set up the sharing circle with the turn marker.
- Plan the four-station rotation if using it (listen, see, belong, help).

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. The capstone is a **celebration of growth, not a test**: observe
what each learner can now name and show, and record it for the portfolio without
ranking anyone. Honor every way of showing — voice, sign, drawing, acting, or a written
line — all count fully, and every family and community shape is welcomed. Keep the
closing warm and inclusive; the measure of this unit is that each learner leaves
feeling they belong and can help others belong. Where a camera or tablet records the
pages for a class book, notice what the tool can do — keep a picture to remember — and
what it cannot: it does not feel the warmth of a shared circle or the fairness of a
given turn. Tools help us remember; the caring is done by people, with the living world
around them. Close with joy: caring, perspective, and belonging are skills for life, and
these learners now hold them.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Look back over the unit. Name one skill of caring you learned.
   What are you proud of?
2. **Name the eight skills (10 min).** Together, say and show the eight skills: **take
   turns and build on** (pass the marker); **say how I feel without blame** (one I
   statement); **retell from another's view** (one line as a character); **a day in
   someone else's shoes** (one imagined detail); **my community and one far away** (one
   alike, one different); **a fair rule** (one rule and why); **a fair group goal** (one
   shared part); **help one another** (one example).
3. **Fill the caring-community page (20 min).** In each of the eight frames, draw or
   write your own example of that skill — a turn you took, an I statement you used, a
   story you retold, a place you imagined, two communities you compared, a rule you
   explained, a group goal you shared, a way people help.
4. **Share (15 min).** In the circle, pass the turn marker. When it comes to you, show
   one frame and tell why it matters. Build on the person before you if you can: "I
   like what ___ showed, and…"
5. **Close (5 min).** You can care, you can see through another's eyes, and you can help
   a community belong. Carry these skills everywhere — they are how the world becomes a
   kinder place.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer the eight frames with picture prompts and a starter sentence for
  each; a non-speaking learner fills each frame with a drawing.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to write one sentence beneath each frame connecting all
  eight skills into a short "how we care" paragraph.

## Assessment

- **Summative (performance/observation):** Can the learner name and show each of the
  eight skills — taking turns and building on, an I statement, a retell from another's
  view, an imagined day, a community comparison, a fair rule, a fair group contribution,
  and an example of helping — in their own way?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The "caring community" page, kept as the unit's portfolio
  artifact.

## Home connection

Share your caring-community page with someone at home, and tell them one skill you will
keep practicing — and one way you saw people help each other this week.
