# Lesson 6 — Rules That Help Us All

## Summary

Learners look at the rules and routines they already live by — one turn each, wait in
line, quiet at the library, look before crossing — and explain **how each one helps a
community work fairly for everyone**. They discover that fair rules protect the
smallest and quietest, not just the biggest and loudest, and they propose a fair rule of
their own.

## Objectives

- Explain how rules and routines help a community work fairly for everyone.
  _(D02.S3.03.02)_

## Connection

Imagine a water pump with a crowd around it and no rule. The biggest and loudest get
water first; the smallest wait and wait. Now imagine the same pump with one simple
rule — **one turn each, wait in line**. Suddenly everyone gets water, no matter their
size. A fair rule is a promise the whole community makes: *everyone matters, and no one
is skipped.* Rules are not just about keeping order — they are how a community protects
its fairest self.

## Materials

- "Rules we know" cards
- "What does this rule help?" chart
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Prepare the rule cards (one turn each, wait in line, quiet at the library, wash hands
  before eating, look before crossing).
- Post the two-column chart: "rule" and "how it helps everyone."

## Facilitator note

This lesson is about **fairness, not obedience**. The goal is that a learner can
*explain how a rule helps everyone*, not merely recite rules. Frame rules as agreements
a community makes so that all its members — especially the small, the slow, the quiet —
are protected (the ethics and egalitarian lenses). Invite learners to notice a rule
that feels unfair and wonder together whether it helps *everyone* or only some; that
critical question is the lesson's deepest skill and should be welcomed, not shut down.
Keep the examples small and everyday, and honor that different communities hold
different rules (what is polite in one culture differs in another) — rules are made by
people for their shared life, and they can be changed when they stop being fair. Read
the procedure aloud; model one "how it helps everyone" answer before learners sort the
rest (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006).

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of a rule or routine you follow every day — in class, at
   the market, on the road. Why do you think it is there?
2. **Meet the rules (10 min).** Look at the rule cards. Say each one and act it out.
   For the first card, ask together: *who does this rule protect? how does it help
   everyone?*
3. **Watch a worked example (10 min).** Fill the first chart row together: **rule** —
   "wait in line"; **how it helps everyone** — "everyone gets a turn, not just the
   fastest or loudest, and no one is pushed." Notice: the reason is about fairness, not
   just order.
4. **Sort and explain (15 min).** Sort the rest of the cards onto the chart, saying
   how each one helps everyone. Then draw one fair rule in action — the water-pump line,
   the shared swing, the quiet library.
5. **Close (5 min).** A fair rule is a promise: everyone matters. Rules help a
   community work fairly when they protect everyone — and they can be changed if they
   stop being fair.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer the rule cards with pictures and the starter "This rule helps
   everyone because…". A non-speaking learner points to a picture and a helper says the
   reason.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to propose a new fair rule for the classroom and give
   the reason it helps everyone, then test it by asking who it leaves out.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner explain how a rule or routine helps a
  community work fairly for everyone, naming who it protects and why?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The "one fair rule in action" drawing, kept in the
  learner's portfolio.

## Home connection

At home, notice one rule or routine your family follows and ask together how it helps
everyone — and whether there is a rule that could be fairer.
