# Lesson 9 — Perspective Rehearsal

## Summary

A rehearsal that joins the unit's two big skills. Learners run a real discussion —
taking turns and building on — and then **retell the same discussion from a partner's
point of view**. They practice listening, adding, and seeing through another's eyes all
at once.

## Objectives

- Take turns speaking in a group discussion and build on what a classmate said.
  _(D02.S1.03.01)_
- Retell a story from another character's point of view. _(D02.S2.03.01)_

## Connection

You have practiced two skills this unit: taking turns and building on, and seeing
through another's eyes. But in real life, they happen *together*. In a real
conversation, you listen, you wait your turn, you add on — and all the while you are
imagining what the other person sees and feels. Today you rehearse the two skills woven
into one, because that is how caring talk actually works.

## Materials

- A soft turn marker
- A build-on and retell starters chart
- A short discussion scene

## Preparation

- Arrange a circle for gathering.
- Post the combined starters chart (build-on from Lesson 1, retell from Lesson 3).
- Choose one friendly discussion topic everyone can speak to.

## Facilitator note

This rehearsal is **retrieval and spacing in action**: it deliberately revisits and
combines two earlier skills (Lessons 1 and 3) rather than teaching something new. The
goal is fluent, automatic practice — turn-taking and building on *while* holding another
person's point of view. Run the discussion lightly; the point is that every learner gets
a turn and that at least one idea gets retold from another's view. Keep the topic
friendly so no one's life is on display, and protect the two rules from earlier lessons:
every voice gets one turn (egalitarian), and retelling another's view never ranks it as
wrong (ethics). Learners who do not speak with their mouth take their turn with sign,
gesture, or a written line, and retell by drawing the other's view. Read the procedure
aloud; model the combined move once — "I like what Amara said, and as the new student, I
think…" — before learners try (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006).

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Quick recall: what are the two rules of a good discussion? What
   does it mean to retell from another's point of view?
2. **Review the starters (5 min).** Say the build-on starters and the retell starters
   together so they are warm in your mouth.
3. **Run the discussion (15 min).** Pass the turn marker around the circle on the
   chosen topic. Each turn, either share an idea, **build on** the idea before you, or —
   the new move — **say a line from another person's point of view** ("As the ___, I
   think…"). Everyone gets a turn.
4. **Retell the discussion (15 min).** Pick a partner. Retell the whole discussion you
   just had, but *from your partner's point of view* — what did they see, feel, and
   say, and why? Then swap so each of you retells the other's view.
5. **Close (5 min).** You just listened, took turns, built on, and saw through another's
   eyes — all at once. That is caring talk, and you can do it.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer the exact combined starter "I like what ___ said, and as the ___,
  I think…". A non-speaking learner draws the other's view on a card.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to retell the discussion from *two* different people's
  points of view and compare how the two retellings differ.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Does the learner take a turn, build on an idea, and
  retell at least one idea from another's point of view during the discussion or the
  retell?
- **Peer (check):** In the retell swap, did each partner hear their own view told back
  fairly and without ranking?

## Home connection

At home, have a small family discussion, then retell it back from someone else's point
of view — and ask them to retell yours — and notice how the two views differ.
