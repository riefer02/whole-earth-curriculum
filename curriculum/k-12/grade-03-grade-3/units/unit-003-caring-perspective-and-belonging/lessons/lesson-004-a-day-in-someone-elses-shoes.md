# Lesson 4 — A Day in Someone Else's Shoes

## Summary

Learners step inside another person's day in a **familiar place** — the classroom, the
market, the playground — and describe what that person sees, hears, feels, and needs.
They discover that the same place feels very different depending on who you are in it.

## Objectives

- Imagine and describe what it is like to be someone else in a familiar place.
  _(D02.S2.03.02)_

## Connection

Walk into your classroom. To you, the ball on the shelf is right there. To a child who
uses a wheelchair, it may be too high to reach. To a new student who does not yet speak
the language, the teacher's words are a blur. To a grandparent, the floor may feel far
down and the noise very loud. It is the *same room*, but it is a different room to each
person in it. Imagining another person's day in a place you know well is how you learn
to make that place kinder for everyone.

## Materials

- A "familiar place" poster (classroom, market, or playground)
- "Someone else" cards
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Hang or draw the familiar-place poster.
- Prepare the "someone else" cards with a mix of people of different ages, abilities,
  and situations.

## Facilitator note

This lesson carries CASEL's social-awareness competency — perspective-taking — into a
place the learner knows, which is the deepest and most honest test of it. Keep every
"someone else" respected and specific, never pitied: a wheelchair user is not "sad," a
new student is not "behind" — each has a real, full experience worth understanding. Name
barriers as things the *place* could change (a step, an unexplained routine), not
shortcomings of the *person* — that is the egalitarian heart of the lesson, and it turns
empathy into the power to make a place fairer. Invite many kinds of people across the
cards, including older people, people who are Deaf or hard of hearing, people who use
mobility aids, and people new to a language — and welcome learners to add their own.
Read the procedure aloud; model one full "day" first, then let learners try (Kirschner,
Sweller & Clark, 2006).

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Look around a familiar place in your mind — your classroom, the
   market, the playground. It feels one way to *you*. Does it feel the same to everyone?
2. **Meet the place (10 min).** Look at the poster. Walk through it together: what do
   you see, hear, touch? Now pick a "someone else" card. Does the ball on the high
   shelf look the same to them? Does the noisy corner sound the same?
3. **Watch a worked example (10 min).** The facilitator imagines one full day as one
   "someone else": "I am new here, and I do not know the words yet. When the teacher
   speaks, I watch faces and hands to understand…" Notice: the place stays the same; the
   experience changes.
4. **Describe a day (15 min).** Choose your own "someone else." Walk them through the
   place in your mind and describe their day: what do they see, hear, feel, and need?
   Draw one moment of their day as they see it.
5. **Close (5 min).** The same place is a different place to each person in it. Seeing
   that is how you make it kinder — and fairer — for everyone.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer one person to imagine and a sentence starter ("As a ___, I
  see…"). A non-speaking learner draws the place from that person's view.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to name one small change to the place that would make
  it easier for their "someone else," and explain why.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Does the learner describe the familiar place from the
  chosen person's point of view — naming what they see, hear, feel, or need?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The "a day in someone else's shoes" drawing, kept in the
  learner's portfolio.

## Home connection

At home, imagine one familiar room through someone else's eyes — a younger sibling, a
grandparent, a visitor — and tell someone what you think that person sees and needs
there.
