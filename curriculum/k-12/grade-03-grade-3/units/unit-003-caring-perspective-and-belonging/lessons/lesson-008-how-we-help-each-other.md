# Lesson 8 — How We Help Each Other

## Summary

Learners gather examples of how people in a community **help one another** — harvesting
together, sharing food, watching each other's children, rebuilding after a storm — and
discover that this helping is not a favor from above but a pattern every community
depends on: **mutual aid**.

## Objectives

- Give an example of how people in a community help one another. _(D02.S4.03.02)_

## Connection

A storm blows through and a neighbor's roof is torn. The next morning, people gather —
not because anyone paid them, but because they are neighbors. They lift the beam, pass
the food, watch the children, and the roof rises. Or at harvest time, families help each
other bring in the crop, and everyone eats. This is called **mutual aid**: people
helping one another as equals, because caring for each other is how communities survive
— in every place, in every time.

## Materials

- "Helping hands" story cards
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Prepare the helping-hands cards (harvesting together, sharing food, watching
  children, rebuilding after a storm).
- Write the big idea large: "helping one another is how communities survive."

## Facilitator note

This lesson teaches **mutual aid**, the curriculum's name for care that flows between
equals rather than down from above. The distinction matters: mutual aid is not charity —
it is neighbors helping neighbors as part of one shared life, and the one who helps today
is helped tomorrow. Keep it concrete and close to the learner's own world so the concept
is real, not abstract. Invite every kind of helping and every kind of family and
community shape; a single-parent home, an extended family, a chosen community, and a
village council are all full ways of belonging. Be gentle: some learners have seen
storms, scarcity, or hard seasons, and a helping story may touch real memory — honor it
calmly, and connect it to hope and agency ("what did people do? what could we do?").
Read the procedure aloud; model one example fully before learners share their own
(Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006).

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of a time someone helped you, or you helped someone. What
   happened? How did it feel?
2. **Meet the examples (10 min).** Look at the helping-hands cards. For each, say what
   people did and why it mattered: harvesting together fills everyone's table; sharing
   food keeps a neighbor from going hungry; watching children lets every parent work.
3. **Watch a worked example (10 min).** The facilitator tells one full example — the
   storm and the roof — and names the pattern: **neighbors helping neighbors, as
   equals.**
4. **Gather your own (15 min).** Think of your own community. What help have you seen or
   given? Draw or write one example — a shared harvest, a meal brought to a sick
   neighbor, children watched together, a road cleared after a storm.
5. **Close (5 min).** Helping one another is not a rare kindness; it is how communities
   survive. And the one who helps today is helped tomorrow.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer picture cards to point at and the starter "People help by…". A
  non-speaking learner draws one example.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to tell the same example from two sides — the one who
  helped and the one who was helped — and notice how both gain.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Does the learner give a clear example of how people in a
  community help one another, and say why it matters?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The "how we help each other" drawing or writing, kept in the
  learner's portfolio.

## Home connection

At home, ask about a time your family helped neighbors or was helped by them, and add
that story to your picture or page.
