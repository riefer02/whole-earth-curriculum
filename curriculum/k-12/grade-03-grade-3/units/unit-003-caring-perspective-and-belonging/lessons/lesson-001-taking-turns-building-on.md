# Lesson 1 — Taking Turns, Building On

## Summary

Learners practice the two halves of a good group discussion: **taking turns** (waiting
for a turn instead of interrupting) and **building on** (adding to what a classmate
said, not just waiting to say your own thing). They see how ideas grow taller when
everyone adds on, like blocks in a tower.

## Objectives

- Take turns speaking in a group discussion and build on what a classmate said.
  _(D02.S1.03.01)_

## Connection

Think of a circle of friends talking about how to share a game, or where to plant a
garden. If everyone talks at once, no one hears anything. If each person waits for a
turn and then adds to what the last person said, the talk grows like a tower of blocks —
each new block rests on the one below it. "I like what Amara said, and I think we
should…" That is called *building on*, and every group of people, everywhere, uses it
to think together.

## Materials

- A soft turn marker (ball, stone, or beanbag)
- A build-on starters chart ("I like what ___ said, and…", "That makes me think…",
  "I want to add…")
- Discussion topic cards

## Preparation

- Arrange a circle for gathering.
- Post the build-on starters where everyone can see them.
- Choose one friendly discussion topic to begin with.

## Facilitator note

Taking turns and building on are the two parts of **discussion**, a skill CASEL places
inside relationship skills. Teach them as explicit, worked instruction: name the rule,
model it, then guide practice before learners try it alone (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark,
2006). The key idea to protect: *building on* is not "waiting for your turn to say your
own thing" — it is listening and then adding to what came before. Model the difference
with a clear example. Keep the topic friendly and small so no learner's life is put on
display. A learner who does not speak with their mouth builds on with sign, a gesture, a
picture card, or a written note — all count fully, and their turn is honored the same as
any other. This is an egalitarian moment: the quiet voice and the loud voice each get
one turn, and no idea is "better" for being louder. Read the procedure aloud; model each
starter with voice and body.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Sit in a circle. Think: when did a group talk get tangled
   because everyone spoke at once? What would have helped?
2. **Meet the two rules (10 min).** Learn the two rules of a good discussion:
   **one, take turns** — wait until it is your turn, and look at the speaker; **two,
   build on** — listen, then add to what the last person said. Say the starters
   together: "I like what ___ said, and…", "That makes me think…", "I want to add…".
3. **Watch a worked example (10 min).** Two volunteers (or the facilitator and a
   learner) model it: the first shares an idea; the second says, "I like what you
   said, and I think…" Notice how the second person *added on*, not just waited to
   speak.
4. **Build-on circle (15 min).** Pass the turn marker around the circle. When it
   comes to you, take one of two paths: share a new idea, or **build on** the idea
   that just came before you using a starter. Everyone gets a turn; no one is skipped.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: a group thinks better when each person takes a turn
   *and* builds on the person before. Your turn, plus your listening, grows the tower.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer the exact starter "I like what ___ said" and model one build-on
  with a partner. A non-speaking learner signs or points to a starter card.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to build on *two* ideas at once ("I like what both
  Amara and Jae said, and I think…").

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Does the learner wait for a turn, and do they add on to
  a classmate's idea rather than only stating their own?
- **Peer (check):** In the circle, did each person get a turn, and did at least one
  idea get built on?

## Home connection

At home, the next time your family or friends talk about a plan, wait for your turn and
try a build-on: "I like what you said, and I think…" Notice how the plan grows.
