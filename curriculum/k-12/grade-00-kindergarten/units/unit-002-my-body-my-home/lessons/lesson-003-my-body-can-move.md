# Lesson 3 — My Body Can Move

## Summary

Learners practice big whole-body movements — running, hopping, balancing, jumping, and
stretching — with growing control. They notice how their bodies feel while moving and
celebrate that every body moves in its own way.

## Objectives

- Demonstrate big movements (run, hop, balance) with growing control. _(D03.S2.K.01)_

## Connection

Your body loves to move. You run to greet a friend, hop over a puddle, jump to reach a
high branch, and balance on a curb, a log, or the edge of a boat. Big movements are how
you play, how you get places, and how you feel strong and happy. Children everywhere move
in their own ways — through dances, games, and songs passed down in their families and
communities — and every way of moving is worth honoring.

## Materials

- Open floor or outdoor space
- A line or rope (chalk line, rope, or low curb) to balance on
- Music (optional)

## Preparation

- Clear a safe space with room to move and a soft boundary.
- Lay a straight line or rope on the ground for balancing.
- Remind learners to watch for others and to move at their own speed.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). As facilitator, keep the space safe and
the mood joyful. **Honor every body:** a learner who cannot run can move their arms,
roll, or glide in a wheelchair; a learner who cannot hop can march or step; balancing
can be done seated, with one foot lifted, or with eyes closed. Say this aloud so no one
feels left out. Emphasize control and trying over speed or height.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Stand or sit tall. Wriggle your fingers, then your toes, then
   your whole body. Your body is ready to move.
2. **Warm up (5 min).** March in place, then stretch tall like a tree, and curl small
   like a seed. Breathe in and out slowly.
3. **Big movements (20 min).** Try each one your way:
   - **Run:** run gently to the far line and back. If running is hard, roll, glide, or
     pump your arms fast.
   - **Hop:** hop on one foot, then the other, then both together. If hopping is hard,
     step high or lift one knee.
   - **Balance:** walk slowly along the line, one careful foot in front of the other.
     If you wobble, that is okay — wobbling is your body learning. If standing is hard,
     balance a small object on your open hand or knee.
   - **Jump:** jump up and land softly, like a frog. Bend your knees when you land.
   - **Stretch:** reach high, touch low, and make the biggest shape you can.
4. **Freeze game (10 min).** When the music or a clap stops, freeze like a statue.
   Notice: is your heart beating fast? Are you warm? That is your body working.
5. **Close (5 min).** Sit and rest. Put a hand on your chest and feel your breath
   slow down. What was your favorite big movement today?

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer one movement at a time and model it; allow seated or assisted
  versions of every movement.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to join two movements together (hop then balance) and
  to invent a new big movement to teach a friend.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner attempt each big movement with growing
  control, using an adapted version if needed?
- **Self:** The learner names the movement they felt most proud of.

## Home connection

Show a grown-up your favorite big movement. Try balancing on a safe line at home — a
crack in the floor, a rope, or the edge of a mat.
