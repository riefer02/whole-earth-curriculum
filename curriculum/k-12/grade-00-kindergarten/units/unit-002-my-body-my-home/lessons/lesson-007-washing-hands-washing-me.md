# Lesson 7 — Washing Hands, Washing Me

## Summary

Learners practice a complete handwashing routine — wet, lather, scrub every surface,
rinse, and dry — and learn *when* to wash. They discover how soap and water carry germs
away, and they use water with care so there is enough for everyone.

## Objectives

- Show a self-care routine (washing hands) with growing independence. _(D03.S4.K.01)_

## Connection

Hands touch everything: a door, a pet, the ground, a friend's hand, your food. Washing
your hands is one of the simplest, most powerful ways to keep yourself and the people
around you healthy. Whether you wash at a sink, a tap, a bucket, or a stream, the trick
is the same: soap, water, and a good scrub.

## Materials

- Soap and water (a sink, basin, or bucket with safe water)
- A towel or clean cloth for drying

## Preparation

- Set up a handwashing station with safe water and soap.
- Have a short 20-second song ready to time the scrub.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). As facilitator, model the steps slowly,
then watch each learner and gently correct. **Honor every context:** where water is
scarce, teach the "wet, turn off, lather, rinse" method that saves water, and mention
that ash can work when there is no soap. Where a learner cannot use their hands, a
caregiver or older peer can help wash them. This is care, never a test — celebrate the
effort, not perfection.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of all the things your hands touched today. Hands carry
   tiny things you cannot see — we call them **germs** — that can make bodies sick.
   Washing hands washes germs away.
2. **Learn the steps (10 min).** Say the steps and practice the motions in the air:
   - **Wet** your hands.
   - **Soap** them up.
   - **Scrub** every part: palms, backs, between fingers, thumbs, wrists, and under
     nails.
   - **Rinse** the soap away.
   - **Dry** with a clean cloth.
3. **Wash for real (15 min).** Go to the station and wash your hands, doing all six
   parts. Sing a short song to yourself while you scrub — about 20 seconds is enough.
4. **When do we wash? (10 min).** Name the times hands need washing: before eating,
   after the bathroom, after playing outside, after coughing or sneezing, and after
   touching an animal. Point to a partner and share one time you will remember.
5. **Close (5 min).** Show your clean hands. Remember: washing your hands is a small
   act of care — for you and for everyone around you.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Practice the motions in the air first, then wash together; use a visual
  step card if it helps.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to teach the six steps to someone younger, and to
  notice one way to save water while washing.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner show the steps of handwashing and name
  one time to wash, with growing independence?
- **Self:** The learner checks their own hands for missed spots after washing.
