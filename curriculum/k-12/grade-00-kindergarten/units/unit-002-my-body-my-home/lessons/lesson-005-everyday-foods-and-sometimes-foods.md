# Lesson 5 — Everyday Foods and Sometimes Foods

## Summary

Learners sort common foods into "everyday" and "sometimes" groups with guidance, and
discover that everyday foods — fruits, vegetables, grains, beans, fish, and more — help
a body stay strong and full of energy. The lesson is taught with kindness: no food is
"bad," and every family's way of eating is honored.

## Objectives

- Sort common foods into "everyday" and "sometimes" groups with guidance.
  _(D03.S3.K.01)_

## Connection

Everywhere in the world, people eat — but not the same things. In one home, the
everyday food might be rice and beans; in another, flatbread and lentils; in another,
fish and root vegetables. Some foods give our bodies steady strength every day, and
some are special treats we enjoy only sometimes. Knowing the difference helps us care
for our bodies.

## Materials

- Food picture cards (fruits, vegetables, grains, beans, fish, bread, sweets, fried
  snacks, and more)
- Two sorting baskets or mats labeled "everyday" and "sometimes"

## Preparation

- Gather or draw food picture cards that include everyday foods and treat foods from
  many places and cultures.
- Label two baskets or mats: "everyday" and "sometimes."

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). **Teach it without shame.** The words
are "everyday" and "sometimes" — never "good" and "bad." No child should feel that
their family's food, culture, or budget is being judged. Some children may not have
steady access to food; keep the tone warm and matter-of-fact, and never ask a child to
report what they ate. Frame the sorting as a way to *notice* what helps a body feel
strong, not a rule to obey or a test to pass.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of a food you love. Everyone loves different foods, and
   every family eats in its own way. Today we will sort foods into two groups.
2. **Meet the two groups (10 min).** Look at the two baskets. "Everyday" foods are the
   ones that give our bodies steady strength and energy, like fruits, vegetables,
   grains, beans, fish, and milk foods. "Sometimes" foods are treats we enjoy now and
   then, like sweets and fried snacks. Neither one is bad — they just have different
   jobs.
3. **Sort together (20 min).** Pick up each food card. Which basket does it go in?
   Talk about why with a partner: "A banana is everyday because it gives me energy to
   play." If you are not sure, ask — sorting is about learning, not being right.
4. **Talk about it (5 min).** Look at the two full baskets. Which basket would help a
   body feel strong every day? Which basket would you choose for a special treat?
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: foods are friends, not rules. We care for our bodies
   by enjoying everyday foods most of the time and sometimes foods once in a while.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Sort just four familiar foods first; use real foods from the learner's
  own kitchen.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to think of one food from their family's table and
  decide which group it fits, then share why.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner place a food into the everyday or
  sometimes group and give a simple reason, with guidance?
- **Self:** The learner names one everyday food they already enjoy.

## Home connection

With a grown-up, look in the kitchen and name two everyday foods and one sometimes
food you share as a family. No food is good or bad — just noticed with kindness.
