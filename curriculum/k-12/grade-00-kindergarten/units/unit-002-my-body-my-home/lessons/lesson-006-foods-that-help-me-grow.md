# Lesson 6 — Foods That Help Me Grow

## Summary

Learners connect everyday foods to what a body needs to grow, play, and learn, and
begin to notice where food comes from — a seed, a plant, a garden, a farm, or the sea.
They close by drawing one meal that would help their body grow.

## Objectives

- Sort common foods into "everyday" and "sometimes" groups with guidance.
  _(D03.S3.K.01)_

## Connection

Food is not just for filling a tummy — it is the building material for your body and
the fuel for your play. A seed becomes a bean sprout with water and food from the soil;
a child becomes bigger and stronger with food and water, too. Wherever food comes from —
a rooftop pot, a field, a fishing boat — it helps living things grow.

## Materials

- Food picture cards (fruits, vegetables, grains, beans, fish, milk foods, eggs)
- A small plant or a sprouting seed
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Gather food picture cards focused on everyday foods.
- Have a small plant or a bean sprouting in a jar to show growth.
- Clear a space for acting out and drawing.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). As facilitator, keep the focus on what
foods *do* for a body, not on amounts or weight. Never comment on a child's size or
appetite. Be sensitive: some children have little food at home. Speak of food as
something all living things need, and honor the many ways people get it — farming,
fishing, gathering, buying, growing in pots.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Look at the little plant or seed. What does a plant need to
   grow? Water and food from the soil. Your body is like that too.
2. **Grow like a plant (5 min).** Curl up small like a seed. Pretend rain falls, the
   sun shines, and food reaches your roots. Slowly grow tall, stretch your arms like
   leaves. Food and water help you grow!
3. **What foods help me grow? (15 min).** Look at the food cards. Which foods are
   everyday foods that help a body grow, play, and learn? Sort them: fruits and
   vegetables, grains and beans, fish and eggs and milk foods. Talk with a partner
   about one food and what it helps — "Fish helps my body grow strong," or "Rice gives
   me energy to run."
4. **Where does food come from? (10 min).** Choose one food card and wonder: where did
   it come from before it was food? A seed in the ground? A tree? The sea? A garden?
   All food starts as something living.
5. **Draw a grow meal (5 min).** Draw a plate with foods that would help your body
   grow and play.
6. **Close (5 min).** Share your drawing. Food and water help every living thing grow
   — including you.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use three familiar foods and act out "grow" and "play" energy; skip the
  where-food-comes-from step if it is too much.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to sort foods by where they come from — plant, animal,
  or the sea — and to wonder which take longer to grow.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name an everyday food and connect it to
  growing or playing, with guidance?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The "grow meal" drawing.

## Home connection

With a grown-up, plant one seed or bean in a cup and put it in the sun. Give it water
and watch it grow — then notice that food and water help you grow, too.
