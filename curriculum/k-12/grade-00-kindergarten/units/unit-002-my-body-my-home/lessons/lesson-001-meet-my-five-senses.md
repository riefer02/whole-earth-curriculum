# Lesson 1 — Meet My Five Senses

## Summary

Learners discover that their body has five ways to know the world — seeing, hearing,
smelling, tasting, and touching — and match each sense to the body part that does the
job. They close by using each sense with safe, familiar objects.

## Objectives

- Name the five senses and identify a body part used for each. _(D03.S1.K.01)_

## Connection

Every day your body is quietly learning about the world. You know a mango before you
bite it — by its sweet smell and its smooth skin. You know rain is coming when you
hear it tapping the roof or the leaves, whether you live by a busy road, a field, the
sea, or a mountain. Your senses are how the world comes into you.

## Materials

- A mystery bag or covered bowl
- Four to six safe mystery items (a smooth stone, a leaf, a spoon, a soft cloth, a
  small bell or shaker)
- Crayons and paper

## Preparation

- Gather safe items that feel, smell, and sound different from each other.
- Place them in a bag or bowl the learner cannot see into.
- Arrange a circle or a comfortable spot for exploring.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. As facilitator, prepare the mystery items and guide the closing
reflection. Describe each sense aloud as you go ("the eyes see — a bright color, a
moving shadow") so learners who cannot see, hear, or use their hands can still join
every step. Remind everyone: bodies sense in different ways, and that is wonderful.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Sit comfortably. Ask yourself: how did you know what the sky
   looked like today? How did you know your food was warm? Your body is always busy
   noticing things.
2. **Meet the senses (10 min).** There are five ways your body notices the world.
   Say each one and touch the body part that does it: **see** (eyes), **hear** (ears),
   **smell** (nose), **taste** (tongue and mouth), and **touch** (skin, especially
   your hands). If you cannot see or hear, know that your other senses work extra hard,
   and that is its own kind of strong.
3. **The mystery bag (15 min).** Close your eyes or cover your eyes if you like. Reach
   in and hold one item. Do not look yet. What does it feel like — smooth, bumpy, soft,
   cold? Can you hear it if you shake it? Does it have a smell? Guess what it is, then
   look and check. Which senses did you use?
4. **Taste and smell together (5 min).** With an adult's help, notice how smell and
   taste work as a team. Close your nose gently while you take a small taste of
   something safe, then let go and taste again. Smell helps you taste!
5. **Draw it (5 min).** Draw one thing you noticed today and the body part that
   helped you notice it.
6. **Close (5 min).** Say the five senses one more time and touch each body part as
   you go. Your body is always listening to the world.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Introduce two senses at a time (touch and hearing first), using fewer
  items.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to add a sixth "sense" of their own imagining (how
  do you know you are hungry, tired, or happy?) and share it.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name a sense and point to the body
  part that does it, for at least three senses?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The sense drawing, kept in the learner's portfolio.

## Home connection

Play the mystery bag game at home with a grown-up: hide three safe things and guess
each one by feel, sound, and smell before you look.
