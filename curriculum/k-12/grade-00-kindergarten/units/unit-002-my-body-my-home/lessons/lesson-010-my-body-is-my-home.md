# Lesson 10 — My Body Is My Home

## Summary

Learners celebrate everything they learned in the unit — the five senses, big and small
movement, everyday foods, and self-care — by creating a "My Body Is My Home" picture or
book. It is a joyful capstone that honors every body.

## Objectives

- Name the five senses and identify a body part used for each. _(D03.S1.K.01)_
- Demonstrate big and small movements with growing control. _(D03.S2.K.01)_
- Sort common foods into "everyday" and "sometimes" groups. _(D03.S3.K.01)_
- Show a self-care routine and tell when to ask for help. _(D03.S4.K.01)_

## Connection

You have spent this whole unit getting to know your body — the home you will live in
your whole life. You learned what it senses, how it moves, what feeds it, and how to
wash and care for it. A home is a place you keep safe and take care of, and your body
is the closest home you have.

## Materials

- Paper and crayons or paints
- A large paper outline of a body (to fill in together)

## Preparation

- Draw or tape out a large body outline, or give each learner a blank page.
- Gather crayons or paints and any found natural materials.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). As facilitator, this is a celebration,
not a test. Help each learner recall one thing from each part of the unit and add it to
their picture or the class body outline. Keep the tone joyful and inclusive — every
body, every ability, and every way of living is welcome here.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Look down at your hands and wiggle your fingers. This is your
   body — your home. Today we remember everything we learned about caring for it.
2. **Remember the senses (10 min).** Name the five senses and touch the body part for
   each: eyes, ears, nose, tongue, and hands. Draw or place a mark for each one on
   your body picture.
3. **Remember movement (10 min).** Move your body your way! Run, roll, glide, or
   stretch; hop or step high; balance seated or standing. Then draw a small shape or
   stack something small. Add a drawing of your favorite movement to your picture.
4. **Remember food and care (10 min).** Name one everyday food that helps you grow, and
   one way you care for your body — washing hands, brushing teeth, or asking for help
   when you need it. Add those to your picture too.
5. **Share and celebrate (5 min).** Show your "My Body Is My Home" picture to a
   partner and tell them one thing you love about the home that is your body.
6. **Close (5 min).** Put your hand over your heart. Thank your body for carrying you,
   and promise to care for it — with food, movement, washing, and asking for help when
   you need it.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Work with one strand at a time, or let the learner choose just one thing
  to add to a shared class picture.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to make a small "My Body Is My Home" book with one
  page for each strand, and to add a page about a new thing they want to learn.

## Assessment

- **Portfolio:** The "My Body Is My Home" drawing or book becomes a portfolio artifact
  capturing the whole unit.
- **Observation (formative):** Can the learner recall one thing from each of the four
  strands — a sense, a movement, a food, and a self-care habit?

## Home connection

Show your "My Body Is My Home" picture to a grown-up and tell them the four ways you
care for your body: sensing, moving, eating everyday foods, and washing. Ask them what
they love about their own body.
