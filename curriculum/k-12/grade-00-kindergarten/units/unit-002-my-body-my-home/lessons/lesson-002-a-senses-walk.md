# Lesson 2 — A Senses Walk

## Summary

Learners bring their five senses outside and onto a short, safe walk, using each sense
to notice the world around them. They close by sharing one find for each sense.

## Objectives

- Name the five senses and identify a body part used for each. _(D03.S1.K.01)_

## Connection

Wherever you are, the world is full of things to notice. A market hums with voices and
banging pots; a field carries the smell of wet earth; the sea roars and sprays salt;
a mountain path is quiet except for wind and birds. A senses walk is how you meet the
place you live in — with your whole body.

## Materials

- A senses checklist with simple pictures (an eye, ear, nose, hand, mouth)
- Crayons or a pencil

## Preparation

- Choose a short, safe walking route — a garden, schoolyard, quiet street, or shore.
- Prepare or draw a simple checklist with a picture for each sense.
- Remind learners about safety: stay together, and do not taste anything unless an
  adult says it is safe.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). As facilitator, choose a safe route and
keep the pace gentle. Make the route work for every body: pick a path a wheelchair can
roll, or let a learner who cannot walk do a "senses sit" — one comfortable spot where
they notice with all their senses without moving. Describe each sense aloud as you go
so learners who cannot see or hear fully can still collect finds with their other
senses. The only sense we do **not**
use freely outside is taste — make clear that tasting unknown plants or objects is not
safe, and offer a safe food to taste afterward if you like.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Before you walk, touch the five body parts that sense the
   world: eyes, ears, nose, tongue, and hands. Today you will use all five to explore
   the place where you are.
2. **Start the walk (5 min).** Walk slowly and quietly for a moment. If you use a
   wheelchair, roll; if you cannot walk far, stay in one comfortable spot and let the
   senses come to you. What is the very first thing you **hear**? Point to your ear.
3. **Sense stops (20 min).** Pause along the way to collect one find for each sense:
   - **See:** one thing with a color or shape you love.
   - **Hear:** one sound, near or far.
   - **Smell:** one smell in the air.
   - **Touch:** one thing that is safe to touch — rough, smooth, wet, warm, or cool.
   - **Taste:** do not taste anything outside. Instead, remember a taste from your day.
   Mark each one on your checklist as you go.
4. **Share (10 min).** Find a partner and trade: "I saw…", "I heard…", "I touched…"
   If you cannot see or hear, share what your other senses found — a smell, a texture,
   a taste you remember.
5. **Close (5 min).** Look at your checklist. Which sense did you use the most today?
   Which one surprised you?

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Focus on two senses (hearing and touching) and use a shorter route.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to describe a find using two senses at once (how a
  leaf looks *and* feels), and to sketch it from memory.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name a sense and share one thing they
  found with it during the walk?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The completed senses checklist.

## Home connection

Take a grown-up on a short senses walk near home. Ask them what they hear, and tell
them what you notice that they miss.
