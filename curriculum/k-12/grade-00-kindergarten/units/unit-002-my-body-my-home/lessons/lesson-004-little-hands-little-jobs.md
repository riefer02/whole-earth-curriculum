# Lesson 4 — Little Hands, Little Jobs

## Summary

Learners practice small, careful hand movements — drawing, stacking, pinching, and
threading — with growing control. They discover that hands (and feet, mouths, and
tools) can do many little jobs that help us every day.

## Objectives

- Demonstrate small movements (draw, stack) with growing control. _(D03.S2.K.01)_

## Connection

Your hands do little jobs all day long: buttoning a shirt, peeling fruit, turning a
page, stacking cups, picking up a grain of rice, drawing a picture. People everywhere —
in a kitchen, a workshop, a garden, or a boat — use their hands for small, careful
work. Practice makes hands strong and clever.

## Materials

- Stacking objects (blocks, cups, or small boxes)
- Drawing tools and paper
- Threading objects (large beads and a thick string or shoelace)

## Preparation

- Set up a few "little job" stations: a stacking spot, a drawing spot, and a threading
  spot.
- Choose objects that are big enough for small hands to grip safely.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). As facilitator, keep the stations simple
and the pace unhurried. **Honor every body:** a learner who cannot use their hands can
do the same jobs with their feet, their mouth, or a helper tool, or use larger objects;
a learner with low grip strength can start with bigger, softer items. Growth in control
is the goal, not speed or perfection.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Look at your hands. Wiggle your fingers, then make a fist, then
   open wide. Hands can do so many little jobs.
2. **Stack (10 min).** At the stacking spot, build a tower of blocks or cups as high
   as you can before it falls. Place each one slowly and carefully. If stacking with
   your hands is hard, use bigger objects or stack with help from a friend.
3. **Draw (10 min).** At the drawing spot, draw one small thing you can see — a leaf,
   a spoon, your own hand. Hold your drawing tool in a comfortable grip and go slowly.
4. **Pinch and thread (10 min).** Pinch one bead between your thumb and finger, and
   slide it onto the string. Add more beads one at a time. This is the same pinch you
   use to pick up a crumb or button a shirt.
5. **Little job hunt (5 min).** Look around. Name one little job your hands do at home
   or at school — carrying a cup, closing a lid, turning a page.
6. **Close (5 min).** Shake your hands out and rest them. What was the hardest little
   job today? What felt easy?

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer larger, softer objects and one station at a time; model the pinch
  grip slowly.
- **Extension:** Challenge learners to stack with the non-dominant hand, or to thread
  beads in a color pattern (red, blue, red, blue).

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner stack a few objects and draw with
  growing control, using an adapted version if needed?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The drawing, kept in the learner's portfolio.

## Home connection

Ask a grown-up to show you a little job their hands do (peeling, folding, braiding).
Try a little job of your own: stack the cups, thread a button, or sort the beans.
