# Lesson 1 — Big Movements

## Summary

Learners return to the body in motion, practicing big movements — running, hopping,
jumping, and galloping — and learning to control how fast, how far, and when to stop.
They close by noticing how their bodies feel during and after moving.

## Objectives

- Demonstrate big movements (run, hop, balance) with growing control. _(D03.S2.K.01)_

## Connection

Your body is made to move big. You run to meet a friend, hop over a puddle, jump to
reach a branch, and gallop like a horse in a game. Children everywhere move big in
their own games, dances, and celebrations — running races on a beach, hopping in a
playground, jumping at a festival — and every way of moving big is worth honoring.
Moving big helps you feel strong, warm, and happy.

## Materials

- Open floor or outdoor space
- Markers or cones for a start line and a turn line
- Music (optional)

## Preparation

- Clear a safe space with a soft boundary and room to move without bumping.
- Mark a starting line and a turning line a short, safe distance apart.
- Remind learners to watch for others and to move at their own speed.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). As facilitator, keep the space safe and
the mood joyful. **Honor every body:** a learner who cannot run can pump their arms,
roll, or glide in a wheelchair; a learner who cannot hop can step high or march;
galloping can become a seated arm swing. Say this aloud so no one feels left out.
Emphasize **control** — starting, stopping, and changing speed — over speed or height.
Add a visual signal beside every sound cue — a raised hand for **stop**, a low hand for
**slow**, a waving hand for **fast** — so learners who cannot hear the cue can join fully.
This is a spiral step beyond "My Body Can Move" (U.00.002): now the focus is on
controlling movement, not just trying it.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Stand or sit tall. Wriggle your fingers, your toes, and your
   whole body. Today you will move big and learn to control how you move.
2. **Warm up (5 min).** March in place, stretch tall like a tree, then curl small
   like a seed. Breathe in and out slowly.
3. **Learn each big movement (20 min).** Try each one your way, from the start line
   to the turn line and back:
   - **Run:** run gently to the turn line and back. If running is hard, roll, glide,
     or pump your arms fast.
   - **Hop:** hop on one foot, then the other, then both together. If hopping is
     hard, step high or lift one knee with a big swing.
   - **Jump:** jump forward and land softly like a frog, bending your knees.
   - **Gallop:** step-hop like a horse — one foot leads, the other chases. If
     galloping is hard, swing your arms like a horse's legs while you sit or stand.
4. **Control game (10 min).** When the music or a clap says **fast**, move quickly;
   when it says **slow**, move in slow motion; when it says **stop**, freeze. Notice:
   can you stop your body all the way still? That is control.
5. **Close (5 min).** Sit and rest. Put a hand on your chest and feel your heartbeat
   slow down. Which big movement made you feel strongest?

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer one movement at a time and model it; allow seated or assisted
  versions of every movement and a shorter course.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to join two movements together (run then gallop) and
  to invent a new big movement to teach a friend.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner attempt each big movement with growing
  control — starting, stopping, and changing speed — using an adapted version if
  needed?
- **Self:** The learner names the movement they felt most proud of.

## Home connection

Show a grown-up your favorite big movement. Practice starting and stopping on a word:
play "go and stop" in a safe open space at home.
