# Lesson 2 — Small Movements

## Summary

Learners practice small movements of the hands and fingers — stacking, drawing,
pinching, and threading — with growing care and control. They close by choosing one
small movement they are proud of and showing it.

## Objectives

- Demonstrate small movements (draw, stack) with growing control. _(D03.S2.K.01)_

## Connection

Your hands do careful work all day long. You stack blocks into a tower, draw a picture
for someone you love, pinch a berry from a bush, and thread a button through its hole.
People everywhere use their hands to make and build — stacking stones into a wall,
threading beads onto a necklace, drawing patterns in the sand. Small movements are how
your hands learn to be careful and strong.

## Materials

- Blocks or small stacking objects (blocks, cups, or smooth stones)
- Crayons and paper
- Beads or large buttons and a string

## Preparation

- Set up three simple hand stations: stacking, drawing, and threading.
- Make sure small objects are safe for little hands (nothing too tiny to manage).
- Remind learners to keep small objects away from mouths.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). As facilitator, move among the
stations encouraging patience and care, not speed or perfection. **Honor every body:**
a learner with limited hand control can stack larger, lighter objects, draw with a
chunky crayon or a whole-hand grip, and thread a wide shoelace through large holes.
Celebrate effort — "look how carefully your fingers worked" — more than the finished
product.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Look at your hands. Open and close them slowly, then wriggle
   each finger. Your hands are ready to do careful work.
2. **Warm up (5 min).** Squeeze your hands into fists, then open them wide like a
   star. Pretend to play a tiny piano with your fingers in the air.
3. **Try each small movement (25 min).** Move between the stations, trying each one
   with care:
   - **Stack:** build the tallest tower you can before it wobbles. Stack slowly —
     one block at a time.
   - **Draw:** draw a picture of something you love. Hold your crayon however feels
     comfortable, and make slow, careful lines.
   - **Pinch:** pick up a bead or button between your thumb and first finger. Move
     it from one pile to another.
   - **Thread:** slide beads or buttons onto a string, one at a time.
4. **Show your best (5 min).** Choose the one small movement you feel most proud of
   and show it to a partner. Tell them what your hands did carefully.
5. **Close (5 min).** Shake your hands out and rest them. What is one thing your
   hands got better at today?

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer larger, lighter objects to stack and thread; allow whole-hand or
  assisted drawing.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to combine two small movements (draw a shape, then
  stack blocks around it) and to thread a simple repeating pattern (red, blue, red).

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner attempt each small movement with
  growing care and control, using an adapted version if needed?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The learner's drawing, kept as a sample of growing hand
  control.

## Home connection

Help with a careful hand job at home — stacking clean cups, threading a button, or
sorting beans. Tell a grown-up which small movement your hands are best at.
