# Lesson 6 — Senses on the Move

## Summary

Learners notice that moving changes what their senses find. They walk, hop, roll, and
pause to collect one find for each sense — how the wind feels, what sounds come and go,
what smells drift past — then share what they discovered.

## Objectives

- Name the five senses and identify a body part used for each. _(D03.S1.K.01)_

## Connection

When you move, the world moves past you. Run and you feel the wind on your face; ride
and the sounds rush by; walk through a market and the smells change at every stall.
People everywhere know this — a fisher feels the salt spray and smells the sea, a child
running through a field feels the grass brush their legs and hears the birds scatter.
Moving lets your senses notice more, and differently, than sitting still.

## Materials

- A short, safe route to move along
- A senses-in-motion checklist (eye, ear, nose, hand, mouth pictures)
- Crayons or a pencil

## Preparation

- Choose a short, safe route — a path, yard, garden, or hallway.
- Prepare a simple checklist with a picture for each sense.
- Remind learners: stay together, and do not taste anything outside unless an adult
  says it is safe.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). As facilitator, keep the pace gentle
and make the route work for every body — a path a wheelchair can roll, or a "senses
sit" in one comfortable spot where a learner notices while the world moves around them.
Describe each sense aloud as you go. The only sense we do **not** use freely outside is
taste — make clear that tasting unknown plants is not safe, and offer a safe food to
taste afterward if you like. This lesson extends U.00.002's "A Senses Walk" by adding
motion — ask learners what *changed* when they moved.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Touch the five body parts that sense the world: eyes, ears,
   nose, tongue, and hands. Today you will move and notice all at once.
2. **Warm up (5 min).** Walk in place, then hop gently. Notice: what do you already
   hear and feel as you start to move?
3. **Move and sense (20 min).** Move slowly along your route, and try a different way
   of moving as you go — walk, hop, roll, or glide. As you move, collect one find for
   each sense:
   - **Feel:** the wind, the sun, or the ground under your feet or wheels.
   - **Hear:** a sound that comes closer as you move, and one that fades behind you.
   - **See:** something you only notice because you are moving past it.
   - **Smell:** a smell that drifts to your nose as you move.
   - **Taste:** do not taste anything outside — instead remember a taste from your day.
   Mark each one on your checklist.
4. **Share (10 min).** Find a partner and trade: "when I moved, I felt…", "I heard…",
   "I smelled…". If you cannot see or hear, share what your other senses found.
5. **Close (5 min).** What was different because you were moving? Would you have
   noticed it sitting still?

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Focus on two senses (feel and hear) and a shorter route; move slowly
  and pause often.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to compare one spot while still and while moving, and
  to sketch the difference.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name a sense and share one thing they
  noticed with it while moving?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The completed senses-in-motion checklist.

## Home connection

Take a grown-up on a short moving-and-sensing walk near home. Ask them what they notice
only because they are moving, and tell them what you found too.
