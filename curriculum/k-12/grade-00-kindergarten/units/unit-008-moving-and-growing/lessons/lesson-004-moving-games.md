# Lesson 4 — Moving Games

## Summary

Learners bring their growing movement skills into play, joining cooperative games —
follow the leader, mirror, and freeze dance — where everyone belongs and no one is
"out." They practice moving together, taking turns to lead, and inviting every body
to join.

## Objectives

- Demonstrate big movements (run, hop, balance) with growing control while playing
  together. _(D03.S2.K.01)_

## Connection

Games are how children everywhere move and play together. You might chase and tag on a
beach, play hopscotch on a sidewalk, follow a leader through a field, or clap and dance
in a circle at a celebration. Some games are the same in many places, and some belong
to just one family or one people. Moving games help you have fun, make friends, and
practice moving your body with control.

## Materials

- Open floor or outdoor space
- Music (optional)

## Preparation

- Clear a safe space with enough room to move without bumping.
- Remind learners that these are together-games: everyone plays, nobody is "out."
- Plan simple, large, safe movements for the follow-the-leader rounds.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). As facilitator, keep every game
**cooperative** — cooperation over competition is a core commitment. No elimination, no
winners and losers. **Honor every body:** choose and model leader movements that can
be done big or small, standing or seated, so a learner who uses a wheelchair or has
limited mobility can lead as easily as anyone. Add a visual "freeze" cue — a raised
hand, a flag, or the leader lifting both arms — alongside the clap or music so learners
who cannot hear can play fully. Rotate who leads fairly so everyone who wants a turn
gets one.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Stand in a loose circle. Wave to the friends around you.
   Today you will move and play together, and everyone is welcome.
2. **Warm up (5 min).** March in place together, then freeze on a clap. Shake your
   hands and feet.
3. **Follow the leader (10 min).** One person is the leader and makes a big movement —
   hop, gallop, stretch, or roll — and everyone follows. The leader chooses movements
   every body can do. Change leaders often so many people get a turn.
4. **Mirror (10 min).** Face a partner. One person moves slowly — raise an arm, wiggle
   a finger, bend a knee — and the other copies like a mirror. Then switch.
5. **Freeze dance (10 min).** When the music or a clap plays, dance or move your way.
   When it stops, freeze like a statue. Everyone plays; no one is out.
6. **Close (5 min).** Sit together and rest. What did it feel like to move with your
   friends? What movement did you learn from someone else today?

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer seated or small versions of every movement; pair a learner who
  wants support with a calm partner.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to invent a new moving game and to lead a round using
  a movement they created themselves.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner join cooperative movement games,
  following and taking a turn to lead, with growing control?
- **Peer:** Partners tell each other one movement they enjoyed doing together.

## Home connection

Teach a grown-up or a friend a moving game you played today. Play follow the leader or
freeze dance together, and let them lead a round too.
