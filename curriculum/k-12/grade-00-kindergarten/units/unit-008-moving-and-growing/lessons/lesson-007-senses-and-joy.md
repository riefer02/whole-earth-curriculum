# Lesson 7 — Senses and Joy

## Summary

Learners turn their senses toward joy, naming one favorite thing for each sense — a
beloved smell, taste, sound, sight, and touch — and discovering that delight is shared
and yet different for everyone. They close by drawing or sharing their favorite.

## Objectives

- Name the five senses and identify a body part used for each. _(D03.S1.K.01)_

## Connection

Your senses don't just tell you what is there — they also bring you joy and comfort.
The smell of bread baking, a song that makes you dance, the warmth of a hug, the taste
of a favorite fruit, a beautiful sunset. People everywhere find joy through their
senses, and every person has different favorites — one child loves the sea's roar,
another the quiet of a snowy field. Sharing what delights us is a way we know each
other.

## Materials

- Crayons and paper
- A large "joy senses" chart with one section per sense
- A few safe favorite objects (optional)

## Preparation

- Draw a large chart with five sections labeled with a picture: eye, ear, nose, tongue,
  hand.
- Gather a few safe objects that feel good to sense (a soft cloth, a smooth stone).
- Remind learners that favorites are personal and every answer is welcome.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). As facilitator, keep the mood warm and
free of judgment — there are no "wrong" favorites. **Honor every body:** a learner
with low vision may find joy in touch and sound; a learner with limited hearing may
love color and texture. This is also a values moment: the lesson teaches that joy is
shared and personal at once, and that every way of sensing and delighting is worthy.
No one's favorites should be mocked or ranked.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Sit comfortably and take a slow breath. Think of one thing that
   makes you smile just by sensing it.
2. **Name the senses (5 min).** Touch each sensing body part: eyes, ears, nose, tongue,
   hands. Today we ask: what does each one *love*?
3. **One favorite for each sense (15 min).** Go around your group or think to yourself
   and fill the chart:
   - **See:** a color or sight you love.
   - **Hear:** a sound that makes you happy.
   - **Smell:** a smell that comforts you.
   - **Taste:** a taste that makes you smile.
   - **Touch:** a feeling on your skin that is soft, warm, or cozy.
   Add your favorites to the big chart, in words or pictures.
4. **Draw your favorite (10 min).** Choose your most-loved one and draw it. If you
   cannot see, draw by feel or describe it aloud.
5. **Share (5 min).** Tell a partner one favorite and listen to theirs. Did any of
   your favorites match? Were some different? Both are wonderful.
6. **Close (5 min).** Notice how your body feels right now. Joy often feels warm and
   light. What is one sense you want to enjoy more today?

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer one or two senses at a time; let learners answer with a gesture or
  a single word instead of a full sentence.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to share a favorite that belongs to their family or
  culture — a special song, food, or smell — and tell its story.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name a sense and share one favorite
  thing it brings them?
- **Self:** The learner identifies which sense brings them the most joy.

## Home connection

Ask a grown-up what their favorite smell, sound, and taste are, and tell them yours.
Notice how your favorites are alike and different — and enjoy one together.
