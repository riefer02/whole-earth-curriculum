# Lesson 9 — Sorting Everyday and Sometimes Foods

## Summary

Learners sort common foods into "everyday" and "sometimes" groups with gentle
guidance. They discover that foods are not "good" or "bad" — some we eat often to keep
our bodies going, some we enjoy on special days — and that every family's "everyday"
is different.

## Objectives

- Sort common foods into "everyday" and "sometimes" groups with guidance.
  _(D03.S3.K.01)_

## Connection

Think about the foods in your home. Some you eat often — they keep your body going
every day, like rice, bread, fruit, vegetables, or beans. Some are special treats you
enjoy once in a while — a sweet at a festival, a special dish at a holiday, a cool
drink on a hot day. Every family is different: what is "everyday" in one home may be a
treat in another, and both ways of eating are okay. There is no shame in food, and no
food is "bad" — we just enjoy different foods in different amounts.

## Materials

- Food picture cards (a mix of everyday and special-occasion foods from many places)
- Two sorting baskets or mats labeled "everyday" and "sometimes"
- Crayons and paper

## Preparation

- Prepare food cards showing a variety of everyday and special foods from different
  cultures.
- Set out two sorting baskets or mats labeled "everyday" and "sometimes."
- Remind learners that this sorting is about *how often*, not about good or bad.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). As facilitator, hold the line on
**no food shaming** — the categories are about *how often* a food is eaten, never about
whether a food (or a body) is "good" or "bad." Frame "sometimes" as *special and
enjoyable*, not forbidden. **Honor every family:** a food you might call "sometimes"
could be a daily staple for a learner's family, and vice versa — let learners place
cards by their own family's real life, and celebrate the differences. This extends
U.00.002's "Everyday Foods and Sometimes Foods" by centering the sorting as a shared,
discussion-rich activity.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** What is a food you eat almost every day? What is a food you
   only have on special days? Both are part of eating well.
2. **Meet the two groups (10 min).** Look at the two baskets. "Everyday" foods are the
   ones that keep your body going day after day — like grains, fruits, vegetables,
   beans, or milk. "Sometimes" foods are the ones you enjoy once in a while — a sweet
   at a celebration, a special holiday dish, a fizzy drink on a hot day.
3. **Sort together (15 min).** Pick up a food card. Think: do I eat this often, or only
   sometimes? Place it in a basket and say why. Remember: there is no wrong answer,
   because every family is different. A food can be "everyday" for one friend and
   "sometimes" for another.
4. **Draw one of each (10 min).** Draw one everyday food and one sometimes food you
   enjoy. Notice: do you like them both? Of course — and that is fine.
5. **Close (5 min).** Everyday foods keep us going; sometimes foods make days special.
   Both have a place in a happy, healthy life.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer a small set of very familiar foods and sort together one card at
  a time; let learners point, gesture, or name foods aloud instead of picking up cards,
  so the sort does not require fine hand control.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to sort by their own family's real routine and to
  explain why a food goes where it does.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner place a common food in an "everyday" or
  "sometimes" group and give a simple reason?
- **Self:** The learner names one everyday food and one sometimes food they enjoy.

## Home connection

With a grown-up, look at the foods in your home and sort a few into "everyday" and
"sometimes." Talk about which treats your family saves for special days.
