# Lesson 1 — Making with Many Materials

## Summary

Learners meet many materials and discover that anything — paper, chalk, leaves, or
string — can become a picture or a shape. They explore freely, then share what they
made. The point is the making, not a "perfect" result.

## Objectives

- Create art with different materials and describe what they made.
  _(D07.S1.K.01)_

## Connection

Think of the last time you drew in the sand with a stick, or made a mark with your
finger on a steamy window. People everywhere make things from what is around them —
paper, clay, cloth, shells, leaves. Your hands can turn almost anything into a
picture or a shape.

## Materials

- Paper (scrap or fresh)
- Crayons, pencils, or chalk
- A tray of loose parts: leaves, bottle caps, cloth scraps, string, small sticks

## Preparation

- Gather materials into easy-to-reach trays or piles.
- Make space to sit and make, at tables or on the floor.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. As facilitator, protect the freedom: there is no right way to
use a material. Praise effort and curiosity, not "correctness." Some learners will
not want to talk about their work — that is fine; pointing or showing is enough.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Sit with the materials. Look at each one: paper, crayon,
   leaf, string. Touch them. Which feels smooth? Which feels bumpy?
2. **Try one material (10 min).** Pick one material and make something with it — a
   mark, a line, a pile, a shape. There is no wrong way to use it.
3. **Try a second and third material (15 min).** Swap materials. What happens when
   you press a leaf onto paper, or lay string in a circle? Make something new with
   two materials together.
4. **Share (10 min).** Show a friend or grown-up what you made. You can say what it
   is, or just point to your favorite part.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: any material can become art, and everything you make
   is yours.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer just two materials and model one simple mark ("I made a line;
   now you try").
- **Extension:** Invite learners to combine three materials into one piece and name
  the feeling of each texture.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Does the learner handle more than one material and
  make a mark or shape?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The learner's piece (or a photo of it) kept in the
  portfolio.

## Home connection

Ask a grown-up at home: "What can we find around the house to make art with?" Make
one small thing together from what you find.
