# Lesson 10 — Making & Imagining

## Summary

This capstone gathers everything the unit explored. Learners lay out their work like
a gallery and share one thing they drew, sang, danced, or built. They see, all at
once, that drawing, music, movement, and building are all ways of making and
imagining — and that every piece has value and a story.

## Objectives

- Create art with different materials and describe what they made.
  _(D07.S1.K.01)_
- Sing, clap, or play a simple rhythm and say how it makes them feel.
  _(D07.S2.K.01)_
- Move or pretend to be a person, animal, or character in response to a story or
  music. _(D07.S3.K.01)_
- Build or make something from available materials and explain what it does.
  _(D07.S4.K.01)_

## Connection

All through this unit, you have made things with your hands, your voice, your body,
and your ideas. Today you get to look at it all together, the way a family or a
village shows its work at a fair or festival. Making is how you share who you are with
the world — and every piece you made has a story worth hearing.

## Materials

- Each learner's favorite piece from the unit
- A simple display space (table, wall, or cloth)

## Preparation

- Help learners choose one favorite piece (a drawing, a rhythm, a movement, a build).
- Arrange a display space and a circle for sharing.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. As facilitator, make this a true celebration, not a contest.
There are no "best" pieces — the point is that every child shares and every child is
heard. Let learners share in whatever form suits them: talk, point, sing, move, or
show. Close by naming the unit's big idea together: making is a voice, and every
voice matters.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Bring your favorite thing from this unit. Look around at what
   everyone brought. It is a gallery of making.
2. **Set up the gallery (10 min).** Lay out the drawings and builds. Remember the
   songs and dances are part of the gallery too — they live in your voice and body.
3. **Walk the gallery (10 min).** Move quietly around and look. Find one thing that
   makes you curious. Ask its maker, "Tell me about this."
4. **Share and celebrate (15 min).** One at a time, share your piece — show it, sing
   it, dance it, or explain what it does. Everyone claps for everyone, because every
   piece has a story.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: your hands, your voice, your body, and your ideas can
   all make something new. That is making and imagining — and it is yours forever.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer a gentle prompt to share ("show us one color you used") and let
  the learner point rather than speak.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to name one thing they want to make next, and how
  they would make it.

## Assessment

- **Summative (portfolio):** The learner's favorite piece, kept in the portfolio with
  a note of its story.
- **Performance (observation):** The learner shares one piece from the unit, in any
  form.

## Home connection

Make a tiny gallery at home: lay out a few things you made this week and give a
grown-up a "tour," telling the story of each one.
