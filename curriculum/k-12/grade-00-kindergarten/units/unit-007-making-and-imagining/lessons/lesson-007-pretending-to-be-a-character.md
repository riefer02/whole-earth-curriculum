# Lesson 7 — Pretending to Be a Character

## Summary

Learners step into a story and become its characters. Using their voice, face, and
body, they play a helper, a seeker, a kind neighbor. Pretending lets a child feel
what another person feels — a small, safe lesson in walking in someone else's shoes.

## Objectives

- Move or pretend to be a person, animal, or character in response to a story or
  music. _(D07.S3.K.01)_

## Connection

When you play make-believe, you get to be someone else for a while — a cook, a
builder, a helper, a hero. People everywhere tell stories and act them out to share
ideas and to understand each other. Your voice, face, and body are your costume.

## Materials

- A simple story with a few characters
- Open floor space
- A simple prop (optional): a cloth, basket, or hat

## Preparation

- Choose or tell a short story with two or three clear characters.
- Clear a space for acting and set out any simple props.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. As facilitator, choose or tell a story where characters are
kind and recognizable, from any culture, told respectfully as a story to play with.
Never push a shy learner to perform; watching and copying is full participation.
Celebrate every character — there is no "correct" way to play one.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Sit and listen to a short story. Who is in it? What do they
   do?
2. **Meet the characters (10 min).** Pick a character. How do they stand? How do they
   talk — fast, slow, high, low? Try it: their walk, their voice, their face.
3. **Become the character (15 min).** Act out a small part of the story as your
   character. Use your voice, your face, and your whole body. Trade characters and
   try another.
4. **Show and guess (10 min).** Act your character for a friend. Can they guess who
   you are? Then tell them how it felt to be that person.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: pretending helps us feel what others feel. There is a
   whole world of characters in every story.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Give one simple character and one action ("you are the helper carrying
  the basket"); let the learner copy.
- **Seated/adapted:** A character lives in the face, voice, and hands — play the whole
  character from a chair; a "walk" becomes a lean, a nod, or a hand gesture, and
  acting with just the face or voice is full participation.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to add a new character to the story and act how that
  character would join in.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Does the learner use voice, face, or body to show a
  character?
- **Performance:** The learner's character, observed live.

## Home connection

With someone at home, pick a person from a story or a memory and both pretend to be
them for a minute. Ask each other how it felt.
