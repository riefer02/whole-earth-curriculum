# Lesson 4 — Singing and Clapping

## Summary

Learners make rhythm with nothing but their hands and voices. They echo simple clap
patterns, keep a steady beat together, and invent their own. Rhythm belongs to
everyone and needs no instrument at all.

## Objectives

- Sing, clap, or play a simple rhythm and say how it makes them feel.
  _(D07.S2.K.01)_

## Connection

Think of a song you know — maybe a lullaby, a clapping game, or a song you sing
while working. In every part of the world, people clap, sing, and tap to keep a beat
together. Your hands and your voice are an instrument you always carry with you.

## Materials

- Hands and voices (the only true instruments)
- A simple clapping or tapping rhythm to echo

## Preparation

- Choose one short clap pattern (for example: clap, clap, pat-knees) to teach as an
  echo.
- Arrange the group in a circle so everyone can see and hear.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. As facilitator, keep patterns very short and repeat them many
times. There is no "wrong" clap — closeness to the beat is something that grows. Let
every learner lead a pattern if they want to; leadership in rhythm is for everyone.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Sit or stand in a circle. Pat your knees slowly. Can you feel a
   steady beat?
2. **Echo claps (10 min).** Listen to a short clap pattern, then clap it back, just
   like an echo. Try a few different patterns. Say the sounds too — "clap, clap,
   pat."
3. **Keep the beat (10 min).** Sing a simple song you know, or a line of one, while
   you clap or tap the beat. Stay together, like one big heartbeat.
4. **Invent a rhythm (10 min).** Make up your own short pattern. Teach it to a friend
   and clap it together.
5. **Close (5 min).** Notice how the rhythm makes you feel — bouncy, calm, excited?
   Remember: you can make music with your own body, anywhere.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Echo one clap at a time, very slowly, and let the learner lead with a
  single beat.
- **Deaf/HH path:** Make the beat visible — clap and pat where everyone can see — and
  invite learners who do not hear to feel the beat as vibration by resting a hand on a
  drum, a tabletop, or the floor while others tap.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to layer two rhythms — one group keeps the steady
  beat while another claps a pattern over it.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Does the learner clap or tap a simple rhythm and
  name a feeling it gives them?
- **Performance:** The learner's echo or invented pattern, observed live.

## Home connection

Teach someone at home a clap pattern and clap it together. Ask them how it made them
feel.
