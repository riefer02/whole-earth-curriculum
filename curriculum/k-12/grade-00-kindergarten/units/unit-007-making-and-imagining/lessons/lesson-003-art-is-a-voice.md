# Lesson 3 — Art Is a Voice

## Summary

Learners discover that a picture can "say" a feeling without a single word. They draw
a feeling, share it, and guess what others' pictures say. People in every part of the
world use pictures, weaving, carving, and paint to share what is inside them.

## Objectives

- Create art with different materials and describe what they made.
  _(D07.S1.K.01)_

## Connection

Have you ever seen a picture and known, right away, that it felt happy or stormy or
calm? A drawing can speak before you say a word. All over the world, people make
pictures, patterns, weavings, and carvings to share feelings and stories — art is a
voice that does not need words.

## Materials

- Paper and crayons or paint
- A set of feeling pictures (happy, sad, calm, excited, cozy)

## Preparation

- Draw or print a simple set of feeling pictures to show as examples.
- Set out drawing supplies where everyone can reach them.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. As facilitator, frame art as communication, not decoration:
"your picture is saying something." When guessing, never say a feeling is wrong —
say "I see something a little different; tell me what it says to you." Honor
non-speaking learners fully; a pointing guess is a real guess.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Look at the feeling pictures. What does each one seem to say —
   happy, sad, calm, excited, cozy? You can say it or point to it.
2. **Choose a feeling (5 min).** Pick one feeling you want your picture to say. Feel
   it in your face and body first: how would you show it?
3. **Draw your voice (15 min).** Draw a picture that says that feeling. It can be a
   face, a place, colors, or shapes — whatever says it for you.
4. **Share and guess (15 min).** Show your picture. Ask a friend, "What does my
   picture say?" Then tell them what it says to you. Every answer is a good answer.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: art is a voice. You said something today, and no one
   needed to hear a word.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer a single feeling to draw and let the learner trace or color a
  simple face.
- **Non-visual path:** Show a feeling with sound (a happy hum, a slow sad sigh) or
  with touch (soft cloth for calm, rough bark for stormy), then build a textured
  "picture" or sound-story instead of a drawn one.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to draw a picture with two feelings in it and tell
  where each one lives.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Does the learner make a picture that they connect to a
  feeling, in words or by pointing?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The feeling picture, kept in the portfolio.

## Home connection

Make a picture for someone at home that says how you feel about them, and ask them to
guess what it says before you tell them.
