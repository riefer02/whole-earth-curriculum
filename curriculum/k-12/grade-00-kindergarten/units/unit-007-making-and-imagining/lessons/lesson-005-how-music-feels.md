# Lesson 5 — How Music Feels

## Summary

Learners discover that music changes how they feel: fast and loud can feel exciting,
slow and soft can feel calm. They make and listen to different kinds of sound and name
the feeling each one brings, with words, a face, or a movement.

## Objectives

- Sing, clap, or play a simple rhythm and say how it makes them feel.
  _(D07.S2.K.01)_

## Connection

Think of a time you heard a fast, loud drum and wanted to jump, and a time a slow,
soft song made you feel sleepy or safe. Music talks to your body and your heart.
People everywhere use fast and slow, loud and soft sounds to share feeling.

## Materials

- A way to make music (a drum, a voice, a clap, or a song)
- Feeling face cards (happy, sad, calm, excited)

## Preparation

- Have the feeling face cards ready to point to.
- Plan a few contrasting sounds: one fast and loud, one slow and soft.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. As facilitator, keep the contrasts simple and obvious at first
(fast vs slow, then loud vs soft). Remember there is no single "right" feeling for any
music — ask "what does it feel like *to you*?" and honor every answer, including a
pointed face or a movement.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Sit quietly. Make a soft sound with your voice, then a loud
   one. How does each one feel?
2. **Fast and slow (10 min).** Clap or tap very fast, then very slow. Point to the
   feeling face that matches each one. Which made you want to move?
3. **Loud and soft (10 min).** Sing or drum loud, then soft. Notice how your body
   changes — does loud make you sit up? Does soft make you lean in?
4. **Feel it together (15 min).** Take turns making a sound for the group. Everyone
   else points to a feeling face or moves their body to show how it feels.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: music is a feeling you can hear. Fast, slow, loud, and
   soft all carry a feeling, and yours is yours.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer one contrast at a time (only fast vs slow) and accept a pointed
  face as the answer.
- **Deaf/HH path:** Fast/slow and loud/soft can be felt, not only heard — a drum held
  on the lap, a hand on a table being tapped, or watching the group's big visible
  movements all carry the music to the body.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to make one sound that is fast *and* soft, then name
  the feeling that mix gives them.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name or show a feeling (word, face, or
  movement) for at least one kind of sound?
- **Self:** The learner points to a feeling card for their own sound and says why.

## Home connection

At home, make a fast sound and a slow sound and ask someone how each one feels. Do
they feel it the same way you do?
