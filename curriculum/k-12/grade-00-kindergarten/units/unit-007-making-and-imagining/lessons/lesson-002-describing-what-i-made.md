# Lesson 2 — Describing What I Made

## Summary

Learners revisit their own artwork and practice describing it. They name what they
see — a color, a shape, a line — and tell a little of the story behind it. Pointing
counts as describing; art does not require words.

## Objectives

- Create art with different materials and describe what they made.
  _(D07.S1.K.01)_

## Connection

When you make something and show it to someone, they might ask, "What is it?" or
"Tell me about it." Describing your work helps them see it the way you do. Even
pointing to a part and saying "red" or "round" is a way of sharing your making.

## Materials

- Each learner's own artwork (from Lesson 1 or made fresh)
- Paper and crayons
- A turn marker (a stone, shell, or soft ball to show whose turn it is)

## Preparation

- Gather or finish each learner's artwork.
- Arrange a circle with a turn marker for sharing time.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. As facilitator, model describing with the same gentle,
non-judging words you want to hear: "I see a blue circle and a long line." Never ask
"what is it *supposed* to be?" — instead ask "what do you see?" or "tell me about
  this part." Honor pointing and gestures as full answers. Learners who cannot see may
  describe their piece by touch and feel — rough or smooth, big or small, round or
  straight — and that is full describing too.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Sit with your artwork in front of you. Look at it slowly. What
   do you notice first?
2. **Look closely (10 min).** Find one color, one shape, and one line in your piece.
   Say each one, or point to it: "red," "circle," "wiggly line."
3. **Tell its story (10 min).** Think: what were you doing when you made it? What
   does this part make you think of? You can say a whole story or just one word.
4. **Share with a partner (15 min).** Take turns. Hold the turn marker while you
   describe your work; listen while your partner describes theirs.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: every piece has a story, and describing it — with
   words or by pointing — helps others see it your way.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer a single prompt ("find one color") and accept pointing as the
  answer.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to describe a friend's artwork and notice something
  the maker might not have said.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner point to or name one color, shape, or
  line in their own work?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The artwork paired with a one-sentence (or drawn)
  description.

## Home connection

Show someone at home one thing you made and tell them about one color and one shape
in it. Ask them what they notice, too.
