# Lesson 9 — Building Something

## Summary

Learners build something that does a job: a tower that stands, a bridge that spans, a
boat that floats, a little home that holds. They plan a little, build with what is at
hand, and explain what their creation does. Building is thinking made visible.

## Objectives

- Build or make something from available materials and explain what it does.
  _(D07.S4.K.01)_

## Connection

Think of the homes, bridges, boats, and baskets people make everywhere in the world.
Every one is built from whatever is around — wood, stone, clay, reeds, cloth — to do a
job. When you build, you are doing what people have always done: turning an idea and
some materials into something useful.

## Materials

- Blocks, boxes, or cups
- Tape, string, or cloth for joining

## Preparation

- Gather building materials into piles for small groups.
- Set out a few simple joiners (tape, string) so structures can hold together.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. As facilitator, ask "what does it do?" rather than "what is
it?" — purpose is the heart of this strand. If a build falls over, treat it as data,
not failure: "what could you change so it stands?" Honor every structure; a wobbly
tower is still a thinker's tower.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Look at the materials. What could you build with them? Think
   of one thing with a job: a tower, a bridge, a boat, a home.
2. **Plan a little (5 min).** Before you build, say or think: what will it do? What
   will hold it up?
3. **Build (15 min).** Stack, join, and arrange. If it falls, that is a clue — try
   a wider base or a tighter join. Build, look, change, build again.
4. **Test it (10 min).** Try its job: does the tower stand? Does the bridge span the
   gap? Does the boat hold a leaf?
5. **Explain it (5 min).** Tell a friend or grown-up what you built and what it does.
   You can say it or point to the part that does the job.
6. **Close (5 min).** Remember: builders everywhere turn materials into things that
   help. Your hands just did that too.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer a single goal ("make a tower of five blocks") and model one
  steady base.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to improve their build after testing and explain the
  one change they made and why.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Does the learner build something and say or show what
  it does?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** A photo or drawing of the build, kept in the portfolio.

## Home connection

At home, build something small from what you find — boxes, cups, sticks — and show a
grown-up what its job is.
