# Lesson 8 — Moving to Music

## Summary

Learners let music move their bodies. When the music is fast they move fast; when it
slows, they slow; when it stops, they freeze. They discover that dance is a kind of
listening, and that every person's dance is their own — there is no wrong way to move.

## Objectives

- Move or pretend to be a person, animal, or character in response to a story or
  music. _(D07.S3.K.01)_

## Connection

Have you ever noticed your feet tapping or your shoulders bouncing when a song comes
on? Your body wants to move to music. In every corner of the world, people dance —
fast and slow, together and alone — to celebrate, to play, and to feel.

## Materials

- A way to make or play music (drum, clap, voice, or recording)
- Open floor space

## Preparation

- Clear a safe open space to dance.
- Prepare music with clear fast, slow, and stop parts.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. As facilitator, make the changes in the music very clear —
fast, slow, stop — and dance along. Freeze games are great for safety and delight.
Never correct a learner's dance; every movement is a real response to the music.
Dance is listening with the whole body, and there is no wrong answer.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Stand in open space and listen. Wiggle your fingers to the
   music. How does it make your body want to move?
2. **Fast and slow (10 min).** When the music is fast, move fast. When it slows, move
   slow. Let your whole body follow.
3. **Stop and freeze (10 min).** When the music stops, freeze like a statue. When it
   starts, move again! Try loud and soft, too — big jumps, tiny steps.
4. **Dance your own way (15 min).** Move however the music tells you — no one else's
   dance looks like yours. Try moving one part at a time: only your arms, only your
   head, then everything.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: your dance is yours. Music moves everyone a little
   differently, and that is exactly right.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Keep to one change (fast vs stop) and let the learner copy your
  movement.
- **Seated/adapted:** Dance entirely from a chair — arms, head, shoulders, and a
  swaying torso all move to the music; "freeze" is holding still wherever you are, and
  one moving hand is a full dance.
- **Deaf/HH path:** Make every change visible and feelable — the leader claps or
  stamps the beat where everyone can see, raises an open hand for "freeze" and drops
  it to move again. Learners who do not hear can follow the hand signal and feel the
  beat as vibration through the floor or a drum, so no sound is needed to dance.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to lead the group's music (clapping fast and slow)
  while others dance to their beat.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Does the learner change their movement when the music
  changes speed or stops?
- **Performance:** The learner's dance, observed live.

## Home connection

At home, sing or play a song and dance with someone. Try freezing when the music
stops. Ask each other what the music made you feel like doing.
