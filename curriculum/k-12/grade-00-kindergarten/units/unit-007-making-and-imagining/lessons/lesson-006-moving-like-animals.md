# Lesson 6 — Moving Like Animals

## Summary

Learners turn their bodies into animals. They crawl like a cat, leap like a frog,
slither like a snake, and fly like a bird, watching and copying each other and
inventing new ways to move. The moving body is the first and most honest instrument
of pretending.

## Objectives

- Move or pretend to be a person, animal, or character in response to a story or
  music. _(D07.S3.K.01)_

## Connection

Have you ever watched a bird hop, a cat stretch, or an ant hurry? Animals move in
amazing ways, and your body can try them all. In stories and dances everywhere in the
world, people move like animals to play, to celebrate, and to understand them.

## Materials

- Animal picture cards (bird, fish, snake, frog, cat, elephant)
- Open floor space

## Preparation

- Clear a safe open space to move.
- Choose a few animals everyone can try; pick animals learners might know.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. As facilitator, move along with the learners — your modeling is
  the best instruction. Encourage big, free movement, but keep the space safe. Never
rank whose animal is "best"; the goal is expression, and every animal movement is
right. Invite learners who use a chair, move differently, or prefer to stay seated to
adapt each animal from where they are — an animal is in the hands, arms, and face as
much as the legs.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Stand in open space. Shake out your arms and legs. Your body
   is ready to pretend.
2. **Watch and copy (10 min).** Look at each animal card. How does it move? Try it:
   crawl low like a cat, hop like a frog, slither on your belly like a snake.
3. **Move like many animals (15 min).** Call an animal and move as it across the
   space. Then freeze. Now another! Fly like a bird, swim like a fish, stomp like an
   elephant.
4. **Invent an animal move (10 min).** Make up your own animal movement — a mix of two
   animals, or an animal no one has seen. Teach it to a friend.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: your body can become anything. Animals all over the
   world have their own ways of moving, and now you have tried them.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Do one animal at a time, moving a single body part first (arms as
  wings) before the whole body.
- **Seated/adapted:** Every animal works from a chair or the floor — a bird flaps
  only its arms, a snake is a wiggling hand, an elephant sways a slow arm; "crawling"
  becomes reaching and pulling. Watching and copying counts as full movement.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to move like a baby animal and then the grown
  animal, and describe the difference.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Does the learner move their body in a way that shows
  an animal?
- **Performance:** The learner's animal movement, observed live.

## Home connection

At home or outside, watch an animal for a minute and try to move like it. Show a
grown-up your animal walk and let them guess which animal it is.
