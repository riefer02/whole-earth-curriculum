# Lesson 1 — Listening to a Story

## Summary

Learners learn three "listening jobs" — ears open, eyes watching, body still — and
practice them while a story is told. They close by remembering and sharing one thing
they heard.

## Objectives

- Listen to a story and retell one important part in their own words.
  _(D04.S1.K.01)_

## Connection

Stories are everywhere — at bedtime, around a fire, in a song, or read from a book.
When you listen to a story, you can step into another world and meet new people and
places. Listening carefully is how a story travels from one person to another, and it
is how the story gets to live inside you too.

## Materials

- A short picture story (or a story told from memory)
- Storytelling props — a scarf, stick, shell, or soft toy (optional)

## Preparation

- Choose a short story with one clear, important event.
- Arrange a circle where everyone can see the storyteller.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Tell the story slowly, with pauses and expression, so there is
time to listen. Tell the story in the language the learners understand best — the home
language is always welcome here. "Listening with the body" is a skill to name kindly,
never to scold; some learners listen best while moving, and that is okay. "Watching
with the eyes" also looks different across cultures — in many places a listener shows
attention and respect by looking down or away, especially toward an elder — so honor
every learner's own way of listening. For a learner who is deaf or hard of hearing,
"listening" can mean watching a story told in sign, lip-reading, or following pictures —
offer the story visually (signed, pictured, or acted out) so that learner "listens" with
eyes and hands instead of ears. Retelling may be spoken, signed, pointed, drawn, or acted
out; all of these are real retelling and all are welcome.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Sit comfortably. Close your eyes for a moment and listen: what
   do you hear right now, without looking?
2. **Meet the three listening jobs (10 min).** A good listener uses **ears** (open and
   ready), **eyes** (watching the storyteller), and a **body** (still and calm). If you
   listen with your eyes and hands instead — watching a story told in sign or in
   pictures — those are your listening tools too. Try all three (or the tools that work
   for you). Why does each one help you take in and remember the story?
3. **Hear the story (15 min).** Listen to the whole story with your ears, eyes, and
   body. You can hold a soft prop if it helps your body stay calm. When something in
   the story makes you feel something — happy, worried, curious — notice it.
4. **Remember one thing (10 min).** Think: what is one important thing that happened?
   Tell it to a partner in your own words.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: listening is a gift you give. Your ears, eyes, and body
   are the tools that carry a story to you.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Give one listening job at a time; let a learner echo a repeated phrase
  or sound from the story instead of retelling an event.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to tell back two things and say which one felt most
  important, and why.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Does the learner show ears, eyes, and body engaged
  during the story?
- **Formative (oral):** Can the learner share one thing that happened in the story in
  their own words — spoken, signed, pointed, drawn, or acted out?

## Home connection

At home, ask a grown-up to tell you a story you have never heard. Practice the three
listening jobs, then tell back one thing you remember.
