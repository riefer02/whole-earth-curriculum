# Lesson 10 — Drawing and Telling My Idea

## Summary

Learners draw an idea and then tell someone what it means — a picture plus spoken
words. They learn that a drawing and its telling together make a complete message, the
root of all writing.

## Objectives

- Draw, dictate, or write marks to express an idea and tell what they mean.
  _(D04.S3.K.01)_

## Connection

Sometimes a picture says what words are still learning to say. When you draw an idea
and then tell someone what it means, you are sharing your thinking. Your drawing plus
your words make one complete message — and that is how writing begins for everyone.

## Materials

- Paper and drawing tools
- A scribe (a facilitator or older peer) to write dictated words (optional)

## Preparation

- Set out paper and drawing tools.
- Arrange a quiet way for each learner to tell their idea to someone who can listen.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. "Dictate" means the child speaks and an adult or older peer
writes the words down. The drawing is the child's own; the scribe writes exactly what
the child says, in the child's language, without correcting or "improving" it. This is
a cornerstone of emergent writing: children compose long before they can spell, and
their spoken words are real authorship.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of an idea you want to share — something you love, a place,
   a wish, a memory. Hold it in your mind.
2. **Draw your idea (15 min).** Draw your idea on your paper. Make it as detailed or
   as simple as you like. Your drawing does not have to look like anyone else's.
3. **Tell what it means (15 min).** Show your drawing to a partner or a grown-up. Tell
   them what it means, in your own words, in whatever language feels best. If a grown-up
   can, let them write your words beside your drawing.
4. **Read it back (5 min).** Look at your drawing and your written words together. You
   made a message! Point to the parts as someone reads your words back to you.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: a drawing plus your words is a complete idea. You are
   already an author.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Let a learner tell their idea first and draw second, or draw and tell
  about a single object they can hold.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to add a second sentence to their dictation, or to
  draw a sequence of two pictures telling what happened next.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner produce a drawing and tell what it
  means in their own words?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The drawing with its dictated caption, kept as evidence of
  early composition.

## Home connection

At home, draw a picture of something that matters to you and tell a grown-up what it
means. Ask them to write your words down beside it, then read it back together.
