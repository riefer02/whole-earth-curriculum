# Lesson 5 — Meet the Letters of My Language

## Summary

Learners meet the marks of their own home writing system — letters or characters,
in whatever script their family uses — by looking, tracing, and building their shapes.
They begin to name a few.

## Objectives

- Recognize and name the letters or characters of their home writing system and match
  some to their sounds. _(D04.S2.K.01)_

## Connection

The words you hear can also be written down as marks. Every writing system has its own
set of marks — and yours is the one your family uses at home. These marks are like a
code that carries your words across time and space, so someone far away can read what
you thought.

## Materials

- Letter or character cards in the learners' home writing system
- A tray of loose parts — sticks, pebbles, seeds, or clay (optional)

## Preparation

- Prepare cards showing the marks of each learner's home writing system. If learners
  use more than one script, prepare more than one set.
- Gather loose parts for building shapes.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. This lesson is **script-agnostic**: a learner's home writing
system may use the Latin alphabet, Arabic script, Devanagari, Chinese characters,
Cyrillic, Ethiopic, Hangul, or another system entirely. Never assume the English
alphabet. Ask families which marks their child uses at home, and prepare those. Teach
the lesson in the learners' home language where possible. No writing system is "the
right one" — each is a complete, equal way of writing. For a learner with low or no
vision, offer the marks as raised or three-dimensional shapes (traced in sand, built from
loose parts, or molded in clay) so a mark can be known by touch and by its spoken name,
not only by sight.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Look around: can you see any written marks anywhere? What do
   you think they are for?
2. **Meet the marks (10 min).** Look at the cards of your home writing system. These
   are the marks your family uses to write words. Each mark has a shape and a name.
   Trace one with your finger.
3. **Build a mark (15 min).** Choose one mark. Build its shape with sticks, pebbles,
   seeds, or clay. Or make its shape with your whole body. Say its name out loud.
4. **Name a few (10 min).** Show a partner the mark you built. Can you name it? Can
   your partner find the same mark on a card?
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: your writing system is yours, and it is a treasure.
   The marks you use are just as real and just as good as any other writing system's
   marks.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer just two or three marks to choose from; let a learner match a
  loose-parts shape to an identical card rather than naming it.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to build or name several marks and compare their
  shapes — which are round, which have lines, which have dots?

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner recognize a mark from their home
  writing system when shown it?
- **Formative (oral):** Can the learner name one or more marks of their writing
  system?

## Home connection

At home, ask a grown-up to show you the marks of your writing system. Practice naming
them together, and build one out of anything you can find.
