# Lesson 12 — Greetings in My Language

## Summary

Learners share a greeting from their home language, teach it to others, and try a
greeting from a different language. Greetings are treated as acts of welcome — every
language's greeting is honored equally.

## Objectives

- Share a greeting or word from their home language and try one from another language.
  _(D04.S4.K.01)_

## Connection

A greeting is how you say "I see you." Every language has its own way, and the one you
learned first is yours to keep and share. Saying hello in your home language is a small
act of pride — and learning someone else's hello is a small act of friendship.

## Materials

- Greeting cards from the learners' home languages (optional)
- A welcome mat or circle marker (optional)

## Preparation

- Learn each learner's home-language greeting ahead of time (ask families if unsure).
- Prepare greeting cards or a simple welcome circle.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Home language is honored **first** — each child greets in their
own language before any other is attempted. There is no "correct" accent; every
pronunciation is welcomed with warmth. If a learner is shy or does not want to share,
never force it — they may nod, smile, or gesture instead. A greeting also lives in the
body (a bow, a wave, a hand on the heart), and those count fully.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** How do you say hello to someone you love? Is it a word, a wave,
   a hug, a bow?
2. **Greet in your home language (10 min).** One at a time, greet the group in your
   home language. Say it proudly — it is yours. The group greets you back the same way.
3. **Teach your greeting (10 min).** Teach a partner your greeting. Say it slowly, and
   help them try it. Now learn theirs. Every greeting is a welcome.
4. **Try one from another language (15 min).** Pick one greeting you just learned and
   practice it. Use your voice and a warm face. How does it feel to greet in a language
   that is not your own?
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: every language says "welcome" in its own beautiful way.
   Your greeting is a treasure, and so is everyone else's.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Let a learner greet with a gesture or a smile only; offer one new
  greeting to echo rather than several.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to collect greetings from the whole group into a class
  "greeting book," or to greet using a bow or gesture from their culture and explain
  it.

## Assessment

- **Formative (performance):** Can the learner share a greeting from their home
  language and attempt one from another language?
- **Formative (observation):** Does the learner show respect for others' greetings by
  listening and echoing?

## Home connection

At home, teach your greeting to your family and ask them for a greeting they know in
another language. Practice it together.
