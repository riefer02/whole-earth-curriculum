# Lesson 2 — Retelling What I Heard

## Summary

Learners retell a familiar story by choosing one important part and putting it into
their own words. They discover that their own words are a good way to tell a story
back.

## Objectives

- Listen to a story and retell one important part in their own words.
  _(D04.S1.K.01)_

## Connection

When you hear something wonderful, you want to tell someone about it. Retelling is
how a story lives on — one person listens, then tells it again in their own way. There
is no single "right" way to tell a story back; your own words are a good way, because
they are yours.

## Materials

- A familiar short story (reuse the story from Lesson 1)
- Story sequence cards — a few pictures of the main events (optional)

## Preparation

- Re-read or re-tell the familiar story.
- Lay out or prepare simple pictures of its main events.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Celebrate every retelling — a one-sentence retelling is a real
retelling. Learners may retell in their home language; that counts fully. A retelling may
also be signed, drawn, pointed to on sequence cards, or acted out — all are real
retelling, so a learner who does not speak aloud is never asked to. The goal is
"one important part in my own words," not a perfect summary, and never a memorized
script.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of a story you love. If you told it to a friend, how
   would you start?
2. **Hear it again (10 min).** Listen to the familiar story one more time. As you
   listen, hold up a finger for each important part you notice. How many parts did you
   count?
3. **Find the important part (10 min).** Choose ONE part that feels important to you.
   Maybe it is the biggest surprise, the funniest moment, or the part that changed
   everything. There is no wrong choice — important to you is what matters.
4. **Retell in your own words (15 min).** Tell that one part to a partner, in your own
   words, in whatever language feels best. You do not have to say it exactly how the
   storyteller said it.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: your own words can carry a story. When you retell, the
   story becomes a little bit yours too.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer two pictures of story parts and let the learner point to the one
  they will retell; model one sentence to begin.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to retell two parts and put them in order using the
  sequence cards.

## Assessment

- **Formative (performance):** Can the learner share one important part of the story in
  their own words — spoken, signed, drawn, pointed, or acted out — without repeating it
  word-for-word?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** A drawn picture of the part they chose, with a dictated
  sentence.

## Home connection

At home, listen to a story or a piece of news from a grown-up, then retell one
important part in your own words and ask if you got it right.
