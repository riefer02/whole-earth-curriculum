# Lesson 11 — Writing My Own Story

## Summary

Learners write their own story in a home-made book, using pictures, marks, and
dictated words to tell a beginning, a middle, and an end — then read it aloud as its
author.

## Objectives

- Draw, dictate, or write marks to express an idea and tell what they mean.
  _(D04.S3.K.01)_
- Listen to a story or speaker and retell one important part in their own words.
  _(D04.S1.K.01)_

## Connection

Everyone has stories inside them — things that happened, things they dreamed, things
they wish for. Putting a story onto a page, with pictures and marks, lets it live
outside you where others can find it. You are an author the moment you tell your own
story.

## Materials

- A folded paper booklet for each learner
- Drawing and writing tools (crayons, pencils, markers)

## Preparation

- Fold and bind a small booklet for each learner (a few pages stapled or tied).
- Set out drawing tools.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. This is the S3 capstone: the child authors a complete story. It
may be told in pictures, in marks, in dictated words, or any mix — all are real
writing at this age. Write down exactly what the child says, in their language, without
"fixing" it. Some children will make one page; others many — both are celebrated. This
lesson also closes the S1 loop: retelling their own story, in their own words, is the
highest form of "one important part in my own words."

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** What story lives inside you today? It can be real or make-
   believe. Choose one.
2. **Plan the three parts (5 min).** A story has a beginning, a middle, and an end.
   Say yours out loud to a partner, or just in your head: what happens first? next?
   last?
3. **Make your book (20 min).** On your pages, draw and write your story — one part
   per page. Use pictures and any marks you like. If a grown-up can, tell them your
   words and let them write them beside your pictures.
4. **Read it aloud (10 min).** Read your book to a partner or the group, pointing to
   each page as you go. You are the author, so you know the story best.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: you have a story no one else has, and now it lives on
   your pages. An author is anyone who tells their own story.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a three-page booklet with one picture starter per page; let a
  learner tell the story aloud while an adult scribes every word.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to add a cover with a title, or to make a sequel page
  telling what happens after the end.

## Assessment

- **Artifact (portfolio):** The learner's home-made book, kept as a first authored
  story.
- **Formative (performance):** Can the learner "read" their story aloud, telling what
  each page means in their own words?

## Home connection

At home, make a family storybook together: each person draws one page of a story you
all invent, then read the whole book aloud.
