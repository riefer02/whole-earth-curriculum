# Lesson 8 — My Name in Marks

## Summary

Learners study their own name written in their home writing system, build its marks
with their hands, and begin to name the letters or characters that make it up. Their
name becomes their first "word" they know by sight.

## Objectives

- Recognize and name the letters or characters of their home writing system and match
  some to their sounds. _(D04.S2.K.01)_
- Draw, dictate, or write marks to express an idea and tell what they mean.
  _(D04.S3.K.01)_

## Connection

Your name is one of the first words you can call your own. It is made of the marks of
your writing system, arranged in an order that means only you. Your name belongs to
you wherever it is written, and writing it is a way of saying "this is me."

## Materials

- A name card for each learner, written in their home writing system
- Loose parts for building names — sticks, stones, seeds, or clay (optional)

## Preparation

- Write each learner's name large and clear, in the script their family uses at home.
- Gather loose parts.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Write each name in the learner's **home writing system** — this
may be Latin, Arabic, Devanagari, Chinese characters, Cyrillic, or another script. If
a child's name uses more than one script, write it in each. Never "correct" a name's
spelling into a different script or language. A name is a gift and a belonging; honor
it exactly as the family gives it.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** What is your name? Who gave it to you, or what does it mean?
   Your name is yours.
2. **Meet your name card (10 min).** Look at your name written in your writing
   system. Point to each mark. How many marks are in your name? Which mark do you like
   best, and why?
3. **Build your name (15 min).** Build your name with sticks, stones, seeds, or clay.
   Or trace it with your finger in sand, dust, or the air. Say each mark's name as you
   go, if you can.
4. **Show and tell (10 min).** Show a partner your name in marks. Tell them one mark's
   name or sound. Can they find the first mark of your name?
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: your name is made of marks, and those marks spell
   **you**. Every name, in every writing system, is worth writing beautifully.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Let a learner trace over their name card rather than build from scratch;
  praise naming even one mark.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to write or build their name a second time without
  looking, or to name every mark in their name and match each to its sound.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner point to and name one or more marks of
  their own name?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** A photograph or drawing of the learner's built or traced
  name, kept as their first piece of "writing."

## Home connection

At home, ask a grown-up to write your name in your home writing system. Practice
writing or building it together, and learn the name of each mark.
