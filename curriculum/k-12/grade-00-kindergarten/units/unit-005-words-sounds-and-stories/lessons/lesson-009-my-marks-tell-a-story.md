# Lesson 9 — My Marks Tell a Story

## Summary

Learners discover that marks can carry meaning — the same idea that powers every
writing system on Earth — and invent their own marks to stand for ideas, objects, and
feelings.

## Objectives

- Draw, dictate, or write marks to express an idea and tell what they mean.
  _(D04.S3.K.01)_

## Connection

Long before you could write letters or characters, you could make marks — lines, dots,
swirls. A mark can stand for an idea, a feeling, or a whole story. The marks you make
are already writing, because they carry meaning from you to someone else.

## Materials

- Paper and drawing tools (crayons, pencils, charcoal, or chalk)
- Examples of marks that carry meaning — pictograms and symbols (optional)

## Preparation

- Gather paper and drawing tools.
- Collect or draw a few examples of marks that carry meaning from around the world.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. The deep idea is egalitarian and global: **writing began as
marks that carry meaning**, and every writing system — from pictograms to alphabets to
character scripts — is a beautiful, equal achievement. No mark is "scribble" that
should be corrected; every mark a child makes with intent is writing. Ask learners what
their marks mean rather than what they "should" mean.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Can a picture say a word? Think of a sun, a heart, an arrow.
   What do they mean?
2. **See marks that mean something (10 min).** Look at marks from around the world:
   a picture of water, a symbol for home, a mark from an old writing system. Every one
   of them carries meaning. Your marks can too.
3. **Make your own marks (15 min).** Draw three marks. Make one stand for a thing, one
   for an idea, and one for a feeling. Let the marks look however you like — lines,
   dots, swirls, shapes.
4. **Tell what they mean (10 min).** Show a partner your marks. Can they guess what
   each one means? Then tell them. The meaning you give your mark is the meaning it
   has.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: marks carry meaning. When you draw a mark and tell
   what it means, you are writing — the same way people everywhere have always
   written.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer one mark to copy and a simple meaning (a circle for the sun); let
  the learner trace and explain it.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to build a small "mark dictionary" — several marks
  with their meanings drawn beside them — and teach it to a friend.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner make a mark and explain its meaning?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The learner's three marks, kept with a dictated caption of
  what each means.

## Home connection

At home, make up a family mark for something you all love — a park, a food, a pet —
and draw it together. Teach your mark to someone new.
