# Lesson 7 — Letters All Around Me

## Summary

Learners go on a "mark hunt" to find writing in their everyday world — on signs,
labels, and packages — and practice recognizing and naming the marks of their home
writing system where they actually appear.

## Objectives

- Recognize and name the letters or characters of their home writing system and match
  some to their sounds. _(D04.S2.K.01)_

## Connection

Writing is not only in books — it is on signs, jars, shirts, buses, and screens. The
marks of your writing system are waiting to be found everywhere. Once you start
looking, you see them all around you, and they begin to mean something.

## Materials

- Environmental print samples — a sign, a label, a book cover
- A "mark hunt" recording sheet (optional)

## Preparation

- Gather a few real examples of writing from the learners' environment (in their home
  writing system).
- Prepare a simple recording sheet with boxes to tick or draw in.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Use writing from the learners' **real environment and home
writing system** — if the community writes in Arabic, Devanagari, or Chinese
characters, hunt for those marks, not the English alphabet. If learners are new to the
community's script, honor both: their home marks and the ones around them are both
welcome. Pointing and naming are the goals; reading full words is not expected. For a
learner with low or no vision, adapt the hunt to touch — raised lettering on jars and
boxes, or marks formed in clay or soil — so they can "find" and name marks by feel.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Where do you see writing every day? Think of one place.
2. **Look at real writing (10 min).** Look at the samples: a sign, a label, a book
   cover. Can you find any marks you know? Point to them and say their name or sound.
3. **Go on a mark hunt (15 min).** Walk around your room, home, or school. Find marks
   from your writing system on jars, clothes, boxes, and signs. Point, name, and tick
   each one on your sheet.
4. **Share your finds (10 min).** Tell a partner one mark you found and where you
   found it. Did you find any mark more than once?
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: writing is all around you, and you are learning to
   read your world one mark at a time.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Give the learner one specific mark to hunt for, and celebrate each time
  they point to it.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to count how many times they found one mark, or to
  find the first mark of their own name somewhere in the environment.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner locate and point to marks of their home
  writing system in real environmental print?
- **Formative (oral):** Can the learner name a mark they found?

## Home connection

At home, go on a mark hunt with a grown-up. Find and name the marks of your writing
system on food, clothes, and signs, and count how many you find.
