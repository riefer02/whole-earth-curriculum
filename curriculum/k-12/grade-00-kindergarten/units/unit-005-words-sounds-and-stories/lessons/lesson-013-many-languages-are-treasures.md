# Lesson 13 — Many Languages Are Treasures

## Summary

Learners explore the idea that people speak thousands of languages, that no language
is better than another, and that every home language is a treasure. They build a
"treasure chest" of words from their own languages and new ones they learn.

## Objectives

- Share a greeting or word from their home language and try one from another language.
  _(D04.S4.K.01)_

## Connection

People all over the world greet, sing, and tell stories in thousands of different
languages. Each language is a treasure chest of words, songs, and ways of seeing the
world. When we share languages, everyone gets richer — and no one has to give up
theirs.

## Materials

- Greeting cards from many languages (optional)
- A "treasure chest" box or basket

## Preparation

- Prepare a few greeting cards from languages around the world.
- Set out the treasure chest and slips of paper for home-language words.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. The central value is **egalitarian**: no language is "better"
or "more correct" than another, and multilingualism is wealth, not a problem. Treat
every home language as a treasure the learner gives, never as a barrier. This is a
value we teach by modeling — never rank languages, never mock an accent, and let the
learners' own languages fill the room. For the factual claim below, cite UNESCO.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** How many languages do you think people speak in the whole
   world? (Guess! There are thousands.)
2. **Hear the treasure idea (10 min).** Each language is like a treasure chest — full
   of words, songs, and stories found nowhere else. No language is better than another;
   they are all treasures. Sadly, many people still cannot learn in the language they
   speak best at home, so we must work to honor every language.
3. **Fill the treasure chest (15 min).** Put one word from your home language into the
   chest — say it aloud, then add a slip with the word (or a drawing of it). Then learn
   one word from a friend's language and put that in too.
4. **Share the treasures (10 min).** Open the chest together and read or say the words
   inside. Notice how many different languages live in your room.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: every language is a treasure, and every person who
   speaks one is a keeper of that treasure. Sharing them makes us all richer.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Let a learner offer just a smile or a single sound from their home
  language; one word is a full contribution.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to compare the same word — "hello" or "thank you" —
  across the languages in the room and notice what is alike and different.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner offer a word from their home language
  and show curiosity or respect toward another's?
- **Formative (oral):** Can the learner try one word from a language that is not their
  own?

## Resources

- UNESCO, International Mother Language Day (UNESCO reports that around 40 per cent of
  the world's people still cannot learn in a language they speak or understand; the
  value of every learner's language): <https://www.unesco.org/en/days/mother-language>

## Home connection

At home, ask a grown-up what languages your family or your neighbors speak. Learn one
new word together and add it to your family's word treasure.
