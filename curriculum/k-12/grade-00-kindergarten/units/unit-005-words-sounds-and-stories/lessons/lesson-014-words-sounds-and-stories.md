# Lesson 14 — Words, Sounds & Stories

## Summary

The unit's capstone: learners gather their work — a name, a mark, a story, a greeting —
and share who they are through words, sounds, and stories in a celebration circle. All
four strands come together.

## Objectives

- Listen to a story or speaker and retell one important part in their own words.
  _(D04.S1.K.01)_
- Recognize and name the letters or characters of their home writing system and match
  some to their sounds. _(D04.S2.K.01)_
- Draw, dictate, or write marks to express an idea and tell what they mean.
  _(D04.S3.K.01)_
- Share a greeting or word from their home language and try one from another language.
  _(D04.S4.K.01)_

## Connection

All unit long you have listened, told, made marks, and shared words. These are the
tools every person everywhere uses to be understood and to understand others. Words,
sounds, and stories are how you show the world who you are — and how the world shows
itself to you.

## Materials

- A celebration circle or mat
- Each learner's storybook, name card, and marks from earlier lessons

## Preparation

- Gather the unit artifacts: storybooks (Lesson 11), name cards (Lesson 8), marks
  (Lesson 9), and greetings (Lesson 12).
- Arrange a circle where everyone can be seen and heard.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. This is a celebration, not a test. Each learner shares in their
own way — a story, a mark, a name, a greeting, or all of them — and the group's job is
to listen with the warmth practiced all unit. Honor every language and every writing
system. Capture the sharing (in memory or on a recording) as a portfolio keepsake, but
keep the tone joyful and unpressured.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Look at everything you have made this unit: your name, your
   marks, your storybook, your greeting. You made all of these. You are a listener, a
   teller, a writer, and a greeter.
2. **Remember the four tools (5 min).** The unit's four tools are **listening**,
   **marks**, **telling**, and **greeting**. Which one did you love most?
3. **Share in the circle (25 min).** One at a time, share a piece of who you are: tell
   a part of your story, show your name in marks, share a greeting or a word from your
   home language. The group listens the way we practiced — ears, eyes, and a warm,
   still body — and celebrates each person.
4. **Echo and celebrate (5 min).** For each person, echo their greeting or a favorite
   word back to them. Notice: your room is full of languages, marks, and stories, and
   every one is a treasure.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: you have a voice, a name, a mark, and a story that are
   yours alone. Words, sounds, and stories are how you share them with the world.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Let a learner share just one artifact — a mark or a greeting — with a
  partner instead of the whole group.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to retell a partner's story back to them, showing they
  listened, and to add one new word they learned from a friend to their collection.

## Assessment

- **Summative (portfolio):** The learner's gathered unit artifacts — storybook, name
  card, marks, and greeting — together show growth across all four strands.
- **Performance (observation):** Can the learner share at least one piece of their
  work and use one of the unit's four tools (listen, mark, tell, greet) before the
  group?

## Home connection

At home, hold a family celebration: share your storybook, your name in marks, and your
greeting, and ask each family member to share a word or story that is theirs.
