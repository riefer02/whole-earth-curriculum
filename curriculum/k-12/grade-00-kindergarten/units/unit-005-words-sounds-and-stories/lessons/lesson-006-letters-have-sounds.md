# Lesson 6 — Letters Have Sounds

## Summary

Learners learn that marks and sounds are partners: each letter or character of their
home writing system carries a sound, and they practice matching a few marks to the
sounds they make in their own language.

## Objectives

- Recognize and name the letters or characters of their home writing system and match
  some to their sounds. _(D04.S2.K.01)_

## Connection

Each mark in your writing system is a friend of a sound. When you know which mark goes
with which sound, you can begin to unlock words — to read what others wrote and write
what you want to say. Marks and sounds are partners that work together.

## Materials

- Letter or character cards (reuse from Lesson 5)
- A bag of small objects or picture cards whose names begin with a mark's sound

## Preparation

- Choose a few marks and their sounds from the learners' home writing system.
- Gather small objects or picture cards whose names begin with those sounds.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Sounds are matched to the **learner's home writing system and
home language** — this may be an alphabet (such as Latin or Cyrillic), a script that
pairs marks with sounds (such as Arabic or Devanagari), or a character-based system
where a character maps to a syllable or a whole word. Do not assume "one letter = one
sound" in the English way; honor how the learners' own system works. No accent is
"correct"; every learner's pronunciation is welcomed. For a learner who is deaf or hard
of hearing, shift the goal from hearing to seeing and feeling: match a mark by its shape,
its name, the way the mouth forms its sound, or a gesture or sign that goes with it —
recognition, not auditory discrimination, is the heart of this lesson. A learner who does
not speak aloud can point to the mark, move to it, or match it with a picture instead of
saying the sound.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Say your name slowly. Listen: what is the very first sound?
2. **Meet the partners (10 min).** Look at your mark cards. Each mark has a sound that
   lives inside it. Say the mark's sound together, and feel where the sound lives in
   your mouth.
3. **Hunt for sounds (15 min).** Pick an object from the bag. Say its name slowly and
   feel the first sound. Which mark starts that sound? Put the object on that mark's
   card.
4. **Match more (10 min).** With a partner, choose another object and find its mark.
   Check together: does the sound match?
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: marks and sounds are partners. Every writing system
   has its own way of pairing them, and yours works perfectly for your language.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Work with one mark and one sound at a time; let the learner echo the
  sound many times before matching.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to find the ending sound of an object's name and
  match it to a mark, or collect more objects for the same sound.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner match a mark to the sound it makes in
  their home writing system?
- **Formative (oral):** Can the learner name the sound of one or more marks — or match
  a mark to its sound by pointing, moving, or using a picture or gesture?

## Home connection

At home, play "first sound" with a grown-up: say the names of things around you, feel
the first sound, and find the mark that starts it.
