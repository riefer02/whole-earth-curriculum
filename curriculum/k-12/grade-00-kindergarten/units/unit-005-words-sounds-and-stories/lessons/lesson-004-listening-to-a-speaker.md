# Lesson 4 — Listening to a Speaker

## Summary

Learners practice listening to a live speaker — a person sharing one short thing — and
retell one important part they heard, then ask a wondering question. Listening to a
person is treated as a way of caring.

## Objectives

- Listen to a story or speaker and retell one important part in their own words.
  _(D04.S1.K.01)_

## Connection

Every day people talk to you — a grown-up giving directions, a friend telling a joke,
a neighbor describing how to fix something. Listening to a person is how you understand
what they know, what they need, and how they feel. Listening well is a way of showing
someone that they matter.

## Materials

- A guest speaker or a short spoken message
- A "talking stick" or passing object (optional)

## Preparation

- Invite a speaker (a family member, a community worker, an older learner) to share one
  short, simple thing.
- Prepare the talking stick or a substitute.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. If no visitor is available, you are the speaker, or use a
recording. Keep the talk short — under three minutes — and about one concrete thing.
Frame asking questions as "wondering together," never as challenging the speaker.
Honor the speaker's language; if they speak a language some learners don't know,
invite translation with patience and curiosity. For a learner who is deaf or hard of
hearing, arrange what they need to receive the message — a sign-language interpretation,
lip-reading with the speaker facing them, a written or pictured key point, or captions on
a recording — so "listening to a person" is open to every learner. Learners may tell back
what they heard by speaking, signing, pointing, drawing, or acting; all are valid.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of a time someone really listened to you. How did it feel?
   How did you know they were listening?
2. **Meet the speaker (5 min).** Learn who is speaking and what they will share. Get
   your ears, eyes, and body ready — the three listening jobs from Lesson 1.
3. **Listen (10 min).** Listen to the whole message without interrupting. If a question
   pops into your head, hold it like a ball in your lap until the speaker is done.
4. **Tell back one thing (15 min).** With a partner, say one important thing you heard,
   in your own words. Then, if you have one, ask a "wondering question" — something you
   are curious about.
5. **Close and thank (10 min).** Thank the speaker. Say one thing you learned. Remember:
   when you listen to a person, you are giving them your full attention, and that is a
   gift.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Let a learner retell by pointing to a picture the speaker brought, or
  by echoing one phrase they remember.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to ask a follow-up question and connect the speaker's
  idea to their own life.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Does the learner hold attention during the speaker's
  talk without interrupting?
- **Formative (oral):** Can the learner name one important thing the speaker said in
  their own words — spoken, signed, pointed, drawn, or acted out?

## Home connection

At home, practice listening to a grown-up's short story without interrupting. Tell
back one thing and ask one wondering question.
