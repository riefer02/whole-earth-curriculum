# Lesson 4 — Living Things Grow

## Summary

Learners explore what it means for a living thing to grow and change — from seed to
plant, baby to grown-up — and discover that growing is one of the ways we recognize
that something is alive.

## Objectives

- Observe and describe how living things grow and change. _(D06.S4.K.02)_

## Connection

You were once a tiny baby, and now you are bigger. A seed becomes a plant, a puppy
becomes a dog, a chick becomes a hen. Living things grow and change — that is how we
know they are alive.

## Materials

- Baby/grown-up picture pairs (seed–plant, puppy–dog, chick–hen)
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Draw or print simple pairs: a seed and a plant, a puppy and a dog, a chick and a
  hen, a baby and a grown-up person.
- Clear space for acting out growing.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). As facilitator, keep it gentle and
concrete. Growth differs everywhere: a learner's local plants and animals grow in
their own way and at their own pace — honor that. If the "baby to grown-up person"
pair feels tender for any learner (for example, a new sibling), let them choose a
plant or animal pair instead. Care for living things is the quiet through-line.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of something that grew: a plant, an animal, or you. What
   was it like when it was small?
2. **Living and growing (10 min).** Look at a seed and a rock. A seed can grow into a
   plant; a rock mostly stays the same. Growing and changing is something living
   things do. Act out growing: curl up small like a seed, then stretch up tall and
   leafy.
3. **Match baby to grown-up (15 min).** Match each card to its grown-up: seed to
   plant, puppy to dog, chick to hen, baby to grown-up person. Which is the baby?
   Which is the grown-up?
4. **Draw it growing (10 min).** Draw one thing small, and then the same thing bigger.
5. **Close (5 min).** Living things grow and change. That is what makes them alive —
   and it happens everywhere, to every living thing.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use two pairs only (seed–plant, chick–hen) and match together with a
  partner.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to draw three stages of one living thing — tiny,
  middle, and grown — in order.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner match at least one baby to its grown-up
  and say "it grew"?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The "small and big" drawing.

## Home connection

At home, ask a grown-up to show you a picture of you or someone in your family when
they were small. Talk about one way they have grown.
