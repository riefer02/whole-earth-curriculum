# Lesson 9 — A Wonder Walk

## Summary

Learners take a slow walk through their own world and practice noticing and asking.
At each stop they wonder aloud — "why" and "how" — and offer their own ideas, ending
with one thing they want to remember.

## Objectives

- Ask "why" and "how" questions about the living world and offer their own ideas.
  _(D06.S1.K.01)_

## Connection

A walk outside is full of things to wonder about — a bird, a plant, a cloud, a puddle,
a bug. You do not need special tools; you just need to notice and ask. Your own
neighborhood, field, or path is as full of wonder as any place on Earth.

## Materials

- A small bag for gentle collecting (optional)
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Plan a slow, safe walk route (indoors or out) with a few good spots to stop and look.
- Remind learners of the one rule: be gentle with every living thing you meet.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It works best outside, but a hallway
or window walk works too. Learners who use wheels or tire easily can hold a "sit
spot" or "window wonder" — stay in one place and let the world come to them. The
ethical heart is care: look with eyes, touch softly, and return living things to where
you found them. Every learner's local place is worth wondering about — there is no
"correct" environment, only the one that is home.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** You are going on a wonder walk. What will you look for?
2. **Walk and wonder (20 min).** Walk slowly (or sit in one spot and let the world
   come to you). At each stop, notice something — a plant, a cloud, a shadow, a sound,
   a smell, a texture. Ask a "why" or "how" question about it, and share your own idea
   too: "Why is this leaf full of holes? Maybe an insect was eating it."
3. **Collect wonder (10 min).** Draw (or gently collect) one thing that made you
   wonder. If you collected a living thing, be gentle and put it back where you found
   it.
4. **Share (5 min).** Tell a partner your best question and your best idea.
5. **Close (5 min).** The world is full of questions. Keep wondering wherever you go —
   your walk home is a wonder walk too.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Stop at just one spot and wonder together; the learner draws that one
  thing. Wonder found by ear (a bird), nose (a flower), or fingers (rough bark) counts
  the same as wonder found by eye.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to keep a "wonder list" all day and share their
  three best questions at the next gathering.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner notice something on the walk and ask a
  "why" or "how" question about it?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The drawing of the thing that made them wonder.

## Home connection

On the way home today, find one thing that makes you wonder. Ask a grown-up about it,
and tell them your own idea too.
