# Lesson 6 — Changes All Around Me

## Summary

Learners discover that change is happening everywhere, all the time — in the sky, the
weather, the light, and the living things around them. They hunt for changes, then
draw one they noticed.

## Objectives

- Observe and describe changes in the natural world all around them. _(D06.S4.K.02)_

## Connection

The world is always changing a little: the weather changes, the sky changes, water
moves, and living things grow. You do not have to go far — changes are happening
right where you are, and you can find them.

## Materials

- Paper and crayons
- A change hunt checklist (simple pictures)

## Preparation

- Prepare a simple picture checklist: sky, weather, a plant, an animal, light, water.
- Decide on a walk route or a good window for watching.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). As facilitator, keep the change hunt
safe and gentle. Weather and growing food are natural tie-ins: farmers and gardeners
everywhere watch the weather and the seasons to know when to plant and harvest —
that is people and the natural world changing together. Keep the tone of wonder, not
of worry.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** What changed between yesterday and today? The sky? The weather?
   A plant? You?
2. **Change hunt (15 min).** Walk around (or look out the window). Point to three
   changes you notice with your eyes, ears, nose, or skin: the sky, the weather, a
   plant or animal, the light, the water, a sound, a smell, warmer or cooler air.
   Check them off your picture list.
3. **Weather watch (10 min).** What is the weather today — hot, cool, wet, dry, windy,
   still? Has it changed since this morning? How can you tell?
4. **Draw a change (10 min).** Draw one change you noticed on your hunt.
5. **Close (5 min).** Change is everywhere, all the time — if you look for it. You
   found it today.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Hunt for just one change together, and describe it aloud before
  drawing. A change found by ear (a new sound), nose (a smell), or skin (cooler air)
  counts just as much as one found by eye.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to keep a "change list" all day and share three
  changes they noticed after the lesson.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner point to and name one change in the
  world around them?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The "a change I noticed" drawing.

## Home connection

At home, watch one thing — a window, a plant, or the sky — in the morning and again
in the evening. Tell a grown-up one thing that changed.
