# Lesson 8 — Looking Closely

## Summary

Learners practice the scientist's first skill: slow, careful looking. They study one
small object for a full minute, find details they missed at first, and draw what they
see.

## Objectives

- Ask "why" and "how" questions about something by looking closely at it. _(D06.S1.K.01)_

## Connection

Most of us look quickly and miss a lot. When you slow down and look closely — at a
leaf, a hand, a shadow — you notice things you never noticed before. "Looking closely"
can also mean touching, smelling, and listening closely. Noticing carefully is how
good questions are born.

## Materials

- A small natural object per learner (a leaf, stone, seed, shell)
- Paper and crayons
- A magnifier (optional)

## Preparation

- Gather one small natural object for each learner (a leaf, stone, seed, or shell).
- If using magnifiers, set them out in pairs.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). As facilitator, keep the looking slow
and unhurried — a full minute feels long at age five, so count together or watch a
timer. "Looking" here means noticing with whatever senses a learner has: eyes, fingers,
nose, ears. If the object is a living thing (an insect, a flower still attached), teach
gentleness: look with eyes, touch softly, and return it where it was found.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Pick up your object. From far away, what do you see? Just the
   big shape?
2. **Notice close (15 min).** Hold your object and look at it very slowly for a whole
   minute. Turn it over. Use a magnifier or make a finger frame to look at one tiny
   part. What new details do you notice — lines, colors, bumps, edges? If you cannot
   see well, "look" with your other senses: feel the bumps and edges with your
   fingers, smell it, and tap it gently to hear it.
3. **Draw what you see (10 min).** Draw the small details you found, the ones you
   missed at first.
4. **Share a detail (10 min).** Tell a partner one detail you noticed that you did not
   see at the beginning. Ask one "why" or "how" question about it.
5. **Close (5 min).** Looking slowly showed you a world you were missing. Careful
  looking is where wonder begins.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Look together and point to one detail at a time; the learner draws just
  one detail. For learners with low or no vision, notice by touch (bumps, edges),
  smell, and sound (tap it), and describe the detail aloud.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to compare two objects closely and tell one way they
  are the same and one way they are different.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name a detail they did not see at first
  and ask a question about it?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The "close looking" drawing.

## Home connection

At home, look closely at one thing with a grown-up — a fruit, a hand, a shoe. Tell
them one detail you both notice for the first time.
