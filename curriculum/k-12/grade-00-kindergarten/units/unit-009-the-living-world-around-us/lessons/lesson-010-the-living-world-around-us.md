# Lesson 10 — The Living World Around Us

## Summary

Learners gather everything from the unit — day and night, the sky, the seasons,
growing things, and their own questions — into one big picture of the living world
around them, with themselves in it, and share a change they noticed and a question
they still wonder about.

## Objectives

- Ask "why" and "how" questions about the living world and offer their own ideas.
  _(D06.S1.K.01)_
- Observe and describe changes in the natural world around them. _(D06.S4.K.02)_

## Connection

You have watched the sky change, seen living things grow, and asked big questions.
All of that — the light and dark, the seasons, the growing things, and your wonder —
is the living world around you. And you are part of it, too.

## Materials

- Large paper (or several sheets taped together)
- Crayons or paint

## Preparation

- Give each learner a big sheet of paper (or join sheets together for a class mural).
- Gather all the drawings and journals from the unit so learners can look back at them.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It is the unit's capstone — celebrate
what learners have noticed and wondered, and resist the urge to "review facts." The
unit's promise is science as observation and wonder. Honor every learner's local
world: there is no correct sky, season, or growing thing — only the one that is home.
Close with genuine delight at their questions.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think about everything you have explored: day and night, the
   changing sky, the seasons, growing things, and your own questions.
2. **Make a living-world picture (20 min).** On your big paper, draw the living world
   around you: the sky and its light, a change you noticed (a season, the weather, the
   night), a growing thing, and you in the middle of it all. Add one "why" or "how"
   question you still wonder about — write it or draw it.
3. **Share (15 min).** Share your picture with a partner or the group. Tell one change
   you noticed in this unit, and one question you still have.
4. **Close (5 min).** The living world is always changing, and your wonder is part of
   it. Keep watching, keep asking. You are a scientist already.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Draw just three things — the sky, one growing thing, and yourself — and
  tell the change out loud instead of writing.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to add one more part to their picture — a change they
  think will happen next — and tell why they think so.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name one change in the natural world
  and ask one "why" or "how" question about it?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The living-world picture, kept as the unit's closing
  artifact.

## Home connection

At home, show a grown-up your living-world picture. Tell them one change you noticed
and one question you still wonder about, and invite them to wonder with you.
