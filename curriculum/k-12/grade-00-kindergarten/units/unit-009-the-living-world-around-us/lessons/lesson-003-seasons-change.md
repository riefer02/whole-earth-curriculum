# Lesson 3 — Seasons Change

## Summary

Learners notice that the world around them changes through the year — and that
different places change in different ways. They describe and draw their own place at
two different times of the year. There is no one "right" set of seasons.

## Objectives

- Observe and describe how the natural world changes through the year. _(D06.S4.K.02)_

## Connection

Some places have four seasons; some places have two — a wet time and a dry time; some
places stay warm all year, and some are cold much of the year. Where you live, the
year has its own pattern. The world around you changes through the year — watch how
your place changes.

## Materials

- Paper and crayons
- Change pictures (leaves, rain, sun, snow, wind)

## Preparation

- Draw or print simple pictures of different changes: rain, dry ground, falling
  leaves, new leaves, snow or frost, wind, hot sun.
- Think ahead about what changes actually happen where you live, so the examples match.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). This is the one place a temperate
"four seasons" assumption can sneak in — do not let it. A learner near the equator
has a wet/dry year; a learner near a desert or a coast or the poles has yet another
pattern. Celebrate whatever pattern is true locally, and gently add that other places
have different patterns. The science is observation, not a calendar.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** What time of year is it now? What is it like outside today?
2. **My place through the year (10 min).** Look at the change pictures. Point to (or
   name) the changes you have seen where you live, and when you see them: "The rains
   come," "the leaves fall," "it gets very hot," "the snow stays."
3. **Match the changes (15 min).** Match each change to a time of year in your place,
   or act the change out: fall like a leaf, patter like rain, stretch like a new
   shoot. Not every picture fits your place — and that is fine.
4. **Draw your year (10 min).** Fold your paper in half. Draw your place at one time
   of the year on one side, and at a different time of the year on the other.
5. **Close (5 min).** Every place changes through the year in its own way. Your
   place's way is worth watching.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer two contrasting change pictures and ask, "Which of these have you
  seen here?" — just yes or no.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to draw their place at three different times of the
  year and put them in order.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name one way their own place changes
  through the year?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The "two times of year" drawing.

## Home connection

At home, ask a grown-up what their place looked like when they were small, and
whether it has changed over the years. Tell them one change you have seen this year.
