# Lesson 7 — Asking Why and How

## Summary

Learners practice the two most powerful question words there are — "why" and "how" —
and pair each question with their own idea. They learn that wondering out loud is how
every scientist and every curious person begins.

## Objectives

- Ask "why" and "how" questions about things in the world and offer their own ideas.
  _(D06.S1.K.01)_

## Connection

You wonder about things all the time: Why is the sky dark at night? How does a seed
know to grow? Asking "why" and "how" is how every curious person starts — and your
own idea is always a good place to begin.

## Materials

- An interesting object or picture
- A wonder box (any container)
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Choose one interesting object or picture everyone can look at together.
- Prepare a wonder box (a basket, jar, or bag) where questions can be dropped.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). As facilitator, make wondering safe:
say there are no silly questions, and model your own "why" and "how" questions. In
settings where questioning adults is not safe or usual, frame this as wondering
together, not challenging anyone, and offer the wonder box so learners can ask
without speaking up (see `docs/contexts.md`).

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** What have you wondered about today? Anything at all.
2. **Question words (10 min).** Look at the object or picture. Ask it questions: "Why
   is it this color?" "How did it get here?" "Why does it move like that?" "How does
   it change?" There are no silly questions.
3. **Wonder box (15 min).** Think of your biggest question about the world. Draw it or
   write it (or have a grown-up write it for you), and drop it in the wonder box — or
   say it aloud if you want. Then add your own idea: your best guess.
4. **My idea (10 min).** Choose one question and draw or tell your own answer to it.
   You do not have to be sure — a guess is a great place to start.
5. **Close (5 min).** Asking "why" and "how" — and sharing your ideas — is how we
   learn about the world. Keep asking.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer a sentence frame: "I wonder why ___" and "I think maybe ___."
- **Extension:** Invite learners to write or draw three questions and star the one
  they most want to explore next.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner ask a "why" or "how" question and offer
  their own idea about it?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The drawn or scribed question with the learner's idea.

## Home connection

At home, ask a grown-up a "why" or "how" question about the world, then tell them
your own guess. Wonder together.
