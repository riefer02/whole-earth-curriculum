# Lesson 2 — The Changing Sky

## Summary

Learners look up and notice what lives in the sky — the sun, the moon, and the stars —
and how the sky changes from day to night and from one night to the next. They sort,
look, and draw their own sky.

## Objectives

- Observe and describe changes in the sky from day to night. _(D06.S4.K.02)_

## Connection

Lie on your back and look up — the sky is never empty. In the day you might see the
sun, and sometimes the moon too; at night you might see the moon and stars. In a
bright city you may see fewer stars, but the moon still comes and goes. Wherever you
live, the sky changes, and you can watch it.

## Materials

- Sky pictures (sun, moon, stars)
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Draw or print simple cards for the sun, the moon, and stars.
- If you can, note when the moon will be visible tonight so learners know when to look.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). As facilitator, honor the many stories
people everywhere have told about the sun, the moon, and the stars; hold them side by
side without ranking one above another. The goal is wonder at a changing sky, not
astronomy facts. Be ready for the moon to appear in the day sky too — that is a real,
wonderful thing to notice.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** What did the sky look like this morning? What do you think it
   will look like tonight?
2. **Sort the sky (10 min).** Look at the sun, moon, and star cards. Sort them: which
   do you usually see in the day? At night? Which can you sometimes see in both? If
   you cannot see the cards, tell the sky aloud instead: "the sun warms my skin,"
   "the night is cool and full of sounds."
3. **Moon watch (15 min).** The moon seems to change a little night after night —
   sometimes round, sometimes thin, sometimes hiding. Draw the moon the way you
   remember it. If you can, check the moon over the next few nights and see if it
   changed.
4. **Draw your sky (10 min).** Draw the sky you saw most recently — day or night —
   with everything you noticed in it.
5. **Close (5 min).** The sky is full of changing lights, and people everywhere have
   looked up and wondered about them.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use just two cards (sun and moon) and act out "bright day" and "dim
  night" with a partner. For learners who cannot see the cards, describe the day sky
  and night sky with skin (warm vs cool) and sound (busy vs quiet).
- **Extension:** Invite learners to watch the moon for several nights and draw three
  of its shapes in order.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name what they might see in the day sky
  and the night sky?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The "my sky" drawing.

## Home connection

At home, look at the night sky with a grown-up. Count what you see — the moon, some
stars — and tell them whether the moon looked round or thin.
