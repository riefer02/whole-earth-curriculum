# Lesson 5 — Watching Something Grow

## Summary

Learners begin a slow, real observation: they plant a seed (or mark their own
height), draw what they see now, and make a guess about what will change. The
watching continues over the next days — growth is seen, not told.

## Objectives

- Observe and describe how a living thing grows and changes over time. _(D06.S4.K.02)_

## Connection

A seed does not become a plant all at once — it grows a little every day. You can
watch a seed, or watch yourself grow, a little bit at a time. Watching closely, day
after day, is how we see change happen.

## Materials

- A seed (bean or lentil)
- A cup or small jar
- Damp cloth or a little soil, and water
- Paper and crayons (for a grow journal)

## Preparation

- Set out a seed, cup, and damp cloth (or soil) for each learner.
- If seeds are unavailable, prepare a way to mark each learner's height (a wall mark
  or a knotted string).

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It begins an observation that runs
several days — plan to return to the journals every day or two. If seeds are not
available, watching a learner's own height, a plant outside, or a pet works just as
well; what matters is the repeated looking. Remind learners to be gentle with the
living thing they are watching.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** What could you watch grow? A seed, a plant, your own height, a
   pet, a younger person?
2. **Set up a grow-watch (15 min).** Put a damp cloth (or a little soil) in your cup.
   Tuck your seed in where you can see it, and add a few drops of water. If you are
   watching your height instead, make a mark on the wall or tie a knot in your string.
   That mark is your "small."
3. **Start a grow journal (10 min).** On the first page, draw your seed (or your
   height mark) exactly as it is right now. Write or draw the date if you can.
4. **Guess and wonder (10 min).** What will it look like in a week? Draw your guess
   on the next page. You are not sure — and that is okay.
5. **Close (5 min).** You will look again tomorrow, and the next day. You will see the
   change with your own eyes.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Watch together as a group; the facilitator draws the first journal
  page while learners watch and describe.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to draw the seed every day for a week and compare the
  pages to describe the change.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner describe their seed (or height) "now"
  and make a guess about "later"?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The grow journal, added to over the following days.

## Home connection

At home, ask a grown-up to help you find something small that is growing — a plant,
a sibling, a pet, or you. Tell them what you think it will look like in a week.
