# Lesson 1 — Day and Night

## Summary

Learners notice the change between day and night — the light comes and goes — and
show what they know by sorting, moving, and drawing the two skies. Science here is
watching and wondering, not facts to memorize.

## Objectives

- Observe and describe the change from day to night. _(D06.S4.K.02)_

## Connection

Look out your window in the morning, and look again at night — the sky changes from
light to dark. Every place on Earth has light time and dark time, every single day.
Day and night are the biggest change you can watch, and you can watch them again and
again.

## Materials

- Paper and crayons
- A sun/moon picture set (drawn or printed)
- A flashlight or lamp (optional)

## Preparation

- Draw or print a simple bright sun card and a dark night card.
- Clear a space for acting things out.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. As facilitator, hold the core idea lightly: day and night are
a repeating change of light and dark. If learners ask, you may add that near the
equator day and night are about equal all year, while near the poles some seasons
have very long days or very long nights — but only if the question comes up; the
goal is wonder, not a diagram.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Look around (or out the window). Is it light or dark right
   now? What will it be later?
2. **Light and dark (10 min).** Look at the sun card and the night card. The sun
   gives us light. Act it out: bright (arms wide, smile) and dark (arms in, still).
   Point to the card that matches now. If you cannot see the cards, notice day and
   night with your skin and ears: the sun's warmth on your face means day; cooler air
   and quieter sounds mean night.
3. **What do you do when? (15 min).** Sort or act out these moments: you wake up, you
   see the moon, you eat breakfast, you see stars, you sleep, you see your shadow.
   Is each one day or night?
4. **Draw both skies (10 min).** On one side of the paper, draw your sky in the day.
   On the other, draw your sky at night.
5. **Close (5 min).** Day and night follow each other, over and over, all around the
   world. Tomorrow you can watch it happen again.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use only two cards and two moments ("I sleep" = night, "I play" = day);
  act together with a partner. For learners who cannot see the cards, tell light from
  dark with skin (sun warmth) and sound (birdsong vs quiet).
- **Extension:** Invite learners to draw three moments in a day and put them in order:
  morning, afternoon, night.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner point to or act out which moments are
  day and which are night?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The "two skies" drawing.

## Home connection

At home, look out the same window in the morning and again at night. Tell a grown-up
one thing that is the same and one thing that changed.
