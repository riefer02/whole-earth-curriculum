# Lesson 4 — Finding Feelings in Others

## Summary

Learners practice noticing how other people show feelings through their face, voice,
and body. They learn that a guess is not always certain — so we check kindly by
asking.

## Objectives

- Name and identify basic feelings (happy, sad, angry, scared, calm) in self and
  others. _(D01.S1.K.01)_

## Connection

You can often tell how a friend is feeling before they say a word. A smiling mouth,
a quiet voice, shoulders that drop — these are clues. People show feelings in many
ways, so we look, listen, and then ask to be sure.

## Materials

- Feeling face cards (happy, sad, angry, scared, calm)
- A mirror (optional)
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Reuse the feeling face cards.
- Clear space for partners to face each other.
- Prepare the checking question: "Are you feeling ___?"

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. As facilitator, emphasize three clue-spots: face, voice, body.
Model guessing gently and checking by asking. Remind learners that faces and voices
show feelings differently in different families and cultures — a guess is a guess,
and asking is how we learn.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Sit with a partner. Look at each other's faces. What might
   your partner be feeling? Do not say it yet.
2. **Three clue-spots (10 min).** Feelings show in three places: the face (eyes,
   mouth), the voice (loud, quiet, fast, slow), and the body (shoulders, hands,
   feet). Look at the feeling cards and find the clues on each face.
3. **Guess and check (15 min).** One partner secretly chooses a feeling and shows it
   with face, voice, and body. The other guesses: "Are you feeling sad?" Then check:
   "Yes!" or "No, I'm feeling tired."
4. **Draw the clues (10 min).** Choose one feeling. Draw a face with that feeling,
   and add a voice word (like "quiet" or "loud") and a body clue (like "shoulders
   down").
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: look, listen, and ask. Asking kindly helps us know
   for sure.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer two feeling cards to choose between when guessing.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to show the same feeling two different ways and ask
  a partner which was easier to read.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name a feeling from a face, voice, or
  body clue and check their guess?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The feeling-clue drawing.

## Home connection

At home, play the guessing game with a family member: show a feeling with your face
and body, let them guess, then check by asking. Swap turns.
