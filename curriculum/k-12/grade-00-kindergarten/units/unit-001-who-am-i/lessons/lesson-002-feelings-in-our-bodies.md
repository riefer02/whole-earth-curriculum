# Lesson 2 — Feelings in Our Bodies

## Summary

Learners discover that feelings are not only in the head — each one shows up
somewhere in the body. They scan their bodies for happy, sad, angry, scared, and
calm, then mark the spots on a simple body outline.

## Objectives

- Name and identify basic feelings (happy, sad, angry, scared, calm) in self and
  others. _(D01.S1.K.01)_
- Describe a feeling using words, a drawing, or a movement. _(D01.S1.K.02)_

## Connection

Have you ever felt your tummy rumble when you were hungry, or your legs get bouncy
before something exciting? Feelings live in your body too. When you know where a
feeling shows up, you can notice it sooner and take care of yourself.

## Materials

- Feeling face cards (happy, sad, angry, scared, calm)
- Crayons and paper
- A simple body outline for each learner (drawn or printed)

## Preparation

- Draw or print a child-shaped body outline for each learner.
- Reuse the feeling face cards from Lesson 1, or draw them again.
- Arrange space for a body scan where learners can sit or lie quietly.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. As facilitator, lead the body scan slowly and calmly, naming
one feeling at a time. Some learners will feel a feeling "everywhere" or "nowhere" —
honor both. There is no wrong answer; the goal is noticing, not matching a chart.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Sit comfortably and take one slow breath. Put a hand on your
   tummy. What do you notice?
2. **Meet the body (10 min).** Look at the body outline. Point to your head, chest,
   tummy, hands, and feet. Every feeling has a home in your body.
3. **Body scan (15 min).** One feeling at a time: think of a time you felt happy.
   Where does it live? (A warm chest, a smiling mouth.) Now sad — heavy shoulders, a
   slow head. Angry — hot cheeks, tight hands. Scared — a fast heart, a wobbly
   tummy. Calm — soft breathing, still hands.
4. **Mark it (10 min).** On your body outline, color the spot where each feeling
   lives. Use a color that fits the feeling for you.
5. **Close (5 min).** Share one spot with a partner: "When I feel scared, my tummy
   wobbles." Remember: your body tells you what you feel.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer two feelings at a time; model by touching your own chest or
  hands.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to draw two different spots for one feeling and
  describe how the feeling can feel big or small.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner point to and name a body spot for at
  least one feeling?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The colored body outline, kept in the learner's
  portfolio.

Ask a grown-up at home: "Where do you feel happy in your body?" Compare your spot
with theirs — it might be the same or different.
