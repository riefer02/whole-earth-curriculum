# Lesson 5 — Many Ways to Say a Feeling

## Summary

Learners explore the many "languages" of feeling: words, drawings, movement, and
sound. They practice showing one feeling in several different ways and discover that
every person has ways that fit them best.

## Objectives

- Describe a feeling using words, a drawing, or a movement. _(D01.S1.K.02)_

## Connection

People everywhere show feelings in different ways. Some sing when they are happy,
some dance, some draw, some tell a friend. There is no single right way — your way is
the one that helps you be understood.

## Materials

- Feeling face cards (happy, sad, angry, scared, calm)
- Paper and crayons
- A simple drum, shaker, or clapped hands (optional)

## Preparation

- Reuse the feeling face cards.
- Clear space for movement and sound.
- Gather any simple sound-makers, or plan to use voices and bodies.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. As facilitator, honor every "language" — a quiet learner may
prefer drawing to dancing. The point is choice and expression, not performance. If
learners speak different languages at home, invite them to share a feeling word in
their home language — every word is welcome.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Look at the feeling cards. How many ways can you show "happy"
   without saying the word "happy"?
2. **Four languages (15 min).** A feeling can travel four ways: **words** ("I feel
   scared"), **drawing** (a wobbly line, a dark cloud), **movement** (hide your face,
   shrink small), and **sound** (a soft "oh," a stomp, a drum tap). Try "happy,"
   "sad," and "angry" in all four ways.
3. **Choose your way (15 min).** Pick one feeling. Show it three ways — word,
   drawing, and movement. Which one felt easiest? Which helped you be understood
   best?
4. **Share (10 min).** With a partner or the group, show your feeling in your
   favorite way. Can others guess it?
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: many ways, one feeling. Your way is a good way.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer two ways to choose from (word or movement) instead of all four.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to combine two languages — a movement with a sound —
  and explain why they chose them.

## Assessment

- **Formative (performance):** Can the learner show one feeling in at least two
  different ways (word, drawing, movement, or sound)?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The feeling drawing from "Choose your way."

## Home connection

At home, teach a family member the four "languages" of feeling and play the guessing
game — show a feeling in sound or movement and let them name it.
