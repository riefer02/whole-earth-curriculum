# Lesson 3 — Feelings Come and Go

## Summary

Learners explore how feelings move and change over time. A feeling arrives, stays
for a while, and then passes — and another one comes. They practice retelling a
feeling that came and went, and learn that change is okay.

## Objectives

- Name and identify basic feelings (happy, sad, angry, scared, calm) in self and
  others. _(D01.S1.K.01)_
- Describe a feeling using words, a drawing, or a movement. _(D01.S1.K.02)_

## Connection

Think of a time you were sad, and then later you felt better. Or angry, and then a
hug or a game made it soften. Feelings are like weather: rain comes, and then the
sun comes out again. No feeling lasts forever.

## Materials

- Feeling face cards (happy, sad, angry, scared, calm)
- Paper and crayons
- A soft ball or rolled cloth to pass around

## Preparation

- Reuse or redraw the feeling face cards.
- Arrange a circle so the ball can pass from learner to learner.
- Prepare a gentle sentence frame to share aloud.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. As facilitator, model first: tell your own "I felt ___, and
then it ___" story. Keep it light — this lesson is about the natural passing of
feelings, not about fixing or judging them. If a learner shares something heavy,
honor it calmly and help them notice it softened or passed.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Sit in a circle. Think of a feeling you had today. Does it
   still feel the same now?
2. **Watch feelings move (10 min).** Look at the feeling cards. A feeling is like a
   guest: it knocks, it stays a while, and then it goes. Act it out — happy arrives
   (big smile), happy grows (jump), happy leaves (soft and still).
3. **A feeling story (15 min).** Pass the ball. When it comes to you, finish this
   sentence: "I felt ___, and then it ___." (Example: "I felt angry when my turn was
   skipped, and then it went away when I played.")
4. **Draw then and now (10 min).** On paper, draw a feeling you had earlier, and
   beside it, draw how you feel now. They can be different.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: feelings come and go. That is okay. A new feeling is
   always on its way.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer two feeling cards to choose from and a partner to help finish
  the sentence.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to tell a "before, during, after" story of one
  feeling with three quick drawings.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner retell a feeling that changed using
  "and then"?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The "then and now" drawing.

## Home connection

At home, ask a grown-up to tell you about a feeling they had today that changed.
Take turns telling your "I felt ___, and then it ___" stories.
