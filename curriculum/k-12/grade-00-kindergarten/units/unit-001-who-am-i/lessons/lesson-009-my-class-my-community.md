# Lesson 9 — My Class, My Community

## Summary

Learners widen the circle from family to the other groups they belong to: their
class, their friends, their neighbors, and their community. They draw the groups and
the people who help hold them together.

## Objectives

- Name the groups to which they belong (family, class, community). _(D01.S2.K.02)_

## Connection

You belong to more than your family. You belong to your class, to your friends, and
to your community — the people who live and work near you. The people who sell food
at the market, who teach at your school, who help when someone is sick — these people
are part of your community too.

## Materials

- Paper and crayons
- One large shared paper for a class "we belong" picture

## Preparation

- Clear space for a group drawing.
- Prepare a list of possible groups: class, friends, neighbors, community, a place
  of gathering.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. As facilitator, keep "community" concrete — the people and
places a learner actually sees, not an abstraction. Where learners come from
different neighborhoods or places of worship or gathering, honor each equally and
avoid naming one as the norm.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think: besides your family, what group are you part of? Your
   class? Your friends? Your neighbors?
2. **Many groups (10 min).** Name groups one by one and raise a hand for each you
   belong to: class, friends, neighbors, community. Notice how many hands go up.
3. **Who holds us (10 min).** In your community, who helps? (Someone who sells food,
   teaches, heals, fixes, or keeps us safe.) Name one helper and what they do.
4. **We belong (15 min).** On the big shared paper, each learner draws one place or
   person from the community. Add your mark. Look — many hands made one big picture.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: many groups hold you. You belong in each one.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer pictures or gestures for each group so learners can point
  rather than name.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to name one way they help their community, even in a
  small way, and tell a partner.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name at least one group they belong to
  besides family?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The shared "we belong" picture (or a photo of it).

## Home connection

On your way home, notice three people who help your community. Tell a grown-up who
they are and what they do.
