# Lesson 8 — My Family and My People

## Summary

Learners name the people they belong to — the grown-ups and others who care for
them, whether they share a home or a wider circle. They draw their people and
celebrate the care that holds them.

## Objectives

- Name the groups to which they belong (family, class, community). _(D01.S2.K.02)_

## Connection

Think of the people who take care of you — the ones who feed you, hold you, teach
you, and play with you. They might be parents, grandparents, aunties and uncles,
older siblings, or neighbors. These are your people, and you belong to them just as
they belong to you.

## Materials

- Paper and crayons
- A "My people" circle or house outline for each learner

## Preparation

- Draw or print a big circle or house shape for each learner.
- Think of one example to model: a person who cares for you.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. As facilitator, use the wide word "people" so every family
shape is honored — one caregiver, two, grandparents, guardians, a chosen family, or a
community that raises a child. Do not assume a single household form. Invite sharing
about care, not about who lives where.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of one person who takes care of you. What do they do?
2. **Who cares, and how (10 min).** People care in many ways: they feed you, hold
   you when you cry, teach you things, and play with you. Can you name a person for
   each kind of care? They are your people.
3. **Draw my people (15 min).** In your circle or house, draw the people who care
   for you. They do not all have to live in one home — everyone who cares belongs in
   your circle.
4. **Share one (10 min).** Tell a partner one thing your people do together — a
   meal, a story, a game, a walk.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: you belong to your people, and they belong to you.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer a sentence starter: "One person who cares for me is ___."
- **Extension:** Invite learners to add a wider circle around the first — people
  beyond home, like neighbors or caregivers — and name them.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name at least one group of people they
  belong to?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The "My people" drawing.
