# Lesson 1 — Naming Our Feelings

## Summary

Learners learn to name five basic feelings and practice recognizing them in
themselves and others. They close by expressing one feeling through words, a drawing,
or a movement.

## Objectives

- Name and identify basic feelings (happy, sad, angry, scared, calm) in self and
  others. _(D01.S1.K.01)_
- Describe a feeling using words, a drawing, or a movement. _(D01.S2.K.02)_

## Materials

- Feeling face cards (happy, sad, angry, scared, calm)
- Crayons and paper
- A mirror (optional)

## Preparation

- Print or draw a set of feeling face cards.
- Arrange a circle for gathering and discussion.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Sit in a circle. Ask: "How does your body feel right now?"
2. **Introduce the feeling cards (10 min).** Show each card; name the feeling; make
   the face together. Invite learners to point to a card that matches how they feel
   today.
3. **Body check (10 min).** Guide a simple scan: "Where do you feel happy in your
   body?" (e.g., a warm chest, a smile). Use the mirror so learners can see their
   own faces.
4. **Express it (15 min).** Learners choose one feeling and show it in one of three
   ways: a word, a drawing, or a movement. Share in pairs.
5. **Close (5 min).** Reflect: "Everyone has feelings, and feelings change. That is
   okay."

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer a smaller set of cards (two feelings); model a word and gesture.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to draw two feelings and describe a time they felt
  each.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner point to or name a feeling that
  matches a scenario?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The feeling drawing, kept in the learner's portfolio.
