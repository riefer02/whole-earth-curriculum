# Lesson 6 — What Makes Me Me

## Summary

Learners explore what makes each person one-of-a-kind: their name, the things they
like and love, the things they can do, and the people who care for them. They build
the first page of a "Me" collage.

## Objectives

- Share one thing that makes them unique and one thing they share with others.
  _(D01.S2.K.01)_

## Connection

No one else in the whole world is exactly like you. Your name, your favorite food,
the song you love, the way you laugh — these are yours. At the same time, you share
some things with everyone else, and both are worth celebrating.

## Materials

- Paper and crayons
- A "Me" sheet with empty spaces (name, favorite thing, something I can do, my
  people)

## Preparation

- Draw or print a "Me" sheet for each learner, or fold plain paper into four spaces.
- Think of one example to share first — the facilitator models being "me."

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. As facilitator, model first by sharing something about
yourself. Keep the frame broad and kind: "unique" is about what a learner chooses to
share, not about ranking or comparing. Avoid asking learners to label themselves in
ways that feel private. Every answer is welcomed equally.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think: what is one thing that is true about you and no one
   else here? It could be your name, a favorite thing, or something you can do.
2. **Four corners of me (15 min).** Look at your "Me" sheet. Fill each space: your
   name, one thing you like or love, one thing you can do, and the people who care
   for you. Draw or tell each one.
3. **Share one (10 min).** Choose one thing that makes you you, and share it with a
   partner. Listen to theirs too. Notice how different they are — and how good that
   is.
4. **A hand of me (10 min).** Trace your hand. On each finger, draw one thing that
   makes you you. Five fingers, five things.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: you are you, and that is good. No one else can be
   you.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer two prompts at a time and allow a single drawing instead of
  four.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to add a sixth thing to their hand and describe why
  they chose it.

## Assessment

- **Formative (portfolio):** Can the learner name at least one thing that makes them
  unique?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The "Me" sheet and hand tracing, saved for the Lesson 10
  celebration.
