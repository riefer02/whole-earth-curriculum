# Lesson 5 — Our Wonder Box

## Summary

Learners celebrate the unit's biggest lesson — that wondering is good — by gathering
their questions into a shared wonder box. They learn that some questions stay open,
that keeping a question is a way of loving it, and that the box will be there to
wonder with again.

## Objectives

- Ask "why" and "how" questions about things in the world and offer their own ideas.
  _(D06.S1.K.01)_

## Connection

Grown-ups do not know everything, and neither does anyone — that is why people keep
asking questions their whole lives. A question is a little gift; when you keep it, you
can wonder about it again tomorrow. Today we gather our questions together so we can
keep wondering, now and later.

## Materials

- A decorated box (the "wonder box")
- Slips of paper
- A soft ball or "wonder stone"

## Preparation

- Set out the wonder box where everyone can reach it.
- Cut slips of paper and set out crayons.
- Have the "wondering wall" from Lesson 1 nearby to revisit old questions.

## Facilitator note

This lesson closes the unit and seals its promise: no question here will be graded,
ranked, or dismissed. Model the comfort with not knowing — say a question you still
wonder about yourself. If a child asks something you cannot answer, say "I wonder too
— let's keep it in the box." The wonder box should stay in the room after the unit, so
wondering continues.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Look at the wondering wall. Remember all the questions you have
   asked this week. Were any of them easy to answer? Were some very big?
2. **Decorate the box (10 min).** Make the wonder box beautiful — with crayons,
   drawings of stars and eggs and rivers, or whatever you like. This box will hold
   your questions.
3. **Fill the box (15 min).** Pass the wonder stone around. When it comes to you, say
   one "why" or "how" question you still wonder about, and offer your own first idea.
   Then draw or write your question on a slip and drop it in. Passing is always okay.
4. **Wonder about someday (10 min).** Pull out one question (yours or a friend's).
   Wonder about it together: what might the answer be? What could we watch or try to
   find out? Some questions we cannot answer yet — and that is wonderful too.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: wondering is good, questions are treasures, and this
   box will be here whenever you want to wonder again.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer question starters — "Why…?" "How…?" — and accept a drawing or a
  point as a question; scribe the words for learners who want them written.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to sort questions into "we can find out now" and
  "we will wonder about someday," and to plan one thing they could watch to learn more.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Does the learner offer a "why" or "how" question and an
  idea of their own, and speak of wondering as good?
- **Self:** The learner reflects — "one question I am keeping" — as the unit closes.
