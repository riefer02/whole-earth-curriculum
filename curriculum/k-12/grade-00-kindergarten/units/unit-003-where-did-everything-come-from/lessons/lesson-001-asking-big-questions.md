# Lesson 1 — Asking Big Questions

## Summary

Learners discover that a question is a way of looking closer at the world. They turn
things they can see into "why" and "how" questions and offer their own first guesses.
They close by drawing one question and posting it on the wondering wall.

## Objectives

- Ask "why" and "how" questions about things in the world and offer their own ideas.
  _(D06.S1.K.01)_

## Connection

Every day you see things that make you wonder — why the sky is blue, how a seed
becomes a plant, where the rain goes after it falls. Wondering is a kind of looking,
and a question is a way to look closer. When you ask "why" or "how," you are doing
what scientists and explorers and storytellers have always done: paying attention to
the world.

## Materials

- A large paper "wondering wall"
- Picture cards of everyday things (sun, rain cloud, a seed, a bird, the moon, a baby)
- Crayons and paper

## Preparation

- Put up the wondering wall where everyone can reach it.
- Set out the picture cards where they can be picked up and passed around.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Your main job is to *model wonder*: wonder aloud yourself, and
treat every learner's question as a real question, never as a silly one. Do not judge
answers as right or wrong. If a learner says "I don't know," say "I wonder too — what
is your first idea?"

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Sit comfortably. Look around the room or out a window. What is
   one thing you can see right now?
2. **Meet the question words (10 min).** Some questions begin with "why" and some with
   "how." Hold up a picture card — say, the rain cloud. Ask it a "why" question: "Why
   does it rain?" Now ask a "how" question: "How does rain get up into the cloud?"
   A question is a wondering said out loud.
3. **Wonder together (15 min).** Pass the cards around. For each card, say one "why"
   or "how" question out loud. Then offer your own idea — any guess is welcome, even a
   wild one. There is no wrong question and no wrong first idea.
4. **Draw your question (10 min).** Choose the thing you wonder about most. Draw it,
   and draw or tell your question. Post it on the wondering wall.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: everyone wonders, and every question is a little
   light. Keep wondering.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer one card at a time and model the pattern — "I see a ____. I
  wonder why/how ____." Accept a pointing or a word as a question.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to chain a question: "Why does it rain? …And how does
  the cloud get full?" and to offer two different ideas for the same question.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner turn something they see into a "why" or
  "how" question and offer an idea of their own?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The learner's drawn question, kept for the unit's closing
  wonder box.
