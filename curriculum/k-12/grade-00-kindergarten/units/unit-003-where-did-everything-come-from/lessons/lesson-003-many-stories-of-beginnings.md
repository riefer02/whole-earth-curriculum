# Lesson 3 — Many Stories of Beginnings

## Summary

Learners meet several origin stories from different peoples of the world, told
gently and held side by side as equal, treasured tellings of the same question. They
learn that every people's story is meaningful and that no one story is everyone's
story — the scientific story from the day before is one among many.

## Objectives

- Listen to an origin story about the universe and retell it in their own words,
  knowing it is one story among many. _(D06.S4.K.01)_

## Connection

People everywhere — in hot places and cold places, by the sea and in the mountains —
have looked at the same sky and asked the same question: how did it all begin? Each
people has made a story to hold their answer, the way your family might tell stories
about how things began for you. No one story is everyone's story, and all of them are
kept safe and treasured.

## Materials

- Story picture cards (one per story)
- A "story blanket" or mat
- A small basket to hold the cards

## Preparation

- Make or gather one simple picture card per story: a golden egg; a sky and an earth
  holding each other; water with dry land; a winding river through hills.
- Set out the story blanket and place the cards in the basket.

## Facilitator note

Tell these stories with warmth and respect, as gifts from real peoples, not as
curiosities. Every one of these stories has many versions, and storytellers in each
place tell them a little differently; you are telling one simple version. The word
"the Dreaming" is an English way to point to something many Aboriginal nations each
name in their own language; it is not one people's single story. Name the people and
place of each story without ranking any above another, and without caricature. If a learner shares their own family's story, welcome it as a treasured
story too. Do not ask anyone to believe any of them.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Sit on the story blanket. Remember yesterday's story — the
   "Everywhere Stretch." That is *one* story. Today we meet more, from people all over
   the world.
2. **Hear the stories (20 min).** Reach into the basket and bring out one card at a
   time. Listen to each story:
   - **The golden egg (India):** Some people in India have told of a great golden egg
     that floated in the nothing. When it split, the world spilled out — the sky from
     one half, the earth from the other.
   - **The sky and the earth (Aotearoa, the Māori people):** The Māori people of
     Aotearoa tell of Ranginui the sky and Papatūānuku the earth, who once held each
     other so close that there was no light between them — until their children pushed
     them gently apart, and light rushed in.
   - **Water and land (West Africa, Yoruba storytellers):** Yoruba storytellers in
     West Africa tell that at first the world was all water and sky, until a kind
     spirit came down and scattered soil on the water to shape the dry land.
   - **The Dreaming (Australia, Aboriginal peoples):** Many Aboriginal peoples of
     Australia tell of the time when ancestor beings traveled the land and shaped the
     rivers, hills, and trees — and the land woke up. English speakers often call this
     "the Dreaming," but each nation has its own name for it in its own language.
3. **Hold them side by side (5 min).** Lay all the cards out together. Are any two
   alike? Are they all beautiful? They are all answers to the same big question, and
   all of them are treasures.
4. **Your turn to keep one (10 min).** Pick the story that feels most like a treasure
   to you. Hold its card and tell it back in your own words to a partner.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: there are many stories, and every one matters. Nobody
   has to believe any of them; everyone may love as many as they like.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Tell only two stories, slowly, with a movement for each; let the
  learner point to the card that matches a picture.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to add a story they know from their own family or
  place, and to wonder what all the stories share (many begin with light, water, or
  the land).

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner hold more than one story in mind and
  say they are all meaningful, with none ranked above the others?
- **Performance:** Retelling a chosen story in their own words.
