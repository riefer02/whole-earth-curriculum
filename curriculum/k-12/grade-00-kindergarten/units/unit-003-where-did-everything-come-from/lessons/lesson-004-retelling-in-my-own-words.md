# Lesson 4 — Retelling in My Own Words

## Summary

Learners choose one beginning story — the scientific "Everywhere Stretch" or one of
the peoples' stories they met — and retell it in their own words, then make a picture
of it. The retelling and picture become a portfolio keepsake that shows they can hold
a story as their own while knowing it is one among many.

## Objectives

- Listen to an origin story about the universe and retell it in their own words,
  knowing it is one story among many. _(D06.S4.K.01)_

## Connection

When you hear a story you love, you want to tell it again your own way — maybe to a
grandparent, a friend, or a younger child. Retelling a story is how we share what
moved us and make it our own. Today you will choose one beginning story and tell it
with your own voice and your own picture.

## Materials

- Crayons and paper
- Story picture cards (from Lesson 3)
- A "listening partner" card for each pair

## Preparation

- Lay out the story picture cards where everyone can see and reach them.
- Pair learners (or set up a place for a self-directed learner to tell to a toy or
  mirror).

## Facilitator note

This is the unit's portfolio moment. Encourage learners to choose the story *they*
love, not the one you love — the point is ownership, not correctness. When listening,
model the listening partner card: eyes look, ears listen, mouth quiet. Do not correct
a child's retelling; a retelling is allowed to be their own. Praise the act of
telling, and note on the picture which story it tells.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Look at all the story cards. Which beginning story do you love
   most? The "Everywhere Stretch"? The golden egg? The sky and the earth? Water and
   land? The Dreaming?
2. **Practice your retelling (10 min).** Pick your story. Whisper it to yourself.
   Remember how it begins, what happens in the middle, and how it ends. It does not
   have to match the way I told it — it is your turn now.
3. **Tell it to a partner (10 min).** Sit with your listening partner. Tell your
   story in your own words while your partner listens with eyes, ears, and a quiet
   mouth. Then swap. You may also tell it to a toy or a mirror.
4. **Draw it (15 min).** Draw your story — the egg splitting, the sky lifting, water
   and land, the stretching universe, or the land waking up. Make it big and bright.
   You may also act it out with your whole body instead of, or along with, drawing.
5. **Close (5 min).** Share your picture with the circle if you like. Remember: this
   story is yours to love, and it is still one story among many.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer a sentence starter — "First… then… at the end…" — and let the
  learner point to their picture as the "words."
- **Extension:** Invite learners to combine two stories into one telling or to add
  what they wonder comes *next* in their story.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Does the learner retell a beginning story in their own
  words, in order, and speak of it as one story among many?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The drawn retelling, labeled with the story it tells,
  kept in the learner's portfolio.
