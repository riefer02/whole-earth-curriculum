# Lesson 8 — Where Is It? Over, Under, Beside

## Summary

Learners learn to say *where* a thing is using position words — **over**, **under**,
**beside**, **on**, **in**. They move objects and their own bodies to match each word,
turning position words into something they can feel and act out.

## Objectives

- Use position words (over, under, beside) to describe where things are.
  _(D05.S3.K.01)_

## Connection

Every day you answer "where?" — your shoe is **under** the bed, a bird flies **over**
the tree, you sit **beside** a friend. These little words tell people exactly where to
look, in every language. When you can say where a thing is, you can help someone find
it, and find your own way in the world.

## Materials

- A small toy or object per learner or pair
- A table or box to move things around

## Preparation

- Give each learner or pair a small object to move.
- Provide a table, box, or other object to place things over, under, on, in, and
  beside.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Position words are spatial reasoning
made speakable, and young children learn them best by *doing* — putting a body or an
object in the position and saying the word at the same time. Teach each word with a
clear, repeated action (hand **over** head, hand **under** the table, hand **beside** a
friend) so the word and the place stick together. Model with your own body first, then
invite learners to do it. Notice that position words are relative — "beside me" is
different from "beside you" — and let learners discover this by standing in different
places. This relativity is the same in every language and culture, so the concept is
global even when the words differ. Learners who are blind or low-vision can feel the
positions (a hand on a hand that is over, under, beside); learners who are deaf or hard
of hearing can act out and sign each position. Learners with limited movement describe
positions by voice or sign — telling a partner where to place an object, or choosing a
position with eye-gaze or a mouth stick — instead of moving it themselves. Keep it
playful and never quiz-like; naming where things are is a skill that grows with lots of
joyful practice.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Pick up your small object. It is somewhere right now — in your
   hand. Today you will learn the words that say *where*.
2. **Meet the position words (15 min).** Say each word and do it with your body. Put
   your hand **over** your head. Put your hand **under** the table. Put your hand
   **beside** a friend. Now do it with your object: put it **on** the table, **in** the
   box, **over** the box, **under** the box, **beside** the box.
3. **Move and say (15 min).** Move your object to a new place and say where it is in
   a full sentence: "The shell is under the table." Try as many positions as you can.
4. **Hide and describe (5 min).** With a friend, one of you hides the object and
   says only its position: "It is beside the box." Can the other find it?
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: little words like over, under, and beside tell people
   exactly where to look. They help you find, and be found.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Teach one position word at a time (start with **on** and **under**),
   matching the word to the action before adding more.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to describe positions from another person's point of
   view ("beside me" vs. "beside you") and to give two-word directions ("under, then
   over").

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner place an object over, under, or beside
  another and say the position word correctly?
- **Performance:** The learner acts out positions with their body while naming them.

## Home connection

At home, play "where is it?" with a grown-up: hide a small thing and tell each other
its position — over, under, or beside — so the other can find it.
