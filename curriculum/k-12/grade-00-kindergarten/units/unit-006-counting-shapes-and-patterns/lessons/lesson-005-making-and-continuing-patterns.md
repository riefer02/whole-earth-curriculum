# Lesson 5 — Making and Continuing Patterns

## Summary

Learners move from *seeing* patterns to *making* them. They build simple repeating
patterns with objects and with their voices and bodies, and practice continuing a
pattern by answering the big question: "what comes next?"

## Objectives

- Continue or copy a simple repeating pattern of colors, shapes, or sounds.
  _(D05.S2.K.01)_

## Connection

A weaver does not think about every single thread — she knows the pattern and keeps it
going, over-under-over-under. A drummer keeps the beat going, and a dancer keeps the
steps going. When you know a pattern, you can keep it going and know what comes next.
Today you will be the one who makes the pattern and keeps it alive.

## Materials

- About 20 objects in two or three kinds or colors (two kinds of seeds, two colors of
  stones, blocks in two shapes)
- A line or strip to build along

## Preparation

- Give each learner or pair a collection of objects in at least two kinds/colors and a
  strip or line to build on.
- Clear space for sound-and-movement patterns.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Building and extending a simple
repeating pattern (like AB or AAB) is the target skill here; keep the patterns simple
and let learners work with two, and then three, elements at their own pace. The
magic question is always "what comes next?" — asked with curiosity, never as a trick.
Learners will make "mistakes" (two reds in a row, a skipped element); ask them to
read their pattern aloud ("red-blue-red-blue...") and let the rhythm reveal the fix —
do not rush to correct. Sound and movement patterns are fully equal to object
patterns and are a gift for learners who learn best with their bodies and ears.
Learners who are blind or low-vision can build patterns by feel (smooth-bumpy-smooth)
and by sound; learners who are deaf or hard of hearing can build visual patterns and
feel a drumbeat through a surface. Learners with limited hand or body movement build
patterns by voice or sign — saying the sequence aloud while a partner lays each piece,
or choosing each next piece with eye-gaze or a mouth stick. Every learner's pace is
honored, and no one's
pattern is "better" — a correct pattern made slowly is just as true as a fast one.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** You already know a pattern repeats. Today you will be the
   pattern maker.
2. **Copy a pattern (10 min).** Watch a friend make a tiny pattern: red, blue, red,
   blue. Now copy it with your own objects. Copying is how we learn a pattern by
   heart.
3. **Make your own (15 min).** Build a pattern with two kinds of things. Say it aloud
   as you lay it: "leaf, stone, leaf, stone." Or make a sound pattern: clap, stomp,
   clap, stomp.
4. **Continue — what comes next? (10 min).** Look at your pattern. Cover the last
   piece. Ask yourself: what comes next? Lay it down, then check. Now try a friend's
   pattern and keep it going for them.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: a pattern is alive when someone keeps it going. You
   can always ask "what comes next?" and find the answer by reading the pattern.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Start by copying a two-piece repeating pattern made by the facilitator,
  saying each piece aloud together, before the learner makes their own.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to build a three-element pattern (red-blue-yellow,
  red-blue-yellow) and to leave a gap in the middle for a partner to fill.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner continue a simple repeating pattern by
  placing the correct next piece?
- **Performance:** The learner's built pattern, checked for a clear repeat that they
  can say aloud.

## Home connection

At home, make a pattern with two kinds of things (spoons and cups, or two colors of
anything), say it aloud, and ask a grown-up "what comes next?"
