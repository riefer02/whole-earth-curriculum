# Lesson 2 — Counting to Ten, Then Twenty

## Summary

Learners practice counting to ten, then stretch onward to twenty — the "teen"
numbers — using their bodies and real objects. They discover that ten fingers plus
ten toes make twenty, so the whole body can be a counting tool.

## Objectives

- Count objects up to twenty and say how many there are. _(D05.S1.K.01)_

## Connection

Ten is a number you carry with you: ten fingers. Twenty is two sets of ten: your ten
fingers and your ten toes. Long before number marks were written down, people in many
parts of the world counted on their bodies — fingers, toes, and other parts — and
many still do. Counting to twenty is just counting to ten, and then ten more.

## Materials

- A collection of about 20 small objects (pebbles, seeds, buttons)
- Two cups or small bowls

## Preparation

- Give each learner or pair a collection of about 20 objects and two cups.
- Clear space to move and count aloud.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Counting to twenty is a bigger
stretch than many five-year-olds find easy, and the "teen" numbers (eleven through
nineteen) are irregular in many languages — they take time. Keep the tone playful and
unhurried; **there is no such thing as "too slow" at counting**. Count in the home
language, since number words differ across languages and all of them are correct. Some
learners count on their fingers and toes as a physical anchor; others prefer objects
or a spoken chant — honor every method. For learners who are deaf or hard of hearing,
count in sign or by touching each object; for learners who are blind or low-vision,
count by moving objects from one cup to another so the number grows by feel and sound.
Learners with limited hand or body movement count by voice or sign, directing a
partner to move each object, or indicating each one with eye-gaze or a mouth stick
while the partner counts it.
Do not rank learners by how high they can count; mastery arrives at its own pace for
each child.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Hold up your two hands. Count your fingers: one to ten. Now
   wiggle your toes — there are ten more. Ten fingers and ten toes make **twenty**.
2. **Count to ten (10 min).** Count your objects up to ten, touching each one. Put
   those ten into one cup. Say "ten."
3. **Count on to twenty (15 min).** Now keep going with the rest of your objects.
   Count on from ten: eleven, twelve, thirteen ... all the way to twenty. Put the
   second group into the second cup. How many are there now? Twenty.
4. **Body count game (10 min).** With a friend, take turns: one of you says a number
   from one to twenty, and the other points to that many things — fingers, toes,
   objects, or claps. Check each other, kindly.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: twenty is just ten and ten more. Your own body is a
   counting tool you carry everywhere, for your whole life.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Focus only on counting to ten today, then begin the teen numbers one
  or two at a time, echoing each word.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to count backward from ten, or to count twenty
  objects and then count them again starting from a different object to see the total
  stays the same.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Does the learner count aloud to twenty with one number
  for each object, including the teen numbers?
- **Formative (oral):** Can the learner say the total after counting a group of up to
  twenty objects?

## Home connection

At home, count your fingers and toes with a grown-up — that is twenty. Then count
twenty small things you can find: beans, stones, buttons, or steps.
