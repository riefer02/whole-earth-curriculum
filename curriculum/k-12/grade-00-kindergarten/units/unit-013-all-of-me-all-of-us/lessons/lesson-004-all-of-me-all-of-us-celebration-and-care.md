# Lesson 4 — All of Me, All of Us — Celebration & Care

## Summary

This capstone closes the year. Each child shares a piece of their own work; the class
celebrates how everyone is unique and how everyone belongs together; and together they
care for their shared space. Joy, not competition; belonging, not ranking.

## Objectives

- Describe a feeling using words, a drawing, or a movement. _(D01.S1.K.02)_
- Name the groups to which they belong (family, class, community). _(D01.S2.K.02)_
- Name a community they belong to and one thing people do together in it.
  _(D02.S3.K.01)_
- Help a classmate or care for a shared space when shown how. _(D02.S4.K.01)_

## Connection

You are one of a kind, and you are also part of the whole — your family, your class,
your community. Today we celebrate all of you and all of us: every one's work, and
the care that keeps our shared space good for everyone. A celebration is something
people do together, in every community, everywhere in the world.

## Materials

- Each learner's portfolio of work from the year
- A shared celebration space (a table, rug, or wall)
- Simple decorations (paper, ribbon, flowers, or found objects)

## Preparation

- Lay out each learner's portfolio so they can find their favorite piece.
- Prepare a shared space to display everyone's work together — one wall, table, or
  rug where all pieces fit.
- Gather simple decorations for the space.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). This is a celebration, not a
competition: there are no "best work" awards, no winners and no ranking. Every
child's work is displayed and honored equally, and every family shape is honored.
Sharing is invited, never forced: a child who prefers not to take a turn may simply
hang their piece alongside everyone else's, and that counts as sharing. The caring
step matters as much as the sharing — model it and let children take the lead in
tidying and caring for the shared space. Keep the tone joyful, warm, and inclusive.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Come to the celebration space. Today is our closing day — the
   day we celebrate all of you and all of us.
2. **Choose your work (5 min).** Look through your portfolio. Choose one piece of your
   work from this year that you want to share.
3. **Make the space beautiful together (10 min).** Help decorate the shared space with
   everyone's help. Hang or place the work where it can all be seen together — every
   piece has a place, and no piece is left out.
4. **Share (15 min).** One by one, share your piece with the class. You can say what
   it shows, or point to it while a friend or grown-up says the words with you. Show
   one feeling it brings (a word, a face, or a movement) and one group you belong to
   (a word, or a point to a picture). Speaking is one way, not the only way. Everyone's
   turn matters; everyone is listened to.
5. **Care for our shared space (5 min).** Now care for the space together — put
   things back, pick up paper, tidy the area. A shared space stays good when everyone
   helps.
6. **Close (5 min).** Look around at everything we made and shared. We are each
   unique, and we belong together. All of me, all of us — this is worth celebrating.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Let a child point to their piece while a peer or facilitator says the
  words with them; pair children who need support with a helper.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to name one way they want to help their class or
  community next year, and say it as a closing wish.

## Assessment

- **Performance:** Each child shares one piece of work and, in their own way (speaking,
  pointing, gesturing, or with a helper's voice), shows a feeling and a group, and
  takes part in caring for the shared space. No child is required to speak publicly.
- **Portfolio:** The finished display and the learner's collected year of work serve
  as the culminating artifact of the unit.
- **Observation (formative):** Does the child take part in the celebration and join in
  caring for the shared space alongside others?

## Home connection

Tell a grown-up about the celebration: the piece of work you shared, the feeling it
brought, and one group you belong to. Ask them how their family or community
celebrates together.
