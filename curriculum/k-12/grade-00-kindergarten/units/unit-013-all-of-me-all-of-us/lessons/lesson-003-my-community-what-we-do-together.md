# Lesson 3 — My Community, What We Do Together

## Summary

Learners name a community they belong to and one thing people do together in it —
working, helping, celebrating, or caring for a shared place. They notice that
communities everywhere are built on people doing things together.

## Objectives

- Name a community they belong to and one thing people do together in it.
  _(D02.S3.K.01)_

## Connection

A community is a group of people who share a place or a purpose. In your community,
people do things together — they work, celebrate, help each other, and care for shared
places. Today you name one community you belong to and one thing people do together in
it.

## Materials

- Paper and crayons
- A large shared sheet or board for a class picture

## Preparation

- Gather picture books or photos of communities near and far, if available.
- Prepare a large shared sheet or board for the class picture of "what we do
  together."
- Arrange a circle for gathering and sharing.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Communities look very different around
the world — a fishing village, a high-rise neighborhood, a mountain village, a city
block, a market square — and none is better than another. Let each child name the community that is
theirs. If a child names a community others have not seen, welcome it with curiosity:
"What do people do together there?" Keep the lens global and egalitarian: we learn
about many ways to live, not one correct way.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Sit comfortably. Think of the place where you live or spend
   your days — the people around you there are a community.
2. **Name your community (10 min).** Say or show the name of a community you belong to
   — you can point, draw, or gesture if speaking is not your way. It can be your
   neighborhood, your village or town, your class, or your family's place. What is one
   thing people do together there?
3. **Act it out (10 min).** Choose one thing people do together in your community —
   planting, cooking, building, singing, celebrating, helping a neighbor — and show it
   with your body. Let others guess what it is.
4. **Draw it (15 min).** Draw your community and the thing people do together in it.
   Add your mark to the big class picture of "what we do together."
5. **Close (5 min).** Look at the class picture. Communities everywhere — near and far
   — are built on people doing things together. That shared doing is what makes a
   community.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer two example communities (class, neighborhood) and model one thing
  people do together in each.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to name a second community and one thing people do
  together there, and to draw both.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name one community they belong to and
  one thing people do together in it?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The community drawing, kept in the learner's portfolio.

## Home connection

Bring your community drawing home and show it to a grown-up. Name the community and
the thing people do together in it. Ask the grown-up: what community do you belong to,
and what do people do together there?
