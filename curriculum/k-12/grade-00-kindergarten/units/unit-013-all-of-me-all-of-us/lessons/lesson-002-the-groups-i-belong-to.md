# Lesson 2 — The Groups I Belong To

## Summary

Learners name the groups they belong to — family, class, and community — and draw
themselves inside each one. They notice that every person is part of more than one
"us," and that each group matters.

## Objectives

- Name the groups to which they belong (family, class, community). _(D01.S2.K.02)_

## Connection

You are one person, and you also belong to many groups — your family, your class, and
your community. Everywhere you go, you are part of more than just yourself. Today you
name the groups that make you part of an "us."

## Materials

- Paper and crayons
- A large sheet of paper for each learner's "me in my groups" picture

## Preparation

- Fold or draw circles on each learner's large sheet — one space for each group
  (family, class, community), with the learner in the middle.
- Arrange a circle for gathering and sharing.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). There is no one "right" family or
community — some children live with grandparents, aunts and uncles, one parent,
several generations together under one roof, or chosen family; some belong to a faith
community, a neighborhood, a sports team, a market, or a language group. Honor every family shape and every community equally; never rank one
as better than another. Let each child name the groups that are true for them.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Sit comfortably. Think: who is "us" for you? Who are the people
   you are with, in different places?
2. **Name your groups (10 min).** Think of the groups you belong to: your family, your
   class, your community. Say each one aloud, or point, draw, or gesture to show it.
   Who is in it with you?
3. **Draw yourself in (15 min).** In the middle of your page, draw yourself. Around
   yourself, draw a circle for each group you belong to, and draw or scribble the
   people in it. You can use one circle for family, one for class, one for community —
   and add any other group that is yours.
4. **Share (10 min).** Share your picture with a partner. Point to each circle and
   name the group and one person in it.
5. **Close (5 min).** Look around. Everyone here belongs to more than one group. You
   are in many "us-es" — and each one is a place where you belong.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Start with just two groups (family and class), and model naming one
  person in each.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to add a circle for a group they hope to belong to or
  a new friend they want in one of their groups.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name at least one group they belong to
  and point to a person in it?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The "me in my groups" picture, kept in the learner's
  portfolio.

## Home connection

Bring your "me in my groups" picture home and show it to a grown-up. Point to each
circle and name the group. Ask the grown-up: what groups do you belong to?
