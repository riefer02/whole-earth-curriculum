# Lesson 1 — My Feelings This Year

## Summary

Learners look back over the year and remember the feelings they felt — happy, sad,
proud, scared, calm, and more. They choose one feeling from the year and describe it
using a word, a drawing, or a movement, noticing how much they have grown.

## Objectives

- Describe a feeling using words, a drawing, or a movement. _(D01.S1.K.02)_

## Connection

Everyone feels many feelings in a year — happy, sad, proud, scared, calm, and more.
Today you look back at your year and tell how you felt, using a word, a drawing, or a
movement. Remembering your feelings helps you see how much you have grown.

## Materials

- Feeling face cards (happy, sad, angry, scared, calm)
- Crayons and paper
- Your portfolio of work from this year

## Preparation

- Gather the feeling face cards used earlier in the year.
- Have each learner's portfolio (or a small pile of their own work) ready to look
  back through.
- Arrange a circle for gathering and sharing.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). This is a reflective close to the
year, not a test. Help each learner recall feelings from across the year — their own,
not a "correct" answer. Every feeling is welcome; there are no right or wrong
feelings. Honor that families and cultures express feelings differently, and let each
child show their feeling in the way that feels natural to them.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Sit comfortably. Think back over your year — all the days from
   the start of school until now.
2. **Feelings memory walk (10 min).** Look at each feeling face card. Make each face
   together. Which feelings do you remember feeling this year? Happy? Sad? Proud?
   Scared? Calm? Point or name each one you remember.
3. **Choose one feeling (5 min).** Pick one feeling you had this year. Think of the
   time you felt it. What happened? Where were you? Who was with you?
4. **Show it (15 min).** Describe your feeling in one of three ways: a word, a
   drawing, or a movement. You can draw the time you felt it, or act the feeling out
   with your whole body.
5. **Share (5 min).** Share with a partner or the class. Say or show the feeling and,
   if you like, the memory that goes with it.
6. **Close (5 min).** Feelings come and go, and they change. You felt many feelings
   this year — and you are still growing.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer two feeling faces to choose from, and model a word and a gesture
  to go with each.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to draw two feelings from the year and tell what
  changed between them.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name a feeling they felt this year and
  describe it with a word, drawing, or movement?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The feeling drawing or a photo of the movement, kept in
  the learner's portfolio as a record of the year.
