# Lesson 11 — My Neighborhood

## Summary

Learners widen the circle once more — from family and class to the neighborhood. They
name their neighborhood as a community they belong to and one thing people do
together there, and see that neighborhoods everywhere are places where people share
space and help each other.

## Objectives

- Name their neighborhood as a community they belong to and one thing people do
  together in it. _(D02.S3.K.01)_

## Connection

Your neighborhood is a bigger community than your family or class — the streets,
buildings, fields, and people around your home. People in a neighborhood share a place
and do things together: buying food at a market, drawing water, gathering to talk,
caring for a shared path or well. Whether your neighborhood is a city block, a
village, or a few homes along a road, it is a place where people belong to each other.

## Materials

- Paper and crayons
- One large shared paper for a class map of the neighborhood

## Preparation

- Prepare the shared paper for a group map.
- Think of neighborhood places to prompt memory: home, market, water source, school,
  meeting place, field, path.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. As facilitator, keep "neighborhood" concrete and local — the
places a learner actually sees. Honor every kind of neighborhood equally: dense city
streets, rural roads, coastal villages, mountain hamlets. None is the default; all are
real communities.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think about the place around your home — the paths, buildings,
   fields, or streets. That is your neighborhood.
2. **Places and people (10 min).** Name the places in a neighborhood: homes, a
   market, a school, a well or tap, a meeting tree, a field, a path. Who do you see
   there?
3. **What people do together (15 min).** In your neighborhood, what do people do
   together? (Buy and sell food, fetch water, play, worship or gather, fix a shared
   path, watch over each other's homes.) Tell a partner one thing.
4. **Map it (10 min).** On the shared paper, draw one neighborhood place and add it
   to our map. Look — together we have drawn our whole neighborhood.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: your neighborhood is your community. You belong to
   the places and people around you.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer picture cards of neighborhood places to point to instead of
  naming.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to name one way people in the neighborhood help each
  other, and explain.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name their neighborhood and one thing
  people do together in it?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The shared neighborhood map (or a photo of it).

## Home connection

On your way home, notice one place people in your neighborhood share. Tell a grown-up
what people do together there.
