# Lesson 2 — Sharing Our Things

## Summary

Learners move from taking turns to sharing — dividing what they have so another
person also has some. They practice fair sharing with real objects and close by
noticing that sharing is a way of caring for the whole group, not a contest.

## Objectives

- Share materials during play with a gentle reminder. _(D02.S1.K.01)_

## Connection

Sharing is letting someone else use or enjoy a little of what you have — and
sometimes they share with you in return. When everyone shares a little, everyone has
enough, whether you are splitting crayons at a table or bread at a family meal.
Sharing is caring made into a habit.

## Materials

- Objects to split (crayons, blocks, or snacks)
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Prepare a pile of objects that can be divided evenly into two or more shares.
- Arrange pairs or small groups so each has a pile to share.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. As facilitator, keep sharing voluntary and warm — never force
a child to hand over a beloved object; offer an object that is "ours to share." The
aim is the feeling of fairness and enoughness, not obedience.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Look at a pile of crayons. What if one learner took them all?
   Others would have none. What could we do instead? Share.
2. **Split it fair (10 min).** With a partner, split a pile of objects into two
   shares — one for you, one for your friend. Count: do you both have the same? Now
   everyone has some.
3. **Share in play (15 min).** Play with your shared materials. If your friend wants
   one of your crayons, try saying "yes, we can share." If you want one of theirs,
   ask kindly. A gentle reminder helps when we forget.
4. **Draw it (10 min).** Draw a picture of you and a friend sharing — a snack, a toy,
   or a game. Show what each of you has.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: when we share, everyone has enough. That feels good
   for the whole group.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Begin with a pile of exactly two objects so sharing is one for me, one
  for you.
- **Extension:** Give an uneven pile and ask learners to make the shares fair, then
  explain how they did it.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Does the learner share materials with a peer, with at
  most a gentle reminder?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The "sharing" drawing.

At your next family meal, help share something out — slices of fruit, cups of water,
or a serving of rice — so everyone gets a fair share.
