# Lesson 3 — Asking for a Turn with Kind Words

## Summary

Learners learn that grabbing gets one thing, while kind words build friendship. They
practice asking for a turn with "please" and "may I," and see how kind words change
the way asking and waiting feel.

## Objectives

- Take turns during play with a gentle reminder, using kind words to ask. _(D02.S1.K.01)_

## Connection

When you want a toy someone else is using, you can grab — or you can ask with kind
words. "Please, may I have a turn?" sounds very different from a snatch, and it makes
the other person much more likely to say yes. Kind words are a small tool that makes
asking fair and friendly.

## Materials

- A few popular toys or objects
- A "kind words" poster or cards (please, may I, thank you)

## Preparation

- Choose a couple of objects everyone will want.
- Post or hold the "kind words" cards where all can see them.
- Arrange pairs for practice.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. As facilitator, model the words warmly and often, and praise
the asking — not just the outcome. If a learner says no to a request, honor that too:
asking with kindness does not force a yes, and both voices matter. Every language and
culture has its own kind words — invite learners to share how they ask kindly in the
languages they speak at home, so the skill, not the English phrase, is the point.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of a time you really wanted a toy someone else had.
   What did you do? What could you say instead of grabbing?
2. **Meet the kind words (10 min).** Learn two phrases: "please" and "may I have a
   turn?" Say them together. Listen to how gentle they sound.
3. **Practice asking (15 min).** One partner holds a toy. The other asks: "Please,
   may I have a turn?" The first partner says "yes" and passes it. Then switch. Say
   "thank you" when you receive it.
4. **Try it for real (10 min).** Use the kind words when you actually want a turn
   during play. If you forget, a friend gives a gentle reminder: "ask with kind
   words."
5. **Close (5 min).** Talk: how did asking feel? How did being asked feel? Remember —
   kind words turn asking into a gift.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Practice with the facilitator or an older learner first, then with a
  peer.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to think of other kind words (for example, "after
  you") and try them out.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Does the learner use "please" or "may I" when asking
  for a turn, with only a gentle reminder?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** A drawing of two learners asking and sharing kindly.

around you answer when you ask kindly.
