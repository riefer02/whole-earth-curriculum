# Lesson 8 — Being a Good Listener

## Summary

Learners practice giving a friend their full attention — each in the way their own
body listens best — and learn that listening is how we come to understand what a
friend feels and needs.

## Objectives

- Describe how a friend might feel by listening carefully to them. _(D02.S2.K.01)_

## Connection

Listening is how we learn what is inside another person — their joy, their worry,
their need. When you stop, give your attention, and let a friend finish speaking, you
give them a gift: the feeling of being heard. Good listening is kindness you do with
your whole self.

## Materials

- A "talking object" (a shell, stick, or ball)
- Feeling face cards

## Preparation

- Choose a talking object to mark whose turn it is to speak.
- Arrange pairs and a circle.
- Have feeling face cards ready for guessing.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. As facilitator, model attention yourself and narrate it: "I am
turning toward you and giving you my attention; I will let you finish." Say the
important part aloud: different bodies listen differently. Some keep their eyes on
the speaker, some look away or close their eyes to hear better; some keep their hands
still, some need to move a little. No one way is more correct — what matters is being
present and letting the speaker finish. Never insist on eye contact or a fixed
posture: in some cultures and for some autistic learners, looking away *is* listening.
For learners who listen with their eyes (sign language or lip-reading), "listening"
means watching the speaker's face and hands — honor that as full listening too. Keep
speaking turns short at this age — one sentence is plenty.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** What does it feel like when someone really listens to you?
   What does it feel like when they don't? Today we learn to be the kind who listens.
2. **What listening looks like for you (10 min).** A good listener gives a friend
   their full attention. Different bodies listen differently: some keep their eyes on
   the speaker, some look away or close their eyes to hear better; some keep their
   hands still, some move a little. What matters is: be present, don't interrupt, and
   let them finish. Find the listening body that works for you.
3. **One turn at a time (10 min).** Pass the talking object. Only the one holding
   it speaks or signs; everyone else listens (with ears or eyes). Say or sign one true
   thing: "Today I feel…"
4. **Listen and guess (15 min).** As your partner speaks, give them your attention —
   watch their face and body, or just listen with your ears, whichever helps you.
   After, point to a feeling card or say: "I think you feel happy." Ask them — were
   you right? Then swap.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: listening is how we understand a friend's heart. Give
   your attention, let them finish, and show you heard. That is the gift.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Practice listening for a very short turn (one word or a wave) before
  longer turns.
- **Extension:** After listening, learners retell one thing their partner said, to
  show they heard it.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner give a speaking partner their
  attention (in any posture) and name how that partner felt?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** A drawing of "a good listener" — however the learner
  pictures listening.

## Home connection

At home, ask someone "how are you feeling?" and practice listening with your whole
self. Then tell them what you heard.
