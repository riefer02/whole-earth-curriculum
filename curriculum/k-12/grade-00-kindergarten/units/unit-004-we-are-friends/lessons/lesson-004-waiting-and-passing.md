# Lesson 4 — Waiting and Passing

## Summary

Learners practice waiting and passing — the engine that keeps turn-taking and sharing
going. By passing one object around a group in rhythm, they learn that waiting is not
doing nothing; it is how we hold the game open for everyone.

## Objectives

- Take turns during play with a gentle reminder, waiting and passing without grabbing.
  _(D02.S1.K.01)_

## Connection

Waiting is the quiet part of every game — the moment before your turn, the pause while
someone else goes. It is not nothing; it is the part that keeps the game fair for
everyone. When you wait and then pass the thing along, you keep the play going for the
whole circle.

## Materials

- A ball or beanbag to pass
- A simple rhythm instrument or clapping pattern

## Preparation

- Arrange a circle.
- Choose a slow, steady chant or clapping pattern to mark the beat while waiting.
- Have the passing object ready.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. As facilitator, keep the beat slow and steady — a calm rhythm
helps young bodies wait. If a learner grabs, stay warm: a gentle reminder and a smile
return the game to its rhythm. This lesson closes Strand S1; celebrate how far the
learners have come in taking turns and sharing.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Sit in a circle. We will pass one thing all the way around.
   To do it, everyone must wait and everyone must pass.
2. **Find the beat (5 min).** Clap a slow beat together: clap … clap … clap. This
   beat will help us wait.
3. **Pass it around (15 min).** Pass the ball to the beat. When it reaches you, hold
   it for one beat, then pass it on. Your hands are patient; the ball keeps moving.
4. **Slow it down (10 min).** Try passing even more slowly. How does it feel to wait
   longer? What does your body do while you wait? Breathe, watch, and stay ready.
5. **Close (10 min).** Talk: was waiting hard or easy? What helped you wait?
   Remember — waiting and passing keep the play fair for everyone.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Pass quickly at first, then gradually slow the beat as learners grow
  comfortable.
- **Extension:** Pass two objects at once in opposite directions and see if the
  group can keep both moving.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner wait for the object and pass it along
  without grabbing, with only a gentle reminder?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** A note or drawing about what waiting feels like.

## Home connection

Play a passing game at home — a ball, a cup, or a song where each person adds a word.
Feel how waiting keeps the game alive for everyone.
