# Lesson 14 — We Are Friends

## Summary

The capstone brings the whole unit together. Learners recall the four friendship
skills they have practiced — taking turns and sharing, reading feelings, belonging to
communities, and helping — and weave them into one shared "we are friends" creation.

## Objectives

- Take turns and share during play. _(D02.S1.K.01)_
- Describe how a friend might feel. _(D02.S2.K.01)_
- Name a community they belong to and something people do together in it.
  _(D02.S3.K.01)_
- Help a classmate or care for a shared space. _(D02.S4.K.01)_

## Connection

Being a friend is not one big thing — it is many small things practiced every day:
sharing a turn, noticing a feeling, belonging together, lending a hand. When you put
them all together, you get something wonderful: people who live well with each other.
That is what "we are friends" means.

## Materials

- One large shared paper or banner
- Paper and crayons
- A shared object for a passing game

## Preparation

- Prepare the shared banner and drawing materials.
- Gather any photos from across the unit.
- Choose a passing game and a closing song.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. As facilitator, this is a celebration, not a test. Let learners
show what they remember in their own way — words, pictures, movements — and affirm
that friendship is practice, not perfection. Keep the four skills simple and let the
shared creation hold the meaning.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** We have spent many days learning to be friends. Today we put
   it all together.
2. **Remember the four skills (10 min).** A friend: **shares and takes turns**,
   **notices feelings**, **belongs to a community**, and **helps**. For each, name
   one thing you have done.
3. **Play it (10 min).** Play one passing game together, remembering to take turns
   and wait. Notice a friend's happy face as the game goes round.
4. **Make "we are friends" (15 min).** On the shared banner, each learner adds a
   mark or picture for one friendship skill. Say your part as you add it: "I share."
   "I notice feelings." "I belong." "I help."
5. **Close (5 min).** Stand together and look at what we made. Many hands, one
   picture — like many friends, one caring group. We are friends.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer picture cards of the four skills to point to instead of naming.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to make a small "how to be a friend" book showing
  all four skills with an example of each.

## Assessment

- **Summative (observation):** Can the learner show each friendship skill — taking a
  turn, naming a feeling, naming a community, helping — in word or action?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The shared "we are friends" banner (or a photo of it) and
  each learner's contribution.

With your family, make a small "friendship circle" — each person draws or names one
way they share, notice feelings, belong, or help. Put them together.
