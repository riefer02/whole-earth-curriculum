# Lesson 12 — Helping Hands

## Summary

Learners learn that helping is something you can *do*, not just feel — and that their
own hands can help a classmate. They practice a few helping jobs and carry out one
real act of help.

## Objectives

- Help a classmate when shown how. _(D02.S4.K.01)_

## Connection

Sometimes a friend needs a hand — a coat that won't zip, blocks that spilled, a
pencil out of reach. Helping is caring made visible: your hands do what your heart
wants. And helping is a skill you can learn, one small act at a time.

## Materials

- Simple classroom tasks to practice (zipping, picking up, passing)
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Choose two or three real helping jobs learners can practice and then actually do.
- Prepare the "helping hands" chart if using one.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. As facilitator, model the helping first ("watch, then try")
and celebrate the *trying*, not the result. Keep helping voluntary and mutual —
helping is something we offer each other as equals, not a favor from above.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Look at your hands. They can do many things — including help
   a friend. Today we will learn how.
2. **Watch and learn (10 min).** I will show you a helping job: zipping a coat,
   picking up spilled blocks, passing a pencil. Watch first, then try it with a
   partner.
3. **Practice helping (15 min).** Take turns being the helper and the helped. Show
   your partner how, then let them show you. Helping is a skill — we practice it
   together.
4. **Do one real help (10 min).** Look around the room. Choose one real way to help a
   classmate and do it. If you are not sure how, ask: "Can I help?" and learn.
5. **Close (5 min).** Talk: how did helping feel? How did being helped feel?
   Remember — your hands can help a friend.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Pair with an older buddy and practice one simple job at a time.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to notice a need *before* being asked and offer help
  on their own.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner help a classmate when shown how?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** A drawing of "my hands helping a friend."

## Home connection

At home, find one thing you can help with — setting out cups, carrying something,
holding a door — and do it. Tell a grown-up how it felt.
