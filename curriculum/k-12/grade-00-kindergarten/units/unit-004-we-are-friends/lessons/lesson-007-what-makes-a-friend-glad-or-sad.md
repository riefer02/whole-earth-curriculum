# Lesson 7 — What Makes a Friend Glad or Sad

## Summary

Learners connect actions to feelings: kind actions make friends glad, hurtful ones
make them sad. By sorting small situations, they discover that what they do has real
power over how another person feels.

## Objectives

- Describe how a friend might feel in a simple situation. _(D02.S2.K.01)_

## Connection

Every day, small things change how your friends feel. A shared snack makes someone
glad; a knocked-over tower makes someone sad; a pushed friend feels hurt. When you
notice which is which, you begin to choose the kind thing — and that choice is yours
to make.

## Materials

- Two small baskets or boxes (glad, sad)
- Picture cards of small situations

## Preparation

- Label the two baskets "glad" and "sad."
- Prepare situation cards: sharing, hugging, helping, a fallen tower, a pushed
  friend, a kind word.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. As facilitator, keep the situations simple and free of blame —
the aim is noticing, not shaming. Affirm that all people sometimes make friends sad
by accident, and the caring thing is to notice and try again.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think: what is one thing that made you glad today? One thing
   that made you sad? Our actions do this to others too.
2. **Meet glad and sad (5 min).** Here are two baskets: glad and sad. We will sort
   small happenings into them.
3. **Sort the situations (15 min).** For each card, ask: would this make a friend
   glad or sad? Put it in the right basket. A shared snack — glad. A pushed friend —
   sad. Talk about why.
4. **I can change it (10 min).** Choose one "sad" card. What could change it to
   "glad"? (A fallen tower — a friend helps build it again.) Move it to glad.
5. **Close (10 min).** Remember: your choices reach other people's hearts. Kind
   actions make friends glad; hurtful ones make them sad. You get to choose.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use only two contrasting cards at first (a hug, a push) and sort
  together.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to invent a new situation and decide which basket it
  belongs in, then explain.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner say whether a simple situation would
  make a friend glad or sad?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The sorted cards, or a drawing of "one thing that makes a
  friend glad."

At home, notice one thing you do today that makes someone glad. Tell a grown-up what
it was and how you could tell.
