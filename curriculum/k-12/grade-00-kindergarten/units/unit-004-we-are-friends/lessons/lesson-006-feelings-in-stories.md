# Lesson 6 — Feelings in Stories

## Summary

Learners practice reading feelings at one remove: in a story. As they follow a
character through moments of joy, sadness, and fright, they guess what the character
feels and why — the same skill they will use with real friends.

## Objectives

- Describe how a story character might feel in a simple situation. _(D02.S2.K.01)_

## Connection

Story characters feel things just like you do. When the little bird loses its nest,
it feels scared; when it finds it again, it feels glad. Stories from every land let us
practice understanding feelings that are not our own — which is exactly what makes us
kind friends.

## Materials

- A picture book with strong emotional moments
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Choose a story with clear feeling moments.
- Mark two or three places to pause and ask "how does the character feel?"

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. As facilitator, choose a story from any culture — the point is
the feeling, not the setting — and describe the pictures aloud for learners who cannot
see them well. Honor every guess; ask "what makes you think so?" rather than "is that
right?"

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** We will read a story and play "feeling detective." Our job is
   to guess how the character feels.
2. **Listen and watch (10 min).** As we read, look at the character's face and body
   and listen to the words. What is happening to them?
3. **Pause and guess (15 min).** At each pause, I will ask: "How does the character
   feel?" Guess, and tell me the clue — "their face is droopy," "they are crying,"
   "they are jumping." Is it happy, sad, scared, or angry?
4. **Ask why (10 min).** For one feeling, ask: *why* do they feel that way? What
   happened? That is the story's secret — feelings come from what happens.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: characters show feelings the same way friends do.
   When you read a face, you read a heart.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer a feeling face card to point to at each pause instead of naming
  the feeling.
- **Extension:** Ask learners to retell the story's happiest moment and the feeling
  that goes with it.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name how a story character feels and
  point to a clue?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** A drawing of a story character showing a feeling.
