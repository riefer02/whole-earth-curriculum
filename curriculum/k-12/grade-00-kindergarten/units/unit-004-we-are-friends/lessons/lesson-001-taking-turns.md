# Lesson 1 — Taking Turns

## Summary

Learners share one object by taking turns and practice the small skill underneath it:
waiting. They close by noticing how turn-taking feels fair — everyone gets a chance,
no one is left out.

## Objectives

- Take turns with a shared object during play with a gentle reminder. _(D02.S1.K.01)_

## Connection

When many children want the same swing, ball, or drum, only one can use it at a time.
Taking turns is the way everyone gets a chance — you wait, then you get your go, then
you pass it on. It is fairness you can practice with your own hands.

## Materials

- One shared object (ball, tambourine, or shell)
- A "turn" marker (a stick, card, or pebble)

## Preparation

- Arrange a circle so everyone can see each other.
- Choose one object worth wanting — the point is that waiting is real.
- Prepare the turn marker that will pass hand to hand.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. As facilitator, keep the tone light: waiting is hard at age
five, so celebrate every wait, no matter how short. Give a gentle reminder rather
than a correction — the standard itself asks only for turn-taking *with a gentle
reminder*.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Sit in a circle. Look at the one object in the middle. If
   everyone grabbed it at once, what would happen? We would bump and fuss. Instead,
   we take turns.
2. **Meet the turn marker (5 min).** This marker tells us whose turn it is. Whoever
   holds it is "in." Everyone else waits and watches.
3. **Pass it around (15 min).** The first learner holds the object, enjoys it for a
   moment, then says "your turn" and passes the marker to the next learner. Around
   the circle it goes. You waited — now it is your turn!
4. **Try it in play (10 min).** Now use one toy or one ball in a small group. Play
   together, passing the turn. If you forget, a friend gives a gentle reminder:
   "my turn next."
5. **Close (10 min).** Talk together: how did waiting feel? How did it feel when
   your turn finally came? Remember — when we take turns, everyone gets a chance.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Keep the object with one learner for only a short moment so no one
  waits too long.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to invent their own turn-taking game and teach it to
  a partner.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner wait and then take a turn with only a
  gentle reminder?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** A note or drawing the learner makes about "my turn, your
  turn."

## Home connection

At home, play a game that needs turns — a board game, a ball, a story where each
person tells one part. Notice: everyone gets a chance.
