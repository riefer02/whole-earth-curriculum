# Lesson 9 — My Family Community

## Summary

Learners name their family as their first community and share one thing their family
does together. They see that families come in many shapes — and that what makes a
family is caring and being together, not any single shape.

## Objectives

- Name their family as a community they belong to and one thing people do together in
  it. _(D02.S3.K.01)_

## Connection

A family is the first community most of us ever belong to. Families look different
everywhere — some are big, some are small, some have grandparents or aunts or chosen
people, and no shape is better than another. What families share is this: people who
care for each other and do things together.

## Materials

- Paper and crayons
- One large shared paper for a group "families" picture

## Preparation

- Arrange a circle for sharing.
- Prepare the shared paper for a group drawing.
- Have a gentle, open way to talk about family shapes — some learners live with
  grandparents, aunts, one parent, or caregivers.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. As facilitator, honor every family shape equally — never ask
"where is your mother/father." Use open words: "the people who care for you at home."
Some children may carry hard family stories; let any learner pass, and never rank one
family as more real or complete than another.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think: who are the people who care for you at home? That is
   your family.
2. **Many shapes of family (10 min).** Families are all different. Some are big, some
   small; some have grandparents, aunts, one parent, two, or chosen people. All of
   them care. There is no one right shape.
3. **What we do together (15 min).** What is one thing your family does together?
   (Cooking, telling stories, walking, eating, playing, working side by side.) Tell a
   partner, then share with the group.
4. **Draw it (10 min).** Draw your family doing that thing. Add your picture to the
   big shared paper so all our families are together.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: your family is your first community. You belong there.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer a sentence starter: "My family likes to ___ together."
- **Extension:** Invite learners to name two things their family does together and
  how each one shows caring.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name their family and one thing the
  family does together?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The family drawing.
