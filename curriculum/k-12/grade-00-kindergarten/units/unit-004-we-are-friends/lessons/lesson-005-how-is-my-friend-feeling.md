# Lesson 5 — How Is My Friend Feeling?

## Summary

Learners turn their attention outward: from noticing their own feelings (Unit 1) to
noticing a friend's. They read the clues a face, voice, and body give, and practice
naming what a friend might be feeling.

## Objectives

- Describe how a friend might feel in a simple situation. _(D02.S2.K.01)_

## Connection

Every day, your friends tell you how they feel without saying a word — a smile, a
slumped shoulder, a teary eye, a loud laugh. When you notice those clues, you can
understand your friend and know how to help. Reading feelings is a language everyone
can learn, in every place on Earth.

## Materials

- Feeling face cards (happy, sad, angry, scared, calm)
- A mirror (optional)

## Preparation

- Print or draw a set of feeling face cards.
- Arrange pairs or a circle for the guessing game.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. As facilitator, describe each face aloud as well as showing
it, so learners who cannot see the card can still join. Keep feelings open: a guess
is a guess, and the "right" answer is always "let's ask them."

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Look at a friend near you. What is their face doing right
   now? Can you guess how they feel?
2. **Meet the clue-finders (10 min).** Three places tell us feelings: the face, the
   voice, and the body. For each feeling card, look at the face, hear the voice, and
   watch the body. Describe what you notice: "a smile," "eyes down," "arms crossed."
3. **Guess the feeling (15 min).** One partner makes a feeling with their face and
   body. The other guesses: "I think you feel happy." Then swap. Try sad, angry,
   scared, and calm too.
4. **A simple situation (10 min).** I will tell a tiny story: "A friend's block
   tower falls down." How might that friend feel? Guess, then say what clue you used.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: friends show feelings with face, voice, and body.
   When you notice, you can care.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use only two feeling cards (happy, sad) and pair with an older buddy.
- **Extension:** Ask learners to describe the *clue* they used (eyebrows, shoulders,
  voice) along with the feeling.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name how a friend might feel in a
  simple situation and point to a clue?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** A drawing of a friend showing a feeling, with the feeling
  labeled.

At home, play "guess my feeling" with your family — make a face and let them guess,
then guess theirs.
