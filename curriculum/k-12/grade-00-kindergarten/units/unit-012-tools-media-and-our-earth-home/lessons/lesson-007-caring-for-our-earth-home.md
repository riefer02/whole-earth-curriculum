# Lesson 7 — Caring for Our Earth Home

## Summary

Learners do one real, small act of care for the Earth — watering a plant, turning off
a tap, or putting litter in the right place — and say why it helps. The lesson frames
the Earth as a home to care for, not a thing to use up, and shows that small acts add
up when everyone does their part.

## Objectives

- Do one small act of care for the environment and say why it helps. _(D10.S4.K.01)_

## Connection

The Earth is our home, the way a house is a home. We keep a home clean and cared for,
and we can do the same for the Earth. A plant needs water; a dry tap left running
wastes water other living things need; a piece of litter does not belong on the path.
One small act of care, done by many hands, keeps our home healthy.

## Materials

- Supplies for one small act of care (a watering can or cup, a plant or seed, a bin
  for litter, or a tap to practice turning off)
- Paper and crayons for a "care promise" card

## Preparation

- Choose one or two safe, simple acts of care learners can actually do: water a plant,
  turn off a tap, place litter in a bin, plant a seed.
- Set out the supplies for each act.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). As facilitator, make the act real, not
symbolic — learners should actually water, turn off, or tidy something, with safe,
clean hands. Keep the tone hopeful, never frightening: the Earth is our shared home and
we are its helpers, not its saviors or its enemies. Small acts matter, and no learner's
act is too small. If water is scarce where you are, adapt — turning off a tap or
shading a plant can be the act of care, and is just as real.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Put your hand on the ground (or on the floor). This Earth is
   your home. Homes need care.
2. **Choose your act of care (10 min).** Pick one small act you can really do: water a
   plant, turn off a tap, put a piece of litter in the right place, or plant a seed.
   Say why it helps: "Watering helps the plant grow." "Turning off the tap saves
   water for other living things."
3. **Do it (15 min).** Do your act of care now, with a grown-up or partner nearby.
   If your body makes the doing hard, choose the act and guide a partner's hands
   while you say why it helps. Feel what it is like to help.
4. **Make a care promise (10 min).** Draw a "care promise" card: one small act of care
   you will do again, and why it helps.
5. **Close (5 min).** One small act, done by many people, becomes a big act. When we
   each care a little, the whole Earth home is cared for.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Do the act together hand-over-hand and say the "why" together aloud.
- **Motor differences:** A learner who cannot physically perform the act can still do
  the caring — choose the act, tell a partner or grown-up exactly what to do, and say
  why it helps; the choosing and the saying are the act of care. Adapt the tool too (a
  light cup, a foot-operated tap, a helper's hands).
- **Extension:** Invite learners to name a second act of care they could do at home or
  in their neighborhood, and tell who or what it would help.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Did the learner complete one small act of care and say
  why it helps?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The "care promise" card.

## Home connection

Take your "care promise" card home. Do the act of care with a grown-up — water a
plant, turn off a tap, or put litter in the bin — and tell them why it helps our Earth
home.
