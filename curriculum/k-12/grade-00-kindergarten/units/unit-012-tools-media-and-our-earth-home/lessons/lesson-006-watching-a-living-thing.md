# Lesson 6 — Watching a Living Thing

## Summary

Learners practice the gentle skill of observing: sitting quietly and watching one
living thing — a plant, a bug, a bird, a fish, a pet — and noticing what it does and
what it needs. They record what they see in words or a drawing, learning that looking
closely is a kind of caring.

## Objectives

- Observe and name a living thing and something it needs to live (sun, water, food).
  _(D10.S3.K.01)_

## Connection

There is a secret way to learn about living things: you slow down, get quiet, and
watch. A scientist is first a watcher. When you watch a plant turn toward the sun or a
bird look for food, you learn what it needs and what it is like — just by paying
attention.

## Materials

- A living thing to observe (a plant, a tree, a bug, a bird, a fish, or a pet)
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Choose a living thing that is easy and safe for learners to observe quietly.
- Arrange a comfortable place to sit and watch.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). As facilitator, protect the quiet —
silence is the heart of observation, and young learners may need a little structure to
settle into it. Guide gentle hands and eyes: we watch without harming, and we never
hurt a living thing just to look at it. If a bug is observed, remind learners it is a
guest, not a toy. Whatever living thing is at hand is perfect; there is no "right"
animal to watch.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Find your living thing and get comfortable. Take one slow,
   quiet breath.
2. **Watch (10 min).** Use your senses — eyes, ears, hands, nose. What does the living
   thing do? Does it move, grow, or turn toward the sun? What does it need — sun,
   water, food, air? Watch without harming. If it is safe to touch (a leaf, a stem, a
   piece of bark), feel it gently; if you cannot see it, listen for its sounds and
   smell what is near it.
3. **Notice and name (10 min).** Say three things you noticed. "It is green. It reaches
   for the sun. It drinks water from the ground." Name one thing it needs to live.
4. **Draw what you saw (15 min).** Draw your living thing and one thing it needs — draw
   the sun above it, the water beside it, or the food it eats.
5. **Close (5 min).** When we watch a living thing quietly, we get to know it — and
   knowing it helps us care for it. What did you learn that you did not know before?

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Observe together and name just one thing the living thing does and one
  thing it needs; draw together.
- **Low vision / blind:** Observe through touch (a leaf, a stem, bark), sound (a bird
  call, leaves rustling), and smell (a crushed leaf, damp soil); a partner or
  facilitator describes what they see as the learner feels and listens.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to watch the same living thing again tomorrow and tell
  one thing that is the same and one thing that changed.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name the living thing and one thing it
  needs, based on what they watched?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The observation drawing, kept in the learner's portfolio.

## Home connection

At home, pick one living thing — a plant on the windowsill, a pet, a tree outside —
and watch it quietly for a little while. Tell a grown-up one thing you noticed and one
thing it needs.
