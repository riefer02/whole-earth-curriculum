# Lesson 3 — Real or Make-Believe?

## Summary

Learners practice telling real things from make-believe ones by holding a real object
and sorting picture cards into "real" and "make-believe" piles. The lesson is gentle
and joyful: make-believe is a treasure to love, not a trick to fear — we just learn to
notice which is which.

## Objectives

- Tell the difference between a real thing they see and a made-up picture or story.
  _(D10.S2.K.01)_

## Connection

Some things are real — you can see them, touch them, and hold them, like a leaf or a
stone or a spoon. Some things are make-believe — a dragon, a unicorn, a talking moon —
wonderful and fun, but not real. Both kinds are good. We just learn to notice which is
which.

## Materials

- A set of "real" and "make-believe" picture cards
- Two sorting baskets or hoops labeled "real" and "make-believe"
- A real leaf, stone, or spoon to hold

## Preparation

- Make or gather picture cards: real things (dog, tree, cup, leaf) and make-believe
  things (dragon, unicorn, talking moon).
- Label two baskets or hoops "real" and "make-believe."

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). As facilitator, keep it warm and
wonder-full, never cynical. Make-believe is not "bad" or "a lie" — it is pretend, and
pretend is one of the great gifts of being human. The goal at age five is a soft
distinction, not suspicion. If a learner insists a dragon could be real, honor the
wonder: "Some people tell stories about dragons — what a wonderful story!" Then gently
return to "a real thing is one I can see and touch." Do not shame any media or screen;
frame it as noticing, not judging.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Hold the real leaf, stone, or spoon in your hand. This is real —
   you can see it and touch it.
2. **Meet make-believe (5 min).** Now think of a dragon or a talking moon. It is
   wonderful — and it is make-believe. You cannot touch it. Pretend is fun, and it is
   not real.
3. **Sort the cards (15 min).** Look at each card. Ask: "Can I see and touch this in
   the real world, or is it made up?" Drop each card into the "real" basket or the
   "make-believe" basket.
4. **Real or make-believe voices (10 min).** Say one real sentence ("I saw a bird") and
   one pretend sentence ("The bird sang me a song about the moon"). Which is which?
   How do you know?
5. **Draw one of each (5 min).** Draw one real thing and one make-believe thing.
6. **Close (5 min).** Real and make-believe are both treasures. Real is what we can see
   and touch; make-believe is what we imagine — and both can live happily in the same
   story, as long as we know which is which.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Sort just two cards — one real, one make-believe — and hold the real
  object while deciding.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to make up a make-believe creature and tell one real
  thing about it (it has fur, it breathes) and one pretend thing (it breathes fire).

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner place a real card and a make-believe
  card in the correct pile and say why?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The "one real, one make-believe" drawing.

## Home connection

At home, find one real thing you can touch and think of one make-believe thing from a
story. Tell a grown-up which is which and how you know. Ask them to tell you a
make-believe story they loved as a child.
