# Lesson 4 — Stories Real and Pretend

## Summary

Learners hear two short stories — one true, one pretend — and learn to tell them
apart. They signal "real" or "pretend" and say why, then create one of each. Real and
pretend stories are both treasures; this lesson is about noticing the difference, not
ranking them.

## Objectives

- Tell the difference between a real thing they see and a made-up picture or story.
  _(D10.S2.K.01)_

## Connection

People tell stories every day. Some stories are true — they tell about things that
really happened, like "yesterday I saw a bird on the fence." Some stories are pretend —
they tell about talking animals and flying houses and magic. Both are wonderful. We
just learn to listen and notice: did this really happen, or is it made up?

## Materials

- Two short stories to read or tell aloud (one true, one pretend)
- "Real" and "Pretend" picture cards to signal with

## Preparation

- Choose or make up a short true story ("This morning I drank water from a cup") and a
  short pretend story ("A mouse put on a tiny coat and sang a song").
- Set out the "real" and "pretend" signal cards.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). As facilitator, tell the two stories
with warmth and delight — pretend stories are a joy, not a trap. Keep the distinction
soft: a true story is about something that really happened; a pretend story is made up.
If a learner asks whether a favorite tale is "true," say gently: "It is a wonderful
story — it is make-believe, and that is one reason we love it." No story, screen, or
culture is being judged here; we are only practicing noticing. If a learner names a
story their family holds as true — a sacred story or a tale of their people — honor it
and do not call it make-believe; gently steer the practice back to clearly fictional
examples like talking animals and magic. Noticing is the goal, never ranking what
another family holds dear.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of a story you love. Is it about something that really
   happened, or something made up?
2. **Hear two stories (15 min).** Listen to Story One. Then listen to Story Two. Which
   one is true — about something real that happened? Which one is pretend — made up,
   with talking animals or magic? Hold up your "real" or "pretend" card and say why.
3. **How do we know? (10 min).** A true story can be checked — you could see it or ask
   someone who was there. A pretend story cannot happen in the real world — animals do
   not talk, houses do not fly. That is how we tell them apart.
4. **Make up one of each (10 min).** Tell a partner one true story (something that
   really happened to you) and one pretend story. Ask your partner to guess which is
   which.
5. **Close (5 min).** Real and pretend stories are both worth telling. A good listener
   knows which is which — and loves both.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use just the two told stories and a thumbs-up (real) or a wiggle
  (pretend) instead of cards; decide together aloud.
- **Deaf / hard of hearing:** Tell the stories in sign language, acted out, or with
  drawn pictures; use the thumbs-up (real) / wiggle (pretend) signals or sign "real"
  and "pretend." A non-speaking learner can signal, point, gesture, or draw to show
  which is which.
- **Low vision / blind:** The two stories are told aloud, so they can be followed by
  ear; signal "real" and "pretend" with a spoken word, a clap, a thumbs-up, or a
  wiggle instead of holding up a card, and retell the story back by voice.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to tell a partner one true and one pretend story and
  explain one clue that gives each away.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner signal "real" and "pretend" correctly
  for the two stories and give a simple reason?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The learner's own one-real, one-pretend drawing or
  retelling (recorded in words by the facilitator).

## Home connection

Ask a grown-up to tell you one true story from their day and one made-up bedtime tale.
Then tell them which is which and how you know.
