# Lesson 1 — Tools Help Us

## Summary

Learners discover that a tool is something people make to help them do a job. They
hold, name, and act out everyday tools — a spoon, a cup, a broom, a water jug, a
mortar and pestle — and tell what each one helps people do. Along the way they learn
that people everywhere, in every time and place, have made tools.

## Objectives

- Name a tool or device people use and tell what it helps them do. _(D10.S1.K.01)_

## Connection

Think about your hands. There are jobs they cannot do alone — scooping soup, carrying
water, sweeping a floor, pounding grain. People make tools to help their hands, and
every home and every kitchen has some. A spoon, a cup, a broom, a water jug: each one
is a tool that helps.

## Materials

- A "tool bag" or tray of safe everyday tools (spoon, cup, broom, basket, water jug or
  gourd, mortar and pestle if available)
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Gather a set of safe, everyday tools and place them in a bag or on a tray.
- Clear a circle for acting out tool jobs.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). As facilitator, keep the message
gentle and true: tools are wonderful helpers, but they are not magic and they are not
better just because they are new. Honor every tool equally — a stone for pounding and
a spoon for scooping both matter, and people in every place and every time have made
tools for their own hands and their own lives. Avoid presenting any one tool (like a
phone) as the "real" or "modern" tool; a mortar and pestle and a water jug are just as
much tools. Supervise any handling of tools so everyone stays safe.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Look at your hands. What is one job your hands cannot do alone?
2. **What is a tool? (10 min).** A tool is something people make to help them do a
   job. Reach into the tool bag and pull one out. Name it, and tell what it helps
   people do: "A spoon helps us scoop and eat. A broom helps us sweep."
3. **Act it out (10 min).** Choose a tool and act out its job with your body — scoop,
   sweep, carry, pound. Can a partner guess which tool you are using?
4. **Tools everywhere (10 min).** People make tools in every land and every time: a
   water jug to carry water, a mortar and pestle to grind grain, a basket to carry
   food, a loom to weave cloth. Which tools have you seen at home or in a market?
5. **Draw it (5 min).** Draw one tool and the job it helps with.
6. **Close (5 min).** Tools help people. Every person who makes a tool is using their
   head and hands to make a job easier.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer two tools and ask the learner to point to the one that "carries"
  or "scoops," then say the job together.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to draw or name two tools that do the same job in
  different ways (a basket and a bag both carry; a broom and a mop both clean).

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name one tool and tell what it helps
  people do?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The "tool and its job" drawing.

## Home connection

At home, find one tool with a grown-up — a spoon, a jug, a broom, a knife for cooking.
Name it and tell the grown-up what job it helps do. Ask: who first thought to make a
tool like this?
