# Lesson 2 — Tools All Around Me

## Summary

Learners hunt for tools close by and far away. They name the tools people use at home,
in a kitchen, in a field, at a market, and on a road, and say what job each one helps
with. They close by drawing one tool and the place it lives.

## Objectives

- Name a tool or device people use and tell what it helps them do. _(D10.S1.K.01)_

## Connection

Tools are not hidden away in one place — they are all around you, right now. The cup
you drink from, the door you open, the broom by the wall, the pot in the kitchen, the
jug by the well: every one is a tool someone made. Look around, and you will find
tools everywhere.

## Materials

- Picture cards of tools in different places (home, kitchen, field, market, road)
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Draw or gather a few picture cards of tools, each in a place: a pot in a kitchen, a
  hoe in a field, a basket at a market, a bicycle on a road.
- Prepare to walk the space and spot real tools.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). As facilitator, guide the tool hunt
but let learners do the finding — noticing is the learning. Keep the lens wide and
equal: a farmer's hoe and a cook's pot and a road's bicycle are all real tools, and no
place's tools are more important than another's. A phone is one tool among many, not
the star. Honor the tools of the learners' own homes, whatever they are.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Look around the room right now. Can you already see one tool?
2. **Tool hunt at home (10 min).** Walk and point to tools you can see — a cup, a
   chair, a door handle, a broom. For each, say the tool and its job: "A chair helps
   us sit and rest."
3. **Tools far away (15 min).** Look at the picture cards. People in every place use
   tools: a pot in a kitchen, a hoe in a field, a basket at a market, a bicycle on a
   road. Sort the cards by place. Which place would you like to visit?
4. **Draw a tool in its place (10 min).** Draw one tool and the place it lives.
5. **Close (5 min).** Every place has its own tools, and every tool helps someone do a
   job. What tool did you find that you did not notice before today?

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Go on the hunt with a partner and name tools together; match just two
  tool cards to two places.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to name a tool from their own home and tell what it
  helps a grown-up do there.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner point to or hold a tool in their space
  and say what it helps people do?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The "tool in its place" drawing.

## Home connection

At home, go on a tool hunt with a grown-up in one room — the kitchen or the yard. Find
three tools and say each one's job. Then ask the grown-up which tool they use the most.
