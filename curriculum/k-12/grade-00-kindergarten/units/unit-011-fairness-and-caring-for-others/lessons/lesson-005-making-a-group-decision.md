# Lesson 5 — Making a Group Decision

## Summary

Learners make a real, simple decision together. They practice hearing every voice,
listening, and choosing as a group — learning that a group decision belongs to
everyone, not to one person.

## Objectives

- Take part in a group decision or a small job that helps the class. _(D09.S3.K.01)_

## Connection

Groups decide things all the time: a family chooses what to eat, a class chooses a
song, a village decides where to put a well. When everyone has a voice and everyone
listens, the choice belongs to the whole group. You have a voice too — what you think
matters, and so does what everyone else thinks.

## Materials

- Two or three simple choice cards (which song, which game, which name for a class
  plant)
- A talking object (shell, stone, or stick)

## Preparation

- Choose a real, small decision the group can make today (a song, a game, a name).
- Prepare two or three choice cards and a talking object.

## Facilitator note

Written to the learner ("you"). This is *real* decision-making, not a simulation —
carry out the choice the group makes. Every voice matters equally: no one's idea is
worth more because of age, status, or volume. Guide but do not impose; if a quiet
child has not spoken, make gentle space without forcing. Across cultures, groups
decide in many ways — talking it through, raising hands, stepping to a corner — and
none is "the" right way. The talking object echoes talking circles used by many
Indigenous peoples around the world, where an object passes from speaker to speaker
so each voice is heard — we use it with respect, not as decoration. Keep the decision
small enough to finish and honor today. "Voice" means *however a learner shows their
choice* — spoken words, sign, a nod, a gesture, pointing to or tapping a choice card,
eye-gaze, or a communication board. A learner who does not speak aloud still shares a
choice; a grown-up or peer can voice it. A learner who cannot hold or pass the talking
object, raise a hand, or step to a corner can point, nod, or eye-gaze to their choice,
and a partner can move or pass for them. Never force anyone to speak aloud or to move —
pointing and signing are full participation.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of a time a group made a choice you were part of. How
   was it decided?
2. **Meet the choices (10 min).** Look at the choice cards. Here are two or three
   things we could choose. What does each one mean?
3. **Hear every voice (15 min).** Pass the talking object (or have a partner pass it
   for you). When it is your turn, show which choice you like and one reason — say it,
   sign it, or point to the card. When it is not your turn, listen (with ears or
   eyes). Every voice gets a turn.
4. **Choose together (10 min).** Show your choice — raise a hand, step to your
   choice, point, or nod (or have a partner move for you). Count together (a scribe
   can count for the group). Look at what most people chose, and talk: can we all
   agree to this? If not, is there a choice that includes more people?
5. **Do it (5 min).** Make your decision real — sing the song, play the game, or
   name the plant. A group decision belongs to everyone.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer just two choices; model giving one reason with a word and a
  gesture. A non-speaking learner points to the choice card; a learner who cannot
  pass the object or step has a partner move for them.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to notice whose voice was not heard and think of a
  way to include them next time.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Does the learner share a choice, listen to others,
  and join in the group's decision?
- **Performance:** The group's finished decision — carried out and named as the
  group's own.

## Home connection

At home, help make one small family choice together — what to eat, what game to
play — where every person says their idea before you decide.
