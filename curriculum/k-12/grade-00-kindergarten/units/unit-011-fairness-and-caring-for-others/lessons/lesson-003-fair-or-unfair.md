# Lesson 3 — Fair or Unfair?

## Summary

Learners meet the words *fair* and *unfair* and practice telling which is which in
simple, familiar situations — sharing, taking turns, being left out. They sort
scenes and give a reason for each.

## Objectives

- Tell when something is fair or unfair in a simple situation like sharing or taking
  turns. _(D09.S2.K.01)_

## Connection

Have you ever felt "that's not fair!" — maybe someone grabbed all the crayons or
took every turn? Everyone, in every part of the world, knows that feeling. Fairness
is a question we all ask: did everyone get a turn? Did anyone get left out? Learning
to see fair and unfair is the first step to making things fairer.

## Materials

- Fair-or-unfair picture cards
- Two sorting trays or hoops labeled "fair" and "unfair"
- Crayons and paper

## Preparation

- Prepare or draw fair-or-unfair picture cards showing simple scenes.
- Label two sorting spots "fair" and "unfair."

## Facilitator note

Written to the learner ("you"). Fairness here is *not* a rule to obey but a question
to explore together — hold each scene open ("what do you think? why?") rather than
declaring a single right answer. Some scenes are genuinely tricky, and that is good:
invite learners to notice when they are unsure. Fairness is universal, but how people
share differs across cultures — affirm different ways of being fair. Avoid shaming;
name the *act* ("that leaves someone out"), never the child.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of a time you felt something was fair or unfair. What
   happened?
2. **Meet the words (10 min).** Learn the words *fair* and *unfair*. Fair means
   everyone gets a turn or a share and no one is left out. Unfair means someone gets
   left out or gets everything.
3. **Sort the scenes (15 min).** Look at each card. Is it fair or unfair? Put it in
   the right tray or hoop, and say why. For example: "Everyone gets a turn — that is
   fair" or "One child keeps the swing the whole time — that is unfair."
4. **Draw one of each (10 min).** Draw one fair scene and one unfair scene. Tell a
   partner which is which and why.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: fair means everyone is included; unfair means someone
   is left out. You can notice both.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use just two very clear scenes; model "fair/unfair" with a thumbs-up
  or thumbs-down gesture.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to think of a scene that is *tricky* — fair to one
  person but not to another — and talk it through with a partner.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner sort a simple scene as fair or unfair
  and give a reason?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The "fair and unfair" drawing pair, kept in the learner's
  portfolio.

## Home connection

At home, notice one fair moment and one unfair moment (like sharing a snack or a
toy). Tell a grown-up which was which and why.
