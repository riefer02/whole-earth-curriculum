# Lesson 2 — Helping a Friend or Animal

## Summary

Learners focus their kindness on a friend and on an animal (or other living thing).
They practice gentle, specific ways to help, and say why caring for a living thing is
good.

## Objectives

- Name something kind or helpful to do for a friend or animal and say why it is
  good. _(D09.S1.K.01)_

## Connection

A friend who trips, a thirsty plant, a bird with no water — living things need help
sometimes, and you can give it. Around the world, people care for friends, pets,
livestock, and wild creatures in many small ways: filling a water dish, giving a
hug, making sure an animal has shade. Helping a living thing is a way of saying "you
matter."

## Materials

- Friend-and-animal picture cards
- Crayons and paper

## Preparation

- Prepare or draw friend-and-animal picture cards showing gentle, safe ways to help.
- Arrange space for acting out (and a soft toy "animal" if available).

## Facilitator note

Written to the learner ("you"). Keep help *gentle and safe*: a child never touches a
strange or wild animal without an adult, and never puts themselves in danger to help.
Frame caring as a responsibility we *choose*, not a rule to obey. Across cultures
people care for animals differently — as pets, as working partners, or as wild
neighbors — and all are valid. Avoid ranking one kind of caring above another.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of a friend and an animal you know. What is one thing
   each of them might need?
2. **Help a friend (10 min).** Look at the friend cards and name what the friend
   needs. Act out gentle help: a hand up, a kind word, sitting together. Say why it
   is good.
3. **Help an animal (10 min).** Look at the animal cards. Act out gentle care: a
   dish of water, a soft voice, stepping carefully around a tiny creature. Say why
   it is good — because the animal is safe and well.
4. **Draw your help (15 min).** Draw yourself helping a friend *or* an animal in one
   gentle, safe way. Tell a partner what you did and why it matters.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: helping a living thing — friend or animal — is
   caring in action. Always be gentle, and ask a grown-up if you are not sure.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Choose only a friend *or* an animal; model one gentle helping gesture.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to think of a wild animal or plant they could help
  (leaving water out safely, not pulling leaves) and draw it.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name one gentle way to help a friend
  or animal and give a reason it is good?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The "helping a friend or animal" drawing, kept in the
  learner's portfolio.

## Home connection

At home, find one gentle way to help a living thing — a person, a pet, or a plant —
and tell a grown-up why it was good.
