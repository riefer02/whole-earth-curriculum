# Lesson 6 — A Class Job That Helps

## Summary

Learners choose and carry out a small, real job that helps the class — watering
plants, passing paper, tidying, greeting. They see how many small helps add up to a
group that works for everyone.

## Objectives

- Take part in a group decision or a small job that helps the class. _(D09.S3.K.01)_

## Connection

A class, a family, and a village all run on small jobs done by many hands. One person
waters the plants, one tidies the shelf, one greets people at the door. When each
person does a little, the whole group is cared for — and no one has to do everything
alone.

## Materials

- A class job chart
- Small real tools for the jobs (watering can, basket, cloth)

## Preparation

- Set up a class job chart with a few simple jobs and spaces for names or pictures.
- Gather the small tools and arrange the jobs where learners can reach them.

## Facilitator note

Written to the learner ("you"). Jobs here are *contributions*, not rewards or
punishments — everyone gets to help, and jobs rotate so no one is stuck and no one is
left out. Every job is equally important; resist ranking "big" jobs above "small"
ones. Frame the job as care for the shared space and for each other, never as
obedience. Where a job needs adult help (water near plants, reaching high shelves),
step in alongside the child, not instead of them. A learner with limited movement can
choose a job and direct it while a partner does the reaching, carrying, or watering —
that is doing the job too. Sharing how it helped can be spoken, signed, pointed to on
the chart, or shown with a gesture; no one is required to speak aloud.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** What are some things your class needs every day to feel good
   and work well?
2. **Meet the jobs (10 min).** Look at the job chart. Name each job and what it does:
   watering plants, passing paper, tidying, greeting. Every job helps.
3. **Choose and do your job (20 min).** Pick one job (or take your turn). Do it with
   the real tool, gently and carefully. If you finish, ask: "How else can I help?"
4. **Share how it helped (5 min).** Tell the group — or show with a point, sign, or
   gesture — what you did and how it helped: "I watered the plants so they can grow"
   or "I tidied the shelf so we can find things."
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: when each person does a little, the whole group is
   cared for. Your help matters.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Pair the learner with a partner or adult for a single, very concrete
  job step.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to invent a new job the class needs and propose
  adding it to the chart.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Does the learner complete a small job and say or show
  how it helped the class?
- **Performance:** The finished job, shown in the shared space and reflected on aloud.

## Home connection

At home, pick one small job you can do each day — setting the table, feeding a pet,
wiping a surface — and tell a grown-up how it helps the family.
