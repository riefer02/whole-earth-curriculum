# Lesson 7 — Solving a Small Disagreement

## Summary

Learners practice a simple, gentle way to solve a small disagreement with words —
stop and breathe, use words, ask a grown-up for help — and to repair harm by making
things right, rather than by punishing.

## Objectives

- Use words (or a grown-up's help) to solve a small disagreement and make things
  right. _(D09.S4.K.01)_

## Connection

Disagreements happen everywhere people are together — two children wanting the same
toy, a tussle over a turn. You can solve a small disagreement without hurting anyone:
stop, use your words, and ask a grown-up to help if you need to. Then you make it
right — the hurt is repaired and everyone can play again.

## Materials

- A "stop, words, help" picture strip
- Two soft toys or puppets
- Crayons and paper

## Preparation

- Prepare a three-step "stop, words, help" picture strip.
- Set up a small space for acting out with two soft toys or puppets.

## Facilitator note

Written to the learner ("you"). This lesson teaches *repair, not punishment*: after a
small hurt, the goal is making things right — a kind word, returning what was taken,
checking the friend is okay — not shaming or excluding anyone. Name feelings before
fixes; both sides get heard. The three steps are a scaffold, not a script to obey;
wonder together about what might work in each case. Where an adult is present, the
adult's help is welcome and safe — asking for help is strength, not weakness. Keep
examples small, everyday, and free of blame. "Use your words" means *however a
learner expresses themselves* — spoken words, sign, a gesture, pointing to a feeling
or a picture on the strip, or a communication board. A learner who does not speak
aloud still "uses words": a grown-up or peer can voice what they point to or sign.
Learners who are deaf or hard of hearing follow the strip by sight and sign; learners
who are blind or low-vision hear the steps named and touch or feel the toys. Acting
out with the toys is one way to practice, not the only way — a learner with limited
movement can point to the step on the strip, or direct a partner or adult to move the
toys while they show the feeling and choose the repair. Never require anyone to speak
or to act in front of the group.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of a small disagreement you have seen — two people
   wanting the same thing. What happened next?
2. **Meet the three steps (10 min).** Learn the steps: **Stop** and breathe. **Use
   your words** — say, sign, or point to how you feel and what you need (a grown-up
   or peer can voice it for you). **Ask a grown-up for help** if it is too hard alone.
3. **Act it out (15 min).** With the two soft toys, show a small disagreement — both
   want the same ball. Practice the steps: stop, use words ("I was playing with it;
   can we take turns?" — say it, sign it, or point to it on the strip), ask for help
   if needed. A learner who does not move the toys can point to the step or direct a
   partner to move them.
4. **Make it right (10 min).** After the words, make it right: return the ball, say
   "are you okay?", or find a way to share. Repair is how we fix the hurt, not punish
   the person.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: words can solve small problems, and making it right
   fixes the hurt so everyone can play again.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Practice only "stop and breathe" and "ask a grown-up" first; model the
  repair words word by word. A non-speaking learner can point to the strip's pictures
  for each step; a learner with limited movement can have a partner move the toys.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to invent their own small conflict and practice
  solving it with the three steps and a repair.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner use words (or ask for help) to solve a
  small disagreement and name a way to make it right?
- **Performance:** The acted-out repair, shown with the soft toys and reflected on
  aloud.

## Home connection

At home, if a small disagreement happens, try the three steps — stop, use words, ask
for help — and then make it right together.
