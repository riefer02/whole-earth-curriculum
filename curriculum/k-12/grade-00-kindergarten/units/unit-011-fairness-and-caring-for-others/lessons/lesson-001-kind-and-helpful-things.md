# Lesson 1 — Kind and Helpful Things

## Summary

Learners meet the idea that kindness is something we can *do*. They name kind and
helpful acts, practice saying why each one is good, and draw one kind act they could
do themselves.

## Objectives

- Name something kind or helpful to do for a friend or animal and say why it is
  good. _(D09.S1.K.01)_

## Connection

Think of a time someone helped you — holding a door, sharing a snack, or saying
something warm when you felt sad. That feeling is kindness, and you can give it to
someone else too. Everywhere in the world, people show care in small ways every day:
carrying something heavy for a neighbor, watering a plant, or comforting a friend.

## Materials

- Kind-act picture cards (sharing, helping carry, comforting, saying a kind word)
- Crayons and paper

## Preparation

- Prepare or draw a set of simple kind-act picture cards.
- Arrange a circle for gathering and acting out.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Kindness here is framed as *mutual* — it is good for the
person helped *and* for the person helping — not as obedience or earning praise.
Avoid labeling children "good" or "bad"; describe the *act* ("that was kind") rather
than the child ("you are kind"). Accept every learner's example; there is no single
right kind act.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Sit comfortably. Think of a time someone was kind or helpful
   to you. How did it feel?
2. **Meet kind acts (10 min).** Look at each card and name what the person is doing.
   Act it out together: share, help carry, comfort, say a kind word. For each one,
   say why it is good — because it helps someone feel better or makes something
   easier.
3. **Why is it good? (10 min).** Pick two cards. For each, finish the sentence: "It
   is good because…" (for example, "…the friend smiles and feels less alone").
   Notice that helping feels good for the helper too.
4. **Draw your kind act (15 min).** Draw one kind or helpful thing *you* could do —
   for a friend, a family member, or an animal. Tell a partner what it is and why it
   is good.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: kindness is something you can do, every day, in many
   small ways.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer just two cards; model one sentence "It is good because…" with a
  word and a gesture.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to draw *two* kind acts and tell a time they saw
  someone else do one.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name a kind or helpful act and give
  one reason it is good?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The "my kind act" drawing, kept in the learner's
  portfolio.

## Home connection

Tonight, do one small kind or helpful thing at home — help set the table, put away
shoes, or say something warm. Tell a grown-up why it was good.
