# Lesson 6 — What Did I Notice?

## Summary

Learners return to a story or song from earlier in the unit and practice noticing
more deeply. They share one sound, word, picture, or feeling, taking respectful
turns.

## Objectives

- Listen to a story or song from another culture and share one thing they
  noticed. _(D08.S2.K.01)_

## Connection

Listening is not just hearing — it is paying attention to the small things: the
sound of a voice, a word that repeats, a feeling that rises. When you notice details
in someone's story or song, you are telling them, "Your story matters to me." That is
how people learn to understand each other.

## Materials

- A story or song learners already heard (reused or gently new)
- An "I noticed..." talking object (stick or soft object)

## Preparation

- Choose a story or song to revisit.
- Prepare the talking object and a calm circle.

## Facilitator note

Written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a self-directed
learner. This lesson builds noticing as a skill: guide learners from "I liked it"
toward "I noticed a sound/a word/a picture/a feeling." Honor every honest noticing;
do not correct a child's response. Model "I noticed..." yourself, and keep turns
kind so all voices are heard.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Sit in a circle. Remember the story or song from before. What
   do you remember first?
2. **Listen again (15 min).** Hear it once more, this time listening for one small
   thing: a sound, a word, a picture, or a feeling.
3. **Notice with a helper (10 min).** With a partner, finish the sentence "I
   noticed..." Try to name the kind of thing you noticed: "I noticed the sound of..."
   or "I noticed the feeling of..."
4. **Share (10 min).** Pass the talking object. When you hold it, share your "I
   noticed..." — say it, point to a picture, draw it, or sign it. When you do not
   hold it, attend to the speaker.
5. **Close (5 min).** Listening closely is a way of respecting someone's story. The
   more we notice, the more we understand.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer two starter choices: "Was your noticing a sound or a feeling?"
- **Non-speaking:** Offer a drawn or picture "I noticed..." board (sound, word,
  picture, feeling) so a learner can point to or circle their noticing without
  speaking.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to notice one detail the first time and a *different*
  kind of detail the second time, and compare.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Does the learner move beyond "I liked it" to a
  concrete noticing (a sound, word, picture, or feeling)?
- **Self-assessment:** Does the learner name which kind of thing they noticed?
