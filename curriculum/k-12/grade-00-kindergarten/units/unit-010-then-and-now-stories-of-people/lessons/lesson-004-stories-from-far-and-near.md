# Lesson 4 — Stories from Far and Near

## Summary

Learners listen to a gentle story from another culture, held respectfully, and share
one thing they noticed. The focus is listening well, not retelling facts.

## Objectives

- Listen to a story or song from another culture and share one thing they
  noticed. _(D08.S2.K.01)_

## Connection

Every people on Earth tells stories — to teach, to comfort, to remember. When you
listen to a story from a place far from yours, you are meeting people you may never
see. Listening carefully is a way of honoring their story as a gift.

## Materials

- A told story from another culture (chosen by the facilitator)
- Cushions or mats for a listening circle

## Preparation

- Choose a gentle story from another culture and learn to tell it well, or prepare a
  recording of a storyteller from that culture.
- Set up a calm, comfortable listening circle.

## Facilitator note

Written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a self-directed
learner. **Choose the story with care:** it should be gentle, told by or faithful to
the culture it comes from, and never a caricature or a "costume." Avoid stories that
mock, exoticize, or reduce a whole people to one trait. A recording of a storyteller
from that culture is best. Keep the retelling respectful; the goal is one honest
noticing, not a quiz.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Settle into a calm listening body: hands still, ready to
   listen with your eyes or your ears.
2. **Listen (15 min).** Hear the story all the way through without stopping. If a
   picture comes to your mind, let it stay there quietly.
3. **Notice (10 min).** Think of one thing you noticed: a character, a sound, a
   feeling, a place in the story.
4. **Share (10 min).** Take turns sharing, "I noticed..." — say it, point to a
   picture, draw it, or sign it. Each noticing is welcome; there is no right answer.
5. **Close (5 min).** Many peoples tell stories, and each story is a gift. When we
   listen carefully, we honor it.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer a picture or a gesture to go with the story; ask "what did you
  see in your mind?"
- **Deaf or hard of hearing:** Tell the story in sign or with a picture sequence, so
  the story is seen, not only heard; a learner may "listen" with their eyes.
- **Non-speaking:** A learner may point to a picture, draw, act, or sign their
  noticing instead of saying it aloud.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to draw the one thing they noticed and add a caption.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Does the learner listen through the story and share
  one genuine noticing?
- **Formative (oral):** Is the noticing specific ("I noticed the drum," not just "I
  liked it")?
