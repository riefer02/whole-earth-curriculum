# Lesson 10 — Then & Now: Stories of People

## Summary

Learners gather everything from the unit into one four-part "Stories of People"
page: a then-and-now, a story or song they noticed, a place, and an idea about
fairness. They share their page and build a class book or display.

## Objectives

- Tell a "then and now" story about themselves or their family and how something
  changed. _(D08.S1.K.01)_
- Listen to a story or song from another culture and share one thing they
  noticed. _(D08.S2.K.01)_
- Talk about a place they have been or a family story of moving, and draw or describe
  it. _(D08.S3.K.01)_
- Retell a gentle story about someone being treated fairly or unfairly. _(D08.S4.K.01)_

## Connection

History is the story of many people — and you are one of them. Your "then and now,"
the stories and songs you noticed, the places you have been, and your sense of what
is fair all belong in that big story. When we put our small stories together, we make
a bigger one that belongs to everyone.

## Materials

- A four-box page per learner ("then & now," "a story or song," "a place," "fair")
- Crayons and drawing tools
- Stapler, string, or clips for a class book

## Preparation

- Prepare a four-box page for each learner (or one large shared sheet).
- Set up a gathering place and a spot to assemble the class book or display.

## Facilitator note

Written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a self-directed
learner. This is the unit's capstone: a chance for each child to author their own
small piece of history. Keep all four boxes optional in depth — a word or a drawing
counts. Never force disclosure of family details; a child may draw a place as "home"
without naming it. Celebrate each page as a real contribution to the class story.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Remember the unit: then and now, stories and songs, places,
   and fairness. You have learned all four.
2. **Fill the four boxes (20 min).** On your page, draw or write one thing in each
   box: something that changed (then & now); one thing you noticed in a story or
   song; one place you have been; and one idea about what is fair.
3. **Share (10 min).** With a partner or small group, show your page and tell about
   one box you love. Listen to theirs.
4. **Make the class book (5 min).** Add your page to the "Stories of People" book or
   wall. Now everyone's story is together.
5. **Close (5 min).** History is the story of many people — and you are one of them.
   Your story matters.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Complete one box at a time with a grown-up scribe; a single word or
  mark in each box counts.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to add a fifth box: "someday" — one hope for the
  future of their own story.

## Assessment

- **Summative (portfolio):** The four-part "Stories of People" page shows the
  learner's then-and-now, a noticed story/song, a place, and a fair idea.
- **Observation:** Does the learner connect each box to the right idea from the unit
  and share it in their own words?
