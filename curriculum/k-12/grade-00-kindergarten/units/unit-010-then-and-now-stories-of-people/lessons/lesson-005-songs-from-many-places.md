# Lesson 5 — Songs from Many Places

## Summary

Learners listen to songs from several world regions, feel their rhythms through
clapping and gentle movement, and share one thing they noticed about each. Songs are
held as belonging to the real people who sing them.

## Objectives

- Listen to a story or song from another culture and share one thing they
  noticed. _(D08.S2.K.01)_

## Connection

People everywhere sing — to babies, to each other, to celebrate, to feel less alone.
A lullaby from one place and a clapping song from another sound different, yet both
say, "We are here, and this is ours." Listening to songs from far away is a way of
meeting those people.

## Materials

- Recordings of two or three songs from different world regions (a lullaby, a
  clapping or game song, a call-and-response song)
- A way to play sound

## Preparation

- Choose two or three songs from different world regions, played by artists from
  those cultures, and note the singers' names and places.
- Test the audio and set up a listening space.

## Facilitator note

Written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a self-directed
learner. **Respect is the rule:** name the real singers and places; do not have
learners mimic accents or "perform" another culture. Songs are not toys or costumes.
Keep clapping and movement gentle and in time — joining in, not making fun. Choose
recordings by artists from the culture wherever possible.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Settle your body. Get ready to listen with your ears and feel
   with your hands.
2. **Listen to the first song (10 min).** Hear it once all the way through. Then tap
   or clap a soft beat with it. What does it feel like — fast or slow, loud or soft?
3. **Listen to another (10 min).** Hear a second song from a different place. Is it
   one voice or many? Does it make you want to sway, march, or be still?
4. **Notice (10 min).** Share one thing you noticed about each song — say it, point
   to a picture, or draw it: a sound, a beat, a feeling. "I noticed the drum." "I
   noticed it was slow and soft."
5. **Close (5 min).** People everywhere sing, and every song carries a place and a
   people. Listening well is a kind of respect.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use just one song and focus on a single thing: "was it fast or slow?"
- **Deaf or hard of hearing:** Let the learner feel the beat through vibration (a hand
  on a drum, the floor, or a speaker) and see it in clapping hands or drawn beat
  marks; rhythm can be followed with the body, not only the ear.
- **Non-speaking:** A learner may point, draw, or sign their noticing instead of
  saying it aloud.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to draw the shape of a song's beat (long and short
  marks) and compare two songs.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Does the learner listen and share one concrete
  noticing about a song?
- **Formative (movement):** Can the learner keep or find a simple beat?
