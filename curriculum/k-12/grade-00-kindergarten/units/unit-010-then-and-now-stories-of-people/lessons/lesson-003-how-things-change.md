# Lesson 3 — How Things Change

## Summary

Learners look at old and new ways of doing an everyday job — lighting a room,
carrying water, or playing — and name what changed and what stayed the same. History
here is told through objects, not dates.

## Objectives

- Tell a "then and now" story about themselves or their family and how something
  changed. _(D08.S1.K.01)_

## Connection

Long ago, and still today in many homes, people lit a room with a candle or a
lantern; in other homes a lamp turns on with a switch. Both give light — that part
stayed the same. People everywhere have found many good ways to do the same jobs.

## Materials

- Old-and-new object cards (a candle and a lamp, a water pot and a tap, a simple toy
  and a newer toy)
- A real old object (optional)

## Preparation

- Gather old-and-new pairs as cards or real objects.
- Clear a space for acting out the jobs.

## Facilitator note

Written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a self-directed
learner. **Hold all ways with respect:** never rank "old" as worse or "new" as
better. Some learners' families still light rooms with candles or carry water — these
are valid, living ways, not museum curiosities. The point is *change and sameness*,
not progress or superiority.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Sit comfortably. Think of a job people do every day: getting
   light, getting water, playing.
2. **Meet the pairs (10 min).** Look at an old-and-new pair that does the same job.
   Which is old? Which is new? What does each one do?
3. **Act it out (15 min).** Pretend to light a candle, then flip a switch. Pretend to
   carry water in a pot, then turn a tap. Feel the difference with your body.
4. **Name it (10 min).** For one pair, say one thing that changed ("now it is faster")
   and one thing that stayed the same ("both give light").
5. **Close (5 min).** Things change over time, and people everywhere find many ways
   to do the same important jobs.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Compare just one pair and act out only one job.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to draw a third way the same job might be done in
  the future, and tell what stayed the same.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner say one thing that changed and one
  that stayed the same for an old-and-new pair?
- **Formative (oral):** Does the learner tell a tiny then-and-now story about a tool
  or job?
