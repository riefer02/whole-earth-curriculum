# Lesson 8 — Stories of Moving

## Summary

Learners hear a gentle story about moving and wonder together about why people move
and what it feels like. Every family's story — of moving, staying, arriving, or
adoption — is honored, and no child is asked to share more than they wish.

## Objectives

- Talk about a place they have been or a family story of moving, and draw or describe
  it. _(D08.S3.K.01)_

## Connection

Some people live their whole life in one place; some move across the street, and
some move across the world. Moving can feel exciting, hard, or both at once. The
people we love and the things we remember travel with us wherever we go.

## Materials

- A gentle story about moving (picture book or told story)
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Choose a gentle moving story that names real feelings (happy and hard) without
  blaming anyone.
- Prepare a calm circle and paper for optional drawing.

## Facilitator note

Written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a self-directed
learner. **This is the most sensitive lesson in the unit.** Some children have moved
for hard reasons — displacement, loss, separation; some were adopted or fostered.
Never require a child to tell a moving story, and never treat one kind of family
story as more "correct" than another. Keep the wonder gentle and blame-free: people
move for family, work, safety, or a new home — all are real, and none is a child's
fault.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Sit comfortably. Think quietly: has anyone you know ever
   moved, or have you? You do not have to say.
2. **Hear a story (15 min).** Listen to the story of a child or family moving. Notice
   what they carried, who they missed, and what they found.
3. **Wonder together (10 min).** Why do people move? (For family, work, safety, a new
   home.) What might feel happy? What might feel hard? Both can be true.
4. **Optional share or draw (10 min).** If you want to, draw "my home" or tell one
   small thing. If you would rather listen, that is welcome too.
5. **Close (5 min).** Every family has a story of coming and staying. Each one
   matters, and we listen without judging.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Focus only on the story's character — "how did they feel?" — before
  any personal sharing.
- **Extension:** Invite families (with permission) to share a short moving story, and
  compare one happy and one hard part across stories.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Does the learner engage with the story and name a
  reason people move or a feeling about moving?
- **Formative (oral, optional):** If a learner chooses to share, can they describe a
  place or a moving story in their own words?
