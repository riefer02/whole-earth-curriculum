# Lesson 9 — Fair and Unfair

## Summary

Learners hear a gentle, blame-free story about someone being treated fairly or
unfairly. They name which is which, notice how it felt, and retell the story in
their own words.

## Objectives

- Retell a gentle story about someone being treated fairly or unfairly. _(D08.S4.K.01)_

## Connection

Fair means everyone gets a turn and no one is left out for no reason; unfair means
someone is treated differently for no good reason. You see fair and unfair every
day — who gets a turn, who is included, who is listened to. Noticing it kindly is
the first step to making things fairer.

## Materials

- A gentle fair/unfair story (about sharing turns or being left out, with a kind
  ending)
- Two feeling cards or labeled spots: "fair" and "unfair"

## Preparation

- Choose or write a short, gentle story with one fair moment and one unfair moment,
  ending kindly and without blame.
- Set up the two labeled spots or cards.

## Facilitator note

Written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a self-directed
learner. **Keep the story gentle and blame-free.** The goal is noticing and naming,
not shaming a character. End with repair — someone makes it fair. Avoid scary or
heavy unfairness at this age. Let learners retell in their own words; every retelling
is a step toward understanding fairness.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Sit comfortably. Think of a time everyone got a turn. How did
   that feel?
2. **Hear the story (15 min).** Listen to the story. There is a part where someone is
   treated fairly, and a part where someone is treated unfairly.
3. **Name it (10 min).** Point to the "fair" spot or the "unfair" spot for each part.
   How did the person feel in each part?
4. **Retell it (10 min).** Tell the story back in your own words. Change the order if
   you like, but keep the fair part and the unfair part clear.
5. **Close (5 min).** Fair means everyone gets a turn and is included. We can notice
   fair and unfair, kindly, and help make things fairer.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Tell just the unfair part and ask one question: "How did they feel?"
  Then show the repair.
- **Non-speaking:** A learner may retell by pointing to the fair and unfair spots,
  acting the two parts out with their body, or ordering picture cards, instead of
  speaking.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to make up a tiny new ending where the unfair part
  becomes fair, and act it out.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner point to or say which part was fair
  and which was unfair?
- **Formative (oral):** Does the learner retell the story, keeping the fair and
  unfair parts clear?
