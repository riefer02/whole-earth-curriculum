# Grade 9 Scope

## How to read this scope

Each `unit_id` in the frontmatter resolves to a unit file under `units/`.
`start_day`/`end_day` map the unit onto the 180-day school year; the list is a
continuous sequence from day 1 to day 180 with no gaps or overlaps. `domain` and
`pillar` name the primary domain(s) and pillar(s) each unit serves. `domain_weighting`
expresses the intended balance of instructional time across the ten domains, in
percent of the year. `lesson_count` counts the core lessons; the days between lessons
carry flex time, assessment, integration, and celebration. `assessment_plan`
describes how a learner's growth is seen and recorded at this level.

## Year at a glance

The Grade 9 year is a spiral that deepens what Grade 8 began, and the first year of high
school, when formal reasoning matures and the learner becomes a co-author, not a
student. Where Grade 8 learned to *analyze*, Grade 9 learns to *synthesize and hold
competing truths*: to build a claim from data while naming its limits, to reason across
more than one ethical framework, and to model with functions and prove with coordinates.
Grade 10 will carry each thread further.

The year opens with two demanding invitations that set the bar. *Evidence in a Changing
Climate* (U.09.001, D06) is the science opener: an evidence-based explanation that links
data to a claim and states its limits, and sources read for reliability, bias, and
interest. *Remembering a Painful Past* (U.09.002, D09 + D08) is the ethics-of-memory
opener: a moral question reasoned across more than one framework, with the historical
perspective that people in the past saw their world differently. Inquiry over creed;
more than one answer may stand.

The year turns inward. *Feelings, Identity & Purpose* (U.09.003, D01) consolidates a
mature emotional life: a broad vocabulary for subtle emotions, a coherent personal
narrative, a longer-term goal with milestones, and self-advocacy that respects others.
*My Body, Wellness & Movement* (U.09.004, D03) reads the body as the somatic ground of
thought and feeling, sets and tracks a movement goal, explains how movement shapes
mental health, holds a body-positive view against media's messages, and follows food
from farm to table to waste. *Relationships, Empathy & Community* (U.09.005, D02) makes
care a practice in a plural world: consent, trust, empathic inquiry, community across
cultures, and how belonging both builds and limits a person.

The heart of the year is the three great tools of school. *Language, Literature &
Argument* (U.09.006, D04) turns Grade 8's rhetoric into synthesis and authorship:
argument that cites and weighs sources, and language loss as a question of justice —
home language first, multilingualism as wealth. *Mathematics & Reasoning* (U.09.007,
D05) becomes modeling and proof: real numbers and radicals, functions read in context,
quadratic equations, and two-way tables about fairness. *Living Systems, Physical
Systems & Our Origins* (U.09.008, D06) carries that discipline through ecosystems,
energy, the atom, climate, and deep time, origins held as evidence-grounded stories
among many. *Creative & Aesthetic Expression* (U.09.009, D07) gives craft and a critical
eye to digital work, music, performance, and design. *Health, Sexuality & Safety*
(U.09.010, D03) returns to the body with sexual and reproductive health, consent, and
knowing where to seek help.

The final third steps into the full adult questions. *Ethics, Justice & Political
Economy* (U.09.011, D09) reasons across frameworks, traces systemic injustice through
actions, rules, and structures, and weighs rights, democracy, and reconciliation.
*Global History: Change, Power & Liberation* (U.09.012, D08) analyzes continuity and
change in a global process, suppressed and revived knowledge traditions, forced
migration, empire, and the spread of freedom and dignity — many-voiced, never one
nation's triumph. *Technology, Media & Our Earth Home* (U.09.013, D10) asks how
platforms shape what people see and who holds power, how to trace a viral claim, and how
environmental burdens fall unevenly. The year closes in *All of Me, All of Us*
(U.09.014, D01 + D02), gathering the personal narrative, the caring community, and the
living Earth.

Throughout, the four pillars are interwoven. Intellectual awareness (P3) carries the
most weight — synthesis and argument in language, modeling and proof in mathematics, the
evidence-and-source discipline of science, and the arts — while contextual and
ecological awareness (P4) rises sharply for the history, political economy, ethics, and
technology of high school. Emotional and social awareness (P1) remains the ground:
identity and purpose consolidate, and relationships matter more than ever; a known,
cared-for, and capable body-and-self (P2) is the foundation all of it stands on. Hard
truths are met with agency, never nihilism; no high-stakes tests narrow the year.
