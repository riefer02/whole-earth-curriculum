# Grade 9 Scope

## How to read this scope

Each `unit_id` in the frontmatter resolves to a unit file under `units/`.
`start_day`/`end_day` map the unit onto the 180-day school year; the list is a
continuous sequence from day 1 to day 180 with no gaps or overlaps. `domain` and
`pillar` name the primary domain(s) and pillar(s) each unit serves. `domain_weighting`
expresses the intended balance of instructional time across the ten domains, in
percent of the year. `lesson_count` counts the core lessons; the days between lessons
carry flex time, assessment, integration, and celebration. `assessment_plan`
describes how a learner's growth is seen and recorded at this level.

## Year at a glance

The Grade 9 year is a spiral that deepens what Grade 8 began — and it is the first
year of high school, when formal operations mature and the learner is treated as a
co-author, not a student. Where Grade 8 learned to *analyze* — to pull a reaction
apart, weigh competing accounts, model a relationship with a line, critique a work
with evidence — Grade 9 learns to *synthesize and hold competing truths*: to build a
claim from data while naming its limits, to reason across more than one ethical
framework at once, to model the world with functions and prove with coordinates, and
to face the hardest truths with intellectual tools and a sense of agency, never
nihilism. The year opens with two short, demanding invitations that set the bar for
the whole grade.

*Evidence in a Changing Climate* (U.09.001, D06) is the high-grade science opener.
Learners construct an evidence-based explanation that links data to a claim and
states its limits and uncertainties (D06.S1.09.01), and critically evaluate a source
of scientific information for reliability, bias, and conflicts of interest
(D06.S1.09.02). Inquiry is held over creed, data over assertion. *Remembering a
Painful Past* (U.09.002, D09 + D08) is the ethics-of-memory opener, the bar for
holding a hard topic with nuance. Learners analyze a moral question using more than
one ethical framework — consequences, duties, virtues — and compare what each shows
(D09.S1.09.01), while explaining historical perspective, how people in the past saw
their world differently (D08.S1.09.02). Evidence and values are distinguished
throughout, and more than one defensible answer is allowed to stand.

From there the year turns inward to consolidate identity, purpose, and values.
*Feelings, Identity & Purpose* (U.09.003, D01) builds on Grade 8's separation of
event, thought, and feeling toward a mature emotional life. Learners use a broad
emotional vocabulary to articulate subtle and secondary emotions in complex social
situations (D01.S1.09.01) and analyze how emotions are shaped by social context,
media, and group belonging (D01.S1.09.02). They construct a coherent personal
narrative connecting past, present, and hoped-for future (D01.S2.09.01), analyze how
a person's identity is shaped by society and in turn shapes their choices
(D01.S2.09.02), set a longer-term goal with a realistic plan and milestones
(D01.S3.09.01), and advocate for their own needs and boundaries while considering
others (D01.S3.09.02). Identity, values, and purpose now consolidate, so every self
is honored as a whole in the making, never a label, and every question is held as a
question.

*My Body, Wellness & Movement* (U.09.004, D03) then reads the body as the somatic
ground of thought and feeling. Learners explain the somatic basis of emotion and
thought, showing how body state shapes mood and thinking (D03.S1.09.01), and evaluate
body-image messages from media and peers to articulate a body-positive or
body-neutral view of their own body (D03.S1.09.02). They set a personal movement or
fitness goal and track progress over time (D03.S2.09.01), analyze how movement
affects mental health and learning through mechanisms such as endorphins and focus
(D03.S2.09.02), analyze a local food system from farm to table to waste and its
effects on health, workers, and the environment (D03.S3.09.01), and explain how
hunger, food security, and unequal access to healthy food shape health outcomes
around the world (D03.S3.09.02). The body is taught with dignity and joy, honoring
the wide range of bodies and abilities. *Relationships, Empathy & Community*
(U.09.005, D02) turns outward into the practice of care in a plural world, where
peers and belonging matter most. Learners communicate respectfully across differences
of opinion, culture, and background (D02.S1.09.01), practice healthy relationship
skills including consent, trust, and honest talk about feelings (D02.S1.09.02), and
use empathic inquiry to understand another's feelings and needs before responding
(D02.S2.09.01). They examine how empathy can be limited or expanded by distance,
media, and technology (D02.S2.09.02), compare forms of community across cultures,
including chosen families and mutual-aid networks (D02.S3.09.01), analyze how
nationalism, exclusion, and in-group loyalty can both build and limit community
(D02.S3.09.02), evaluate how economic and social systems shape cooperation,
competition, and inequality (D02.S4.09.01), and design a cooperative project that
meets a real need in their community (D02.S4.09.02).

The heart of the year is given to the three great tools of school, now wielded at
full strength: language, number, and science. *Language, Literature & Argument*
(U.09.006, D04) builds from Grade 8's rhetoric and structured debate toward
*synthesis and authorship*. Learners synthesize information from multiple spoken
sources and present a coherent account or argument (D04.S1.09.01), and lead or
facilitate a discussion, inviting participation and managing disagreement with
fairness (D04.S1.09.02). They analyze how a text's structure, style, and argument
work together to create meaning and effect (D04.S2.09.01), and synthesize ideas and
evidence from multiple texts to form a supported position (D04.S2.09.02). They write
a research-supported argument that integrates and cites sources and evaluates
conflicting evidence (D04.S3.09.01), and compose for real audiences and purposes —
letters, proposals, published digital pieces (D04.S3.09.02). They explain how a
person's languages are part of their identity and reflect on their own multilingual
or monolingual experience (D04.S4.09.01), and describe language loss and
revitalization and why protecting languages matters for humanity and the environment
(D04.S4.09.02) — always home language first, with multilingualism held as wealth.
*Mathematics & Reasoning* (U.09.007, D05) grows from Grade 8's functions and linear
equations into modeling and proof. Learners work with real numbers, roots, and
radicals in expressions and equations, explaining which properties justify each step
(D05.S1.09.01), and choose appropriate units and levels of precision when measuring
and calculating, interpreting results in context (D05.S1.09.02). They model real
situations with linear and exponential functions and interpret slope and intercept in
context (D05.S2.09.01), solve quadratic equations using several methods and interpret
what their solutions mean in real situations (D05.S2.09.02), use rigid motions and
coordinates to prove and reason about geometric relationships such as congruence
(D05.S3.09.01), and prove and apply theorems about lines, angles, triangles, and
parallelograms (D05.S3.09.02). They summarize and compare distributions of a single
variable using plots and summary statistics (D05.S4.09.01), and use two-way tables and
conditional relative frequencies to explore relationships between categories,
including questions of fairness (D05.S4.09.02). Mathematics remains sense-making with
real things, drawn from many traditions, for everyone.

*Living Systems, Physical Systems & Our Origins* (U.09.008, D06) is the full pass
through Grade 9 science, following the evidence-and-source discipline of the opener
into the three great bodies of the natural world. Learners explain how matter cycles
and energy flows through ecosystems and how disruptions affect carrying capacity
(D06.S2.09.01), and describe biodiversity and why it matters for resilient ecosystems
and human well-being (D06.S2.09.02). They explain the law of conservation of energy
and analyze energy transformations and efficiency in a real system (D06.S3.09.01),
and use the structure of the atom to explain bonding and how chemical reactions absorb
or release energy (D06.S3.09.02). They explain how greenhouse gases regulate climate
and how human activity is changing the climate system (D06.S4.09.01), and explain the
evidence for the age of the Earth and the universe and how scientists measure it
(D06.S4.09.02). Origins are held as stories grounded in evidence, among many others —
never as dogma, and never as the only defensible account of a beginning.

*Creative & Aesthetic Expression* (U.09.009, D07) gives craft and a critical eye to
the imagination now that formal reasoning has matured. Learners create a digital or
mixed-media artwork that combines techniques and reflect on the choices technology
made possible (D07.S1.09.01), and analyze how visual media such as advertising, news,
and social media use composition and symbolism to influence viewers (D07.S1.09.02).
They create music using digital or recording tools and reflect on how the technology
shaped the sound and the process (D07.S2.09.01), and analyze how music can build
identity, community, or resistance across more than one culture (D07.S2.09.02). They
create a performance that uses the moving body to explore identity or a personal
question (D07.S3.09.01), analyze how dance, drama, and ritual build community or carry
memory across cultures (D07.S3.09.02), use digital or fabrication tools to design and
make an object and reflect on how the tools changed what was possible (D07.S4.09.01),
and analyze how technology and design shape everyday life and the environment
(D07.S4.09.02). The arts of other cultures are met as living traditions to honor with
respectful questions, never as specimens.

*Health, Sexuality & Safety* (U.09.010, D03) returns to the body at a turning point,
now with the honesty and agency high school demands. Learners explain the basics of
sexual and reproductive health — anatomy, consent, and how to access accurate
information and care (D03.S4.09.01) — and recognize signs of mental-health struggle
in themselves or others and describe how and where to seek help (D03.S4.09.02). Every
body, every pace, and every family's way of living is honored; the body is taught
with dignity, never shame, and questions are met with accurate information and care,
not fear.

The final third steps into the full adult questions of power, justice, and the living
Earth. *Ethics, Justice & Political Economy* (U.09.011, D09) deepens Grade 8's
weighing of consequences, duties, and fairness into reasoning across frameworks.
Learners analyze a moral question using more than one ethical framework and compare
what each shows (D09.S1.09.01), evaluate the role of conscience and self-criticism in
forming one's own values (D09.S1.09.02), analyze a systemic injustice by tracing how
individual actions, rules, and structures combine to produce unfair outcomes
(D09.S2.09.01), and evaluate the idea of human rights across cultures, including
critiques that they reflect some traditions more than others (D09.S2.09.02). They
analyze how power is distributed in a society and how citizens can hold power
accountable (D09.S3.09.01), evaluate the strengths and limits of democratic
participation and compare with alternatives (D09.S3.09.02), analyze the causes of
conflict and violence — inequality, fear, scarcity — and how they can be addressed at
the root (D09.S4.09.01), and evaluate reconciliation and restorative justice as
responses to serious harm, including their possibilities and limits (D09.S4.09.02).
Questions are held as questions, never answered as dogma.

*Global History: Change, Power & Liberation* (U.09.012, D08) gives Grade 9 its full
pass through the long, many-voiced human story, deepening the opener's single
standard of historical perspective. Learners analyze continuity and change in one
global process, such as long-distance trade or the growth of cities, across several
regions and centuries (D08.S1.09.01). They analyze how a knowledge tradition was
suppressed, marginalized, or lost through colonialism or conquest, and how peoples
have worked to preserve or revive it (D08.S2.09.01), and explain how the mixing of
cultures created new traditions and identities (D08.S2.09.02). They analyze how
societies changed and were changed by their environments over long spans of time,
including adaptation and cases of decline (D08.S3.09.01), and analyze the
transatlantic slave trade and other forced migrations as global movements of people
with lasting effects (D08.S3.09.02). They analyze colonialism and empire as systems
of power and the anti-colonial movements that fought for liberation (D08.S4.09.01),
and explain how ideas of freedom and dignity spread and fueled struggles for
liberation across different continents (D08.S4.09.02). History is told from many
perspectives, never as a single nation's triumph, and the voices of those too often
left out are centered with care and accuracy.

*Technology, Media & Our Earth Home* (U.09.013, D10) gathers the outward year into
critical judgment and stewardship — neither worship nor fear. Learners explain how
software and algorithms, such as recommendations and feeds, shape what people see and
choose (D10.S1.09.01), analyze who controls a digital platform and what power that
control gives over users (D10.S1.09.02), explain how algorithmic feeds and echo
chambers can narrow what people see and how to break out of them (D10.S2.09.01), and
evaluate a viral claim by tracing it to its source and checking it against independent
reporting (D10.S2.09.02). They explain the greenhouse effect and how the carbon cycle
connects living things, oceans, atmosphere, and rock over time (D10.S3.09.01), explain
what a feedback loop is and how warming can speed up more warming (D10.S3.09.02),
compare fossil fuels and renewable energy sources in how they work, their limits, and
their effects on people and the planet (D10.S4.09.01), and analyze how the burdens and
benefits of environmental change fall unevenly on different people — environmental
justice (D10.S4.09.02).

The year closes in *All of Me, All of Us* (U.09.014, D01 + D02), gathering every whole
person, the caring community they have built, and the living Earth — and looking ahead
to Grade 10, where a personal narrative becomes a considered position, identity is
examined as socially and historically constructed, science grows into genetics,
quantitative motion, and the Big Bang, history deepens into periodization and the
ethics of remembrance, and the ethics of technology and the environment mature into
full political and ecological stewardship.

Throughout, the four pillars are interwoven. Intellectual awareness (P3) carries the
most weight — synthesis and argument in language, modeling and proof in mathematics,
the evidence-and-source discipline and systems thinking of science, and the creative
and critical work of the arts — while contextual and ecological awareness (P4) rises
sharply, for high school faces history, political economy, rigorous ethics, and the
deep effects of technology on humanity and the environment. Emotional and social
awareness (P1) remains the ground: identity, values, and purpose consolidate, and
relationships and belonging matter more than ever. A known, cared-for, and capable
body-and-self (P2) is the foundation all of it stands on. Science (D06) and global
history (D08) each now carry a full unit of the year — science as the disciplined
study of living, physical, and Earth systems, history as the long, many-voiced record
of change, power, and liberation — and both spiral into still greater depth in Grades
10–12, where the full scientific and historical ladders unfold. The hardest truths of
the world are faced honestly — inequality, ecological crisis, moral ambiguity —
always met with intellectual tools and a sense of agency, never nihilism. No
high-stakes tests anywhere: a learner's growth is seen through explanations that
state their limits, arguments that weigh conflicting evidence, reasoning across more
than one ethical framework, modeling and proof with real things, made and critiqued
works, and a portfolio of what they say, make, and do as co-authors of their own
education.
