# Lesson 2 — Me and My Society: Shaped and Shaping

## Summary

Learners analyze how a person's **identity is shaped by their society** — family, schooling,
media, culture, laws, the economy, and peers — and how that identity, in turn, **shapes the
choices they make and the society around them**. They map the **two-way influence** in their
own lives and in cases, and close by naming one choice through which they are already shaping
their society back.

## Objectives

- Analyze how a person's identity is shaped by their society and, in turn, shapes their
  choices. _(D01.S2.09.02)_

## Connection

No one becomes who they are alone. The language you speak, the food you grew up eating, the
rules your school or family taught you, the ads and feeds you see, the laws that allow or
block what you can do — all of these shape the person you are becoming. But the arrow does
not point only one way. The very choices you make with that shaped self — what you study,
what you refuse, who you stand with, what you make — bend the people and rules around you
back. In a village, a city, or a migrant camp, in a classroom or online, you are both made by
your world and a maker of it.

## Materials

- Two-way influence map ("me" at center; surrounding nodes, each with an arrow in and an arrow out)
- Identity-and-choice case cards

## Preparation

- Copy the two-way influence map and the case cards.
- Retrieval: from Lesson 1, recall the thread of your own story. Today we ask *who or what
  pulled on that thread* — and how you have pulled back.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **identity is not a private
possession but a two-way exchange — society shapes the self, and the self shapes its society
back through the choices it makes.** The shaping side is the classic insight of socialization:
families, schools, media, laws, and the economy teach us what is normal, possible, and
valued (CASEL's "social awareness" names this ability to read those forces; S-001). The
"shaping back" side is **agency** — and the heart of Erikson's account of identity as an
ongoing, lifelong process, never a finished fact (S-155). The group side draws on social
identity: we take part of our sense of self from the groups we belong to, which is why
belonging can be so powerful — for good and for exclusion (Tajfel's minimal-group work;
S-232). Hold the distinction carefully: the *facts* that societies shape people and people
shape societies are documented; the *values* — that everyone deserves a fair chance to shape
their world — is a commitment, not a measurement (philosophy §5).

The **egalitarian** lens is central: ask *who gets shaped most and listened to least?* —
whose choices are widened and whose are narrowed by the same rules. The **ethics** lens:
if society shapes me, how responsible am I for the society I shape back? The **global** lens:
the shaping forces differ across cultures (some are shaped more by family and tradition,
others by media and markets), but the two-way arrow is human everywhere — avoid any single
culture as the "normal" case. The **technology & environment** lens: today the media,
platforms, and built environments around us are among the most powerful shapers, and among
the things learners can most readily shape back. End on agency, not determinism — a learner
who sees the forces shaping them is *more* free, not less, because they can name and answer
them (facilitation.md).

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From your narrative map: who or what helped make you the person in the
   "present" column? Name two forces quietly to yourself.
2. **Meet the two-way map (8 min).** Look at "me" in the center and the surrounding nodes:
   family, school, media, culture, laws and government, the economy and work, peers. For one
   node, watch a worked example: *media* shapes how a person sees success (arrow **in**); the
   same person, choosing to make and post honest, kind content, shapes what others see
   (arrow **out**). Society shapes me; I shape society.
3. **Map your own two-way influence (12 min).** Fill the map for your own life. For each
   node, write one way it has shaped you, and one way your choices have reached back — a
   habit, a word, a stand, a thing you made or joined.
4. **Analyze cases (15 min).** In a group, take the case cards. For each person: *which
   forces shaped them*, and *which choices shaped their society back*? Notice how the same
   force (a family rule, a law, a trend) can widen one person's choices and narrow another's.
5. **Reflect (10 min).** Write one paragraph: name the force that has shaped you most, and
   one choice through which you are — or want to be — shaping your society back.
6. **Close (5 min).** You are not only a product of your world; you are a producer of it.
   What will you shape?

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide the map with two nodes pre-filled (arrows labeled "shapes me" and "I
  shape it") and ask learners to complete one more.
- **Extension:** Analyze one institution — a school rule, a platform's design, a law — and
  propose one concrete change a young person could realistically push for.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name a force that shaped them and a choice
  through which they shaped (or could shape) their society back?
- **Portfolio artifact (summative for the unit):** The completed two-way influence map and
  reflection paragraph, added to the year-end portfolio.

## Home connection

Ask at home: *what is one rule or habit of our household or neighborhood that shaped you —
and one thing about it you have helped change?* Notice the arrow running both ways in your
own family.
