# Lesson 4 — Care Into Action: Our Cooperative Project — and the Road to Grade 10

## Summary

Learners **design a cooperative project that meets a real need** in their community —
listening first, planning together, and sharing both the deciding and the doing. They gather
the year's **portfolio** and hold a **celebration** of each whole person, the caring community
they built, and the living Earth, then **look ahead to Grade 10**, where a personal narrative
becomes a considered position and care matures into fuller stewardship.

## Objectives

- Design a cooperative project that meets a real need in their community. _(D02.S4.09.02)_

## Connection

Care becomes real only when it becomes action. A need sits all around you — a neighbor who
needs a hand, a shared space that is unsafe or unloved, a younger student who needs a mentor,
a garden or waterway that needs tending, a community whose stories deserve to be heard. The
difference between *noticing* a need and *meeting* it is a plan made *with* the people
affected, not *for* them — everyone giving, everyone deciding, everyone sharing the work and
the credit. That is cooperation, and it is how a classroom becomes a community and a community
becomes a force.

## Materials

- Project design canvas (the need, who is affected, our assets, our plan, roles, how we will know it worked)
- Portfolio folders or binders
- Celebration space and simple refreshments

## Preparation

- Copy the project design canvas; prepare the portfolio folders.
- Retrieval: recall the four threads of this unit — your narrative, the society that shapes
  and is shaped by you, your north-star goal, and the forms of community you stand with. Today
  they become one thing: care put into action, and a celebration of the year.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"), and it is the **capstone**. The idea to land:
**a cooperative project meets a real need when the people affected are partners in deciding
it — everyone gives, everyone decides, everyone shares the credit.** The evidence is strong
that cooperative structures — with individual accountability and shared goals — outperform
competitive or individualistic ones for both achievement and relationships (Johnson & Johnson;
S-233, and Slavin; S-147; Bloom's early estimates put cooperative learning's effect near
0.8 SD — S-007). Self-paced, cooperative, learner-driven schooling of the *Escuela Nueva*
kind shows this works even in one-room, low-resource settings (S-008). The design itself
should honor the distinction from Lesson 3: a cooperative project is **mutual aid**, not
charity — the people affected hold power in the deciding, not only in the receiving (S-345).
Model the canvas once, then let teams design (S-011).

The **egalitarian** lens is the spine: run a fairness check on every design — *who benefits,
who decides, who is left out, and how do we bring them in?* The **ethics** lens: a good
project is judged by whether it leaves people more able to care for themselves and each
other, not more dependent. The **global** lens: the same cooperative impulse takes many
forms worldwide (harambee, minga, gotong royong — S-164), and no one form is "the" right
way. The **technology & environment** lens: a project may be ecological (a shared garden, a
clean-up, a repair) or digital (a shared map, a mutual-aid fund), and it should ask what
technology enables and what it costs. End on **agency, celebration, and hope** — never
nihilism (facilitation.md; development.md §9–12). The celebration is not a ranking: every
portfolio is honored equally, because the curriculum's measure is growth and flourishing,
not scores (philosophy §8, §12). Frame the look-ahead to Grade 10 as *possibility*, not
pressure — purpose is found by trying and reflecting, not by certainty (S-400).

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** Gather the unit: what was your narrative thread, the force that
   shaped you, your north-star goal, and the community you will lean on? Speak one of these
   aloud in a circle if you wish.
2. **Meet the design cycle (8 min).** Look at the cycle: **see a need → listen to the people →
   plan together → act → reflect and adjust**. Watch a worked example: *the need* — younger
   students lack a quiet place to read; *listen* — ask them what they want; *plan together* —
   a rotating reading-corner crew with roles and a schedule; *act* — build and staff it;
   *reflect* — ask whether it worked and change what did not. Everyone decides, everyone does.
3. **Design your project (20 min).** In a team, complete the design canvas for a real need in
   your community — your school, neighborhood, town, or the land around you. Fill each
   section: the need; who is affected and how you will *listen* to them; your assets (skills,
   time, things, people); the plan and roles; and how you will know it worked. Run the
   fairness check: who benefits, who decides, who is left out — and how will you bring them
   in?
4. **Gallery-walk and feedback (10 min).** Visit another team's canvas. Give one specific
   piece of kind feedback: one thing that is strong, and one question that could make it
   stronger. Return and improve your own design.
5. **Celebrate (10 min).** Gather your portfolio — this unit's narrative map, influence map,
   goal and milestones, community comparison, and this project design, alongside the year's
   other work. In a circle, each person shares one thing they are proud of and one person who
   helped them. Celebrate each whole person, the caring community you built, and the living
   Earth.
6. **Look ahead (2 min).** In Grade 10, a personal narrative becomes a considered position;
   identity is examined as socially and historically constructed; and the ethics of
   technology and the environment deepen into fuller stewardship. You are ready — and you are
   already walking the road.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a pre-filled canvas with the need and assets given; teams add the plan
  and one role each.
- **Extension:** Add a budget, a timeline, and a "sustainability" check — what happens after
  the first effort ends, and who keeps it going.

## Assessment

- **Formative (peer):** Does the design name a real need, include listening to the people
  affected, share the deciding and the doing, and state how it will know it worked?
- **Portfolio artifact (summative for the unit and year):** The completed project design
  canvas and the assembled portfolio, celebrated at the year-end gathering.

## Home connection

Bring your portfolio home and share it with someone who cares about you. Ask: *what need does
our own household or street have that we could meet together, even in a small way?* Then
choose one small first step to take.

## Resources

- On cooperative goal structures improving achievement and relationships: Johnson & Johnson
  (1981), "Effects of Cooperative, Competitive, and Individualistic Goal Structures on
  Achievement," *Psychological Bulletin* (S-233); and Slavin (1995), *Cooperative Learning* (S-147).
- On cooperative learning's effect size: Bloom (1984), "The 2 Sigma Problem" (S-007).
- On self-paced, cooperative schooling in low-resource settings: Escuela Nueva (S-008).
- On mutual aid versus charity — sharing the deciding, not only the giving: Spade (2020),
  *Mutual Aid* (S-345).
- On communal-work traditions across cultures: "Communal work" (harambee, gotong royong,
  mink'a/minga, barn raising), <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communal_work> (S-164).
- On purpose developing through trying and reflecting, not sudden certainty: Damon (2008),
  *The Path to Purpose* (S-400).
