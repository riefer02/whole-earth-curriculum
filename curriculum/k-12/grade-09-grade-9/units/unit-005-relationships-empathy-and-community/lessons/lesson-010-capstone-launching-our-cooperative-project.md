# Lesson 10 — Capstone: Launching Our Cooperative Project

## Summary

Learners **launch** the cooperative project they designed, finalizing a plan with a goal, roles, timeline,
check-ins, and a committed first step — then **reflect** across the whole unit, connecting communication,
consent, trust, empathic inquiry, empathy across distance, community forms, in-group loyalty, and systems.

## Objectives

- Design a cooperative project that meets a real need in their community. _(D02.S4.09.02)_
- Communicate respectfully across differences of opinion, culture, or background. _(D02.S1.09.01)_
- Use empathic inquiry to understand another's feelings and needs before responding. _(D02.S2.09.01)_
- Compare forms of community across cultures, including chosen families and mutual-aid networks.
  _(D02.S3.09.01)_

## Connection

A design that stays on paper helps no one. The moment a plan meets the world — the first conversation, the
first task done, the first promise kept — is the moment it becomes real. You have spent this unit learning to
talk across difference, to respect consent, to build trust, to understand before responding, to see how
empathy and community are shaped by distance, loyalty, and systems. Today you gather all of it and set it in
motion: you launch.

## Materials

- Launch plan sheet
- Unit reflection sheet

## Preparation

- Copy the launch plan and reflection sheets.
- Retrieval: this capstone is the unit's retrieval — the plan and reflection deliberately ask learners to
  recall and use every major idea from Lessons 1–9.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The capstone has two jobs: **(1) launch — turn the design from
Lesson 9 into a committed plan: a concrete goal, named roles, a short timeline with check-in points, and one
first step someone will actually take this week; (2) reflect — retrieval across the unit, so the ideas are
recalled, connected, and carried forward (spacing and retrieval, see docs/assessment.md).** Keep the launch
small and real: the first step should be doable within days, not a grand plan that collapses. The egalitarian
lens: check that the plan shares work and power — with the community, not for it (S-345). The ethics lens:
cooperation over competition, and care for the people the project touches. The global lens: this same shape —
people meeting a real need together — is how communities everywhere have thrived (S-163, S-164). The
technology/environment lens: name what tools the project will use and what its effect on the environment will
be. End with agency and hope, not exhaustion — the project is a beginning, not a final exam. Celebrate the
launch.

## Procedure

1. **Recall the unit (10 min).** Quickly name, from memory: the three moves for talking across difference;
  the five parts of consent; how trust is built; the four steps of empathic inquiry; what distance and media
  do to empathy; three forms of community; how in-group loyalty builds *and* limits; and what a system's rules
  shape.
2. **Finalize the plan (15 min).** In your group, take your design from Lesson 9 and make it a **launch
  plan**: the goal, each person's role, a short timeline with check-in points, and — most important — the one
  first step someone will take this week. Use what you learned: decide together, share the work, listen
  first.
3. **Present for feedback (12 min).** Share your plan with another group. Ask them: is the first step real
  and doable this week? Is the work and the deciding shared? Do they see a way to make it better? Adjust.
4. **Commit (8 min).** Write the first step with a name and a date next to it. This is your commitment —
  not to be perfect, but to begin, and to meet again at the first check-in.
5. **Reflect and connect (8 min).** Complete the reflection sheet: which idea from this unit will you carry
  into your relationships? Which one will you use in your project? What did you learn about how people build
  community together?
6. **Close and celebrate (2 min).** Share one word for what this unit gave you. A design became a plan; a
  plan became a promise. Now it is in your hands — and your community's.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide the launch plan with the goal and timeline pre-stated; learners assign one role and
  the first step.
- **Extension:** Add a reflection on how the group will sustain shared decision-making and accountability
  beyond the first week.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner turn the design into a committed plan with a real first step?
- **Summative (portfolio):** The completed launch plan and unit reflection, kept in the portfolio as the
  unit's capstone artifact.
- **Self-assessment:** The learner writes which unit idea they will carry forward, and how they will know the
  project is helping.

## Home connection

Take the first step this week, and tell someone what you did. Bring back one thing you learned from doing it.
