# Lesson 9 — Designing a Cooperative Project: Finding a Real Need

## Summary

Learners design a **cooperative project** that meets a **real need** in their community, using a design loop
(listen, name the need, set a shared goal, divide roles, decide together, take a first step) and the
principle of working *with* people, not just *for* them.

## Objectives

- Design a cooperative project that meets a real need in their community. _(D02.S4.09.02)_

## Connection

Think of something in your community that could be better — a place that is neglected, a person who is
lonely, a problem everyone complains about but no one fixes. Wanting to help is the easy part. The hard part
is doing it *well*: knowing what people actually need (not what you assume), sharing the work fairly, and
deciding together instead of deciding for them. That is the difference between a project that helps and a
project that truly belongs to the community. Today you learn to design the second kind.

## Materials

- Project-design sheet
- Worked-example card

## Preparation

- Copy the design sheet and worked-example card.
- Retrieval: from Grade 7, recall organizing or joining a mutual-aid action (D02.S4.07.01); from Grade 8,
  recall taking part in strengthening belonging in a community.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The ideas to land: **a good cooperative project (1) starts
with listening — learn the need from the people who live it, not from assumptions ("with, not for"); (2)
names the need clearly and who is affected; (3) sets a shared goal everyone can see; (4) divides roles so the
work and the credit are shared fairly — with individual accountability (S-147); (5) builds shared
decision-making, so power is held together, not by one leader — mutual aid is solidarity among equals, not
charity from above (S-345); and (6) takes one concrete first step, because a design becomes real only in
action.** This is a *skill*; use a worked example first, then guided practice (S-011). The egalitarian lens is
the heart: with, not for — the people affected decide. The ethics lens: helping without listening can harm;
the first act of care is to ask. The global lens: cooperative and mutual-aid projects exist in every culture
(S-163, S-164), and this design works in a village or a city, with money or without. The technology/environment
lens: the project can use tools to organize and reach people, and a good project asks what its effect is on
the environment. Keep the projects small, doable, and local — the capstone lesson next will launch one.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Grade 7: what did it feel like to help in a mutual-aid action? Today we turn
   helping into *designing*.
2. **Meet the loop (8 min).** A cooperative project follows a loop: **listen** (learn the need from the
   people who live it), **name the need** (and who is affected), **set a shared goal**, **divide roles**
   (fairly, with accountability), **decide together** (share the power), and **take a first step**.
3. **Study the worked example (10 min).** A neighborhood clean-up — but designed *with* residents: the group
   asks residents what bothers them most (litter by the market, not the park), names the need, sets a shared
   goal, divides roles (who collects, who asks for permission, who tells neighbors), decides together how
   often to meet, and takes one first step (talk to three residents). Trace each part and say why "listen
   first" matters.
4. **Design your own (18 min).** In your group, pick a real, local need — something you can actually touch.
   Fill the design sheet: the need, who is affected, how you will listen to them, the shared goal, roles,
   how you will decide together, and one first step you can take this week.
5. **Peer-check (5 min).** Swap sheets with another group. Check: did they start with listening? Is the need
   real and specific? Is the work shared? Is decision-making shared? Fix what is missing.
6. **Close (4 min).** Share your need and your first step. A design is a promise — the capstone lesson turns
   it into action.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide the design sheet with the need and goal pre-stated; learners fill roles and the first
  step.
- **Extension:** Add a plan for shared decision-making that balances power (for example, rotating the
  facilitator, deciding by discussion rather than majority alone).

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner design a cooperative project that starts with listening and
  shares both work and power?
- **Peer check:** Another group confirms the design listens first and shares roles and decisions.
- **Portfolio artifact:** The completed project-design sheet, kept in the portfolio.

## Home connection

Ask two people in your community: "What is one thing here that you wish someone would fix?" Bring what they
say to the capstone lesson.
