# Lesson 1 — Communicating Across Difference

## Summary

Learners practice **communicating across real difference** — of opinion, culture, or background — using a
three-move skill (**paraphrase, acknowledge, ask**) that is modeled first, then practiced on real
disagreements. The goal is understanding, not winning.

## Objectives

- Communicate respectfully across differences of opinion, culture, or background. _(D02.S1.09.01)_

## Connection

Think of a time you disagreed with someone you otherwise care about — a friend who loves a song you can't
stand, a family member with a rule you think is unfair, a classmate whose view on a hot topic is the
opposite of yours. Maybe you argued until someone won and someone lost, or maybe you both went quiet and
dropped it. There is a third way, used in meeting halls and markets and around fires all over the world:
talk across the difference so that both people leave feeling heard, even when neither changes their mind.
Today you learn that way.

## Materials

- Across-difference conversation card
- Disagreement scenarios

## Preparation

- Copy the conversation card and scenario set.
- Retrieval: from Grade 8, recall how tone, body language, and digital messages can change meaning
  (D02.S1.08.01); from Grade 7, recall setting and respecting a boundary during disagreement.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The skill to land: **communicating across difference has
three moves — (1) paraphrase: say back what you heard, in your own words, to check you understood; (2)
acknowledge: name what is reasonable or real in their view before you respond; (3) ask: ask a genuine
question from curiosity, not as a trap.** This is a *skill*, so use a **worked example first, then guided
practice** (explicit instruction carries skills for novices — S-011). The ethics lens: we owe people dignity
even when we disagree — understanding someone is not agreeing with them. The egalitarian lens: in any
conversation, some voices are louder and some are quieter; across difference, notice who has more power and
make room. The global lens: norms of disagreement differ across cultures — in some places, direct
disagreement is rude and indirect hints are respectful; there is no single "right" way, only the shared goal
of being heard. The technology lens: online, tone and body language vanish, so the same three moves matter
even more in text. Model the worked example fully before pairs practice. Emphasize that the goal is
understanding, not winning or giving up.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Grade 8: how can tone, body language, and digital messages change the meaning of
   what we say? Today we add a skill for talking *across* a difference.
2. **Meet the three moves (8 min).** When you talk with someone you disagree with, three moves keep the
   conversation human: **paraphrase** (say back what you heard), **acknowledge** (name what is reasonable in
   their view), **ask** (ask a real question from curiosity).
3. **Study the worked example (10 min).** Scenario: two neighbors disagree about whether a shared field
   should become a market or a garden. One says: "A market would bring money and jobs; a garden is a waste."
   The other responds with all three moves: *"You're saying the market would help people earn and the garden
   wouldn't (paraphrase). The money-and-jobs point is a real one (acknowledge). What worries you about a
   garden, or what would make a garden feel worthwhile to you (ask)?"* Trace each move and say why it works.
4. **Practice in pairs (18 min).** Pick a scenario from the set (a group decision, a family rule, a school
   policy, a community question). Person A states their view. Person B responds using all three moves — and
   does *not* argue back yet. Then switch roles.
5. **Peer-check (5 min).** Ask your partner: did I say your view back accurately? Did I name something real
   in it? Was my question a genuine one? Fix what was missing.
6. **Close (4 min).** Share one move you will keep. Understanding is not the same as agreeing — and that is
   exactly what makes it possible.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide sentence stems — "What I hear you saying is…", "One thing I see in your view is…",
  "I wonder…".
- **Extension:** Add a fourth move: name where your own view might be incomplete ("I might be missing
  something because…").
- **Non-speaking / AAC:** The three moves are ways of *understanding*, not of voice — a learner can
  paraphrase, acknowledge, and ask by writing, pointing to the stems, signing, or using a device; the
  partner responds to whatever the learner produced.
- **Deaf / hard of hearing:** Run the three moves in sign, writing, or text, not only speech; a paraphrase
  signed or written back is a real paraphrase, and the "ask" move works just as well on paper.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner paraphrase, acknowledge, and ask in a real exchange?
- **Peer check:** Partner confirms the three moves were present and genuine.
- **Portfolio artifact:** The completed conversation card, kept in the portfolio.

## Home connection

Notice one difference of opinion at home or in your neighborhood this week. Before responding, try one move:
say back what you heard, or ask one genuine question. Bring what happened to the next lesson.
