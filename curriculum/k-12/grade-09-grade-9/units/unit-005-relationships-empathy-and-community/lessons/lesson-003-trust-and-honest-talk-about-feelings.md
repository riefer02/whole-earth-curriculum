# Lesson 3 — Trust and Honest Talk About Feelings

## Summary

Learners name the small behaviors that **build trust** (reliability, honesty, respect, care, owning
mistakes) and practice **honest talk about feelings** with I-statements, including how to begin **repairing**
a relationship after a rupture.

## Objectives

- Practice healthy relationship skills, including consent, trust, and honest talk about feelings.
  _(D02.S1.09.02)_

## Connection

Think of a person you trust without thinking about it — a friend who keeps your secrets, a sibling who
shows up, an adult who believes you. Now think of how that trust was built: not in one grand gesture, but in
a hundred small moments — kept promises, told truths, respected feelings, owned mistakes. Trust is not
automatic and not free; it is built slowly and can be cracked quickly. And when it cracks, honest talk is
how it begins to mend. Today you learn both halves — building it and repairing it.

## Materials

- Trust-builder sheet
- I-statement worked example

## Preparation

- Copy the trust-builder sheet and worked example.
- Retrieval: from Grade 3, recall using "I" statements to say how you feel without blaming (D02.S1.03.02);
  from Grade 8, recall how tone and body language change meaning.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The ideas to land: **(1) trust is built by small, repeated
behaviors — reliability (doing what you said), honesty (telling the truth, kindly), respect (honoring
boundaries and differences), care (showing up for the other), and owning mistakes (admitting and repairing
them); (2) a rupture — a hurt or a breach — is normal in close relationships, and trust is rebuilt through
repair, not silence; (3) honest talk about feelings uses the I-statement — "I feel ___ when ___, because I
need ___" — which owns your feeling without blaming.** Model the I-statement frame fully, then guided
practice (S-011). The ethics lens: honesty is a form of care, not a weapon; repair is a duty we owe people
we love. The egalitarian lens: trust should be mutual, not one-way — in healthy relationships, both people
can speak and both are heard, across differences of age, gender, or status. The global lens: how openly
feelings are spoken varies across cultures and families — some name feelings directly, some show them through
action; neither is "better," and honest talk can happen in many registers. The technology lens: in text,
tone vanishes, so honest talk about feelings needs extra care and sometimes a spoken follow-up. Keep this
gentle and normalizing — every close relationship has ruptures.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Grade 3: what is an "I" statement, and why does it help? Today we put it to work
   in trust and repair.
2. **Meet trust (10 min).** **Trust** is built by small, repeated behaviors. Sort the examples on your sheet
   into the five builders: **reliability, honesty, respect, care, owning mistakes.** Which builds trust, and
   which cracks it?
3. **Meet the I-statement (8 min).** Honest talk about feelings uses one frame: **"I feel ___ when ___,
   because I need ___."** It owns your feeling and names your need — without blaming the other person.
4. **Study the worked example (8 min).** _Blaming:_ "You always cancel on me and it's so rude." _Honest:_ "I
   feel hurt and a little anxious when plans get canceled at the last minute, because I need to be able to
   count on our time together." Say why the second one is easier to hear and to act on.
5. **Practice in pairs (12 min).** Use the frame on three real-feeling situations: a friend forgot something
   important to you; someone said something that stung; a group left you out of a plan. Switch roles and
   give each other feedback.
6. **Meet repair (6 min).** When trust cracks, repair begins with an honest I-statement, a real apology, and
   an agreed next step. Draft one repair sentence for a rupture you imagined.
7. **Close (3 min).** Share one trust-builder you want to practice this week.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide the I-statement frame as a fill-in, with feeling and need words listed to choose from.
- **Extension:** Draft a full repair conversation — honest I-statement, apology that names the harm, and an
  agreed next step.
- **Non-speaking / AAC / Deaf / hard of hearing:** An I-statement can be written, signed, or pointed to (using
  the feeling and need word bank) — never only spoken. Honest talk is about honesty, not about using a voice;
  trust-building and repair are practiced in whatever mode the learner owns.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner sort trust-building behaviors and form an honest I-statement?
- **Peer check:** Partner confirms the I-statement names a feeling and a need without blaming.
- **Portfolio artifact:** The completed trust-builder sheet and one I-statement, kept in the portfolio.

## Home connection

Notice one small moment this week when someone kept a promise or told the truth — and name it aloud: "I
noticed you… and it builds my trust." Try one honest I-statement at home if a feeling needs saying.
