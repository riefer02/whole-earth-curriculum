# Lesson 2 — Consent: Yes, No, and Changing Your Mind

## Summary

Learners practice **consent** — asking for it, giving it, and hearing "no" — using the **"FRIES" memory aid**
(freely given, reversible, informed, enthusiastic, specific) and everyday ask-and-answer scenarios.

## Objectives

- Practice healthy relationship skills, including consent, trust, and honest talk about feelings.
  _(D02.S1.09.02)_

## Connection

Consent is not only about one kind of relationship; it runs through ordinary life. Borrowing a phone, hugging
a friend hello, adding someone to a group, sharing a photo, making a plan — every one of these is a small
ask and a small answer. A "yes" that is pressured, half-hearted, or not really free isn't a real yes; and a
"yes" that can't be changed into a "no" isn't a real yes either. Today you learn to ask clearly, answer
honestly, and hear "no" without making it hurt.

## Materials

- Consent choices card
- Ask-and-answer scenario set

## Preparation

- Copy the consent choices card and scenario set.
- Retrieval: from Grade 7, recall setting and respecting personal boundaries (D02.S1.07.01); from Grade 5,
  recall body autonomy and "my body is my home."

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The ideas to land: **consent is (1) freely given — not
pressured, not assumed, not owed; (2) reversible — anyone can change their mind at any point, and a later
"no" overrides an earlier "yes"; (3) informed — everyone knows what they are agreeing to; (4) enthusiastic — a genuine yes, not a reluctant shrug; and (5) specific — yes
to one thing is not yes to everything.** Use the
"FRIES" memory aid (freely given, reversible, informed, enthusiastic, specific). This is a *skill*, so model
an ask-and-answer pair fully before learners practice (S-011). The ethics lens: respect for another's
autonomy is the ground of care. The egalitarian lens: everyone's body, belongings, and choices deserve the
same respect, no matter their age, status, or gender — and power differences (popularity, age, authority)
can make a "yes" not truly free, so watch for them. The global lens: the *principle* — that a person must
freely agree — is shared across cultures, even though customs about how to ask and show agreement differ.
The technology lens: consent applies online too — asking before sharing a photo, tagging, or posting about
someone. Keep it ordinary and non-frightening; this is about everyday autonomy, not only about sex.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Grade 7: what is a boundary, and how do you know when someone has set one? Today
   we name the yes-and-no behind every boundary.
2. **Meet the idea (8 min).** **Consent** is a free, informed, changeable "yes." Meet the five parts: freely
   given, reversible, informed, enthusiastic, specific — the "FRIES" aid.
3. **Study the worked example (10 min).** _Asking:_ "Can I borrow your phone to check the time?" _Giving a
   real yes:_ "Sure, for a minute." _A reversible no:_ "Not right now — maybe later." Trace each part: the ask
   is specific, the yes is free and reversible, and a "no" is answered with "okay, thanks" — not pressure,
   not guilt.
4. **Practice in pairs (15 min).** Work through the scenario set — borrowing, a hug, a shared plan, sharing a
   photo. Person A asks. Person B answers — sometimes yes, sometimes no, sometimes "I changed my mind."
   Person A practices hearing the answer with grace, then switch roles.
5. **Peer-check (5 min).** Ask your partner: was my ask clear and specific? Did I hear "no" without pushing?
   Fix what was missing.
6. **Close (4 min).** Share one sentence you now know how to say — to ask, to say yes, or to say no kindly.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide sentence stems — "Is it okay if I…?", "I'm comfortable with… but not…", "Thanks for
  asking, but no."
- **Extension:** Discuss a power-imbalanced scenario (an older or more popular person asking) and name why a
  "yes" there might not be free.
- **Non-speaking / AAC / Deaf / hard of hearing:** A "yes," "no," or "changed my mind" can be given by sign,
  gesture, a point to the card, writing, or a device — never only by voice. Every learner must be able to give
  *and withdraw* consent in their own mode, and "hearing no" means accepting the answer however it is
  expressed.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner ask clearly, answer honestly, and hear "no" without pressure?
- **Peer check:** Partner confirms the ask was specific and the "no" was respected.
- **Portfolio artifact:** The completed consent choices card, kept in the portfolio.

## Home connection

Practice one clear ask and one kind "no thanks" at home this week — something small, like borrowing or
joining a plan — and notice how it feels to be asked and answered clearly.
