# Lesson 6 — Societies & Their Environments Over Deep Time

## Summary

Learners analyze how societies changed — and were changed by — their environments over
long spans of time, through three cases: **Angkor's water system**, the **Maya lowlands**,
and **Great Zimbabwe**. They learn that **adaptation** and **decline** both matter, and
that decline is usually **multi-causal** — not one simple story.

## Objectives

- Analyze how societies changed and were changed by their environments over long spans of
  time, including adaptation and cases of decline, using evidence. _(D08.S3.09.01)_

## Connection

Walk outside and notice the land: a slope, a river, the soil, the weather. Every place
people live, they have *shaped* — by terracing a hillside, digging a well, planting a
field, building a city. And every place has *shaped them back* — what they can grow, how
they build, when they must move. This two-way relationship runs through all of history.
Today we trace it over long spans of time.

## Materials

- Case cards
- Society–environment sheet

## Preparation

- Copy the case cards and the sheet.
- Retrieval: from Lesson 3, recall the five cities and where each stood. Today we ask the
  deeper question — how did land and society shape each other?

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **societies and their
environments shape each other in both directions. People adapt — building terraces,
canals, and reservoirs to manage their land — and environments also constrain people,
through drought, flood, and soil. When societies decline, it is usually *many* causes at
once, not one simple story.** Three documented cases: **(1) Angkor** — the Khmer built a
vast water system of reservoirs and canals to manage the monsoons, an impressive
adaptation; the city's decline in the 14th–15th centuries is debated, with evidence
pointing to climate stress *and* strain on the water infrastructure together (S-471).
**(2) The Maya lowlands** — the Classic Maya (~250–900 CE) adapted with terracing and
reservoirs, but many southern-lowland cities were abandoned around 800–950 CE; scholars
now see drought, warfare, and environmental stress working together, not a single cause
(S-378). **(3) Great Zimbabwe** — the Shona built a stone city and a gold-trading economy
in the 11th–15th centuries CE, an adaptation to the region's resources; its later decline
is likewise multi-causal (S-199). The **environment** lens is the heart here. The
**technology** lens: water and building engineering were the tools of adaptation. The
**global** lens: this two-way story plays out on every continent. The **critical-thinking**
lens: resist single-cause stories of "collapse" — good history weighs multiple causes and
the evidence for each. Hold decline with care and accuracy, and end on the continuity of
peoples, not only on ruin (facilitation.md).

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Lesson 3: name one city and where it stood. Today we ask a
   deeper question — how did the land and the society *shape each other*?
2. **Meet three cases (12 min).** Read the cards. For each, note the *adaptation* and the
   *decline*:
   - **Angkor:** reservoirs and canals managed the monsoons; decline debated — climate
     stress and strained waterworks together.
   - **Maya lowlands:** terracing and reservoirs; many cities abandoned ~800–950 CE under
     drought, warfare, and environmental stress together.
   - **Great Zimbabwe:** a stone city and gold trade; later decline also multi-causal.
3. **Two-way sheet (15 min).** Fill the sheet. *People shaped the land:* terraces, canals,
   reservoirs, stone walls. *The land shaped people:* what could be grown, when the rains
   came, when the soil tired.
4. **Weigh the causes (12 min).** Choose one case. List the causes of decline the evidence
   points to. Why is "it collapsed for one reason" almost always too simple?
5. **Connect to now (8 min).** Where do you see societies and environments shaping each
   other today — in how your region farms, builds, or responds to weather?
6. **Close (5 min).** People adapt, and environments answer back. Next: a forced movement
   of people that changed the whole world.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide the two-way sheet pre-filled for one case and ask learners to
  match each fact to "people shaped the land" or "the land shaped people."
- **Extension:** Evaluate the evidence for one site's decline and argue which causes the
  evidence supports most strongly, naming your reasoning.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name one adaptation and one cause of
  decline for one case, and explain why decline is multi-causal?
- **Portfolio artifact:** The completed two-way sheet, kept in the portfolio.

## Home connection

Ask at home: how has our family or community changed the land where we live — and how has
the land changed how we live? Write down one example of each.
