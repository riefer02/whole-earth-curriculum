# Lesson 1 — The Long Story, Told from Many Voices

## Summary

Learners **recall four Grade-8 ideas** — long-distance trade networks, colonization,
slavery, and resistance — and **preview this unit's four strands and seven goals**. They
meet the unit's organizing idea: global history traces **change and continuity** across
regions and centuries, and it is told from **many voices** — never as a single nation's
triumph.

## Objectives

- Begin to name what a **continuity-and-change analysis** of one global process looks
  like — the unit's first goal. _(D08.S1.09.01)_

## Connection

Think of anything that has been around longer than you have — a road, a river, a
language, a family custom, a market. Some things about it have changed, and some have
stayed the same. History is the careful study of exactly that: what changes, what
endures, and whose voices get to tell the story. Today we remember what we already know
how to do and see where it is taking us.

## Materials

- Unit map sheet
- Recall cards

## Preparation

- Copy the unit map sheet and recall cards.
- Retrieval: from Grade 8, recall (a) long-distance trade networks, (b) colonization,
  (c) slavery, and (d) resistance. Today we link those to the new unit.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It is the unit's **opening and retrieval
pass**, so the goals are modest: **(1) recall four Grade-8 ideas — trade networks (S-323),
colonization (S-326), slavery and the transatlantic trade (S-322, S-274), and resistance;
(2) preview the unit's four strands — time/change/continuity, world cultures & knowledge
traditions, geography & migration, power/resistance/liberation — and its seven goals;
(3) plant the organizing idea: global history traces *change and continuity* and is told
from *many voices*.** Do not try to teach the seven goals today; just name and locate
them. The **global** lens: the same human story looks different depending on whose
records, songs, and memories we read — so we hold many side by side. The **egalitarian**
lens: whose voices have been left out of the textbooks, and whose have been centered?
The **ethics** lens: telling history honestly means naming harm and giving credit
accurately, not glorifying one nation. The **critical-thinking** lens: "change and
continuity" is the analytic habit we carry forward all unit. Keep questions open: history
is offered as questions and competing accounts, not a single revealed story (philosophy
§11). Spacing note (S-039): this recall pass is what makes the unit's ideas stick later.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (10 min).** In a small group, look at four recall cards: **trade networks**,
   **colonization**, **slavery**, **resistance**. For each, say in one sentence what you
   remember from Grade 8.
2. **Connect the four (5 min).** What do all four have in common? (Each is about how
   people, goods, ideas, and power move across the world — and about who gains and who
   is harmed.)
3. **Meet the map (10 min).** Look at the unit map. Four strands: **time, change &
   continuity**; **world cultures & knowledge traditions**; **geography & migration**;
   **power, resistance & liberation**. Seven goals across them. Read each goal aloud
   once; don't worry about understanding them yet.
4. **Plant the big idea (10 min).** The unit's idea in one breath: global history traces
   **change and continuity** — what shifts and what endures — across regions and
   centuries, and it is told from **many voices**, never as one nation's triumph. Say it
   back in your own words.
5. **Wonder (10 min).** Write one question about each strand in the wonder box. Which
   question do you most want answered by the end of the unit?
6. **Close (5 min).** Today we remembered what we know and saw the road ahead. Next: we
   trace one global process — long-distance trade — across centuries.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Give sentence starters for the recall round ("A trade network is…").
- **Extension:** Add a fourth Grade-8 idea of your own and say why it will matter in this
  unit.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner recall each Grade-8 idea in one sentence
  and name the unit's four strands?
- **Artifact:** The completed unit map (or the learner's written recall), kept in the
  portfolio.

## Home connection

Ask someone at home: what is one thing in our family or community that has *changed*
over the generations, and one thing that has *stayed the same*? Bring both to the next
lesson.
