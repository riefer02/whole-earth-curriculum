# Unit 12 — Global History: Change, Power & Liberation

## Overview

This unit gives Grade 9 its full pass through global history, building on the opener's
single standard of historical perspective and on Grade 8's work with sources, trade
networks, colonization, slavery, and resistance. Learners analyze continuity and
change in one global process, such as long-distance trade or the growth of cities,
across several regions and centuries. They analyze how a knowledge tradition was
suppressed, marginalized, or lost through colonialism or conquest and how peoples have
worked to preserve or revive it, and explain how the mixing of cultures created new
traditions and identities. They analyze how societies changed and were changed by
their environments over long spans of time, including adaptation and cases of decline,
and analyze the transatlantic slave trade and other forced migrations as global
movements of people with lasting effects. They analyze colonialism and empire as
systems of power and the anti-colonial movements that fought for liberation, and
explain how ideas of freedom and dignity spread and fueled struggles for liberation
across different continents. History is told from many perspectives, never as a single
nation's triumph; the voices of those too often left out are centered with care and
accuracy, and questions are held as questions.

## Essential questions

- How does one global process, such as long-distance trade or the growth of cities, change and stay the same across regions and centuries?
- How were knowledge traditions suppressed or lost through colonialism, and how have peoples worked to preserve and revive them?
- How did the mixing of cultures create new traditions and identities, and how have societies changed and been changed by their environments over long spans?
- How did empire, slavery, and forced migration reshape the world, and how did ideas of freedom and dignity fuel struggles for liberation?
