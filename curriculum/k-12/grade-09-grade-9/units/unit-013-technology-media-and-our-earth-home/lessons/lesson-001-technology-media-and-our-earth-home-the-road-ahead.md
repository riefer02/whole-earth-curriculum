# Lesson 1 — Technology, Media & Our Earth Home: The Road Ahead

## Summary

Learners **recall three Grade-8 ideas** — framed or edited images, deepfakes, and
ecosystem resilience — and **preview this unit's two arcs and eight goals**. They meet
the unit's organizing idea: the tools and media we make, and the living systems we are
part of, are read with **critical judgment — neither worship nor fear.**

## Objectives

- Begin to name what it means that **software and algorithms shape what people see and
  choose** — the unit's first goal. _(D10.S1.09.01)_

## Connection

Think of two things in front of you right now: a screen, and the world outside a window.
The screen shows you a small, chosen slice of everything there is — chosen partly by
software. The world outside is the one system that holds everyone: its air, water, soil,
and living things. This unit is about reading both with clear eyes: neither trusting a
screen blindly nor fearing the world's changes, but understanding each well enough to act.

## Materials

- Unit map sheet
- Recall cards

## Preparation

- Copy the unit map sheet and recall cards.
- Retrieval: from Grade 8, recall (a) framed or edited images, (b) deepfakes, and
  (c) ecosystem resilience. Today we link those to the new unit.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It is the unit's **opening and retrieval
pass**, so the goals are modest: **(1) recall three Grade-8 ideas — framed/edited images
(S-393), deepfakes (S-393), and ecosystem resilience; (2) preview the unit's two arcs —
media &amp; technology (goals 1–4) and our Earth home (goals 5–8); (3) plant the organizing
idea: read the screen and the Earth with *critical judgment, neither worship nor fear*.** Do
not try to teach the eight goals today; just name and locate them. The **technology** lens
and the **environment** lens are the two halves of the unit, deliberately joined. The
**ethics** lens: how we use tools and how we treat the Earth are questions about what is
right and what the consequences are for others. The **egalitarian** lens: ask who is
centered and who is left out — online and in environmental change. The **critical-thinking**
lens: this unit is one long exercise in holding questions open and weighing evidence
(philosophy §11). Spacing note (S-039): this recall pass is what makes the unit's ideas
stick later.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (10 min).** In a small group, look at three recall cards: **framed or edited
   images**, **deepfakes**, **ecosystem resilience**. For each, say in one sentence what
   you remember from Grade 8.
2. **Connect the three (5 min).** What do all three have in common? (Each is about how
   what we *see* or what a system *can absorb* is shaped — and about learning to look
   closely.)
3. **Meet the map (10 min).** Look at the unit map. Two arcs: **media &amp; technology**
   (algorithms and feeds; who controls the platform; echo chambers and breaking out;
   tracing a viral claim) and **our Earth home** (the greenhouse effect; the carbon
   cycle; feedback loops; energy and environmental justice). Read each goal aloud once;
   don't worry about understanding them yet.
4. **Plant the big idea (10 min).** The unit's idea in one breath: we read the tools and
   media humans make, and the living systems we are part of, with **critical judgment —
   neither worship nor fear.** Say it back in your own words.
5. **Wonder (10 min).** Write one question about each arc in the wonder box. Which
   question do you most want answered by the end of the unit?
6. **Close (5 min).** Today we remembered what we know and saw the road ahead. Next: how
   the feed on your screen is built.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Give sentence starters for the recall round ("A deepfake is…").
- **Extension:** Add a fourth Grade-8 idea of your own and say why it will matter in this
  unit.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner recall each Grade-8 idea in one sentence
  and name the unit's two arcs?
- **Artifact:** The completed unit map (or the learner's written recall), kept in the
  portfolio.

## Home connection

Ask someone at home: what is one thing our family or community *sees on screens* that we
should look at more closely, and one thing about our *local environment* that has changed
over the years? Bring both to the next lesson.
