# Lesson 1 — Three Lenses on Right and Wrong

## Summary

Learners **recall Grade-8 ideas** about weighing consequences, duties, and fairness, and
**preview this unit's four strands and the three ethical lenses**. They meet the unit's
organizing idea: a moral question can be read through **more than one lens**, and each lens
shows something the others miss.

## Objectives

- Begin to name three ethical frameworks — consequences, duties, and virtues — as ways to read a
  moral question. _(D09.S1.09.01)_

## Connection

Think of a time you faced a choice where more than one thing seemed right at once — tell a hard
truth that might hurt a friend, or stay quiet to spare them; share a scarce resource, or keep it
for someone who needs it more. Every such moment is a **moral question**, and none of us has just
one way of answering it. Today we meet three **lenses** — consequences, duties, and virtues — that
people all over the world, in every culture and time, have used to think such questions through.

## Materials

- Unit map sheet
- Recall cards

## Preparation

- Copy the unit map sheet and recall cards.
- Retrieval: from Grade 8, recall (a) weighing consequences, duties, and fairness in a dilemma;
  (b) the difference between a fact, a belief, and a value; and (c) that justice has more than one
  meaning. Today we link those to the new unit.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It is the unit's **opening and retrieval pass**, so
the goals are modest: **(1) recall three Grade-8 ideas — a moral dilemma can be weighed by
consequences, duties, and fairness; facts, beliefs, and values are different kinds of claims; and
justice has more than one meaning; (2) preview the unit's four strands — moral reasoning & values;
fairness, justice & rights; civic participation & collective action; peace, conflict & restorative
practice; (3) plant the organizing idea: a moral question is read through more than one *lens*
(consequences, duties, virtues), and each lens shows something the others miss.** Do not teach the
three lenses in depth today; just name and locate them. The ethics lens: right and wrong are
questions we reason about, not slogans we accept. The egalitarian lens: always ask who benefits
and who is left out. The global lens: every culture has ways of thinking about a good life and a
fair society — none is the default. Hold questions open: nothing here is creed (philosophy §11).
Distinguish evidence from values throughout (philosophy §5). Spacing note (S-039): this recall
pass is what makes the unit's ideas stick later.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (10 min).** In a small group, look at three recall cards: **weighing consequences,
   duties, and fairness**; **fact vs. belief vs. value**; **justice has more than one meaning**.
   For each, say in one sentence what you remember from Grade 8.
2. **Connect the three (5 min).** What do all three have in common? (Each is about *how we think
   about right and wrong* — carefully, and with more than one answer on the table.)
3. **Meet the map (10 min).** Look at the unit map. Four strands: **moral reasoning & values**,
   **fairness, justice & rights**, **civic participation & collective action**, **peace, conflict
   & restorative practice**. Read each goal aloud once; don't worry about understanding them yet.
4. **Plant the big idea (10 min).** The unit's idea in one breath: a moral question can be read
   through **three lenses** — **consequences** (what happens, to whom?), **duties** (what do I owe,
   no matter what?), and **virtues** (what kind of person would I become?). Say it back in your own
   words.
5. **Wonder (10 min).** Write one question about each strand in the wonder box. Which question do
   you most want answered by the end of the unit?
6. **Close (5 min).** Today we remembered what we know and saw the road ahead. Next: we look
   closely through the first lens — consequences.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Give sentence starters for the recall round ("A moral dilemma is a choice where…").
- **Extension:** Add a fourth Grade-8 idea of your own and say why it will matter in this unit.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner recall each Grade-8 idea in one sentence and name
  the unit's four strands and three lenses?
- **Artifact:** The completed unit map (or the learner's written recall), kept in the portfolio.

## Home connection

Ask someone at home: what is one decision you still think about — where you had to weigh what
would happen against what you felt you owed? Bring the shape of the story (no names needed) to the
next lesson.
