# Lesson 14 — Capstone: Reasoning Across the Whole

## Summary

Learners gather the unit's eight ideas into one web and write a short **synthesis**, showing how
reasoning across frameworks, justice and rights, power and accountability, and peace and repair
belong to a single way of seeing a shared life. The capstone doubles as a spaced, interleaved
retrieval pass and the unit's closing celebration.

## Objectives

- Reconnect and apply the unit's eight goals — reasoning across frameworks, conscience and
  self-criticism, systemic injustice, human rights, power and accountability, democracy and its
  alternatives, the roots of conflict, and reconciliation — in one synthesis. _(all eight grade-9
  D09 objectives)_

## Connection

You have spent this unit learning eight ways of seeing the common life — how to reason about a hard
choice, how to spot unfairness built into a system, how to weigh power and democracy, how to
understand conflict and repair harm. None of these stands alone. A person who can hold them
together is a person who can help build a fairer world. Today you weave them into one whole — and
decide what you will carry with you.

## Materials

- Capstone web sheet
- Portfolio folder
- Synthesis prompt

## Preparation

- Copy the web sheet and synthesis prompt.
- Have learners gather their portfolio artifacts from this unit. Prepare the synthesis prompt —
  e.g., "Choose one real question from your own community or the wider world. Read it through the
  three lenses, name one systemic or rights dimension, and one question of power or peace it
  raises. What would you do, and why?"

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It is the unit's **capstone and spaced retrieval
pass**: recall all eight goals, connect them into one web, then synthesize (Dunlosky et al. 2013,
S-039). The goals: **(1) retrieve each idea from memory before opening notes; (2) connect the eight
into a single web, so learners see them as one discipline rather than eight lessons; (3) write a
short synthesis on one real question — the unit's summative demonstration; (4) celebrate mastery
without ranking (philosophy §8).** Keep it open: learners may weigh the ideas differently and reach
different conclusions — the standard is *reasoned synthesis*, not a single right answer (philosophy
§11). The ethics lens: the whole is a way of asking "what is right, and for whom?" The egalitarian
lens: the synthesis must keep asking who benefits and who is left out. The global lens: the
synthesis should honor that these ideas appear in many cultural forms. The community lens: close
with what each learner will *do* — agency, never nihilism (philosophy §12). This is the unit's
final portfolio artifact.

## Procedure

1. **Retrieve (10 min).** Without opening your notes, name the unit's eight ideas from memory:
   three lenses; conscience; systemic injustice; human rights; power; democracy; roots of conflict;
   reconciliation. Check against the web sheet — fill in any you missed.
2. **Build the web (10 min).** On the capstone web sheet, draw one line connecting two ideas and
   write, along the line, how they belong together (e.g., "systemic injustice ↔ power: unfair
   arrangements are held in place by who has power"). Add at least three connections.
3. **Choose a question (5 min).** Read the synthesis prompt and choose one real question — from your
   community or the wider world — that genuinely matters to you.
4. **Synthesize (20 min).** Write one strong paragraph: read your question through the three lenses;
   name one systemic or rights dimension; name one question of power or peace it raises; and say
   what you would do and why. Use at least three of the unit's ideas by name.
5. **Share and celebrate (10 min).** In a closing circle, each person shares one sentence: one idea
   they will carry, and one thing they will do with it. No ranking — only appreciation.
6. **Close (5 min).** You have reasoned across the whole. Carry these tools into the world — and
   into Grade 10, where they deepen further.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide sentence starters for the synthesis ("My question is…"; "The three lenses
  show…"; "The question of power here is…").
- **Extension:** Write the synthesis as a short, reasoned position on one live question, citing the
  unit's ideas and acknowledging the strongest counter-argument.

## Assessment

- **Summative (portfolio):** Does the synthesis use at least three of the unit's ideas by name,
  reason across more than one lens, and end in agency rather than despair?
- **Self:** The learner reviews their own folder and names the idea that grew most in their
  thinking.

## Home connection

Show someone at home your synthesis, and tell them the one thing you will do with it. Ask them
what they think — and listen.
