# Lesson 10 — Power & Accountability

## Summary

Learners analyze how **power is distributed** in a society and how **citizens can hold it to
account**, using a specific case. They learn that power flows down (decisions, resources, rules)
and must flow back up through **accountability** — elections, a free press, independent courts,
protest, and organizing — and they produce a **case study** as a portfolio artifact.

## Objectives

- Analyze how power is distributed in a society and how citizens can hold power accountable, using
  a specific case. _(D09.S3.09.01)_

## Connection

Every day, people with power — officials, employers, landowners, platform owners — make decisions
that shape your life. The question of a fair society is not only *who has power* but *whether the
rest of us can answer back*. A society where power flows only downward is one where abuse can
thrive; a society where power flows both ways is one where people can demand an accounting. Today
you study both directions.

## Materials

- Power and accountability sheet
- Case study

## Preparation

- Copy the sheet and case studies.
- Choose one case where accountability worked and one where it failed (from different regions), each
  short and sourced, so learners can compare, not just admire or condemn.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Its goals: **(1) teach power and accountability
precisely — power is distributed unevenly across a society (government, companies, media,
landowners, platforms), and accountability is the set of ways the governed answer back: elections,
a free press, independent courts, protest, organizing, and other checks; (2) model once, then
guide: analyze one case whole-group, then let learners analyze another (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark
2006, S-011); (3) avoid dogma — present democracy's accountability tools as *some* ways, not the
only ones, and do not rank one society's arrangements above another's (philosophy §11).** The
ethics lens: power without accountability is a moral problem. The egalitarian lens: ask who has
power, who is left out, and which accountability tools the least powerful can actually use. The
global lens: accountability looks different across societies — formal checks in some, community
and customary mechanisms in others — none is the default. Distinguish evidence from values: *that*
power is unevenly distributed is a documented fact; *that* it should be held to account is a value
we argue for. This is a portfolio artifact — keep the case study.

## Procedure

1. **Retrieval (5 min).** From memory: what does "systemic" mean, and how does it differ from one
   person's wrongdoing? (One sentence.)
2. **Meet the diagram (10 min).** Read the power and accountability sheet. Power flows **down** —
   decisions, resources, rules. Accountability flows **up** — elections, a free press, independent
   courts, protest, organizing. A fair society needs both directions.
3. **Worked example (15 min).** Together, analyze one case: (a) who holds power? (b) how is it
   distributed — who is left out? (c) what tools did citizens use to answer back? (d) did
   accountability work, and why or why not?
4. **Analyze a second case (15 min).** In groups, work a contrasting case through the same four
   questions. End by naming the one accountability tool that mattered most — and one that was
   missing.
5. **Compare (5 min).** What did the successful case have that the failed one lacked? Is it always
   a single tool, or a combination?
6. **Close (5 min).** Power is answered by accountability — and accountability must be built.
   Next: how does democracy compare with the alternatives?

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide the four questions pre-printed and one worked answer to model.
- **Extension:** Compare a formal check (courts, elections) with an informal one (press, protest,
  organizing) and argue which mattered more in your case — and why.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name who holds power, how it is distributed, and at
  least two accountability tools, grounded in the case?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The completed power-and-accountability case study.

## Home connection

Ask someone at home: who holds power over something that affects our family, and how — if at all —
can ordinary people answer back? Bring one sentence next time.
