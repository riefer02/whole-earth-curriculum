# Lesson 8 — Tracing a Systemic Injustice

## Summary

Learners apply the three-layer analysis to **one documented case**, producing the unit's
**systemic-injustice trace** artifact. They build a causal chain from a past rule through a
structure and repeated actions to an unfair outcome, then identify **where the loop could be
broken** — moving from analysis to possible change.

## Objectives

- Analyze a systemic injustice, tracing how individual actions, rules, and structures combine to
  produce unfair outcomes, and identify where the pattern could be changed. _(D09.S2.09.01)_

## Connection

Every unfair pattern has a history — a rule set long ago, a structure that hardened around it, and
countless small choices that kept it going. Tracing that chain is the first step to changing it,
because you cannot fix a pattern you cannot see. Today you follow one injustice from **cause to
outcome** and ask the question every reformer asks: **where could this loop be broken?**

## Materials

- Injustice trace sheet
- Case dossier

## Preparation

- Copy the trace sheet and case dossiers.
- Choose a globally varied set of case dossiers (e.g., unequal access to land, water, schooling,
  or fair wages in different regions), each short and sourced, so learners can trace a real,
  documented pattern without overwhelm.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Its goals: **(1) move learners from the *idea* of
systemic injustice (Lesson 7) to a *skill* — building a causal chain from a past rule → a structure
→ repeated small actions → an unfair outcome; (2) model once, then guide: build one chain
whole-group, then let each learner build their own from a dossier (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark 2006,
S-011); (3) end in agency, not despair — the dashed arrow ("where could the loop be broken?")
turns analysis into possible change (philosophy §12).** The ethics lens: naming an injustice
accurately is an act of care. The egalitarian lens: the dossier set must be globally varied so no
single region is framed as the site of injustice. The global lens: each case is one place's story —
present it as an example, not as that people's essence. Distinguish evidence from values: the
facts of each case are sourced and hedged; the judgment that they are unjust is argued, and
learners may reach different conclusions about the best fix. Keep emotional safety: choose cases
at a depth the age can hold, and always connect to what people have done and can do (development
"9–12"; facilitation "holding difficult topics safely").

## Procedure

1. **Retrieval (5 min).** Name the three layers of systemic injustice from memory (actions, rules
   and norms, structures). What does "systemic" mean in one sentence?
2. **Model the trace (15 min).** As a group, build one chain: (a) the **past rule**; (b) the
   **structure** it hardened into; (c) the **small actions** that repeat it; (d) the **unfair
   outcome**. Then, on the dashed arrow, mark one place the loop could be broken.
3. **Choose a case (5 min).** Pick a case dossier — ideally one you have not studied before, and
   from a place different from your own.
4. **Trace it (20 min).** Build the full chain for your case. For each link, write one specific,
   sourced fact. End by naming the break point and one sentence on why you chose it.
5. **Peer check (5 min).** Trade with a partner. Is each link a *cause* of the next, or just a
   nearby fact? Is the break point a change to a rule, a structure, or a repeated action?
6. **Close (5 min).** Analysis done; change imagined. Next: we step back and ask whether the idea
   of human rights itself travels fairly across cultures.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide the chain boxes pre-labeled and one worked link to copy the method.
- **Extension:** Compare two possible interventions and argue, in writing, which is more just and
  which is more feasible — and why they might differ.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Does the learner's chain show genuine causes (not mere facts) and a
  break point that changes a rule, structure, or repeated action?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The completed injustice trace.

## Home connection

Ask someone at home: what is one unfair thing in our town's history that still shapes today? Whose
rule or choice started it? Bring one sentence next time.
