# Unit 11 — Ethics, Justice & Political Economy

## Overview

This unit steps into the full adult questions of power, justice, and the common life,
building on Grade 8's weighing of consequences, duties, and fairness and its study of
social movements toward *reasoning across frameworks*. Learners analyze a moral
question using more than one ethical framework — consequences, duties, virtues — and
compare what each shows, and evaluate the role of conscience and self-criticism in
forming one's own values. They analyze a systemic injustice by tracing how individual
actions, rules, and structures combine to produce unfair outcomes, and evaluate the
idea of human rights across cultures, including critiques that they reflect some
traditions more than others. They analyze how power is distributed in a society and
how citizens can hold power accountable, evaluate the strengths and limits of
democratic participation and compare with alternatives, analyze the causes of
conflict and violence — inequality, fear, scarcity — and how they can be addressed at
the root, and evaluate reconciliation and restorative justice as responses to serious
harm, including their possibilities and limits. Questions are held as questions,
never answered as dogma.

## Essential questions

- How do I reason about a moral question across more than one ethical framework, and what role do conscience and self-criticism play in forming my values?
- How do individual actions, rules, and structures combine into systemic injustice, and how should I evaluate human rights across cultures?
- How is power distributed and held accountable, how does democracy compare with alternatives, and what are the roots of conflict and the limits of reconciliation?
