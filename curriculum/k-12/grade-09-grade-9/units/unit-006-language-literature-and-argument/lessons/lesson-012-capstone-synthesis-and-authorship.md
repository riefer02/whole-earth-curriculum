# Lesson 12 — Capstone: Synthesis & Authorship — Putting It Together

## Summary

Learners synthesize the unit's **eight goals** into one web around **synthesis and authorship**, recalling
each goal, writing one sentence for each, and choosing one to explain fully. This spaced-retrieval pass
closes the unit and gathers the portfolio into a single picture of a mature voice.

## Objectives

- Recall and connect all eight goals: synthesizing spoken sources, leading a discussion, analyzing structure,
  style, and argument, reading world literature, synthesizing across texts, writing a research argument,
  composing for real audiences, and holding languages as identity and as a living inheritance.
  _(D04.S1.09.01, D04.S1.09.02, D04.S2.09.01, D04.S2.09.02, D04.S3.09.01, D04.S3.09.02, D04.S4.09.01,
  D04.S4.09.02)_

## Connection

You began this unit remembering what you already knew how to do — analyze one voice, argue one position,
give credit for words. You end it able to do something larger: bring many voices into one account, lead a
room so every voice is heard, read a text from any tradition with a reader's tools, weigh evidence that
fights, write an argument no one else could write for you, and aim it at a real reader — all while knowing
that the languages you carry are part of who you are. Today you stand back and see the whole thing, one
web, in your own hands.

## Materials

- Capstone map sheet
- Eight goal cards

## Preparation

- Copy the capstone map sheet and goal cards; gather each learner's portfolio artifacts.
- Retrieval: this whole lesson is a retrieval and synthesis pass — the "science of learning" payoff of the
  unit (S-039). Have the learner's earlier artifacts on hand.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It is the **closing capstone and synthesis**. The goal:
**each learner recalls all eight goals and connects them into one web around synthesis and authorship.** The
eight goals are: (1) synthesize multiple spoken sources into one account; (2) lead a discussion that
welcomes every voice and manages disagreement fairly; (3) analyze how structure, style, and argument work
together; (4) read and analyze texts from world literatures across cultures; (5) synthesize across multiple
texts into a supported position; (6) write a research-supported argument that integrates and cites sources
and weighs conflicting evidence; (7) compose for real audiences and purposes; (8) explain how languages are
part of identity and describe language loss and revitalization, and why protecting languages matters for
humanity and the environment. This is a **retrieval and spacing** moment — having learners recall rather
than re-read is what makes the learning durable (S-039). Keep assessment **mastery, not ranking**: a learner
who cannot yet recall a goal revisits that lesson's artifact and tries again — "not yet" is information,
never a verdict (philosophy §8). The four lenses, together: the **critical-thinking** lens asks what
evidence supports each position; the **ethics** lens asks what is right and who is affected; the
**egalitarian** lens asks who benefits and who is left out — in a discussion, in a source, in a language;
the **global** lens asks how this looks in other places, times, and languages; the **environment** lens
asks what language loss costs the living Earth. Celebrate the collective: each learner's web is a thread in
one class tapestry of voices. This lesson also gathers the portfolio — the unit's artifacts are the evidence
of the learner's authorship (facilitation.md).

## Procedure

1. **Recall, don't peek (8 min).** Without looking at notes, list the unit's eight goals from memory. What
   can you remember? (That is the point — recalling *is* the learning.)
2. **Check and fill (5 min).** Compare with the eight goal cards. For any goal you missed, open that
   lesson's artifact and add it. "Not yet" is not a failure; it is a map of what to revisit.
3. **Build your web (15 min).** On the capstone map, write one sentence for each of the eight goals, and
   connect each to the center: **synthesis & authorship**.
4. **Choose a deep dive (15 min).** Pick the one goal that most changed you — or the artifact you are
   proudest of — and explain it fully to a partner: what it means, what you made, and one way it will matter
   beyond this unit. Then listen to your partner's.
5. **Weave the class web (7 min).** As a group, add everyone's maps to one shared web. Notice how the goals
   touch: listening feeds reading, reading feeds writing, and every one of them lives in *language* — the
   thing that makes us human together.
6. **Close (5 min).** Look at your web and your portfolio. You did not memorize eight facts — you built a
   voice and learned to share it. Carry it with you.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Work from the goal cards with their one-line reminders; choose one goal to explain instead of
  eight.
- **Extension:** Choose one goal and explain how it connects to at least two others (for example: weighing
  conflicting evidence strengthens both the research argument and the fairness of a discussion you lead).
- **Non-speaking / AAC:** The web and the deep-dive can be written, pointed to, or presented on a device;
  the explanation is in the *content*, not the voice.
- **Deaf / hard of hearing:** Present the deep dive in sign, writing, or a visual web; the unit's goals are
  the same in a signed as in a spoken language.

## Assessment

- **Summative (portfolio):** The completed capstone map — all eight goals, each with a sentence and a link to
  the center — kept alongside the unit's artifacts in the portfolio.
- **Self/peer:** Learners self-check each goal is present and give one specific "I understood this when you
  said…" to a partner.
- **Mastery, not ranking:** A learner missing a goal revisits its artifact and completes it; the web is
  finished when it is finished, not before.

## Home connection

Show someone at home your capstone web and explain one goal end to end — or better, show them your
real-audience piece and tell them who it was for. Teaching it is the surest sign you have learned it.
