# Lesson 1 — Language, Literature & Argument: Where We've Been, Where We're Going

## Summary

Learners **recall three Grade-8 language ideas** — analyzing a speaker's rhetoric, engaging in structured
debate, and citing sources — and **preview this unit's four strands and eight goals**. They meet the unit's
organizing idea: a mature mind builds a **synthesis**, shapes an **argument**, and becomes an **author**.

## Objectives

- Begin to name the unit's first goal — synthesizing multiple spoken sources into one coherent account.
  _(D04.S1.09.01)_
- Begin to name the unit's second goal — leading a discussion that welcomes every voice and manages
  disagreement fairly. _(D04.S1.09.02)_

## Connection

You already do this without noticing. When friends tell you three different versions of what happened at a
match or a market, you gather them, notice what they agree on, and decide for yourself what you believe —
you **synthesize**. When a group chat or a family argument starts to split, someone steps in to make sure
everyone gets a turn — that person **leads a discussion**. Grade 8 taught you to analyze one voice and argue
one position. Grade 9 turns you into someone who brings many voices together and speaks with a voice that is
truly your own. Today we remember what we can already do and see where it is taking us.

## Materials

- Unit map sheet
- Recall cards

## Preparation

- Copy the unit map sheet and recall cards.
- Retrieval: from Grade 8, recall (a) analyzing how a speaker's word choice, tone, and delivery shape
  meaning (rhetoric), (b) structured debate — arguing a position with reasons while listening to opposing
  views, and (c) citing sources accurately and separating your own words from others'. Today we link those
  to the new unit.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It is the unit's **opening and retrieval pass**, so the goals
are modest: **(1) recall three Grade-8 ideas — rhetoric (word choice, tone, delivery shape meaning),
structured debate (argue with reasons while hearing opposing views), and citation (honoring others' words);
(2) preview the unit's four strands — listening & speaking, reading & literacy, writing & expression,
multilingual & intercultural — and its eight goals; (3) plant the organizing idea: synthesis (many sources →
one account), argument (a supported position), and authorship (a voice of one's own).** Do not try to teach
the eight goals today; just name and locate them. The critical-thinking lens: every supported position
begins by weighing evidence, not by asserting (philosophy §7). The global lens: the same skills of
synthesis and argument are practiced in every literate culture, in every language, from oral epics to
digital essays. The ethics lens: honest argument distinguishes what the evidence shows from what we value,
and honest authorship names whose words we are using (philosophy §5). The egalitarian lens: the unit holds
multilingualism as **wealth**, never a deficit, and every learner's home language is honored first
(philosophy §4; D04 rationale). The technology & environment lens: the tools of language — writing, print,
recording, and now digital media — change who gets to speak and be heard, and a language also carries
knowledge of the place and living world it names; the unit returns to both threads in its closing lessons.
Keep origins and beliefs open: nothing here is creed (philosophy §11).
Spacing note (S-039): this recall pass is what makes the unit's ideas stick later.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (10 min).** In a small group, look at three recall cards: **rhetoric**, **structured debate**,
   **citing sources**. For each, say in one sentence what you remember from Grade 8.
2. **Connect the three (5 min).** What do all three have in common? (Each is about *using language
   carefully with other people* — to persuade, to disagree fairly, to give credit.)
3. **Meet the map (10 min).** Look at the unit map. Four strands: **listening & speaking**, **reading &
   literacy**, **writing & expression**, **multilingual & intercultural**. Eight goals — two per strand.
   Read each goal aloud once; don't worry about understanding them yet.
4. **Plant the big idea (10 min).** The unit's idea in one breath: a mature mind **synthesizes** (brings
   many sources into one account), builds an **argument** (a supported position), and becomes an **author**
   (a voice of its own). Say it back in your own words.
5. **Wonder (10 min).** Write one question about each strand in the wonder box. Which question do you most
   want answered by the end of the unit?
6. **Close (5 min).** Today we remembered what we know and saw the road ahead. Next: we synthesize many
   voices into one account.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Give sentence starters for the recall round ("Rhetoric is how a speaker's…").
- **Extension:** Add a fourth Grade-8 idea of your own (e.g., how language can include or exclude people)
  and say why it will matter in this unit.
- **Non-speaking / AAC:** All recall can be done by writing, pointing to a card, signing, or using a device;
  "saying it in your own words" means expressing it in *your* mode, not necessarily aloud.
- **Deaf / hard of hearing:** Present the strands visually; "synthesis" and "argument" are the same skills
  in a signed language as in a spoken one, and signed languages are honored among the world's languages.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner recall each Grade-8 idea in one sentence and name the unit's
  four strands?
- **Artifact:** The completed unit map (or the learner's written recall), kept in the portfolio.

## Home connection

Ask someone at home: what is one time you brought several people's different stories together to figure out
what really happened? Bring the story (no names needed) to the next lesson.
