# Unit 6 — Language, Literature & Argument

## Overview

This unit is the heart of the year's intellectual work in language, building on Grade
8's rhetoric, structured debate, and cited writing toward *synthesis and authorship*.
Learners synthesize information from multiple spoken sources and present a coherent
account or argument, and lead or facilitate a discussion, inviting participation and
managing disagreement with fairness. They analyze how a text's structure, style, and
argument work together to create meaning and effect, and synthesize ideas and evidence
from multiple texts to form a supported position. They write a research-supported
argument that integrates and cites sources and evaluates conflicting evidence, compose
for real audiences and purposes, explain how a person's languages are part of their
identity, and describe language loss and revitalization and why protecting languages
matters for humanity and the environment — always home language first, with
multilingualism held as wealth.

## Essential questions

- How do I synthesize multiple sources into a coherent account, and lead a discussion that welcomes every voice and manages disagreement fairly?
- How do structure, style, and argument work together in a text, and how do I synthesize evidence across texts into a supported position?
- How do I write research-supported argument for real audiences, and why do my languages matter to who I am and to the wider world?
