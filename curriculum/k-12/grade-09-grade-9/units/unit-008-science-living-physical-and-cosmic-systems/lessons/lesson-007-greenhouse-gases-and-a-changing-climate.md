# Lesson 7 — Greenhouse Gases and a Changing Climate

## Summary

Learners explain the **greenhouse effect** — how certain gases let sunlight in but trap some outgoing
heat, keeping Earth livable — and how **human activity** (burning fossil fuels, clearing forests,
agriculture) has strengthened it, changing the climate system. They separate the natural effect from
the human addition.

## Objectives

- Explain how greenhouse gases regulate climate and how human activity is changing the climate
  system. _(D06.S4.09.01)_

## Connection

A blanket does not make heat; it holds in the heat your body already makes. Earth's atmosphere wears a
thin blanket of **greenhouse gases** — carbon dioxide, methane, water vapor — that lets sunlight in but
holds some of the heat the ground gives back, keeping our planet warm enough for life. The blanket
itself is good; without it, Earth would be a frozen rock. The problem is not the blanket but its
thickness: by burning coal, oil, and gas and clearing forests, humans have been adding to it, faster
than the Earth can keep its usual balance.

## Materials

- Greenhouse-effect diagram sheet
- Human-activity card set

## Preparation

- Copy the diagram sheet and card set.
- Retrieval: from Unit 1 (U.09.001), recall the climate data you examined; from Lesson 6, recall that
  burning carbon-based fuel releases energy and carbon dioxide.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land, precisely: **the greenhouse effect is
natural and essential — sunlight (shortwave) passes through the atmosphere and warms the surface; the
surface re-radiates heat (infrared), and greenhouse gases absorb some of that outgoing heat and send
part of it back, keeping Earth warmer than it would otherwise be. Human activity — chiefly burning
fossil fuels, which adds carbon dioxide, and agriculture and waste, which add methane — has
strengthened this effect, and the measured rise in greenhouse gases and warming is the observed
result.** Use a worked example first (S-011): trace the incoming and outgoing arrows on the diagram.
The environment lens: this is the mechanism behind the climate change learners met in Unit 1, now
understood physically (S-005, S-438). The ethics lens: knowing the cause carries responsibility — the
question is no longer *whether* we are changing the climate but *what we owe* each other as it changes.
The egalitarian lens: the people who have burned the least fossil fuel are often the most exposed to
the harm, so the climate is a fairness problem, not only a physical one (S-005; also D10
environmental-justice lessons later this year). The global lens: the atmosphere is shared by every
nation and has no border — emissions anywhere warm the whole planet, which is why it is a genuinely
global question (S-005). Cite IPCC (S-005) and NASA Climate Kids (S-438); distinguish the mechanism
(fact, well-evidenced) from what we ought to do about it (a values question, held open).

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Unit 1: what did the climate data show? From Lesson 6: what does burning
   fuel release, besides energy?
2. **Meet the mechanism (12 min).** Sunlight comes in and warms the surface. The warm surface radiates
   heat back out. **Greenhouse gases** let sunlight through but absorb some outgoing heat and send part
   of it back down. Label the arrows on the sheet: incoming light, outgoing heat, trapped heat.
3. **Name the gases (8 min).** **Carbon dioxide** (burning coal, oil, gas; clearing and burning
   forests), **methane** (livestock, rice fields, leaks), and **water vapor** (which rises with
   warming). List them on the sheet.
4. **Link activity to gas (12 min).** Sort the card set: match each human activity to the gas it adds.
   Which single activity adds the most carbon dioxide?
5. **Separate natural from human (10 min).** Write two sentences: "The greenhouse effect is natural
   because ___. It is being strengthened by human activity because ___."
6. **Close (8 min).** The blanket is not the enemy; the rate at which we have thickened it is the
   challenge — and it is one we have the tools to meet.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide the diagram with the arrows already drawn; the learner only labels which arrows
  are sunlight, heat out, and trapped heat.
- **Extension:** Explain, using the carbon cycle, why the recent rise in carbon dioxide cannot be
  explained by volcanoes or other natural sources alone.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner explain the greenhouse mechanism and name one human
  activity that strengthens it, and one gas that activity adds?
- **Portfolio artifact:** The labeled diagram with the two "natural vs. human" sentences, kept in the
  portfolio.

## Home connection

At home, find one thing that burns fuel or gas — a stove, a car, a heater — and one thing that pulls
carbon dioxide back out of the air (a plant, a tree). Name both sides of the balance.
