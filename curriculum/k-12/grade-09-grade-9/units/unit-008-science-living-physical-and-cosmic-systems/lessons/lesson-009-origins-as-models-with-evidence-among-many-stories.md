# Lesson 9 — Origins as Models with Evidence, Among Many Stories

## Summary

Learners practice holding **scientific accounts of origins as models with evidence** alongside the
**many cultural and spiritual stories of beginnings**, without ranking one above another. They see
that science answers "how," stories answer "why" and "who we are," and a mature mind can hold both.

## Objectives

- Explain the evidence for the age of the Earth and the universe and how scientists measure it.
  _(D06.S4.09.02)_

## Connection

Every people on Earth has asked the same question — *where did we come from?* — and every people has
an answer. Some answers are **models built from evidence**: the Big Bang, deep time, the slow shaping
of life. Others are **stories that carry meaning**: a people's account of who they are, what the world
is for, and how to live in it. These are not rivals fighting over one chair. They answer different
questions. Science tells us *how* things came to be, with evidence and stated limits; stories tell us
*why we matter* and *how we belong*. A free mind can hold both, side by side, without forcing either
to bow.

## Materials

- Origins two-panel sheet
- Origin-story excerpt cards

## Preparation

- Copy the two-panel sheet and excerpt cards (choose story summaries with care and respect; never mock
  or rank them).
- Retrieval: from Lesson 8, recall the measured ages of the Earth and the universe; from Kindergarten
  through Grade 8, recall that origin stories have been held as stories to wonder at, one among many.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Its job is **epistemic honesty, not persuasion.** The
idea to land: **a scientific account (the Big Bang, deep time, evolution) is a model built from
evidence and held with stated limits — it answers "how"; cultural and spiritual origin stories carry
meaning, belonging, and purpose — they answer "why" and "who we are"; the curriculum holds them side
by side and lets learners weigh them with growing critical skill, never ranking one above another or
imposing either as dogma (philosophy §11).** Use a worked example first (S-011): take one scientific
model and one story, and show how each answers a different question. The ethics lens: the curriculum
does not mock, dismiss, or force any account; treating every learner's tradition with dignity is the
non-negotiable ground of this lesson. The egalitarian lens: no people's story is "primitive" and no
people's science is "theirs alone" — both science and story exist in every culture, and the idea that
some peoples have "real" knowledge and others have "myths" is a colonial habit we explicitly refuse.
The global lens: creation stories and astronomical traditions come from every continent — the Dreaming
of Aboriginal Australian peoples, the Popol Vuh of the Maya, the stories of the Yoruba, the Vedic and
Abrahamic traditions, and many more — each worthy of respectful study. The critical-thinking lens:
hold the distinction between a *model with evidence* (testable, revisable) and a *story of meaning*
(guiding, not falsifiable) — and note that this distinction is a tool for thinking, not a weapon for
ranking. This lesson cites no new factual source; it draws on S-002 and S-308 from Lesson 8.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Lesson 8: how old are the Earth and the universe? From earlier grades: why
   have we always held origin stories as *stories to wonder at*, one among many?
2. **Meet the two kinds of answer (10 min).** Look at the two panels. One side: a **scientific model**,
   built from evidence, answering **how**. The other: **cultural and spiritual stories**, carrying
   meaning, answering **why** and **who we are**. Annotate the sheet with this distinction.
3. **Compare two accounts (15 min).** Worked example: read one scientific account (the Big Bang, deep
   time) and one origin story. For each, ask: What question is it answering? What evidence or what
   meaning does it offer? Write both answers.
4. **Read the excerpt cards (12 min).** In small groups, read two story summaries from different
   traditions. For each, name the question it answers and what it gives its people. Do not rank them.
5. **Write a reflection (8 min).** Complete this frame: "Science answers ___; stories answer ___. I can
   hold both because ___."
6. **Close (5 min).** Wonder is not a contest. The universe is vast enough to hold every honest
   question — and every honest answer.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide the two panels pre-labeled; the learner places each account on the side whose
  question it answers.
- **Extension:** Write a paragraph distinguishing a model with evidence from a story of meaning, using
  the Big Bang and one creation story as examples, and explain why holding both is not a contradiction.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner state that a scientific account is a model with evidence
  answering "how," while origin stories carry meaning, and that neither must be ranked above the other?
- **Self (portfolio):** The "Science answers ___; stories answer ___" reflection, kept in the portfolio.

## Home connection

Ask an elder or family member to tell an origin story they know, and listen for the question it
answers. Then share the scientific model — and notice how both can be true in different ways.
