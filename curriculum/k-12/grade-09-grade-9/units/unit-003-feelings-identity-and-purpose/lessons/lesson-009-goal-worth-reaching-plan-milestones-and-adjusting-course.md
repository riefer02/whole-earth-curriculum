# Lesson 9 — A Goal Worth Reaching: Plan, Milestones, and Adjusting Course

## Summary

Learners set a **longer-term goal** that matters to them and break it into a **realistic plan with
dated milestones**. They learn what makes a goal specific and checkable, plan for an obstacle with a
**backup step**, and understand that **adjusting the plan** is part of succeeding, not failing.

## Objectives

- Set a longer-term goal and break it into a realistic plan with dated milestones. _(D01.S3.09.01)_
- Plan for one obstacle with a backup step, treating adjustment as part of success. _(D01.S3.09.01)_

## Connection

A wish is a direction without a road: "someday I want to be good at something." A **goal** is a road
with a map: what, how much, and by when. Think of a journey you actually know — to reach a far place,
you don't just stare at the horizon; you mark the turnings along the way, and when a road is blocked,
you take another. The same is true of anything worth becoming: a skill, a habit, a body of work, a
change in your life. A goal without milestones is a wish; with milestones, it is a plan. Today you
build one — and you plan to be surprised, because plans change, and changing them is how you get
there.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It is a **skill lesson** in planning — taught with a
**worked example and guided practice** (S-011). The evidence-backed core: **specific, challenging
goals with feedback improve performance more than vague "do your best" goals** (Locke & Latham,
S-223), and the practical translation for a 14–15-year-old is a goal that is **specific and
checkable** — what, how much, by when — with **dated milestones** and a **backup step**. The key
teaching moves: **(1) goal vs. wish** — a goal is specific, checkable, and timed; **(2) milestones**
— break the goal into dated, doable steps so progress can be seen and feedback can arrive; **(3)
adjustment** — obstacles are expected, not shameful; changing the plan to keep the goal is success,
not failure (mastery, not ranking: S-007). The **intellectual** lens: planning is reasoning — a
learner is predicting, sequencing, and testing their own prediction against what actually happens.
The **ethics** lens: a goal is worth more when it harms no one — invite learners to check their goal
against its effects on others. The **egalitarian** lens: not everyone has equal resources or freedom
to pursue a goal, so a "realistic" plan means realistic *for that learner's circumstances*, and the
goal is never judged against someone else's easier or harder road. Be encouraging, never
performative: the goal may be small or large, academic or not — the skill is the planning, not the
glamour. Avoid shame about past unfinished goals; frame them as data, not verdicts. A learner who
cannot think of a goal may plan for an invented character — the skill still lands.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Lesson 8, how does a value become a choice? Today we point that choice at
   something bigger.
2. **Worked example (10 min).** Watch as your facilitator turns a wish into a goal and breaks it into
   dated milestones, then meets one obstacle and adjusts the plan. Notice: goal vs. wish, milestones,
   backup.
3. **Choose your goal (10 min).** Choose one longer-term goal that actually matters to you — a skill,
   a habit, a project, a change. Write it so it is **specific and checkable** (what, how much, by
   when).
4. **Build the plan (15 min).** On your sheet, break the goal into three or four **milestones**, each
   with a date. For one likely obstacle, write a **backup step**.
5. **Test it with a partner (10 min).** Trade plans (or share only the milestones). Ask: is the goal
   specific and checkable? Are the milestones doable? What could go wrong — and what is the backup?
6. **Close (5 min).** A wish became a plan. Next: speaking up for what you need — the last piece of
   authoring your life.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use the worked-example frame ("My goal is…; milestone 1 by…; if X happens, I will…").
- **Extension:** Write a second, bolder goal and identify what would have to be true for it to
  succeed.

## Assessment

- **Formative (portfolio):** The goal-plan sheet — a specific, checkable longer-term goal with dated
  milestones and one backup step — kept in the portfolio.
- **Self:** The learner checks: is the goal specific and checkable, and does each milestone have a
  date?

## Home connection

Share your goal and one milestone with someone at home and ask them to check in with you on that date.
A witness makes a goal stickier.
