# Lesson 8 — Authoring My Choices: How I Shape the World Back

## Summary

Learners analyze how **identity shapes choices** — how a person's values, beliefs, and belonging lead
to what they do — and how those choices, repeated, shape the world and feed back into the self. They
trace one of their own values into a real choice and its effects, seeing themselves as **authors, not
just receivers**, of their lives.

## Objectives

- Analyze how a person's identity shapes their choices and, in turn, their effects on the world.
  _(D01.S2.09.02)_
- Trace one of your own values into a real choice and name its effects on yourself, others, and your
  community. _(D01.S2.09.02)_

## Connection

Last lesson you saw how the world made you. Now turn the arrow around. A person who values loyalty
will make different choices than one who values freedom — in who they defend, what they keep
secret, what they give up. A person who feels they belong to a place will act toward that place
differently than one who feels like a visitor. Your identity is not just a mirror reflecting the
world; it is a hand that moves things. Small choices, repeated, become the person you are becoming
and the world you are helping to build. Today you learn to see yourself as an author, not only a
receiver.

## Materials

- Identity-to-choice sheet
- Choice scenario cards

## Preparation

- Copy the identity-to-choice sheet and choice scenario cards.
- Retrieval: from Lesson 7, recall one influence you were given and one you chose. Today we follow
  the chosen parts into action.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It completes the fourth goal (D01.S2.09.02) by
flipping the arrow: **identity shapes choices, and choices shape the world and, in a feedback loop,
the self.** The core ideas: **(1) values → choices** — what a person values and believes steers what
they do (who they defend, how they spend time, whom they help or hurt); **(2) effects** — choices
have consequences for the self, for others, and for the community; **(3) the feedback loop** — small
choices, repeated, harden into habits, and habits become identity (what a person does comes to be
part of who they are). These are taught with a **worked example and guided practice** (S-011). The
**ethics** lens is central: a choice's rightness is judged by its effects on others, not only by how
it feels — and the same choice can look different from another person's side. The **egalitarian**
lens: agency is not equally distributed — not everyone has the same freedom to act on their values,
because of money, safety, or prejudice — so we honor effort without blaming those with fewer choices.
The **community** lens: a person's choices ripple through the people around them, so authorship is
also responsibility. Keep the balance honest: this is about *agency*, not *blame* — a learner is the
author of their choices but not of everything that happens to them. Emphasize hope and the
possibility of change: a habit can be re-authored. No learner is judged for the choices they trace;
invented examples are always welcome.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Lesson 7, name one part of your identity you were given and one you have
   begun to choose.
2. **Worked example (10 min).** Watch as your facilitator traces an *invented* person's value —
   say, loyalty — into a real choice and its effects on the self, on others, and on the community.
   Notice the arrow: value → choice → effects.
3. **Trace the scenarios (15 min).** In pairs, take the choice scenario cards. For each, name the
   character's value or belief, the choice it led to, and the effects on self, others, and community.
4. **Trace your own (15 min).** On your sheet, choose one value you actually hold. Trace it into a
   real choice you have made or could make, and name its effects. (Keep it private if you prefer.)
5. **See the loop (7 min).** With your pair: how does a small choice, repeated, become part of who
   you are? Name one habit you can see forming — for better or worse.
6. **Close (3 min).** You are not only what happens to you; you are also what you choose to do. Next:
   aim that authorship at a goal worth reaching.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide sentence frames ("I value…; so I chose…; the effects were…").
- **Extension:** Analyze how one repeated small choice became a habit that now shapes your identity
  back — and whether you want to keep it.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner trace a value into a choice and name its effects on
  self, others, and community?
- **Artifact:** The completed identity-to-choice sheet, kept in the portfolio.

## Home connection

Ask someone at home: what is one value you hold that shows up in a choice you make every day? How
does that choice ripple outward?
