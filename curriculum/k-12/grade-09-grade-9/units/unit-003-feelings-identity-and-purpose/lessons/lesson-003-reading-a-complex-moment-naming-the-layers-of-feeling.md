# Lesson 3 — Reading a Complex Moment: Naming the Layers of Feeling

## Summary

Learners apply their growing emotional vocabulary to **complex social situations**. Using a **layered
frame — event, thought, subtle feeling, secondary feeling** — they read realistic scenarios and their
own experience, naming what is happening at each layer. This is the unit's first skill in *reading a
moment* rather than reacting to it.

## Objectives

- Name the event, thought, subtle feeling, and secondary feeling in a complex social situation.
  _(D01.S1.09.01)_
- Use precise emotional vocabulary to articulate a subtle or secondary emotion of your own in a
  complex moment. _(D01.S1.09.01)_

## Connection

A group of friends laughs at an inside joke you were not there for. In an instant, several things
happen at once: the *event* (they laughed, you stood outside it), a *thought* ("I don't belong with
them"), a *subtle feeling* (a small ache of loneliness, maybe a flicker of hurt), and then a
*secondary feeling* (embarrassment at caring so much, or anger at yourself for being sensitive). None
of that is simple "sad." A complex moment is made of layers. Today you learn to read them one at a
time — and reading a moment is the first step toward choosing, not just reacting.

## Materials

- Case cards
- Layers-of-feeling sheet

## Preparation

- Copy the case cards and layers-of-feeling sheet.
- Retrieval: from Lesson 2, recall the difference between a subtle and a secondary emotion. Today we
  use those words to read a whole situation.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It is a **guided-practice lesson** building on
Lesson 2's vocabulary (S-011). Model one case card fully — naming all four layers aloud — before
learners work in pairs; then have them practice two cards together and one independently. The
four-layer frame is: **event** (what a camera would record, without interpretation), **thought**
(the story the person tells about what it means), **subtle feeling** (the fine-grained emotion —
envy, hurt, tenderness, relief), **secondary feeling** (the feeling about the feeling — shame,
pride, guilt). This frame is adapted descriptively from the event-thought-feeling / ABC account
(S-332); it is a **self-awareness tool, not a diagnosis or therapy**, and the facilitator should say
so. Keep the room safe: scenarios are invented and low-stakes; no learner is ever required to reveal
anything personal, and names are never used for real people. The **social-emotional** lens: naming a
moment precisely is the ground of emotional maturity and of caring well for others (S-001). The
**ethics** lens: the same event can be read more than one way, so we hold our reading of another
person's feelings as a guess, offered with humility, never as a verdict on them. The **global** lens:
which feelings are named, shown, or hidden in a moment differs across cultures — invite learners to
notice that without ranking any way as better. Mastery, not ranking: a learner who can name only two
layers today names all four tomorrow; "not yet" is a map, not a grade (S-007).

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Lesson 2, what is the difference between a subtle emotion and a secondary
   emotion? Say one of each.
2. **Worked example (10 min).** Watch and listen as your facilitator reads one case card and names
   all four layers aloud: the event, the thought, the subtle feeling, the secondary feeling. Notice
   how the layers rise one from another.
3. **Guided practice (15 min).** In pairs, take two more case cards. For each, name the four layers
   together. Where you disagree, say why — there is often more than one honest reading.
4. **Independent practice (15 min).** Choose a real-but-safe moment from your own week (or invent
   one). On your sheet, name the four layers for it. You may keep it private.
5. **Share a reading (7 min).** With your pair, share *only the layers of the invented cases*, not
   personal ones — how did your readings differ, and why?
6. **Close (3 min).** You can now read a moment instead of just reacting to it. Next: what shapes the
   feeling in the first place — context, belonging, and media.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a bank of feeling words and sentence starters for each layer ("The event was…;
   the thought might be…").
- **Extension:** Write your own scenario and analyze it; swap with a partner and compare your
  readings of the same moment.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name all four layers in a case scenario, using precise
  vocabulary for the subtle and secondary feelings?
- **Artifact:** The completed layers-of-feeling sheet for one scenario, kept in the portfolio.

## Home connection

Choose a scene from a film or story you share with someone at home and, together, name the layers of
feeling a character is moving through. Bring one new feeling word you found.
