# Lesson 7 — Made in a Society: How the World Shapes Me

## Summary

Learners analyze how **society shapes identity** — through family, school, peers, media, language,
rules, and history — and sort these influences into those they were **given** and those they have
**chosen**. They see identity as a **two-way making**: society shapes the self, and the self in turn
shapes society, beginning with which given parts to keep and which to question.

## Objectives

- Analyze how society — family, school, peers, media, language, rules, and history — shapes a
  person's identity. _(D01.S2.09.02)_
- Distinguish the parts of your identity that were given to you from those you have begun to choose.
  _(D01.S2.09.02)_

## Connection

You did not choose the language you first spoke, the family you were born into, the place, the
school, the rules about how a person should act that surrounded you before you could question them.
Those things were **given** to you — and they have quietly shaped what you value, fear, and want.
Society is not a backdrop to your life; it is one of its authors. And yet the writing is two-way:
as you grow, you begin to look back at what you were handed and decide what to keep, what to
question, and what to change. Today you take stock of the first direction — how the world made you —
so that next time you can see the second: how you make the world back.

## Materials

- Society-and-self sheet
- Influence cards

## Preparation

- Copy the society-and-self sheet and influence cards.
- Retrieval: from Lesson 6, recall your narrative thread. Today we ask where that thread came from —
  the society that helped weave it.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It opens the fourth goal (D01.S2.09.02), analyzing
how society shapes identity. The core ideas: **(1) socialization** — identity is formed inside
society, through family, school, peers, media, language, rules, and history; **(2) given versus
chosen** — some parts of identity are handed to a person at birth (language, family, place, group
memberships, expectations), and some are chosen or still being chosen; **(3) identity as a two-way
making** — society shapes the self, and the self shapes society back, partly by deciding what to
keep, question, or change. For the developmental frame, Erikson's account of identity as a lifelong
process (S-155) and Marcia's identity statuses — that exploration ("moratorium") is a normal and
healthy part of forming identity before commitment (S-403) — support teaching that **uncertainty and
open exploration are not failure; they are the work itself**. The **global** lens is central:
identity is shaped differently in every society, and no single society's way is the default or the
best — invite learners to compare, never rank. The **egalitarian** lens: some people are given
identities that are *not equally valued* by their society — identities that are stereotyped,
excluded, or denied — and that inequality is something society built, not something the person
deserves (see stereotype threat, S-284). The **ethics** lens: noticing what you were given is the
first step toward deciding, with care for yourself and others, what to do with it. **Safety:** do not
require learners to disclose painful parts of their given identities; invented examples and optional
sharing are the norm. Emphasize agency and hope: what is given is real, but it is not a cage.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Lesson 6, what thread runs through your story? Today: where did that
   thread come from?
2. **Worked example (10 min).** Watch as your facilitator sorts three influence cards for an
   *invented* person — language, family, a peer group — into **given** and **chosen**, and says *how*
   each one shapes that person. 
3. **Sort the cards (15 min).** In pairs, sort the full set of influence cards into given and chosen.
   For two of them, say *how* the influence works — what it teaches, values, or forbids.
4. **Map your own (15 min).** On your sheet, list influences on your own identity. Mark each as
   given or chosen. Which given one do you want to keep? Which one do you want to question? (Keep it
   private if you prefer.)
5. **Discuss the two-way street (7 min).** With your pair: society shapes you — but where do you
   begin to shape it back? Name one small way.
6. **Close (3 min).** You are made in a society — and you are not finished being made. Next: how your
   identity shapes your choices.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide sentence frames ("I was given…; I chose…; I want to keep/question…").
- **Extension:** Analyze one influence in depth — how it reached you and how it still operates today.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name how society shaped their identity and distinguish
  given from chosen parts?
- **Artifact:** The completed society-and-self sheet, kept in the portfolio.

## Home connection

Ask someone at home: what is one thing about you that came from your family or your place, that you
never chose? What do they think of it?
