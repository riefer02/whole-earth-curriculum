# Lesson 5 — Performing Identity: The Moving Body Asks a Question

## Summary

Learners choose a **personal question** — "Where do I come from?", "What do I carry?", "What am I
becoming?" — then devise a short **movement phrase**: a sequence of gestures and positions with a
clear beginning, middle, and end. They perform for a partner or small group **with consent**, revise
once, and reflect in writing on what the body revealed that words alone could not. The lesson builds
on Grade 8's devised pieces and treats the body with dignity: every body moves differently, and every
body is enough.

## Objectives

- Create a performance that uses the moving body to explore identity or a personal question, and
  reflect on the process. _(D07.S3.09.01)_

## Connection

Long before you had words for who you are, your body already knew. The way you stand when you are
proud, the way your shoulders fold when you are tired, the way you move through your own home without
thinking — these are a language you have been speaking all your life. Some questions about the self
do not fit into sentences, so people everywhere have turned to the moving body to ask them: a dancer
reaching, a runner at the starting line, a person bowing in greeting. Today you let your body ask a
question and see what answer comes.

## Materials

- Open floor space and comfortable clothing
- A movement journal or paper

## Preparation

- Clear a safe space; agree on norms of **consent** (no one touches anyone without asking, anyone may
  pass or watch instead of perform).
- Retrieval: from Grade 8, recall that a devised piece is shaped through rehearsal — it *changes* as
  you work. Today we devise a short phrase and revise it once.

## Facilitator note

This is a vulnerable lesson; safety comes first. Set clear norms: participation is by invitation,
anyone may watch instead of perform, and bodies are never commented on — only the movement choices.
The **social-emotional** and **physical** lenses carry the lesson: identity is felt *in* the body,
and moving is thinking. The **ethics** lens: **consent** is the foundation — no one touches, films, or
shares another's body without permission, and a learner's "pass" is always honored. The **global**
lens: many traditions use gesture as meaning — a mudra, a bow, a greeting gesture — and learners may
draw on any they know *respectfully*, as a question rather than a costume; if a gesture belongs to a
tradition not their own, they should ask before borrowing its meaning. Keep the **worked example**
brief: model a three-move phrase (still → rise → reach) so novices see what a phrase is, then let
them make it their own (S-011). Feedback is descriptive ("I saw you rise slowly, then reach"), never
a verdict on the person. Honor the wide range of bodies and abilities; stillness, a seated gesture,
or a hand moving are as complete as a leap.

## Procedure

1. **Choose a question (5 min).** Pick one personal question to explore with your body — "Where do I
   come from?", "What do I carry?", or "What am I becoming?".
2. **Warm the instrument (5 min).** Stand and find **stillness**. Notice your posture, your breath,
   your weight. This is your body — your instrument — and it is enough exactly as it is.
3. **Devise a phrase (20 min).** Look at the movement-phrase diagram: a phrase has a **beginning**
   (still), a **middle** (rise, reach, or gather), and an **end** (still again). Build five moves that
   explore your question — slow or fast, high or low, large or small. Repeat the phrase until it feels
   like yours, then **revise once**: change one move and notice what it does.
4. **Perform with consent (15 min).** Show your phrase to a partner or a small circle. Ask first, and
   invite descriptive feedback: what did they *see*, and what did it make them feel?
5. **Reflect (10 min).** Write three sentences: what your phrase explored, one thing your body
   "said" that words could not, and what you discovered in the process.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a three-move scaffold (still → reach → still) to build from.
- **Extension:** Add a second question and answer it with a contrasting **tempo** or **level**, then
  name the contrast.
- **Access (motor):** A phrase can be one seated gesture, a hand, a breath, or stillness itself — the
  beginning–middle–end shape is what matters, not the size or speed of the movement.

## Assessment

- **Formative (performance + reflection):** Does the phrase have a clear beginning, middle, and end,
  explore the chosen question, and show one revision? Does the reflection name what the body revealed
  that words did not?
- **Self-check:** Can you say what each of your five moves *means* to you?

## Home connection

Notice how someone at home moves when they are happy, tired, or welcoming. What does their body say
without words?
