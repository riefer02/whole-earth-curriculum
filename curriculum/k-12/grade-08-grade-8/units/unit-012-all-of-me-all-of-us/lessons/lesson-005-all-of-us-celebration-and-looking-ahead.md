# Lesson 5 — All of Us: Celebration, the Caring Community & Looking Ahead

## Summary

Learners gather the year into a **portfolio and celebration**: the whole-self map, the
choice-and-consequences note, and the belonging-and-solidarity reflection, then name one thing they
will **carry forward** into high school and celebrate each whole person, the caring community, and
the living Earth. This is the unit's and the year's closing capstone.

## Objectives

- Gather the whole year: explain how intersecting identities shape a whole self _(D01.S2.08.01)_,
  choose rather than react _(D01.S3.08.01)_ and weigh consequences _(D01.S3.08.02)_,
  strengthen belonging _(D02.S3.08.02)_,
  and analyze systems of care and stand in solidarity rather than charity _(D02.S4.08.01,
  D02.S4.08.02)_.

## Connection

A celebration is not a party bolted onto the end; it is how a community marks what it has become
*together*. You have spent a year becoming — naming feelings, weighing choices, making room for
others, standing with them rather than over them. Today you do not learn a new thing; you gather the
things you already built, see them as one, and choose what you will carry into what comes next.

## Materials

- Capstone gathering sheet
- Each learner's assembled portfolio

## Preparation

- Gather the three unit artifacts (whole-self map, choice note, belonging-and-solidarity reflection)
  and a note of the belonging act from Lesson 3.
- Retrieval: this whole lesson is a retrieval and synthesis pass — the payoff of spacing across the
  year (S-039). Have each learner's full portfolio on hand.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It is the **closing celebration and synthesis**. The
goal: **each learner assembles the three closing artifacts, states what they learned from each, and
names one thing they will carry forward into Grade 9.** The three artifacts are: (1) the whole-self
map — intersecting identities shaping a whole self; (2) the choice-and-consequences note — pause,
weigh now vs. later, choose; (3) the belonging-and-solidarity reflection — who gives, receives,
decides, and how to stand *with* rather than *above*. Keep assessment **mastery, not ranking**: a
learner missing an artifact simply completes it now — "not yet" is information, never a verdict.
Looking ahead is framed as **purpose and possibility**, not pressure: naming one thing you want to
carry forward is a step toward purpose, which grows by trying, not by being certain (S-400); identity
remains a growing story, never a finished fact (S-155). The four lenses, gathered: the **ethics**
lens asks what you will choose for others' sake; the **egalitarian** lens asks who you will stand
with; the **global** lens remembers that this year's questions are asked, in different forms, by
young people everywhere; the **technology & environment** lens asks how your choices touch people and
the living Earth through the tools you use. Celebrate the collective — each learner's thread is part
of one class web (S-233). End with hope, not a test.

## Procedure

1. **Recall, don't peek (8 min).** Without looking, list from memory what this unit built: the
   whole-self map, the choice note, the belonging plan, the care-system analysis. What did each one
   teach you? (Recalling *is* the learning.)
2. **Assemble your gathering (12 min).** On the capstone sheet, set out your three artifacts and
   write one sentence for each: what it shows about who you are becoming and who you stand with.
   Complete anything that is missing.
3. **Name what you carry forward (10 min).** Write one line: *"I will carry ___ forward into high
   school."* Make it real — a value, a skill, a habit — not a slogan. Then read it once, to
   yourself, slowly — or say it aloud slowly.
4. **Celebrate the web (15 min).** In a circle, each person shares one artifact or one carry-forward
   line. Listen to every voice. Notice: the whole is more than the parts — each of you is a whole
   self, and together you are a caring community, standing on a living Earth.
5. **Mark the moment (7 min).** Together, do one small thing that marks the year — a shared song, a
   clap that builds, a circle that closes, a seed planted, a name written on the "carry forward"
   wall. Whatever fits your place and your people.
6. **Close (3 min).** Look around. You did not memorize a year; you became someone. Carry it
   forward.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Work from the three artifacts one at a time, with a partner; write one carry-forward
  line from a starter ("I will keep…").
- **Non-visual / no-writing path:** Mark the celebration with voice and movement — name each artifact
  aloud, speak your carry-forward line, and join the shared song, clap, circle, or walk. A partner or
  the facilitator can scribe the gathering sheet from your words if a written copy is needed for the
  portfolio.
- **Extension:** Write a short letter to your Grade-9 self, naming one value and one skill you intend
  to carry forward — and one question you hope to answer there.

## Assessment

- **Summative (portfolio):** The completed capstone gathering sheet with all three artifacts and the
  carry-forward line, kept in the year-end portfolio.
- **Self/peer:** Learners self-check each artifact is present and give one specific "I saw ___ in your
  work" to a partner.
- **Mastery, not ranking:** A learner missing an artifact completes it in the moment; the gathering is
  finished when it is finished, not before.

## Home connection

Show someone at home your gathering sheet and read your carry-forward line aloud. Teaching your
family what you learned is the surest sign you have learned it.
