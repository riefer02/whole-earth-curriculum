# Lesson 2 — What I Choose, Not What I React To: Impulse, Choice & Consequences

## Summary

Learners practice the **pause** between an impulse and an action, then weigh **short-term against
long-term consequences** for one real decision, writing a choice-and-consequences note they can
carry forward. This is the second half of the unit's first essential question.

## Objectives

- Manage impulses deliberately, choosing a response rather than reacting in emotionally charged
  moments. _(D01.S3.08.01)_
- Explain the difference between short-term and long-term consequences, and weigh them in a
  decision. _(D01.S3.08.02)_

## Connection

You feel a flush of anger or excitement and want to act *right now* — send the message, say the
sharp thing, tap "buy," quit the practice. That hot instant is real, and it is not the whole story.
Between the feeling and the action there is a small space — a breath, a beat — where you can choose
what happens next. Today you learn to find that space and use it to weigh now against later.

## Materials

- Choice-and-consequences note sheet
- Scenario cards

## Preparation

- Copy the note sheet and scenario cards.
- Retrieval: from Unit 1, recall the pause skill and the idea that short-term and long-term
  consequences are different things. Today we turn that into a written, repeatable decision tool.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The goal: **each learner can (1) pause deliberately
between an impulse and a response, and (2) weigh short-term against long-term consequences in one
real decision.** Model the four steps — **pause, name the feeling, weigh now vs. later, choose** —
as a worked example before independent practice (S-011); the pause is a *skill*, so it is taught
explicitly, not left to discovery. On the science: the delay-of-gratification tradition shows the
pause is learnable, and we teach it as a skill — *not* as a claim that one childhood test predicts a
life (S-336). Adolescence is a period of heightened reward sensitivity, which is exactly why a
practiced pause matters; the brain's brakes are still being built (S-229). The **ethics** lens: a
decision is weighed not only by what it costs *me* now but by what it does to *others* later. The
**egalitarian** lens: whose consequences do we count — the comfortable and the powerful, or everyone
the choice touches? The **technology** lens: many apps are designed to remove the pause (one-tap
buying, auto-playing next, notifications that demand now); naming that design is the first step to
choosing anyway. Keep "react vs. respond" free of moralism — reacting is human; choosing is a
practice, not a verdict.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (6 min).** What did we map last time — and what is the small space between a feeling and
   an action called? Say it in your own words. (The pause.)
2. **Follow the worked example (8 min), read aloud.** Follow a model decision, step by step:
   1. **Pause** — stop; one full breath, a step back.
   2. **Name** — "I am angry / excited / hurt because…"
   3. **Weigh** — short-term: what happens *now*? long-term: what happens *later*, for me and others?
   4. **Choose** — the response you can stand behind tomorrow.
   Example: a friend sends a mocking message. Now: replying instantly feels satisfying. Later: the
   fight grows, the friendship is damaged, others get pulled in. Choose: pause, cool down, reply with
   a calm "that stung — can we talk?"
3. **Guided practice (12 min).** In pairs, take one scenario card (or hear it read aloud). Run the
   four steps *aloud together*, writing the short-term and long-term consequences in the two columns.
   The facilitator checks one pair's work before you continue.
4. **Your own decision (15 min).** Pick one real decision coming up in your life. Fill the note
   sheet: the situation, the impulse, the pause you will use, the short-term consequences, the
   long-term consequences, and your chosen response.
5. **Peer check (8 min).** Trade notes. Give one specific piece of feedback: "I could see the
   long-term consequence when you wrote…" and one question: "What would you do if the pause felt
   impossible in the moment?"
6. **Close (6 min).** You did not promise to be perfect; you built a tool. Next: we turn from "me"
   to "us" — belonging you strengthen.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Work only from the worked example, copying its four steps with your own details;
  choose one consequence per column.
- **Extension:** Add a third column — consequences for *other people* — and weigh one decision whose
  costs fall mostly on others.

## Assessment

- **Formative (self):** Can the learner name the four steps and fill both columns for one real
  decision?
- **Artifact:** The choice-and-consequences note, kept in the portfolio as the unit's closing
  "choice" artifact.

## Home connection

Ask someone at home about a decision where waiting a little changed the outcome for the better.
Bring one sentence of their story (no private details) to the next lesson.
