# Lesson 3 — Belonging I Strengthen

## Summary

Learners analyze who is included and who is left out, then plan one concrete act to **strengthen
belonging** in their school or local community — a welcome they can actually carry out this week.
This opens the unit's second essential question.

## Objectives

- Take part in strengthening belonging in their school or local community. _(D02.S3.08.02)_

## Connection

Remember a time you truly belonged: someone saved you a seat, learned to say your name right, or
made room for you when the circle was already full. Now remember the opposite — the lunch table where
no one shifted, the game where you were picked last. Belonging is not magic; it is made by small,
real acts, and it is *unevenly* made — some people are welcomed again and again, and others wait.
Today you choose to be someone who makes room.

## Materials

- Belonging plan sheet
- Belonging case cards

## Preparation

- Copy the plan sheet and case cards.
- Retrieval: from Unit 3 (Relationships, Empathy & Conflict), recall in-group/out-group dynamics and
  how identity shapes belonging. Today we turn that into an action, not just an analysis.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The goal: **each learner analyzes who is included and
who is left out, and plans one concrete, doable act to strengthen belonging this week.** The act must
be small enough to actually happen — a saved seat, a learned name, an invitation — not a grand scheme.
The **critical-thinking** lens: noticing that circles form almost by habit is the first step to
widening them; in-groups and out-groups can arise from nothing more than a shared label (S-232). The
**egalitarian** lens: the question is never only "are we friendly?" but "who is always left out, and
why?" — belonging is shaped by how a person's identity is received, and inclusive settings treat
difference as ordinary rather than deficient (S-235). The **ethics** lens: welcoming the stranger is
treated across cultures as a core duty, not a nicety. The **global** lens: what "welcome" looks like
differs — a shared meal, a spoken greeting, a place made at the fire — but the underlying act of
making room recurs everywhere. The **technology** lens: online spaces can include (a group that
welcomes a newcomer in a shared language) or exclude (a chat that ignores or mocks). Keep this safe:
no one is required to confess a moment of being excluded; use the case cards as the shared examples.
Celebrate collective action — many small welcomes add up to one wider circle (S-233).

## Procedure

1. **Recall (6 min).** What did we build last time — and what is the word for feeling you are truly
   a part of a group? (Belonging.) Say one thing you remember about who is "in" and who is "out" of
   a circle.
2. **Meet the cases (10 min).** In small groups, take one case card — a scene where someone is left
   out. Name: *who is left out, why, and what one small act could widen the circle?* Write or say one
   answer.
3. **Widen the circle, not just the welcome (10 min).** Discuss: welcoming one person is good, but
   what *habit* keeps the circle open for the next person, and the one after? Example: a team that
   always picks the same friends stays closed even when it is polite. What small change keeps it
   open?
4. **Plan your act (12 min).** On the plan sheet, fill three spaces:
   - **One person** you will welcome this week (or a group that is often left out).
   - **One act** you will actually do — save a seat, learn a name, say a real hello, invite someone
     in.
   - **One habit** you will keep so the welcome is not a one-time show.
5. **Check it is real (7 min).** With a partner, test the plan: *Can you actually do it this week?
   Is it small enough to happen?* Revise if needed.
6. **Close (5 min).** You did not just talk about belonging; you wrote the first move. Do it, and
   bring the story next time. Next: we look at how communities *care* — and the difference between
   charity and solidarity.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Give sentence starters ("I will welcome ___ by ___"). Choose one act instead of three
  spaces.
- **Non-visual / tactile path:** Build the web aloud and by touch — each learner names one person in
  the circle and how they are connected ("I am connected to ___ because ___"), passing a length of
  string, vine, or held hands so each thread is felt rather than seen.
- **Extension:** Identify a *systemic* gap (a place, rule, or habit that shuts people out) and plan
  one way to change it, not just soften it.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name who is left out in one case and commit to one
  concrete, doable act?
- **Artifact:** The belonging plan sheet, kept in the portfolio and revisited after the act is done.

## Home connection

Ask someone at home: "When did someone make room for you, and how did it change how you felt?" Bring
one sentence of their answer to the next lesson.
