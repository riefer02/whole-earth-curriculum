# Lesson 10 — Capstone: Systems, Evidence & Models — Putting It Together

## Summary

Learners synthesize the unit's **eight goals** into one web around **systems, evidence, and models**,
recalling each goal, writing one sentence and drawing one picture for each, and choosing one goal to
explain fully. This spaced-retrieval pass closes the unit.

## Objectives

- Recall and connect all eight goals: designing a controlled experiment, revising models, genes and
  environment, ecosystem ripples, atoms and reactions, fields, stars, and plate tectonics.
  _(D06.S1.08.01, D06.S1.08.02, D06.S2.08.01, D06.S2.08.02, D06.S3.08.01, D06.S3.08.02,
  D06.S4.08.01, D06.S4.08.02)_

## Connection

You began this unit by remembering what you already knew, and you have added eight new ways of
seeing — from the atoms in a drop of water to the stars that made the atoms in your body. Now you
weave them together. A scientist is not someone who knows eight facts; a scientist is someone who
can see how the pieces connect into one living picture of the world. Today you build that picture.

## Materials

- Capstone map sheet
- Eight goal cards

## Preparation

- Copy the capstone map sheet and goal cards.
- Retrieval: this whole lesson is a retrieval and synthesis pass — the "science of learning" payoff
  of the unit (S-039). Have the learner's earlier portfolio artifacts on hand.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It is the **closing capstone and synthesis**. The
goal: **each learner recalls all eight goals and connects them into one web around systems, evidence,
and models.** The eight goals are: (1) design a controlled experiment — hypothesis, variables,
controls, repeated trials; (2) revise models when evidence appears — miasma → germ theory, and
continental drift → plate tectonics; (3) genes + environment shape inheritance and expression; (4)
one population ripples through an ecosystem, reducing or restoring balance; (5) atoms, elements,
compounds, and reactions rearrange matter; (6) electric and magnetic fields produce forces and
currents; (7) stars forge the elements and we live in the Milky Way; (8) plate tectonics shapes
continents, mountains, and ocean basins. This is a **retrieval and spacing** moment — having
learners recall rather than re-read is what makes the learning durable (S-039). Keep assessment
**mastery, not ranking**: a learner who cannot yet recall a goal simply revisits that lesson's
artifact and tries again — "not yet" is information, never a verdict. The four lenses, together: the
**critical-thinking** lens asks what evidence supports each claim; the **ethics** lens asks what is
right and who is affected; the **egalitarian** lens asks who benefits and who is left out;
the **global** lens asks how this looks in other places and times; the **environment** lens asks
what this means for the living Earth. Origins remain held as models with evidence, never creed
(philosophy §11). Celebrate the collective: each learner's map is a thread in one class web.

## Procedure

1. **Recall, don't peek (8 min).** Without looking at notes, list the unit's eight goals from
   memory. What can you remember? (That is the point — recalling *is* the learning.)
2. **Check and fill (5 min).** Compare with the eight goal cards. For any goal you missed, open that
   lesson's artifact and add it. "Not yet" is not a failure; it is a map of what to revisit.
3. **Build your web (15 min).** On the capstone map, write one sentence and draw one picture for
   each of the eight goals. Connect them to the center: **systems**, **evidence**, **models**.
4. **Choose a deep dive (15 min).** Pick the one goal that most interested you and explain it fully
   to a partner — what it means, what evidence supports it, and one way it touches real life. Then
   listen to your partner's.
5. **Weave the class web (7 min).** As a group, add everyone's maps to one shared web. Notice how
   the goals touch: atoms become stars, stars forge elements, elements build life, life forms webs.
6. **Close (5 min).** Look at your map. You did not memorize eight facts — you built a way of seeing.
   Carry it with you.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Work from the goal cards with pictures; choose one goal to explain instead of eight.
- **Extension:** Choose one goal and explain how it connects to at least two others (for example:
   stars forge the atoms that reactions rearrange, and plate tectonics is a model that was revised).

## Assessment

- **Summative (portfolio):** The completed capstone map — all eight goals, each with a sentence and
  a picture — kept in the portfolio.
- **Self/peer:** Learners self-check each goal is present and give one specific "I understood this
  when you said…" to a partner.
- **Mastery, not ranking:** A learner missing a goal revisits its artifact and completes it; the map
  is finished when it is finished, not before.

## Home connection

Show someone at home your capstone map and explain one goal end to end. Teaching it is the surest
sign you have learned it.
