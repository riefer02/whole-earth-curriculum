# Lesson 8 — Stars: Life Cycle, Forging Elements, Our Place in the Galaxy

## Summary

Learners describe the **life cycle of stars**, how stars **forge the elements**, and **our place in
the galaxy**. They trace a star from a cloud of gas, through fusion and supernova, to the elements
in our own bodies — **"we are made of star-stuff."**

## Objectives

- Describe the life cycle of stars, the formation of elements, and our place in the galaxy.
  _(D06.S4.08.01)_

## Connection

Look up at a clear night sky and you are looking at suns — some far bigger than ours, some long
dead, their light still traveling to you. And here is a wonder to hold: almost every atom in your
body — the iron in your blood, the calcium in your bones — was made inside a star that lived and
died long ago. You are, quite literally, made of star-stuff. Today you follow the life of a star and
find yourself in the story.

## Materials

- Star-lifecycle sheet
- Star-stage cards

## Preparation

- Copy the star-lifecycle sheet and stage cards.
- Retrieval: from Grade 6, recall the Sun is a star and Earth's place in the solar system; from
  Grade 7, recall deep time and that the universe is very old.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The ideas to land: **(1) stars are born in nebulae —
clouds of gas and dust that collapse under gravity; (2) a star shines by fusion — crushing hydrogen
into helium in its core and releasing energy (our Sun is doing this now); (3) when a Sun-like star
ages it swells into a red giant; the most massive stars end in a supernova — an explosion that
forges the heavier elements (carbon, oxygen, iron, and more) and scatters them into space; (4) new
stars and planets form from those remains — which is why the elements of Earth and of our bodies
came from stars; (5) our Sun is one of more than 100 billion stars in the Milky Way, a spiral
galaxy, which is one of many billions of galaxies.** Use the NASA star-lifecycle and galaxy sources
(S-361, S-362). The global lens: people in every culture have looked at the same stars and told
stories about them — the science account is *one* way of knowing, held alongside the others, never
above them (philosophy §11). The critical-thinking lens: we cannot watch one star live and die, so
the life cycle is a **model built from evidence** — we see stars at every stage across the sky, like
a forest with seedlings and old trees at once. The ethics lens: the story of star-stuff is a story
of shared origin — every person, everywhere, is made of the same cosmic material; no one is more or
less "of the universe" than anyone else. Never look directly at the Sun (S-047). Tell the story as
wonder and evidence, not creed.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Grade 6: what is the Sun? From Grade 7: how old is the universe? Today
   we follow a star's whole life.
2. **Meet the birth (8 min).** Stars are born in **nebulae** — great clouds of gas and dust that
   collapse under gravity and heat up until **fusion** begins.
3. **Meet the life (10 min).** A star shines by **fusion**: crushing hydrogen into helium and
   releasing energy. Our Sun has done this for about 4.6 billion years, and it will for billions
   more (S-047).
4. **Meet the death — and the gift (12 min).** A Sun-like star swells into a **red giant**. The most
   massive stars end in a **supernova** — an explosion that forges the heavier **elements** and
   scatters them into space. New stars and planets form from those remains. That is why the iron in
   your blood and the calcium in your bones came from stars.
5. **Meet our place (5 min).** Our Sun is one of more than 100 billion stars in the **Milky
   Way**, a spiral galaxy — and the Milky Way is one of many billions of galaxies (S-361, S-362).
6. **Arrange and retell (7 min).** Put the stage cards in order and retell the star's story in your
   own words, ending with "and that is how I came to be made of star-stuff."
7. **Close (3 min).** You are not separate from the universe; you are a piece of it, awake. Wonder
   at that.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide the stages pre-ordered and ask learners to match each to one thing it makes
  or leaves behind.
- **Extension:** Compare the life of a Sun-like star with that of a much more massive star, and name
  which elements each leaves behind.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner describe the star life cycle, where elements are
  forged, and our place in the galaxy?
- **Portfolio artifact:** The labeled star-lifecycle sheet, kept in the portfolio.

## Home connection

On the next clear night, find one star and wonder with someone: which of the atoms in me might have
been made in a star like that?
