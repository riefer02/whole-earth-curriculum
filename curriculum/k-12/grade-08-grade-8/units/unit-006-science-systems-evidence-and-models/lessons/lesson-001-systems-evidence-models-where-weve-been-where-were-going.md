# Lesson 1 — Systems, Evidence & Models: Where We've Been, Where We're Going

## Summary

Learners **recall three Grade-7 science ideas** — correlation versus causation, variation and
natural selection, and deep time — and **preview this unit's four strands and eight goals**. They
meet the unit's organizing idea: science looks at the world in **systems**, builds **models**, and
tests them with **evidence**.

## Objectives

- Begin to name the parts of a controlled experiment — a hypothesis, variables, and a fair test —
  as the unit's first goal. _(D06.S1.08.01)_
- Begin to name what a scientific model is and the idea that models are revised, not thrown away,
  when evidence changes. _(D06.S1.08.02)_

## Connection

Think of something you have wondered about and checked for yourself — whether a plant grows toward
a window, whether a coin always lands one way, why the moon changes shape. Every one of those
questions is a doorway into science. Science is not a list of answers to memorize; it is a way of
asking questions, building a **model** of how something works, and checking that model against
**evidence** from the real world. Today we remember what we already know how to do and see where it
is taking us.

## Materials

- Unit map sheet
- Recall cards

## Preparation

- Copy the unit map sheet and recall cards.
- Retrieval: from Grade 7, recall (a) correlation versus causation, (b) variation and natural
  selection, and (c) deep time. Today we link those to the new unit.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It is the unit's **opening and retrieval pass**, so
the goals are modest: **(1) recall three Grade-7 ideas — correlation is not causation (a pattern
alone does not prove a cause), variation plus inheritance and natural selection shape populations,
and evidence in fossils, rock layers, and ice cores tells the story of deep time; (2) preview the
unit's four strands — inquiry, living systems, physical systems, Earth/space — and its eight goals;
(3) plant the organizing idea: science studies *systems*, builds *models*, and tests them with
*evidence*.** Do not try to teach the eight goals today; just name and locate them. The
critical-thinking lens: "correlation is not causation" is the habit we carry forward (S-306, S-355).
The global lens: the same way of asking questions works everywhere, though the questions and the
living things differ by place. The ethics lens: honest science distinguishes what the evidence shows
from what we value — that distinction runs through the whole unit (philosophy §5). Keep origins
open: nothing here is creed (philosophy §11). Spacing note (S-039): this recall pass is what makes
the unit's ideas stick later.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (10 min).** In a small group, look at three recall cards: **correlation vs. causation**,
   **variation and natural selection**, **deep time**. For each, say in one sentence what you
   remember from Grade 7.
2. **Connect the three (5 min).** What do all three have in common? (Each is about *how we know*
   something — through evidence, not just a guess.)
3. **Meet the map (10 min).** Look at the unit map. Four strands: **scientific inquiry**, **living
   systems**, **physical systems**, **Earth, space & origins**. Eight goals — two per strand. Read
   each goal aloud once; don't worry about understanding them yet.
4. **Plant the big idea (10 min).** The unit's idea in one breath: science studies **systems**
   (things made of parts that work together), builds **models** (simplified pictures of how
   something works), and tests them with **evidence**. Say it back in your own words.
5. **Wonder (10 min).** Write one question about each strand in the wonder box. Which question do
   you most want answered by the end of the unit?
6. **Close (5 min).** Today we remembered what we know and saw the road ahead. Next: we design a
   controlled experiment.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Give sentence starters for the recall round ("A pattern is not a cause because…").
- **Extension:** Add a fourth Grade-7 idea of your own and say why it will matter in this unit.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner recall each Grade-7 idea in one sentence and name the
  unit's four strands?
- **Artifact:** The completed unit map (or the learner's written recall), kept in the portfolio.

## Home connection

Ask someone at home: what is one thing you once believed, then changed your mind about because of
new evidence? Bring the story (no names needed) to the next lesson.
