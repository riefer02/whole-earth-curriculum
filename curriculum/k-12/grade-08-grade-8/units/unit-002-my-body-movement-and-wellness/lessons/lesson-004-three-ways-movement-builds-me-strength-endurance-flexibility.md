# Lesson 4 — Three Ways Movement Builds Me: Strength, Endurance, Flexibility

## Summary

Learners distinguish the three qualities movement builds — **strength**, **endurance**, and
**flexibility** — and sort everyday movements into the three kinds. They practice one of each at
their own level, always with a seated or adapted version available.

## Objectives

- Explain the difference between strength, endurance, and flexibility, and name an example of each
  kind of movement. _(D03.S2.08.01)_

## Connection

Every time you move, your body is quietly getting better at something. Carrying a heavy bag builds
**strength**. Walking or wheeling for a long stretch builds **endurance**. Reaching, bending, and
stretching build **flexibility**. You do not need a gym to grow these three — they are built by
ordinary, everyday movement, done at whatever level is right for your own body. Today you learn to
see the difference.

## Materials

- Movement-kind cards
- Three-kinds sheet

## Preparation

- Copy the movement-kind cards and three-kinds sheet.
- Retrieval: from Grade 7, recall how movement changes during puberty and how to adapt activities
  to your own changing body (D03.S2.07.02). Today we add the "why" — what each kind of movement
  actually builds.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The ideas to land: **(1) strength = muscles working
against a load; endurance = heart, lungs, and muscles working over a longer time; flexibility =
range of motion at the joints; (2) each is built by movement you already do, at your own level;
(3) no movement quality is "better" — a balanced body uses all three, and every body starts where
it starts.** Model each kind once (worked example, S-011), then guide practice. The WHO frames
regular physical activity and a mix of aerobic and muscle-strengthening work (S-341); hold it as a
health guideline, not a ranking. **This is a body lesson — teach it with dignity: never comment on
any learner's body size or shape, never time learners against each other, never rank speed or
strength. "Your level is the right level." Always offer a seated or adapted version alongside the
standing one, and treat assistive devices (wheelchairs, prosthetics, canes) as ordinary parts of a
body in motion.** The ethics lens: how we speak about bodies — our own and others' — is an ethical
choice; we choose respect. The egalitarian lens: everyone can build strength, endurance, and
flexibility, whatever their body or ability — the movements look different, the qualities are the
same. The global lens: people everywhere build these three through the movements of daily life —
carrying water, farming, dancing, cycling, swimming — not only through sport. The technology &
environment lens: a body that mostly sits at screens misses the movement it is built for, and safe,
welcoming places to move are not equally available to everyone. The critical-thinking lens:
"movement builds the body" is a checkable scientific fact; "some bodies are better than others" is
a false value we refuse.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Grade 7: how did movement change during puberty, and how did you adapt
   activities to your own body? Today we ask what movement actually builds.
2. **Meet the three qualities (12 min).** **Strength** is your muscles working against a load —
   lifting, carrying, pushing. **Endurance** is your heart, lungs, and muscles working over a
   longer time — walking, wheeling, cycling, dancing. **Flexibility** is your joints' range of
   motion — reaching, bending, stretching. Notice: no one quality is "best"; a healthy body uses
   all three.
3. **Sort the movements (15 min).** With a group, sort the movement-kind cards into strength,
   endurance, or flexibility. For each, say what the body is building and why. Some movements (like
   walking uphill) build more than one — put those between the columns.
4. **Try one of each (13 min).** Do one strength movement (a wall push or a lift), one endurance
   movement (a steady walk, wheel, or march in place), and one flexibility movement (a gentle
   reach or stretch) — each at your own level, with a seated or adapted version if you prefer.
   Move gently and stop if anything hurts.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: **your body grows stronger, longer-lasting, and freer with every
   kind of movement — and your level is the right level.**

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide two pre-sorted cards and ask learners to match the movement to its quality.
- **Extension:** Find one movement that builds two qualities at once and explain how (e.g., walking
  uphill builds both endurance and strength).

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner sort a movement into strength, endurance, or
  flexibility and say why?
- **Portfolio artifact:** The completed three-kinds sheet, kept in the portfolio.

## Home connection

At home, find one everyday movement for each quality — one that needs strength, one for endurance,
one for flexibility — and name them to someone.
