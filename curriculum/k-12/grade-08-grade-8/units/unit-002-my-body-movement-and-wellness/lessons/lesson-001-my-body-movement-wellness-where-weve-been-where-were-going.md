# Lesson 1 — My Body, Movement & Wellness: Where We've Been, Where We're Going

## Summary

Learners open the unit by **retrieving** what they learned in Grade 7 and **previewing** the
four quadrants of "My Body, Movement & Wellness." They meet the unit map, write three wonders,
and sort them into the quadrants — starting the unit portfolio they will add to every lesson.

## Objectives

- Preview the unit's four learning goals — explaining the nervous system's fight, flight, or
  rest response; describing stress's effect on the body and healthy ways to return to calm;
  designing a movement routine for strength, endurance, and flexibility; and demonstrating fair
  play and inclusive practices. _(D03.S1.08.01, D03.S1.08.02, D03.S2.08.01, D03.S2.08.02 — preview)_

## Connection

Your body has been with you every single day of your life — sensing, moving, resting, and
carrying your feelings. It can speed up to meet a sudden danger and slow down to rest, and it
changes with how you live. This unit is a chance to understand that body from the inside, and to
learn to care for it and move with it in ways that welcome every body around you.

## Materials

- Unit map card
- Wonder slips

## Preparation

- Copy the unit map card and wonder slips.
- Retrieval: from Grade 7, recall four ideas — **internal body signals** (D03.S1.07.01), **how
  media images shape body feelings** (D03.S1.07.02), **game strategies like spacing and timing**
  (D03.S2.07.01), and **how movement changes during puberty** (D03.S2.07.02). Each returns this
  unit, at a deeper level.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The ideas to land: **(1) this unit has four
quadrants — Sense & Respond, Unwind, Move with Purpose, and Play Fair — all meeting in one body;
(2) we build on Grade 7, not start over; (3) wondering is the engine — learners begin by asking.**
Retrieval practice here is deliberate (spaced recall of last year's ideas — Dunlosky et al. 2013,
S-039); do not skip it. **This unit is about the body, so teach it with dignity: never comment on
any learner's body, weight, or ability; never rank bodies or abilities; every body is "the right
body" for this work.** The ethics lens: how we treat our own and others' bodies is a question of
right and wrong. The egalitarian lens: ask early "who gets welcomed into movement and who gets
left out" — it returns in Lesson 6. The global lens: bodies, games, and ways of caring for the
body differ across peoples and places; keep the frame broad, never one region's default. The
technology & environment lens: screens, devices, and the places we can safely move all shape the
body — this returns across the unit. Hold every wonder as valid; the goal is the asking.

## Procedure

1. **Gather and recall (12 min).** Think back to Grade 7. With a partner, recall: what internal
   body signals could you name (hunger, fatigue, stress)? How can media images shape how a person
   feels about their body? What game strategies — spacing, timing, teamwork — did you practice?
   How did movement change during puberty? Share one with the group.
2. **Meet the unit map (8 min).** Look at the unit map. The unit has four quadrants: **Sense &
   Respond**, **Unwind**, **Move with Purpose**, and **Play Fair** — all around one **my body**.
   Notice the quadrants are not separate boxes; they are four ways of looking at one living body.
3. **Write your wonders (15 min).** On three slips, write three things you wonder about your
   body's sensing, your stress, your movement, or how people play together. There are no wrong
   wonders.
4. **Sort the wonders (10 min).** Place each slip under the quadrant it belongs to. If it fits
   two quadrants, put it between them — that is where the best questions live.
5. **Close (5 min).** Keep your wonders. At the end of the unit you will return to them and see
   what you now can say.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Give sentence starters ("I wonder what happens when ___", "I wonder how I could
  ___").
- **Extension:** Write a "wonder + guess" pair — your question and your current best answer — and
  mark it to check again at the capstone.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name the four quadrants and sort a wonder into one
  (or between two) with a reason?
- **Portfolio artifact:** The three wonder slips, kept as the first page of the unit portfolio.

## Home connection

Ask someone at home: "What is one thing your body tells you, and one way you help it settle when
life feels loud?" Bring their answer to mind as the unit unfolds.
