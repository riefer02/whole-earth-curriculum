# Lesson 5 — Designing My Movement Routine

## Summary

Learners **design a short movement routine and explain how each part builds strength, endurance,
or flexibility**. They assemble a warm-up, a strength part, an endurance part, a flexibility part,
and a cool-down, label each, and include a seated or adapted version.

## Objectives

- Design a short movement routine and explain how each part builds strength, endurance, or
  flexibility. _(D03.S2.08.01)_

## Connection

Athletes, dancers, and people who simply care for their bodies do the same quiet thing: they
**design** their movement on purpose, choosing each part for what it builds. You can too, with no
gym and no coach — just a clear plan and your own body. Today you build a routine you could do
anywhere, and you will be able to say exactly why each part helps.

## Materials

- Routine-builder card
- Movement menu

## Preparation

- Copy the routine-builder card and movement menu.
- Retrieval: from Lesson 4, recall the three qualities — **strength**, **endurance**,
  **flexibility** — and one example of each. Today we put them together into one routine.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The ideas to land: **(1) a routine has a shape —
warm-up, a strength part, an endurance part, a flexibility part, and a cool-down; (2) each part
has a job — strength works muscles against a load, endurance works heart and lungs over time,
flexibility works range of motion; (3) a good routine is safe, gentle to start, and adaptable to
any body.** Design is a skill — model one full routine as a worked example, then guide learners
through building their own, then let them do it (explicit instruction → guided → independent,
S-011). The WHO frames a balanced mix of movement (S-341). **Body dignity is non-negotiable: never
rank routines against each other, never comment on a body's shape, never push anyone past comfort.
"Your level is the right level." Ensure every movement on the menu has a seated or adapted version,
and treat assistive devices as ordinary.** The ethics lens: designing for safety and self-care is
acting responsibly toward one's own body. The egalitarian lens: a routine anyone can do — seated,
standing, fast, slow — widens movement's door to every body. The global lens: routines can draw on
movement from many cultures — dance, stretching, martial-arts forms such as capoeira (Brazil) or
tai chi (China), and daily work — all valid sources. The technology & environment lens: a routine that needs no equipment, screen, or paid
space works in more lives and more places. The critical-thinking lens: "this part builds endurance
because it keeps my heart and lungs working over time" is a checkable reason, not a guess.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Lesson 4: name the three qualities and one movement for each. Today we
   arrange them into a routine.
2. **Meet the routine's shape (10 min).** A routine has five parts: a **warm-up** (gentle movement
   to prepare the body), a **strength** part, an **endurance** part, a **flexibility** part, and a
   **cool-down** (slow, easy movement to settle the body).
3. **Watch one modeled (10 min).** Follow a sample routine: **warm-up** — march gently in place or
   roll your shoulders; **strength** — ten wall pushes or lifts; **endurance** — two steady minutes
   of walking, wheeling, or marching; **flexibility** — a slow reach to the sky and a gentle side
   stretch; **cool-down** — slow breaths and a soft shake of the hands. Each part has a label and a
   reason.
4. **Design your own (15 min).** On your routine-builder card, write your own five parts, choosing
   from the movement menu (or inventing your own). Label each part and write one sentence saying
   what it builds and why.
5. **Do it and check (10 min).** Do your routine once. Check: did each part do its job? Move gently
   and stop if anything hurts. Adjust one part if you want to.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a partially filled routine-builder card with the five parts labeled, so the
  learner only chooses one movement for each.
- **Extension:** Add a note to each part explaining how to adapt it for a friend who needs a seated
  or slower version.

## Assessment

- **Formative (performance):** Can the learner do their routine and explain what each part builds?
- **Portfolio artifact:** The completed routine-builder card, kept in the portfolio.

## Home connection

Teach one part of your routine to someone at home — one strength, endurance, or flexibility
movement — and tell them why it helps.
