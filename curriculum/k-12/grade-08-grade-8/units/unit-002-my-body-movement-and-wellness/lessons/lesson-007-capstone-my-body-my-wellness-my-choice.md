# Lesson 7 — Capstone: My Body, My Wellness, My Choice

## Summary

The unit capstone brings all four quadrants together. Learners **retrieve** the unit's ideas,
**revisit the wonders** they wrote in Lesson 1, and assemble a personal **wellness-and-movement
plan** read through all four lenses — ethics, egalitarianism, global, and technology & environment.

## Objectives

- Synthesize the unit's four goals — sensing and responding, unwinding, moving with purpose, and
  playing fairly — into one coherent view of caring for one's own body and welcoming every body.
  _(D03.S1.08.01, D03.S1.08.02, D03.S2.08.01, D03.S2.08.02 — synthesis)_

## Connection

For seven days you have looked at one body through four windows: how it senses and responds, how
it settles from stress, how it grows stronger and freer through movement, and how it plays fairly
with others. Now you bring those windows together. Understanding is not the finish line — it is the
starting point for choosing. Today you write down how you will care for your own body and welcome
every body around you.

## Materials

- Wellness-and-movement plan
- Unit map (from Lesson 1)

## Preparation

- Copy the wellness-and-movement plan.
- Return each learner's Lesson 1 unit map and wonder slips.
- Retrieval: this lesson is itself a **spaced, interleaved retrieval pass** across the whole unit
  (Dunlosky et al. 2013, S-039) — it works because learners must *recall*, not re-read.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The ideas to land: **(1) the four quadrants are one
thing — sensing, unwinding, moving, and playing all meet in how we care for a body; (2) the four
lenses (ethics, egalitarianism, global, technology & environment) are how we check those choices;
(3) a plan is a promise to yourself, and it must fit your own body — no body is the wrong body.**
This is a **summative** moment, but mastery is personal, not ranked — celebrate growth, never
compare learners or bodies. The ethics lens: "what is right in how I treat my body and others'?"
The egalitarian lens: "does my way of moving and playing welcome every body, or leave some out?"
The global lens: "which practices from many cultures do I draw on, and do I honor them?" The
technology & environment lens: "do my screens and my places help or hurt my body, and what will I
choose?" Assess by what each learner can now *explain and choose*, not by a score. Keep hope and
agency central — the unit ends on what each learner can do.

## Procedure

1. **Recall the whole unit (10 min).** With a partner, recall the four ideas: how the nervous
   system senses and moves between fight, flight, and rest; how stress affects the body over time
   and how to unwind; what strength, endurance, and flexibility build; and what fair play and
   inclusion look like. Name them without looking.
2. **Revisit your wonders (8 min).** Open your Lesson 1 map and wonder slips. Which wonders can you
   now answer? Which have grown into new questions? Answer one you can, and mark one to keep
   wondering about.
3. **Build your plan (20 min).** On your plan, write: **(1)** one stress signal you notice in your
   body and one calm practice you will use; **(2)** one movement for each quality — strength,
   endurance, flexibility; **(3)** one way you will make a game or movement welcoming to every
   body; **(4)** one choice about your screens or your spaces that helps your body.
4. **Run it through the lenses (7 min).** Check your plan: Is it right (ethics)? Does it welcome
   every body (egalitarianism)? Does it draw on and honor practices from more than one culture
   (global)? Does it account for technology and place (technology & environment)? Adjust one line.
5. **Close (5 min).** Share one line you are proud of. Remember: **your body is yours to
   understand and to care for — and so is the choosing.**

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide sentence starters for each line ("One calm practice I will use is ___ because
   ___").
- **Extension:** Add a "what I will check back on in a month" line, naming how you will know if it
  is helping.

## Assessment

- **Summative (portfolio + self):** The completed wellness-and-movement plan is the unit's capstone
  artifact, kept in the portfolio.
- **Self-assessment:** Learners mark one line they feel confident explaining and one line they want
  to keep learning about — "not yet" is information, never a verdict.

## Home connection

Share your plan with someone at home, and invite them to add one line of their own — one choice
they will make to care for their body or welcome every body.
