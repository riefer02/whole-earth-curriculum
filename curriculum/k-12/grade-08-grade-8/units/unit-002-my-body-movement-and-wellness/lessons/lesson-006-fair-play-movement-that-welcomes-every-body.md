# Lesson 6 — Fair Play: Movement That Welcomes Every Body

## Summary

Learners **demonstrate fair play and inclusive practices that welcome peers of all abilities into
movement and games**. They name the habits of fair play, adapt a simple game so every ability can
join, and play their adapted version together.

## Objectives

- Demonstrate fair play and inclusive practices that welcome peers of all abilities into movement
  and games. _(D03.S2.08.02)_

## Connection

Every game carries a quiet promise: that everyone who plays is safe, respected, and able to join
in. When a game leaves someone out — because they cannot reach the ball, keep the pace, or stand
the whole time — the promise is broken, and it is usually the rules, not the person, that are the
problem. The good news is that rules can change. Today you learn to play fairly, and to change
games so every body is welcomed.

## Materials

- Game-adaptation cards
- Fair-play circle sheet

## Preparation

- Copy the game-adaptation cards and fair-play circle sheet.
- Retrieval: from Grade 6, recall working cooperatively with teammates and encouraging others
  regardless of skill level (D03.S2.06.02). Today we add adapting the game itself so everyone can
  join.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The ideas to land: **(1) fair play is a set of
habits — follow the rules, play honestly, respect everyone (teammates, opponents, and the spirit of
the game), and win or lose with grace; (2) inclusion is a practice — adapt the equipment, rules,
roles, or pace so every ability can join; (3) it is the game that should bend to fit people, never
people who must bend to fit the game.** Model one adaptation as a worked example, then let learners
adapt and play (S-011). The UNESCO International Charter frames physical activity and sport as a
fundamental right for all, to be practiced inclusively (S-342); hold it as a shared value we act
on. **This is the unit's heart for egalitarianism — who gets welcomed and who gets left out. Never
frame inclusion as "helping the less able"; frame it as fixing a game whose rules were never
designed for everyone. No ranking, no elimination games, no using bodies as the measure of worth.**
The ethics lens: fair play is right conduct — honesty and respect are chosen, every time. The
egalitarian lens: a game is only fair if every body can play; ask "who is left out, and how do we
change the game, not the person?" The global lens: games differ across the world — mancala-family
counting games such as Ayo (West Africa) and Pallanguli (South India), Kabaddi (South Asia), sepak
takraw (Southeast Asia), and countless others — and many traditional games already mix ages and
abilities, welcoming players of different ages and bodies. The technology &
environment lens: some games need expensive equipment or special fields, which quietly exclude;
low-tech, adaptable play opens the door wider. The critical-thinking lens: "the rules leave someone
out" is a claim you can check and fix; "that person isn't suited to games" is a false claim we
refuse.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Grade 6: how did you encourage teammates regardless of skill level?
   Today we adapt the game itself.
2. **Meet fair play (10 min).** Fair play means: **follow the rules** you all agreed on; **play
   honestly** (no cheating, no pretending the ball was in when it was out); **respect everyone** —
   teammates, opponents, and the game itself; and **win or lose with grace** (a win is not a chance
   to gloat, a loss is not a verdict on your worth).
3. **Learn to adapt (10 min).** Look at the game-adaptation cards. To welcome every ability, change
   one of four things: the **equipment** (a bigger, softer, or brighter ball), the **rules** (allow
   two bounces, or let a pass replace a throw), the **roles** (a player who calls, times, or
   officiates), or the **pace** (slower, with rests). The game bends; the person doesn't have to.
4. **Adapt and play a game (20 min).** In your group, choose a simple game and adapt it once so
   that a friend who moves, sees, hears, or thinks differently can join fully. Play your adapted
   version. Notice how it feels when everyone is in.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: **a fair game welcomes every body — and we are the ones who make it
   welcome.**

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a pre-written adaptation and ask the learner to name who it welcomes and why.
- **Extension:** Design a brand-new game from scratch that anyone can play, and write its rules and
  one inclusive adaptation.

## Assessment

- **Formative (performance + observation):** Can the learner name the habits of fair play and
  adapt one game rule or piece of equipment so a peer of a different ability can join?
- **Portfolio artifact:** The completed fair-play circle sheet, kept in the portfolio.

## Home connection

Ask someone at home about a game they loved as a child, and think together about one way to adapt
it so someone of a different ability could play too.
