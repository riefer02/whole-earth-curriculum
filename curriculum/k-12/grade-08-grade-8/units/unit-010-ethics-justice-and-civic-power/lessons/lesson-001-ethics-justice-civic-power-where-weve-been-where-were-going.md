# Lesson 1 — Ethics, Justice & Civic Power: Where We've Been, Where We're Going

## Summary

Learners **recall four Grade-7 ideas** — the *who is helped, who is harmed, what is fair*
frame, *human rights*, *how people influence decisions*, and *restorative steps* — and
**preview this unit's four strands and eight goals**. They meet the unit's organizing idea:
we **weigh dilemmas**, **compare ideas of justice**, **study collective action**, and
**pursue peace** — and we hold every question as a question, never as a settled answer.

## Objectives

- Begin to name the unit's eight goals and see how they trace to the four strands.
  _(D09.S1.08.01–D09.S4.08.02)_
- Recall from Grade 7 the tools we already carry — weighing who is helped and harmed, naming
  human rights, noticing how people influence decisions, and repairing harm through listening.
  _(builds on D09.S1.07.01, D09.S2.07.02, D09.S3.07.01, D09.S4.07.01)_

## Connection

Think of the last time you saw something unfair — a line skipped, a voice talked over, a place
closed to some people but not others. In that moment you were already doing ethics: you noticed
something was wrong, you cared, and you wondered what to do. Ethics is not a subject that lives
only in books; it is the everyday habit of asking *is this right? who is helped? who is left
out?* and then deciding how to act. Today we gather the tools we already have and see where they
are taking us.

## Materials

- Unit map sheet
- Recall cards

## Preparation

- Copy the unit map sheet and recall cards.
- Retrieval: from Grade 7, recall (a) the frame *who is helped, who is harmed, what is fair*,
  (b) *human rights*, (c) *how people influence decisions*, and (d) *restorative steps*. Today
  we link those to the new unit.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It is the unit's **opening and retrieval pass**,
so the goals are modest: **(1) recall four Grade-7 ideas — the helped/harmed/fair frame (S-001),
human rights (S-060, S-149), ways people influence decisions, and restorative steps (S-022,
S-161); (2) preview the unit's four strands — moral reasoning & values, fairness/justice/rights,
civic participation & collective action, peace/conflict & restorative practice — and its eight
goals; (3) plant the organizing idea: we *ask, weigh, and act*, and we hold questions open
(philosophy §11).** Do not try to teach the eight goals today; just name and locate them. The
ethics lens: the whole unit is one long practice in weighing what is right. The egalitarianism
lens: every question asks *who benefits and who is left out*. The global lens: the same
questions are asked differently in every culture, and none of those ways is the "default"
(philosophy §4). The critical-thinking lens: we distinguish evidence from values all year
(philosophy §5). Spacing note (S-039): this recall pass is what makes the unit's ideas stick
later. Keep it warm and open — nothing here is a verdict, a ranking, or a creed.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (10 min).** In a small group, look at four recall cards: **helped/harmed/fair**,
   **human rights**, **influence decisions**, **restorative steps**. For each, say in one
   sentence what you remember from Grade 7.
2. **Connect the four (5 min).** What do all four have in common? (Each is about how people
   decide what is right and act on it — alone and together.)
3. **Meet the map (10 min).** Look at the unit map. Four strands: **moral reasoning & values**,
   **fairness, justice & rights**, **civic participation & collective action**, **peace,
   conflict & restorative practice**. Eight goals — two per strand. Read each goal aloud once;
   don't worry about understanding them yet.
4. **Plant the big idea (10 min).** The unit's idea in one breath: we **ask** what is right,
   **weigh** it with care, and **act** with others — and we never pretend the answer is easy or
   already settled. Say it back in your own words.
5. **Wonder (10 min).** Write one question about each strand in the wonder box. Which question
   do you most want answered by the end of the unit?
6. **Close (5 min).** Today we remembered what we know and saw the road ahead. Next: we weigh a
   real dilemma — consequences, duties, and fairness.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Give sentence starters for the recall round ("A right is…", "To repair harm
  means…").
- **Extension:** Add a fifth Grade-7 idea of your own and say why it will matter in this unit.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner recall each Grade-7 idea in one sentence and name
  the unit's four strands?
- **Artifact:** The completed unit map (or the learner's written recall), kept in the portfolio.

## Home connection

Ask someone at home: what is one thing that felt unfair when they were your age, and what did
they do about it? Bring the story (no names needed) to the next lesson.
