# Lesson 10 — Capstone: Ethics, Justice & Civic Power — Putting It Together

## Summary

Learners **gather the unit's eight goals into one web** around the habit **ask, weigh, act**,
**revisit their own portfolio artifacts**, and write a short reflection on the habit they will
carry forward. The capstone is a **spaced-retrieval pass and a celebration**, not a test.

## Objectives

- Revisit and connect all eight grade-8 goals of the unit, and reflect on the habit of asking,
  weighing, and acting. _(D09.S1.08.01–D09.S4.08.02)_

## Connection

A tool you use once is soon forgotten; a tool you use many times becomes part of your hand. This
unit gave you tools — three lenses for a dilemma, three ideas of justice, a way to see power and
access, a frame for movements, a scale for digital risks, a path through conflict to
reconciliation. Today you gather them all, look at the work your own hands made, and decide
which of these tools you will actually carry — because a habit is a tool you keep using long
after the lesson ends.

## Materials

- Capstone web sheet
- Portfolio artifacts from the unit

## Preparation

- Copy the capstone web sheets.
- Have learners gather their artifacts from Lessons 2–9: the dilemma position, the values
  comparison, the justice chart, the power-and-access chart, the movement evaluation, the
  digital risk-benefit chart, the conflict position, and the reconciliation path chart.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner. It is the unit's **closing retrieval-and-synthesis pass**,
so its goals are modest and its tone is celebratory (S-039; Dunlosky et al. on spaced retrieval).
**(1) Recall the eight goals** — one per strand: weigh consequences/duties/fairness and values
differ across cultures (S1); compare ideas of justice and power/access shape rights (S2); analyze
a movement and digital participation (S3); evaluate a conflict and pursue reconciliation (S4).
**(2) Connect them** through the one habit: **ask, weigh, act.** **(3) Reflect** on the learner's
own work and write a forward-looking sentence. Do **not** introduce new content or grade a
"final answer" — this is mastery-through-recall and a portfolio celebration, not a ranking
(philosophy §8, §11). The four lenses reappear as a summary: ethics (the habit of weighing),
egalitarianism (always asking who is left out), global (the same questions, asked differently
everywhere), critical-thinking (evidence vs. values, questions held open). If a learner has an
incomplete artifact, invite them to finish it now rather than mark it "done" (mastery, not
ranking). End with the unit's through-line, spoken plainly: *we hold questions as questions —
and we act anyway, with care.*

## Procedure

1. **Rebuild the web (12 min).** Look at the capstone web sheet. Around the center **ask,
   weigh, act**, the eight goals are written. For each goal, say aloud one thing you *did* in
   this unit that practiced it.
2. **Connect the goals (8 min).** Choose two goals that feel connected and write one sentence
   saying how — for example, *power and access* (who reaches a right) connects to *social
   movements* (how the left-out organize to widen access).
3. **Revisit your work (15 min).** Open your portfolio artifacts. Pick the one you are proudest
   of and the one you would now do differently. Write one sentence for each: *why this one, and
   what changed in my thinking.*
4. **Name the habit (10 min).** Write three sentences: which of these tools will you actually
   carry — and where in your real life will you use it? Be specific (a place, a relationship, a
   decision).
5. **Share and celebrate (8 min).** In a circle, each learner shares their one-sentence
   takeaway — a goal they now hold more clearly, or a tool they will carry. Listen without
   judging; every takeaway is a real one.
6. **Close (2 min).** We ask, we weigh, we act — and we keep the questions open. Carry the
   habit forward.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Match each artifact to its goal on the web before writing the reflection.
- **Extension:** Write a real dilemma of your own and weigh it with all three lenses as a final
  demonstration of the unit's first goal.

## Assessment

- **Portfolio (summative):** The assembled artifacts from Lessons 2–9, plus the capstone
  reflection, show the learner's growth across the unit's eight goals.
- **Self:** The learner names one tool they will carry and where they will use it.

## Home connection

Share your capstone takeaway with someone at home, and ask them: what is one habit of fairness
or care *they* try to live by? Compare your answers.
