# Unit 10 — Ethics, Justice & Civic Power

## Overview

This unit turns the questions of the year inward to the self and the group, building on
Grade 7's framework of who is helped, who is harmed, and what is fair toward full
*evaluation*. Learners evaluate a real-world moral dilemma by weighing consequences,
duties, and fairness, and defend a reasoned position, and explain how values and moral
codes differ across cultures and argue for respectful engagement with those
differences. They compare different ideas of justice — equal treatment, need, and
restitution — and apply them to a real case, and analyze how power and access shape who
gets their rights protected and who does not. They analyze a social movement — its
goals, strategies, and effects — and evaluate what made it succeed or struggle, and
explain how digital tools change civic participation, weighing the risks and benefits
of organizing online. They evaluate a real conflict and weigh peaceful, restorative,
and other responses, defending a reasoned choice, and analyze how groups and societies
have pursued peace and reconciliation after conflict, using a historical example.
Questions are held as questions, never answered as dogma.

## Essential questions

- How do I evaluate a real moral dilemma by weighing consequences, duties, and fairness, and how do values and moral codes differ across cultures?
- How do I compare ideas of justice and see how power and access shape whose rights are protected?
- How do social movements work, how do digital tools change civic participation, and how do people pursue peace and reconciliation after conflict?
