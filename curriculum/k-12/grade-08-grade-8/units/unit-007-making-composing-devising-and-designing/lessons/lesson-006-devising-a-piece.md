# Lesson 6 — Devising a Piece: One Theme, Made Through Rehearsal

## Summary

Learners devise (choreograph) a short piece exploring one theme through a loop — **choose a theme,
explore movement, shape and rehearse, revise, share** — and reflect in writing on how the work changed
through rehearsal. They meet the idea that devised and improvised traditions, such as capoeira, are
living forms made and remade in the doing.

## Objectives

- Choreograph or devise a piece exploring one theme. _(D07.S3.08.01)_
- Reflect on how the work changed through rehearsal. _(D07.S3.08.01)_

## Connection

You already "devise" movement every time you mime a story, dance to a song, or show a feeling with your
whole body instead of words. Devising is just doing that on purpose: taking **one theme** — an idea
worth moving about — and building a piece by trying many ways to show it, then choosing, ordering, and
practicing the ones that work. The piece will not be the same at the end as it was at the start, and
that is the point: **rehearsal is where the piece becomes itself.**

## Materials

- Devising loop sheet
- Rehearsal reflection sheet

## Preparation

- Copy the devising loop sheet and rehearsal reflection sheet; clear a safe space to move.
- Retrieval: from Grade 7, recall performing a scene or dance with attention to timing, expression, and
  ensemble — today we move from performing to *devising*.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The skill to land: **devising is a loop — (1) choose a
theme, (2) explore movement (try many ways to show it), (3) shape and rehearse (choose, order,
practice), (4) revise (change it after feedback), (5) share — and the *change across rehearsals* is the
point.** This is a *making* skill, so **model the loop once with a tiny example** (show three movement
ideas for one theme, then choose, order, and revise), then let learners devise in small groups or alone
(explicit instruction carries novice skills — S-011). The physical lens: this is body learning — honor
every body and ability; movement can be large or small, seated or standing, and stillness is movement
too. The global lens: devising and improvisation are living traditions worldwide — **capoeira**, for
example, is a Brazilian art that weaves movement, music, and play together, improvised within a shared
circle (S-056). Teach such traditions as *living forms to honor with respectful questions*, never as
specimens to copy carelessly. The egalitarian lens: devising needs only a body and a space — no
equipment, no particular ability. The ethics lens: when devising in a group, every voice shapes the
piece; no one's idea is "better," and consent matters — no one is touched or lifted without asking, and
performing in front of the group is a choice, never a requirement. Keep
assessment mastery-based: a piece that is still changing is "not yet," never a failure — change is the
work.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Grade 7: what did you attend to in performance — timing, expression,
   ensemble? Today we move from performing to **devising**.
2. **Meet the loop (10 min).** Look at the devising loop sheet. Five moves: **choose a theme**, **explore
   movement**, **shape & rehearse**, **revise**, **share**. The loop repeats — each pass changes the
   piece. Capoeira is one example of a living art made in the doing (S-056).
3. **Modeled loop (10 min).** Together, take one theme (for example, *waiting*). Try three movement
   ideas for it. Choose two, order them, practice once, then change one thing after a partner's
   suggestion. Notice the change.
4. **Devise your own (20 min).** Choose your theme. Explore at least three movement ideas. Shape them
   into a short piece (about 30–60 seconds), rehearse it two or three times, and after each rehearsal
   note on the reflection sheet what changed and why.
5. **Reflect (5 min).** Write two or three sentences: how did your piece change from the first rehearsal
   to the last, and why? What did feedback teach you?
6. **Close (5 min).** Share your piece with a partner and say one thing that changed through rehearsal.
   Rehearsal is where the piece becomes itself.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Devise a single movement phrase on one theme and record one change and its reason.
- **Extension:** Add a second element — music, a prop, or a partner — and explain how it changed the
  piece.
- **Access (performance anxiety):** Performing for others is a choice, never a requirement. A learner
  who does not want to perform in front of the group may instead share the rehearsal reflection, a short
  recording, a drawn or spoken description of the movement plan, or the piece with a single trusted
  partner. The goal is the devising and the reflection on change — not performing in front of everyone.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner devise a piece on one theme and name what changed through
  rehearsal?
- **Performance/portfolio artifact:** The performed piece plus the rehearsal reflection, kept in the
  portfolio.

## Home connection

At home, choose a feeling and show it three different ways with your body. Which way works best, and
why? Bring your answer to the next lesson.
