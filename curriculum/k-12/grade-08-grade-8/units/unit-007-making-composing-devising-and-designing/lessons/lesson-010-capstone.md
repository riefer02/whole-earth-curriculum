# Lesson 10 — Capstone: The Series, the Piece, the Performance, the Prototype

## Summary

Learners synthesize the unit's **eight goals** into one web around **make, compose, devise, and design**
— plus **critique with evidence** — recalling each goal, writing one sentence and drawing one picture
for each, and choosing one goal to explain fully. This spaced-retrieval pass closes the unit.

## Objectives

- Recall and connect all eight goals: creating a themed series, critiquing an artwork, composing in a
  style, critiquing music, devising a piece, critiquing a performance, making a prototype, and
  critiquing a design. _(D07.S1.08.01, D07.S1.08.02, D07.S2.08.01, D07.S2.08.02, D07.S3.08.01,
  D07.S3.08.02, D07.S4.08.01, D07.S4.08.02)_

## Connection

You began this unit by remembering that you already make things every day, and you have added eight new
ways of working — from a series of artworks to a working prototype, and from an art critique to a design
critique. Now you weave them together. A maker is not someone who knows eight techniques; a maker is
someone who can **make a thing** and then **critique it with evidence** — over and over, in every
medium. Today you build that one picture of your own craft.

## Materials

- Capstone map sheet
- Eight goal cards

## Preparation

- Copy the capstone map sheet and goal cards.
- Retrieval: this whole lesson is a retrieval and synthesis pass — the "science of learning" payoff of
  the unit (S-039). Have the learner's earlier portfolio artifacts on hand.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It is the **closing capstone and synthesis**. The goal:
**each learner recalls all eight goals and connects them into one web around *make, compose, devise,
design* — plus *critique with evidence*.** The eight goals are: (1) create a series on one theme and
reflect on its change; (2) critique an artwork with evidence, separating observation from judgment; (3)
compose in a chosen style and reflect on the choices; (4) critique a musical work with evidence about
elements, context, and effect; (5) devise a piece on one theme and reflect on its change through
rehearsal; (6) critique a performance with evidence about movement, expression, context, and purpose;
(7) design and make a working prototype and revise it through testing; (8) critique a design with
evidence about function, materials, fairness, and footprint. This is a **retrieval and spacing** moment
— having learners recall rather than re-read is what makes the learning durable (S-039). Keep assessment
**mastery, not ranking**: a learner who cannot yet recall a goal simply revisits that lesson's artifact
and tries again — "not yet" is information, never a verdict. The four lenses, together: the
**critical-thinking** lens asks what evidence supports each claim; the **ethics** lens asks what is
right and who is affected; the **egalitarian** lens asks who benefits and who is left out; the
**global** lens asks how this looks in other cultures and traditions — always honoring living traditions
with respectful questions (S-056); the **environment** lens asks what the made thing costs the Earth.
Celebrate the collective: each learner's map is a thread in one class web.

## Procedure

1. **Recall, don't peek (8 min).** Without looking at notes, list the unit's eight goals from memory.
   What can you remember? (That is the point — recalling *is* the learning.)
2. **Check and fill (5 min).** Compare with the eight goal cards. For any goal you missed, open that
   lesson's artifact and add it. "Not yet" is not a failure; it is a map of what to revisit.
3. **Build your web (15 min).** On the capstone map, write one sentence and draw one picture for each
   of the eight goals. Connect them to the center: **make · compose · devise · design** — and to the
   shared rule, **critique with evidence**.
4. **Choose a deep dive (15 min).** Pick the one goal that most interested you and explain it fully to
   a partner — what it means, what evidence a critique would use, and one way it touches real life.
   Then listen to your partner's.
5. **Weave the class web (7 min).** As a group, add everyone's maps to one shared web. Notice how the
   goals touch: a series becomes an artwork to critique; a composition becomes a piece to critique; a
   devised piece becomes a performance to critique; a prototype becomes a design to critique.
6. **Close (5 min).** Look at your map. You did not memorize eight facts — you built a way of making.
   Carry it with you.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Work from the goal cards with pictures; choose one goal to explain instead of eight.
- **Extension:** Choose one goal and explain how it connects to at least two others (for example: the
  prototype from goal 7 is critiqued in goal 8, and the series from goal 1 is critiqued in goal 2).
- **Access (non-visual):** Each goal can be captured with a spoken sentence (recorded or said to a
  partner) and a tactile or modeled symbol instead of a drawn picture; build the web with string and
  cards so its connections can be felt, not only seen.

## Assessment

- **Summative (portfolio):** The completed capstone map — all eight goals, each with a sentence and a
  picture — kept in the portfolio.
- **Self/peer:** Learners self-check each goal is present and give one specific "I understood this when
  you said…" to a partner.
- **Mastery, not ranking:** A learner missing a goal revisits its artifact and completes it; the map is
  finished when it is finished, not before.

## Home connection

Show someone at home your capstone map and explain one goal end to end. Teaching it is the surest sign
you have learned it.
