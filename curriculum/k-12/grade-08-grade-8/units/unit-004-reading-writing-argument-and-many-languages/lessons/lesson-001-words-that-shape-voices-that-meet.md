# Lesson 1 — Words That Shape, Voices That Meet

## Summary

Learners open the unit by **retrieving** what they learned in Grade 7 and **previewing** the
three clusters of "Reading, Writing, Argument & Many Languages." They meet the unit map, write
three wonders, and sort them into the clusters — starting the unit portfolio they will add to
every lesson.

## Objectives

- Preview the unit's eight learning goals — analyzing a speaker's word choice, tone, and
  delivery; debating while listening to opposing views; analyzing point of view and figurative
  language; comparing texts across cultures; writing in multiple forms; citing sources; noticing
  how language includes or excludes; and using digital translation critically.
  _(D04.S1.08.01, D04.S1.08.02, D04.S2.08.01, D04.S2.08.02, D04.S3.08.01, D04.S3.08.02,
  D04.S4.08.01, D04.S4.08.02 — preview)_

## Connection

Every day you use words to be heard and to hear others — a message to a friend, a line from a
poem, a speaker making a case, a phrase in a language that is not your first. Words can open a
door or shut one, persuade or confuse, welcome or leave out. Today you begin a journey through
three big questions about how words shape meaning, how argument works, and how the world's many
languages meet — always with *your* language honored first.

## Materials

- Unit map card
- Wonder slips

## Preparation

- Copy the unit map card and wonder slips.
- Retrieval: from Grade 7, recall four ideas — **evaluating a speaker's reasoning** (D04.S1.07.01),
  **checking source credibility** (D04.S2.07.02), **developing ideas with precise language**
  (D04.S3.07.01), and **translation vs. interpretation** (D04.S4.07.01). Each returns this unit,
  at a deeper level.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The ideas to land: **(1) this unit has three
clusters — Listening & Speaking, Reading, and Writing & Languages — and they all meet in one
central idea: "my voice among many voices"; (2) we are building on Grade 7, not starting over;
(3) wondering is the engine — learners begin by asking, not by being told.** Retrieval practice
here is deliberate (spaced recall of last year's ideas — see Dunlosky et al. 2013, S-039); do not
skip it. The ethics lens: how we use words toward others is a question of right and wrong. The
egalitarian lens: ask early "who is included and who is left out by the way this is said?" — it
returns every lesson. The global lens: language is many languages, many scripts, many ways of
speaking — keep the frame broad, never one language's default. The technology lens: digital tools
now carry and translate our words, which makes the unit's questions more urgent, not less. Hold
every wonder as valid; the goal is the asking, not the answer.

## Procedure

1. **Gather and recall (12 min).** Think back to Grade 7. With a partner, recall: how did you
   evaluate a speaker's reasoning? How did you check whether a source was credible? What did it
   mean to develop ideas with precise language? What was the difference between translation and
   interpretation? Share one with the group.
2. **Meet the unit map (8 min).** Look at the unit map. The unit has three clusters:
   **Listening & Speaking**, **Reading**, and **Writing & Languages** — all around one idea:
   **"my voice among many voices."** Notice the clusters are not separate boxes; they are three
   ways of looking at one practice — using words with other people.
3. **Write your wonders (15 min).** On three slips, write three things you wonder about how
   words persuade, how argument works, or how the world's languages meet. There are no wrong
   wonders. Write in your home language if you wish.
4. **Sort the wonders (10 min).** Place each slip under the cluster it belongs to. If it fits
   two clusters, put it between them — that is where the best questions live.
5. **Close (5 min).** Keep your wonders. At the end of the unit you will return to them and see
   what you now can say.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Give sentence starters ("I wonder what happens when a speaker ___", "I wonder who
  is left out when ___", "I wonder what is lost when a text is translated ___").
- **Extension:** Write a "wonder + guess" pair — your question and your current best answer — and
  mark it to check again at the capstone.
- **Expression (UDL):** Writing the slips is optional, not the goal. A wonder can be dictated to a
  scribe, spoken into a recording, signed, or acted out — the wondering is what counts. The unit
  map is only three clusters around one idea, so it can be described aloud for anyone who cannot
  see it; no sight or pen is required to meet the shape of the unit.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name the three clusters and sort a wonder into one
  (or between two) with a reason?
- **Portfolio artifact:** The three wonder slips, kept as the first page of the unit portfolio.

## Home connection

Ask someone at home: "What is one thing people say or write that makes you feel heard, and one
thing that makes you feel shut out?" Bring their answer to mind as the unit unfolds.
