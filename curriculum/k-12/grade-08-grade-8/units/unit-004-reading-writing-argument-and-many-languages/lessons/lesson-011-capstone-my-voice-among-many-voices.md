# Lesson 11 — Capstone: My Voice Among Many Voices

## Summary

Learners close the unit by **retrieving its eight goals**, **selecting and revising one finished
piece** from their portfolio, and **reflecting** on how their own voice now sits among many
voices. They share their piece in a circle of readings, and the group names a strength in each.

## Objectives

- Bring together the unit's eight goals — analyzing rhetoric, deliberating fairly, reading point
  of view and figurative language, comparing texts across cultures, writing in many forms,
  citing sources, noticing inclusion and exclusion, and using translation critically — into one
  finished piece of the learner's own voice.
  _(D04.S1.08.01, D04.S1.08.02, D04.S2.08.01, D04.S2.08.02, D04.S3.08.01, D04.S3.08.02,
  D04.S4.08.01, D04.S4.08.02)_

## Connection

A voice is not a single skill; it is everything together — how you choose words, how you listen,
how you read others' eyes and worlds, how you write in your own forms, how you honor the words
of others and the languages of the world. Today you gather all of it into one piece, and you let
your voice stand among the many voices in the room. That is the whole unit, in one act.

## Materials

- Capstone checklist
- Portfolio pieces

## Preparation

- Copy the capstone checklist.
- Have learners gather their portfolio pieces from the unit.
- Retrieval: this whole lesson is a spaced, interleaved retrieval pass across the unit (Dunlosky
  et al. 2013, S-039) — do not skip the recall.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The ideas to land: **(1) the unit's eight goals
are one practice, not eight boxes — using words with other people; (2) the capstone is a
retrieval-and-revision act, not a new task; (3) every voice is worth hearing, and the room holds
many.** Spaced retrieval across the unit is deliberate (S-039). The ethics lens: we end by asking
what is right in how we use words. The egalitarian lens: the circle of readings makes every voice
equal — one after another, each given attention. The global lens: the pieces will come in many
languages and scripts; honor every one. The technology lens: where a tool was used (a translation,
a draft), it served the learner's voice — it did not replace it. The community and
social-emotional lenses: this is a moment of belonging and pride; make it safe, warm, and
unhurried. Celebrate mastery; do not rank.

## Procedure

1. **Retrieve the unit (10 min).** With a partner, recall the eight goals: word choice and tone
   and delivery; arguing with reasons while listening; point of view; figurative language; texts
   from many cultures; writing in many forms; citing sources; language that includes and
   excludes; translation used critically. That is the unit. Which goal felt strongest to you?
2. **Select one piece (10 min).** Look through your portfolio. Choose one piece you are proud of
   or want to make better. It may be in any language you choose.
3. **Revise it (15 min).** Make one real improvement — a sharper word, a clearer reason, a
   citation you were missing, a phrase now made plainer so more people can come in. Mark what you
   changed.
4. **Reflect (8 min).** Write two or three sentences: how does this piece show *your* voice, and
   how does your voice sit among the many voices you met this unit?
5. **Circle of readings (12 min).** In turn, read or speak your piece — or a part of it. After
   each, the group names one thing the voice did well. No ranking; only attention.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a reflection starter ("This piece sounds like me because ___. One voice I
  met this unit that changed how I see words is ___.").
- **Extension:** Assemble two pieces in different forms (an argument and a narrative) and reflect
  on how the form changed the voice.
- **Expression (UDL):** In the circle of readings a learner may sign their piece, have it read by
  a partner, or share it as a recording; the reflection can be dictated to a scribe. Every voice —
  spoken, signed, written, or read-aloud by another — is honored equally, with no ranking.

## Assessment

- **Summative (portfolio):** The learner's finished piece plus reflection, showing at least two of
  the unit's goals in action (e.g., a form with voice, a citation, a rewrite that includes more
  people).
- **Performance (circle of readings):** The learner reads or speaks their piece; the group names
  one strength in each.

## Home connection

Read your finished piece to someone at home, in whatever language you wrote it, and tell them one
thing you learned this unit about how words shape, meet, and include.
