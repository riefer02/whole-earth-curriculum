# Lesson 2 — Word Choice, Tone, and Delivery: How a Speaker Shapes Meaning

## Summary

Learners learn **three lenses** for analyzing a speaker — **word choice**, **tone**, and
**delivery** — and use them to see how a message shapes meaning and persuades an audience. They
work one speech pair as a modeled example, then analyze a second pair with a partner.

## Objectives

- Analyze how a speaker's word choice, tone, and delivery shape meaning and persuade an audience.
  _(D04.S1.08.01)_

## Connection

Think of the same three words said two ways: "come here" spoken with a warm smile, and "come
here" snapped with a frown. The words are identical; the meaning is not. Every speaker shapes a
message twice — once by *choosing the words*, and again by *how the words are said*. Today you
learn three lenses that let you see exactly how a speaker shapes what you understand and feel —
a skill that works on every message in every language you hear.

## Materials

- Three-lens analysis card
- Speech pairs

## Preparation

- Copy the three-lens analysis card and speech pairs.
- Retrieval: from Grade 7, recall how you evaluated a speaker's reasoning (D04.S1.07.01). Today
  we look *closer* — at the words, the tone, and the delivery, not only the logic.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The ideas to land: **(1) a speaker shapes meaning
with three moves — word choice (the words and their connotations), tone (the speaker's attitude
carried by the words), and delivery (pace, volume, pitch, pause, gesture, expression); (2) all
three work together to persuade; (3) analyzing them is a skill of attention, not a ranking of
speakers.** Model the first pair once (worked example, S-011). The concept of persuasion as
analyzed craft is ancient — Aristotle named three appeals (ethos, pathos, logos) in the Greek
tradition (S-346), but it is one tradition among many; many cultures have their own arts of
speaking. The ethics lens: persuasion can be used to help or to harm; noticing *how* it works is
how we choose. The egalitarian lens: the same words persuade some people and leave others out —
ask "who is this speaking to, and who is it leaving out?" The global lens: tone and delivery vary
across cultures; a loud, fast delivery is respectful in one place and rude in another. The
social-emotional lens: noticing tone is also noticing feeling — ours and the speaker's. Use a
low-stakes example so the skill is practiced without fear.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** How did you evaluate a speaker's reasoning in Grade 7? Today we look even
   closer — at the words, the tone, and the delivery.
2. **Meet the three lenses (8 min).** To analyze a speaker, ask three questions. **Word choice:**
   which words did they pick, and what feeling do those words carry? **Tone:** what attitude do
   the words carry — warm, urgent, playful, cold? **Delivery:** how are the words said — fast or
   slow, loud or soft, with what pauses, face, and gesture?
3. **Watch it modeled (15 min).** Here is one message said two ways. Version A: "A few of our
   neighbors have raised a concern about the path." Version B: "A small but worried group of
   families is pleading with us about the danger on the path." Run all three lenses. Version B's
   words ("worried," "pleading," "danger") carry stronger feeling; its tone is more urgent; said
   with a slower, softer delivery, it asks for sympathy, not just attention. Neither is more
   "true" — but each shapes a different meaning.
4. **Analyze a second pair (15 min).** With a partner, take a speech pair. Run the three lenses
   on each version, then say how the word choice, tone, and delivery change what the audience
   understands and feels.
5. **Close (7 min).** Share one difference you found. Remember: **meaning lives in the words
   *and* in the saying.**

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide the three lenses with two possible answers each ("warm words / sharp
  words," "calm tone / urgent tone") and ask learners to circle which each version uses.
- **Extension:** Take one message and write a third version that uses word choice, tone, and
  delivery to persuade a *different* audience, then explain each choice.
- **Expression (UDL):** Analysis can be spoken or dictated to a scribe instead of written — the
  thinking is the skill, not the pen. For learners who do not hear speech: "delivery" also lives
  in the face and hands — gesture, expression, visible sign, and written pace all shape meaning,
  so read the speech pairs as text or sign them rather than only hearing them, and caption any
  recorded clip.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name the three lenses and use them on two
  versions of a message to say how meaning and persuasion change, with a reason?
- **Portfolio artifact:** The completed analysis of one speech pair, kept in the portfolio.

## Home connection

Listen this week to one person speaking — a family member, a shopkeeper, a voice on the radio —
and name one word they chose and one thing their delivery added to the meaning.
