# Lesson 9 — Language That Includes and Excludes

## Summary

Learners analyze how **language can include or exclude people** — through jargon, register,
in-group slang, and the choice of one language over another in a mixed room. They sort real
scenarios as welcoming or shutting out, then rewrite one excluding phrase to include more people.

## Objectives

- Analyze how language can include or exclude people, such as jargon or the use of one language
  over another. _(D04.S4.08.01)_

## Connection

A door can be open or shut, and language is a door too. When a doctor says "your
electrolytes are elevated" to someone who has never heard the word, the door shuts. When a
meeting is held only in one language and half the room speaks another, the door shuts for half
the room. When friends use a private slang you do not share, the door shuts a little. None of
this is usually meant to hurt — but it *does* exclude, and noticing it is the first step to
holding the door open. Today you learn to see which words open the door and which ones close it.

## Materials

- Include-exclude card
- Scenario cards

## Preparation

- Copy the include-exclude card and scenario cards.
- Retrieval: from Grade 6 and 7, recall communicating across a language difference (D04.S4.06.02)
  and recognizing dialect and accent as natural variation (D04.S4.07.02). Today we look at how
  language choices *include or exclude*.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The ideas to land: **(1) language can welcome or
shut out — through jargon (words only insiders know), register (how formal or casual), in-group
slang, and using one language over another in a mixed room; (2) exclusion is usually not meant,
but it is real, and we can notice it; (3) the fix is not "never use technical words" but "make
sure everyone can come in" — explain, translate, invite, and honor every language.** The ethics
lens: whether we hold the door open is a question of right and wrong. The egalitarian lens is
this lesson's heart: language is a gate, and an equal world keeps the gate open — 40 per cent of
people globally cannot learn in a language they understand (S-019), which is why home language
first matters. The global lens: many-language rooms are the normal human condition, not an
exception. The community lens: a group that includes everyone in its words is a stronger group.
The social-emotional lens: being shut out by words *hurts* — name that feeling gently, and do
not ask anyone to relive it if they do not wish. Use scenarios that are real but not pointed at
any one learner. Hold "everyone deserves to come in" as a value, distinct from the descriptive
claim that language can exclude.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** How did you communicate across a language difference in Grade 6 and 7?
   Today we look at how the *choice* of words and languages opens or shuts a door.
2. **Meet the two doors (8 min).** Language can **welcome** or **shut out**. It shuts out with
   **jargon** (words only insiders know), with a **register** too formal or too private, and
   with **using one language over another** when the room speaks many. It welcomes with plain
   words, with explanation, with translation, and with honoring every language.
3. **Sort the scenarios (15 min).** With your group, take the scenario cards. For each, ask:
   **who can understand this, and who is shut out?** Sort each under "welcomes" or "shuts out,"
   and say why.
4. **Rewrite one door open (17 min).** Choose one "shuts out" phrase. Rewrite it so more people
   can come in — use plain words, or add the other languages spoken in the room, or explain the
   jargon. Share your rewrite.
5. **Close (5 min).** Remember: **language is a door — we choose whether it is open.**

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide two labels ("everyone can understand" / "some people are shut out") and
  ask learners to sort each scenario, then circle the one word that shuts people out.
- **Extension:** Analyze a longer real-world case — a form, a sign, a policy — and propose one
  concrete change that would include more people.
- **Expression (UDL):** Learners may sort and rewrite scenarios aloud, by signing, or by pointing
  to "welcomes" / "shuts out"; the rewrite can be spoken to a scribe. The two-doors image is
  described in its alt text, and the open-door/shut-door idea is carried in the lesson's words.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner identify how a phrase includes or excludes (jargon,
  register, or one-language-only) and rewrite one excluding phrase to include more people?
- **Portfolio artifact:** The completed scenario sort and one rewrite, kept in the portfolio.

## Home connection

Notice one sign, form, or announcement this week — at a clinic, a shop, a station — and ask:
who can understand this, and who is shut out? Tell someone what you noticed.
