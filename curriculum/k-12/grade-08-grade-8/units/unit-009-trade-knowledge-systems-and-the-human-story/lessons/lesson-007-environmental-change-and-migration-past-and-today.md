# Lesson 7 — Environmental Change & Migration: Past and Today

## Summary

Learners analyze how **environmental change** — drought, flood, a shifting climate — has driven
people to **move and adapt**, in the past (the **Ancestral Pueblo** of the North American Southwest)
and today (sea-level rise, drought, and flood). They build an environmental-migration analysis as a
portfolio artifact.

## Objectives

- Analyze how environmental change, such as drought, flood, or a shift in climate, has driven people
  to move and adapt, in the past and today. _(D08.S3.08.02)_

## Connection

Have you ever seen a dry riverbed, a flooded street, or a shore the sea has crept over? When the land
or water people depend on changes, their lives change with it — and sometimes they move. This is not
new: for thousands of years, environmental change has been one of the reasons people move and adapt.
Today you look at one story from the past and one from our own time, and see the same pattern at work.

## Materials

- Environmental-migration sheet
- Case cards

## Preparation

- Copy the environmental-migration sheet and the case cards.
- Retrieval: from Grade 7, recall that migration can be voluntary or forced and has causes and effects
  (D08.S3.07.01); from earlier this unit, recall the drought explanation for the Maya collapse
  (D08.S1.08.01).

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **environmental change (drought,
flood, a shifting climate) can be a push factor — a reason people move — and people also adapt,
changing how they live rather than only leaving.** The two cases: **(1) the past — the Ancestral
Pueblo.** Chaco Canyon, in present-day New Mexico, was a thriving regional center of the Ancestral
Pueblo people from about 850 to 1250 CE (S-382). Sustained drought in the late 1200s — recorded in
tree rings — contributed to people leaving Chaco and nearby centers, migrating to new pueblos along
the Rio Grande and elsewhere, where their descendants live today (S-382). **(2) today.** Around the
world, people are moving — within and across borders — because of **sea-level rise** (low-lying
islands and coasts), **drought**, and **flood**, as the climate changes; the International Organization
for Migration documents environment and climate as real drivers of migration (S-383, S-086). People
adapt too: moving to higher ground, changing crops, building differently, and planning together. The
**environment** lens: environmental change has shaped human history for millennia — it is not new,
but today's change is fast and, for many, made worse by human activity (S-005). The **ethics** lens:
people who move because of environmental change are not "the problem" — they are people adapting with
courage; how we welcome them is a moral question. The **egalitarian** lens: those who contributed
least to climate change are often hit first and hardest — a matter of justice, not only of weather
(S-005). The **global** lens: this happens everywhere — small island nations, dry Sahel lands, river
deltas — and it always has. Keep it honest: migration is rarely caused by the environment *alone*;
economy, safety, and family mix in, and we distinguish evidence from values (philosophy §5).

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Grade 7: what makes migration voluntary or forced? From earlier this
   unit: what environmental change may have helped end the Classic Maya cities? Today we watch
   environmental change drive movement — past and present.
2. **Meet the past (12 min).** **Chaco Canyon** was a center of the Ancestral Pueblo people from
   about 850 to 1250 CE. Then a long **drought**, recorded in tree rings, set in through the late
   1200s. People adapted and moved — to new pueblos along the Rio Grande and elsewhere, where their
   descendants live today (S-382).
3. **Meet today (12 min).** Around the world, people now move — within and across borders — because
   of **sea-level rise** on low coasts and islands, and because of **drought** and **flood** as the
   climate changes (S-383, S-086). People also adapt: moving to higher ground, changing crops,
   building differently.
4. **Analyze (12 min).** Fill the sheet: for each case, name the environmental change, the push to
   move, and how people adapted. Notice what is the same across a thousand years.
5. **Share (6 min).** In a group, say one way the past case and today's case are alike, and one way
   they differ.
6. **Close (3 min).** Environmental change has always been part of the human story. What is new today
   is the speed — and the question of how we respond to the people who must move.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide the "change / push / adaptation" ideas pre-written and ask learners to sort
  them into the correct rows.
- **Extension:** Connect this lesson back to the Maya drought (Lesson 2) and write one sentence
  comparing the two cases of drought-driven change.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name an environmental push factor and one way people
  adapted, in both the past and the present?
- **Portfolio artifact:** The completed environmental-migration sheet, kept in the portfolio.

## Home connection

Ask at home: has our family or anyone we know ever had to move or change how they live because of
weather, drought, or flood? Compare with the two cases.
