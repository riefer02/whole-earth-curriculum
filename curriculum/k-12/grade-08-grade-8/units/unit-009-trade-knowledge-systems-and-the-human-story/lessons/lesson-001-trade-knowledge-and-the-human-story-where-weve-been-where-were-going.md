# Lesson 1 — Trade, Knowledge & the Human Story: Where We've Been, Where We're Going

## Summary

Learners **recall Grade-7 history ideas** — multiple causes, comparing knowledge systems, primary
and secondary sources, voluntary and forced migration, colonization, and forms of resistance — and
**preview this unit's four strands and eight goals**. They meet the unit's organizing idea: history
is **the human story, told from many perspectives, never one people's triumph**.

## Objectives

- Preview the unit's eight goals: evaluating competing explanations, change and continuity,
  comparing knowledge traditions, cultural blending, trade networks, environmental migration, and
  slavery, resistance, and the keeping of hope. _(D08.S1.08.01, D08.S1.08.02, D08.S2.08.01,
  D08.S2.08.02, D08.S3.08.01, D08.S3.08.02, D08.S4.08.01, D08.S4.08.02)_

## Connection

Think of a story your family tells — how someone arrived somewhere, what they brought, what they
kept. Every family story is a piece of a much bigger story: the story of how humans have moved,
traded, learned, and held on to hope across thousands of years. History is not a list of dates to
memorize; it is that big story, told from many sides. Today we remember what we already know how to
do, and see where the story is taking us.

## Materials

- Unit map sheet
- Recall cards

## Preparation

- Copy the unit map sheet and recall cards.
- Retrieval: from Grade 7, recall (a) that a major change has multiple causes and consequences,
  (b) that knowledge systems — such as indigenous ecological knowledge and formal science — can be
  compared with respect, (c) the difference between a primary and a secondary source, (d) that
  migration can be voluntary or forced, (e) that colonization brought loss of land, culture, and
  self-rule, and (f) that people resist through protest, refusal, art, and storytelling. Today we
  link those to the new unit.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It is the unit's **opening and retrieval pass**, so
the goals are modest: **(1) recall the Grade-7 ideas that this unit builds on; (2) preview the
unit's four strands — change and continuity, knowledge traditions, migration and trade, power and
resistance — and its eight goals; (3) plant the organizing idea: history is a global story told
from many perspectives.** Do not try to teach the eight goals today; just name and locate them.
The **critical-thinking** lens: weighing competing explanations and checking sources is the habit
we carry forward (S-011). The **global** lens: this unit moves across many regions — Mesoamerica,
the Pacific, the Indian Ocean, East and West Africa, the Andes, the Americas — because the human
story is one story in many places. The **ethics** lens: we hold hard topics (slavery, colonization)
with honesty and care, never as spectacle, and we name the dignity of the people in the story
(philosophy §5, facilitation "Holding difficult topics safely"). The **egalitarian** lens: we ask,
at every turn, who benefits, who is left out, and whose voice is heard. Keep big questions open:
origins and meaning are held as questions, never answered as creed (philosophy §11). Spacing note
(S-039): this recall pass is what makes the unit's ideas stick later.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (10 min).** In a small group, look at the recall cards: **multiple causes**,
   **knowledge-system comparison**, **primary vs. secondary sources**, **voluntary vs. forced
   migration**, **colonization**, **forms of resistance**. For each, say in one sentence what you
   remember from Grade 7.
2. **Connect the six (5 min).** What do all six have in common? (Each is about *how we know and
   tell* the human story — with evidence, from more than one side.)
3. **Meet the map (10 min).** Look at the unit map. Four strands: **change and continuity**,
   **knowledge traditions**, **migration and trade**, **power and resistance**. Eight goals — two
   per strand. Read each goal aloud once; don't worry about understanding them yet. _(A partner or
   facilitator can read the strands and goals aloud, and the four strands can be named while tapping
   four fingers, so the map can be heard and counted, not only seen.)_
4. **Plant the big idea (10 min).** The unit's idea in one breath: history is **the human story,
   told from many perspectives** — and it is still being written, by every one of us. Say it back
   in your own words.
5. **Wonder (10 min).** Write one question about each strand in the wonder box. Which question do
   you most want answered by the end of the unit?
6. **Close (5 min).** Today we remembered what we know and saw the road ahead. Next: we weigh
   competing explanations for one great change.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Give sentence starters for the recall round ("A major change has many causes
  because…"; "A primary source is…").
- **Extension:** Add a Grade-7 idea of your own and say why it will matter in this unit.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner recall each Grade-7 idea in one sentence and name
  the unit's four strands?
- **Artifact:** The completed unit map (or the learner's written recall), kept in the portfolio.

## Home connection

Ask someone at home: what is one story your family tells about moving, trading, or holding on to a
tradition? Bring the story (no names needed) to the next lesson.
