# Unit 9 — Trade, Knowledge Systems & the Human Story

## Overview

This unit steps into the wider human story with the tools of the historian, building on
Grade 7's multiple causes and knowledge-system comparisons toward *evaluation*.
Learners evaluate competing explanations for one major historical change and support a
reasoned judgment with evidence, and analyze how a long-running human practice, such as
trade, farming, or writing, changed over centuries and what stayed the same. They
compare two knowledge traditions in depth and explain what each offers and where they
agree and differ, and analyze a case of cultural exchange and blending — foods,
languages, or beliefs meeting — and what new things grew from it. They analyze how
long-distance trade networks, such as the Silk Roads or Indian Ocean routes, connected
peoples and moved goods, ideas, and beliefs, and analyze how environmental change, such
as drought, flood, or a shift in climate, has driven people to move and adapt, in the
past and today. They analyze slavery and the transatlantic slave trade as systems built
on the denial of human dignity and describe how enslaved people resisted, and explain
how enslaved and colonized peoples kept their humanity, culture, and hope alive under
oppression, with specific examples. History is told as a global story from many
perspectives, never one people's triumph.

## Essential questions

- How do I evaluate competing explanations for a major change, and what changes and what stays the same across centuries?
- How do knowledge traditions compare, what grows when cultures blend, and how did long-distance trade connect peoples?
- How has environmental change driven people to move, and how did enslaved and colonized peoples resist and keep their humanity and hope?
