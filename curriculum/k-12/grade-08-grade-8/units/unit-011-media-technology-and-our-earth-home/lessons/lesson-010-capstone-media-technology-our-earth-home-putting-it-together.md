# Lesson 10 — Capstone: Media, Technology & Our Earth Home — Putting It Together

## Summary

Learners synthesize the unit's **eight goals** into one web around **media, technology, and our Earth
home**, recalling each goal, writing one sentence and drawing one picture for each, and choosing one goal
to explain fully. This spaced-retrieval pass closes the unit.

## Objectives

- Recall and connect all eight goals: weighing a technology's benefits and harms, design trade-offs,
  framed and edited media, deepfakes, ecosystem resilience, biomes and adaptation, sustainability, and
  stewardship. _(D10.S1.08.01, D10.S1.08.02, D10.S2.08.01, D10.S2.08.02, D10.S3.08.01, D10.S3.08.02,
  D10.S4.08.01, D10.S4.08.02)_

## Connection

You began this unit by remembering what you already knew, and you have added eight ways of living well in
a world of screens and living systems. Now you weave them together. A steward is not someone who knows
eight facts; a steward is someone who can **weigh** a technology, **check** a picture, and **care** for
the Earth — all at once, in one life. Today you build that single picture.

## Materials

- Capstone map sheet
- Eight goal cards

## Preparation

- Copy the capstone map sheet and goal cards.
- Retrieval: this whole lesson is a retrieval and synthesis pass — the "science of learning" payoff of
  the unit (S-039). Have the learner's earlier portfolio artifacts on hand.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It is the **closing capstone and synthesis**. The goal:
**each learner recalls all eight goals and connects them into one web around media, technology, and our
Earth home.** The eight goals are: (1) weigh a technology's benefits and harms for different people; (2)
explain design trade-offs among cost, safety, access, and the environment; (3) analyze how a photo,
video, or story can be framed or edited to change meaning, and how to check it; (4) evaluate a possible
deepfake and describe a way to check; (5) explain how an ecosystem responds to disturbance and recovers —
resilience; (6) compare two biomes and explain adaptation; (7) define sustainability and apply it to one
resource; (8) analyze a community's stewardship practice and what makes it work. This is a **retrieval
and spacing** moment — having learners recall rather than re-read is what makes the learning durable
(S-039). Keep assessment **mastery, not ranking**: a learner who cannot yet recall a goal simply revisits
that lesson's artifact and tries again — "not yet" is information, never a verdict. The four lenses,
together, for the final time: the **technology & environment** lens asks what tools and media do to
humanity and the Earth; the **ethics** lens asks what is right and who is affected; the **egalitarian**
lens asks who benefits and who is left out; the **global** lens asks how this looks in other places and
times. Hold values open as values and evidence as evidence (philosophy §5, §11). Celebrate the
collective: each learner's map is a thread in one class web, and together they are the unit's answer.

## Procedure

1. **Recall, don't peek (8 min).** Without looking at notes, list the unit's eight goals from memory.
   What can you remember? (That is the point — recalling *is* the learning.)
2. **Check and fill (5 min).** Compare with the eight goal cards. For any goal you missed, open that
   lesson's artifact and add it. "Not yet" is not a failure; it is a map of what to revisit.
3. **Build your web (15 min).** On the capstone map, write one sentence and draw one picture for each of
   the eight goals. Connect them to the center: **weigh**, **check**, **care**.
4. **Choose a deep dive (15 min).** Pick the one goal that most interested you and explain it fully to a
   partner — what it means, what evidence supports it, and one way it touches real life. Then listen to
   your partner's.
5. **Weave the class web (7 min).** As a group, add everyone's maps to one shared web. Notice how the
   goals touch: weighing a technology meets checking its images; checking meets caring for the Earth the
   technology affects.
6. **Close (5 min).** Look at your map. You did not memorize eight facts — you built a way of living.
   Carry it with you.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Work from the goal cards with pictures; choose one goal to explain instead of eight.
- **Extension:** Choose one goal and explain how it connects to at least two others (for example: a
   technology's design trade-offs create the benefits and harms we weigh, and its waste touches the
   ecosystems we steward).
- **Access (non-visual):** Each goal can be captured with a spoken sentence and a gesture or tactile
  symbol (a stone, a string loop, a raised mark) instead of a drawn picture, so the web is built by voice
  and touch for learners who are blind or have motor differences.

## Assessment

- **Summative (portfolio):** The completed capstone map — all eight goals, each with a sentence and a
  picture — kept in the portfolio.
- **Self/peer:** Learners self-check each goal is present and give one specific "I understood this when
  you said…" to a partner.
- **Mastery, not ranking:** A learner missing a goal revisits its artifact and completes it; the map is
  finished when it is finished, not before.

## Home connection

Show someone at home your capstone map and explain one goal end to end. Teaching it is the surest sign
you have learned it.
