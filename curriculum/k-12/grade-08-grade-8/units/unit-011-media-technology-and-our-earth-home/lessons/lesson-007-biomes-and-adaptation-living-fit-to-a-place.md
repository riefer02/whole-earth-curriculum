# Lesson 7 — Biomes and Adaptation: Living Fit to a Place

## Summary

Learners compare **two biomes or climate zones** — a desert and a rainforest — and explain how the living
things in each are **adapted to their conditions**. They see that form follows climate: the cactus stores
water because water is scarce, the rainforest leaf sheds rain because rain is endless.

## Objectives

- Compare two biomes or climate zones and explain how the living things there are adapted to their
  conditions. _(D10.S3.08.02)_

## Connection

Why does a cactus look the way it does — thick, waxy, spined — while a rainforest leaf is broad and
thin and ends in a point? Because each was shaped by its place. A cactus must hoard every drop of scarce
water; a rainforest leaf must shed endless rain and reach for scarce light. Life is never "one size fits
all" — it is fitted, over long time, to the place where it lives. Today you compare two biomes and read
the adaptations written on their living things.

## Materials

- Biome comparison sheet
- Biome cards

## Preparation

- Copy the sheet and biome cards.
- Retrieval: from Grade 7, recall variation and natural selection — traits that help survival spread in a
  population. An adaptation is the result of that process, written in a species' form.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **a biome is a large region with a
characteristic climate and community of life, and the living things there are adapted — their bodies and
behaviors fit their conditions** (S-396, S-106). The worked comparison is **desert vs. rainforest**:
*desert* — hot, dry, water scarce; plants store water (thick stems), have shallow wide roots or deep
taproots, small or spiny leaves to limit water loss; animals are often nocturnal, conserve water, and
shed heat. *Rainforest* — hot, wet, light scarce at the ground; plants grow tall toward light, with
broad leaves and "drip tips" to shed rain; animals live in layers (canopy, understory, floor) and many
are adapted to climb or glide. The **environment** lens is this lesson's core: adaptation is the living
world's answer to its conditions. The **global** lens: biomes repeat across continents — deserts on
every landmass, rainforests near the equator — so the same problems draw the same kinds of answers
everywhere (S-396). The **egalitarian** lens: human life is adapted too — people living in deserts,
tundra, and rainforests have built knowledge and practices fitted to those places, and no place's people
are "behind" any other. The **ethics** lens: when we change a biome — clearing a forest or damming a
river — we change the conditions the life there was fitted to, and must ask what that costs. The
**technology** lens: maps, satellites, and climate data let us see and compare biomes across the whole
Earth. Keep the biology precise and cited; adaptation is evidence-based, and origins remain open as
models with evidence (philosophy §11).

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Grade 7: what is natural selection? (Traits that help survival spread.) An
   **adaptation** is that process written on a body.
2. **Meet the biome (5 min).** A **biome** is a large region with a signature climate and community of
   life. Today: **desert** and **rainforest**.
3. **Read the desert (12 min).** Worked example: the desert is hot and dry, so water is scarce. The
   cactus stores water in a thick stem, trades leaves for spines to lose less water, and spreads shallow
   roots to catch rare rain. Animals are often **nocturnal** and hoard water. Fill the desert column.
4. **Read the rainforest (12 min).** The rainforest is hot and wet, but light is scarce at the ground.
   Trees race upward for light; leaves are broad with **drip tips** to shed rain; animals live in layers
   — canopy, understory, floor. Fill the rainforest column.
5. **Compare (10 min).** Put the two side by side. Say it in one breath: "In the desert, life is built to
   *save* water; in the rainforest, life is built to *shed* it." Each form fits its place.
6. **Close (5 min).** No biome is "better" — each is a set of living answers to a place's questions.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide one column pre-filled and ask the learner to match three species to their biome.
- **Extension:** Choose two biomes not on the cards (tundra, savanna, temperate forest) and compare their
  adaptations.
- **Access (non-visual):** Describe each biome in words ("desert: hot, dry, water scarce"; "rainforest:
  hot, wet, light scarce") and offer touch examples (a spiny or waxy stem, a broad leaf) so the "because"
  behind each adaptation is felt and heard; the sheet's labels carry the same information as the picture.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name one adaptation for each of two biomes and explain the
  "because" behind it?
- **Portfolio artifact:** The completed biome comparison sheet, kept in the portfolio.

## Home connection

Name your local climate zone and find two local plants or animals; say how each is fitted to your
conditions.
