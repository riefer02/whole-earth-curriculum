# Lesson 1 — Media, Technology & Our Earth Home: Where We've Been, Where We're Going

## Summary

Learners **recall three Grade-7 ideas** — trustworthy sources, misinformation versus disinformation, and
the basics of climate change — and **preview this unit's four strands and eight goals**. They meet the
unit's organizing idea: we read **media** critically, weigh **technology** for who it helps and harms,
and live as part of the **Earth** we call home.

## Objectives

- Begin to name the unit's first goals: analyzing a technology's benefits and harms for different people,
  and weighing design trade-offs among cost, safety, access, and the environment. _(D10.S1.08.01, D10.S1.08.02)_

## Connection

Think of one thing you did today that used a tool, a screen, or a message — a message you read, a device
you charged, water you drank, a picture you saw. Every one of those is a doorway into this unit. Media,
technology, and the Earth are not three separate subjects; they are one story about how we humans live
together on a shared home. Today we remember what we already know and see where the road is taking us.

## Materials

- Unit map sheet
- Recall cards

## Preparation

- Copy the unit map sheet and recall cards.
- Retrieval: from Grade 7, recall (a) what makes a source trustworthy, (b) the difference between
  misinformation and disinformation, and (c) the basic science of climate change. Today we link those to
  the new unit.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It is the unit's **opening and retrieval pass**, so the
goals are modest: **(1) recall three Grade-7 ideas — a trustworthy source is one we can trace and check;
misinformation is false by mistake, disinformation is false on purpose; and greenhouse gases trapping
heat is the basic science of climate change; (2) preview the unit's four strands — Technology & Society,
Media & Information Literacy, Ecology & Environmental Systems, and Sustainability & Stewardship — and its
eight goals; (3) plant the organizing idea: media is read critically, technology is weighed for who it
helps and harms, and the Earth is our shared home to steward.** Do not teach the eight goals today; just
name and locate them. The four lenses run through every lesson: the **ethics** lens asks what is right and
who is affected; the **egalitarian** lens asks who benefits and who is left out; the **global** lens asks
how this looks in other places and times; and the **technology & environment** lens — this unit's core —
asks what tools and media do to humanity and the Earth (philosophy §4). Keep claims precise and cited;
hold values open as values, not facts (philosophy §5, §11). Spacing note (S-039): this recall pass is
what makes the unit's ideas stick later.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (10 min).** In a small group, look at three recall cards: **trustworthy sources**,
   **misinformation vs. disinformation**, **climate change basics**. For each, say in one sentence what
   you remember from Grade 7.
2. **Connect the three (5 min).** What do all three have in common? (Each is about *checking* — checking
   a source, checking a claim, checking how we treat the Earth.)
3. **Meet the map (10 min).** Look at the unit map. Four strands: **technology & society**, **media &
   information literacy**, **ecology & environmental systems**, **sustainability & stewardship**. Eight
   goals — two per strand. Read each goal aloud once; don't worry about understanding them yet.
4. **Plant the big idea (10 min).** The unit's idea in one breath: we **read media** with open eyes, we
   **weigh technology** for who it helps and who it harms, and we live as **stewards of the Earth** we
   share. Say it back in your own words.
5. **Wonder (10 min).** Write one question about each strand in the wonder box. Which question do you
   most want answered by the end of the unit?
6. **Close (5 min).** Today we remembered what we know and saw the road ahead. Next: we weigh a
   technology's benefits and harms.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Give sentence starters for the recall round ("A trustworthy source is one I can…").
- **Extension:** Add a fourth Grade-7 idea of your own and say why it will matter in this unit.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner recall each Grade-7 idea in one sentence and name the
  unit's four strands?
- **Artifact:** The completed unit map (or the learner's written recall), kept in the portfolio.

## Home connection

Ask someone at home: what is one thing you once believed about a picture or a product, then changed your
mind about when you checked? Bring the story (no names needed) to the next lesson.
