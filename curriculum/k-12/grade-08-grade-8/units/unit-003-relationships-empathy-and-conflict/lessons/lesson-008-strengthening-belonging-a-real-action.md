# Lesson 8 — Strengthening Belonging: A Real Action in My Community

## Summary

Learners move from *analyzing* belonging to *strengthening* it: they name a person or group who
feels outside, identify the **barrier**, design one **concrete action**, and commit to carrying it
out. They learn that belonging is something you can actively build — through **invitation and
removing barriers**, not only through receiving it — and leave with a real, doable plan.

## Objectives

- Take part in strengthening belonging in their school or local community. _(D02.S3.08.02)_

## Connection

Belonging is not a fixed fact about who is "in" and who is "out." It is made, every day, by small
decisions: who gets invited, who is spoken to, whose name is learned, which door is left open. When
a new student eats alone, or an older neighbor stops coming out, or a quieter member is never asked
— belonging has a gap, and gaps can be closed. Today you do not just talk about belonging; you plan
one real thing that makes it stronger where you live.

## Materials

- Belonging action planner
- Belonging examples card

## Preparation

- Copy the belonging action planner and examples card.
- Retrieval: recall Lesson 7's finding about who is left out. Today you act on it: the analysis
  becomes an invitation.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The ideas to land: **(1) belonging is shaped by how
identity is received — inclusive settings treat difference as ordinary, not deficient (UNESCO,
S-235); (2) belonging is something you can actively strengthen, not only receive; (3) a good
belonging action names a real person or group who feels outside, the barrier, one concrete action,
and one way to know it helped.** Model the planner once (S-011). **Keep actions small, safe, and
consensual — an invitation, a greeting, a shared activity, a changed routine; nothing that
endangers, overpromises, or turns a classmate into a "project."** The ethics lens: including the
excluded is right, and it must be done with their dignity, not by making them a spectacle. The
egalitarian lens: the deepest belonging actions remove barriers (a rule, a routine, an assumption),
not just add one-off kindness — though kindness is a real start. The global & anthropological lens:
people everywhere welcome newcomers in their own ways — a meal, a name, a place at the table, a
greeting in the newcomer's own language; borrow, do not rank. The technology & environment lens:
belonging also happens online (a group chat that includes the newcomer, a shared message) — but the
screen can also quietly exclude (the group no one is added to); ask both. The social-emotional
lens: inviting someone in takes courage, and being the one outside is painful — hold both with
gentleness. Assess by what the learner *does*, and by whether the action respects the other
person's freedom to say no.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Lesson 7: who was left out, and why? Say to a partner. Today we turn
   that answer into an action.
2. **Meet the planner (10 min).** Look at the belonging action planner. It has four lines: **(1) a
   person or group who feels outside; (2) the barrier — what is keeping them out; (3) one concrete
   action — something you will actually do; (4) one way to know it helped.** A strong action names
   the barrier, not just the feeling.
3. **Model it (10 min).** Watch a plan be made: *A new student sits alone at lunch.* Person: the
   new student. Barrier: no one has invited them, and the seats are "claimed" by habit. Action: sit
   with them this week, learn their name, and invite them to join one activity — without forcing
   it. How we will know it helped: they join once, or say how they would like to be included.
   Notice: the action is small, real, and respects their choice.
4. **Plan your own action (20 min).** Fill your planner with a real person or group in your school
   or neighborhood — not an invented one. Write the barrier, the action, and how you will know it
   helped. Share your plan with a partner and let them ask one question to make it stronger.
5. **Close (5 min).** Commit: this is not a worksheet; it is something you will do this week.
   Belonging is built by exactly these small, real moves.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer a menu of starter actions (learn a name, offer a seat, invite to one
  activity, add to one group chat) and a sentence frame ("The barrier is ___; my action is ___").
- **Extension:** Design an action that removes a barrier at the level of a rule or routine (for
  example, a rotating "newcomer welcome" job), and write how it could become permanent.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name a real person or group, a barrier, a concrete
  action, and a way to know it helped?
- **Performance + portfolio:** The completed belonging action planner, carried out and reflected on
  (the reflection can be added next lesson or at the capstone).

## Home connection

This week, carry out your action. At home, tell someone what you did and what you noticed. If you
can, invite a family member to do one small belonging action of their own.
