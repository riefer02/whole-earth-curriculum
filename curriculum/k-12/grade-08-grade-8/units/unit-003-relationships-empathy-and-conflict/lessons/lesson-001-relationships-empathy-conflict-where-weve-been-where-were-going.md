# Lesson 1 — Relationships, Empathy & Conflict: Where We've Been, Where We're Going

## Summary

Learners open the unit by **retrieving** what they learned in Grade 7 and **previewing** the four
strands of "Relationships, Empathy & Conflict." They meet the unit map, write three wonders, and
sort them into the strands — starting the unit portfolio they will add to every lesson.

## Objectives

- Preview the unit's eight learning goals — how tone, body language, and digital messages change
  meaning; facilitating conflict toward a solution all can accept; analyzing a conflict from
  multiple perspectives; questioning bias and stereotype; analyzing power and resources and who is
  left out; strengthening belonging; analyzing systems of care and mutual aid; and distinguishing
  charity from mutual aid and solidarity.
  _(D02.S1.08.01, D02.S1.08.02, D02.S2.08.01, D02.S2.08.02, D02.S3.08.01, D02.S3.08.02, D02.S4.08.01, D02.S4.08.02 — preview)_

## Connection

Every day you live inside relationships — with family, friends, classmates, neighbors, and people
you have never met but whose messages reach you through a screen. In those relationships, meaning
can get tangled, feelings can get hurt, and some people can be left out while others hold the
power. None of this is fixed; all of it is something you can learn to do better. Today you begin a
journey through four big questions about how we communicate, how we understand each other, how we
belong, and how we care — alone and together.

## Materials

- Unit map card
- Wonder slips

## Preparation

- Copy the unit map card and wonder slips.
- Retrieval: from Grade 7, recall four ideas — **boundaries** (D02.S1.07.01), **repair after a
  disagreement** (D02.S1.07.02), **empathy across difference** (D02.S2.07.02), and **a mutual-aid
  action** (D02.S4.07.01). Each returns this unit, at a deeper level.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The ideas to land: **(1) this unit has four strands —
Meaning in Communication, Empathy & Perspective, Belonging & Power, and Care & Mutual Aid — and they
all meet in one aim, "living well together"; (2) we are building on Grade 7, not starting over; (3)
wondering is the engine — learners begin by asking, not by being told.** Retrieval practice here is
deliberate (spaced recall of last year's ideas — see Dunlosky et al. 2013, S-039); do not skip it.
The ethics lens: living well together is a question of what is right toward others. The egalitarian
lens: ask early "who is included and who is left out" — it will return every lesson. The global &
anthropological lens: people everywhere have their own ways of communicating, belonging, and caring;
keep the frame broad, never one region's default. The technology & environment lens: the screen now
sits inside our relationships — it changes how meaning travels, and we will examine that honestly.
The social-emotional and community lenses (D02's own) run underneath all four strands. Hold every
wonder as valid; the goal is the asking, not the answer.

## Procedure

1. **Gather and recall (12 min).** Think back to Grade 7. With a partner, recall: What does it
   mean to set and respect a boundary? What are the steps to repair a relationship after a
   disagreement? What did "empathy across difference" ask you to do? What was a mutual-aid action
   you learned about or joined? Share one with the group.
2. **Meet the unit map (8 min).** Look at the unit map. The unit has four strands: **Meaning in
   Communication**, **Empathy & Perspective**, **Belonging & Power**, and **Care & Mutual Aid** —
   all around one aim: **living well together**. Notice the strands are not separate boxes; they are
   four ways of looking at one thing: how humans get along.
3. **Write your wonders (15 min).** On three slips, write three things you wonder about how we
   communicate, understand each other, belong, or care. There are no wrong wonders.
4. **Sort the wonders (10 min).** Place each slip under the strand it belongs to. If it fits two
   strands, put it between them — that is where the best questions live.
5. **Close (5 min).** Keep your wonders. At the end of the unit you will return to them and see
   what you now can say.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Give sentence starters ("I wonder what happens when ___", "I wonder who gets left
  out when ___").
- **Extension:** Write a "wonder + guess" pair — your question and your current best answer — and
  mark it to check again at the capstone.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name the four strands and sort a wonder into one
  (or between two) with a reason?
- **Portfolio artifact:** The three wonder slips, kept as the first page of the unit portfolio.

## Home connection

Ask someone at home: "When our family talks something over, how do we make sure everyone is heard
— and who usually is not?" Bring their answer to mind as the unit unfolds.
