# Lesson 10 — Capstone: My Map of Feelings, Identity & Purpose

## Summary

Learners **integrate** the whole unit into one personal map — **analyze reactions, know a
whole self, steer choices** — using **spaced retrieval** to recall each movement's key idea
from memory, then assembling their portfolio and writing a closing reflection. This is the
unit capstone.

## Objectives

- Analyze a strong emotional reaction by separating the event, the thought, and the feeling.
  _(D01.S1.08.01)_
- Explain how emotion can both inform and distort judgment, using a personal example.
  _(D01.S1.08.02)_
- Explain how multiple and intersecting identities shape a whole self. _(D01.S2.08.01)_
- Analyze where self-esteem comes from and distinguish inner worth from outside approval.
  _(D01.S2.08.02)_
- Manage impulses deliberately, choosing a response rather than reacting. _(D01.S3.08.01)_
- Explain the difference between short-term and long-term consequences and weigh them in a
  decision. _(D01.S3.08.02)_

## Connection

You began the year learning to name the water; this unit, you learned to steer. The three
movements — analyzing a reaction, knowing your whole self, and steering your choices — are
not three separate subjects; they are one skill, and it is *you*. Today you gather the whole
thing into a single map you can carry into the rest of your life, in whatever waters you
sail.

## Materials

- Personal map page
- Portfolio folder

## Preparation

- Copy the personal map page.
- Gather each learner's collected artifacts (frames, self-map, worth analysis, journal,
  decision note).
- Retrieval note: this capstone is a deliberate spaced-retrieval pass over the whole unit —
  a technique with strong evidence behind it (Dunlosky et al. 2013, S-039). Prompt from
  memory first; check against the artifacts after.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **the unit's three
movements are one integrated skill — analyze, know, steer — and the learner now owns a
personal map of it.** Use spaced retrieval: have learners recall each movement's key idea
*from memory before* opening their folders, because recall (not re-reading) is what makes
learning stick (Dunlosky et al. 2013, S-039). This is a capstone, so assess *growth and
integration*, not ranking. The *ethics* lens: the map is not only about the self — steering
well means steering toward what is fair to others. The *egalitarian* lens: every learner's
map is valid; there is no "best" map, and no learner's identities or choices are ranked
against another's. The *global* lens: the map travels — the same three skills serve a person
in a city, a village, a boat, or a field, even as the details of their life differ. The
*critical-thinking* lens: the map is held as a living draft, open to revision as the learner
grows — not a fixed verdict on who they are. Have learners return to the "one thing I am
curious about" note from Lesson 1 and see how far they have come. Celebrate completion as a
community; keep the tone warm and forward-looking. See `docs/facilitation.md` and
`docs/art-style.md`.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Look back at the "one thing I am curious to learn" note you wrote in
   Lesson 1. Did you learn it? Say what surprised you most this unit.
2. **Recall, don't re-read (15 min).** Close your folders. From memory, write or say one
   takeaway under each movement on the map: **analyze my reactions** (event, thought,
   feeling — and how emotion informs *and* distorts), **know my whole self** (many identities,
   one me — and inner worth versus outside approval), **steer my choices** (pause and respond,
   not react — and weigh now against later). Only *after* you recall, open your folder and
   check yourself.
3. **Assemble your portfolio (15 min).** Gather your artifacts in order: the reaction frame,
   the informs-and-distorts pair, the self-map, the worth analysis, the impulse journal, the
   decision note. This is the record of what you can now *do*.
4. **Write the closing reflection (15 min).** Write one short paragraph: *what I can do now
   that I could not do at the start of the unit, and one way I will use it this year.* Keep
   it with your map.
5. **Close (5 min).** Form a circle. Each person shares one takeaway aloud. You are the
   author of your own story — and now you hold the pen a little more steadily.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Recall one takeaway per movement with sentence starters ("I can now ___"), and
  a shorter reflection.
- **Extension:** Write a short "letter to my future self" carrying the three takeaways
  forward, sealed to open at the year's end.

## Assessment

- **Summative (portfolio):** The assembled portfolio plus the completed map and reflection,
  showing mastery of all six unit objectives across the three movements.
- **Self-assessment:** "What can I do now that I could not do before, and what will I do
  next?" — the closing reflection serves as the self-assessment.

## Home connection

Share your finished map with someone at home — one takeaway from each movement — and keep the
map somewhere you will see it.
