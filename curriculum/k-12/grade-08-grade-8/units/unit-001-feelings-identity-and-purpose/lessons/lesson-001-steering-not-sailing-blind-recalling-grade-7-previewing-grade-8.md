# Lesson 1 — Steering, Not Sailing Blind: Recalling Grade 7, Previewing Grade 8

## Summary

Learners **recall** what Grade 7 taught about feelings, identity, and choices, and
**preview** the three movements of Grade 8 — analyze reactions, know a whole self, steer
choices. They get a first taste of the event–thought–feeling frame that the next lesson
takes apart in full.

## Objectives

- Begin to recognize that a strong reaction can be separated into an event, a thought, and
  a feeling, as a preview of the analysis to come. _(D01.S1.08.01)_

## Connection

A person who sails without reading the water ends up wherever the wind and current take
them; a person who reads the water gets to *steer*. Last year you learned to name the
water — mixed feelings, the feelings your culture shows or hides, the labels that would
narrow you, the lessons a past choice can teach. This year you learn to steer: to take a
reaction apart, to know the whole self that is doing the steering, and to choose your
course on purpose.

## Materials

- Unit-map poster
- Recall cards

## Preparation

- Print or draw the unit-map poster (three movements + a recall banner).
- Copy recall cards for pairs.
- Retrieval prompt ready: "What is one thing Grade 7 taught you about feelings, identity,
  or choices?"

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The ideas to land: **(1) Grade 7 named
feelings, identities, and choices; Grade 8 analyzes and steers them; (2) the year has three
movements — analyze reactions, know a whole self, steer choices; (3) a reaction can be
previewed as three parts — event, thought, feeling.** Keep this light: it is a retrieval
and a preview, not a deep lesson. Retrieval practice is one of the strongest learning
techniques we have (Dunlosky et al. 2013, S-039), so spend real time *recalling*, not
re-reading. The *ethics* lens: steering is not just about my comfort but about the
consequences my reactions have for others. The *egalitarian* lens: every learner's Grade 7
memory is valid — there is no single "right" feeling or identity to recall. The *global*
lens: feelings are shown, hidden, and valued differently across cultures, and that variety
is exactly what makes a shared map useful. The *critical-thinking* lens: the three parts
(event, thought, feeling) are held as a question to explore, not an answer to accept. Do
not grade the recall; celebrate it. Where questioning adults is unsafe, frame the preview
as wondering together (see `docs/contexts.md`).

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Close your eyes for a moment and remember Grade 7. Without looking
   at anything: what is one thing you learned about feelings, identity, or choices? Say it
   to a partner.
2. **Recall the six ideas (15 min).** With your pair, work through the recall cards:
   (a) mixed feelings, named precisely; (b) which feelings each culture shows, hides, or
   values; (c) identity as a changing story; (d) stereotypes and the labels that narrow us;
   (e) learning from a past decision; (f) a routine that serves my goals. For each, say one
   example from your own life.
3. **Meet the year (10 min).** Look at the unit map. Grade 7 *named* it; Grade 8 *analyzes*
   it. Three movements: **analyze my reactions**, **know my whole self**, **steer my
   choices**. Point to which movement each recalled idea feeds into.
4. **First taste: three parts (15 min).** Try this seed example: *You hear a loud crash in
   the hallway.* What is the **event**? (The crash.) What is the **thought**? (What you
   tell yourself it means.) What is the **feeling**? (What you feel because of that
   thought.) Say all three aloud — you will take this apart properly next lesson.
5. **Close (5 min).** Write or say one thing you are *curious* to learn this year. Keep it;
   you will look back at it in the final lesson.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Recall only two of the six ideas and use sentence starters ("I remember
  ___"; "I wonder ___").
- **Extension:** In the seed example, name *two* different thoughts the crash could spark,
  and say how the feeling would change with each.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name at least two recalled ideas and match
  the three parts (event, thought, feeling) to the seed example?
- **Self-assessment:** "What did I recall that I had forgotten, and what am I curious
  about?" — one written line kept for the capstone.

## Home connection

Ask someone at home what they remember learning at your age about feelings, identity, or
choices — and share one memory of your own.
