# Lesson 7 — Pause Before You Pounce: Managing the Impulse

## Summary

Learners practice **managing impulses deliberately** — choosing a response rather than
reacting in an emotionally charged moment. They meet the hot/cool idea of the mind, walk a
four-step **pause path**, and write a journal entry about a real moment where they chose
instead of reacted.

## Objectives

- Manage impulses deliberately, choosing a response rather than reacting in emotionally
  charged moments. _(D01.S3.08.01)_

## Connection

A charged moment — a sharp word, a stolen turn, a flashing notification — sets off an
**impulse** that says *act now*. And sometimes acting now is exactly wrong: the words you
cannot unsay, the choice you cannot undo. Between the moment and the action there is a gap,
and in that gap is where you live as the author of your own life. Today you learn to widen
the gap and choose what fills it.

## Materials

- Pause-path poster
- Impulse journal page

## Preparation

- Print or draw the pause-path poster (four steps).
- Copy the impulse journal page.
- Retrieval: from Lessons 2–4, recall the event–thought–feeling frame and the check-the-
  thought question — the pause is where that checking happens *before* the reaction fires.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **an impulse is a signal to
act, but you can pause and choose your response instead of reacting — and practice makes the
pause faster.** The hot/cool framing — a fast, emotional "hot" system and a slower,
deliberate "cool" system — comes from Mischel's delay-of-gratification work (S-336); note
the honest caveat that the famous marshmallow findings have been challenged by later, more
careful studies, so we teach the *pause skill*, not a claim that one childhood test predicts
a life. Teach the path as a worked example (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark 2006, S-011) before
independent practice. The *ethics* lens: the pause protects *other people* — it is the
difference between a sharp word that wounds and a chosen response that respects. The
*egalitarian* lens: every learner has impulses; the skill is learnable by anyone, and no one
is "just hot-headed" — that is a verdict, not information. The *global* lens: what counts as
an appropriate pause differs by culture — some traditions value a long, silent beat before
speaking, others a quick response — so the *tool* is shared even where the *style* differs.
The pause itself — watching an impulse arise without acting — is also a long-practiced skill
in contemplative traditions across South and East Asia, held here side by side with the
psychological account (`docs/philosophy.md` §11).
The *technology* lens: notifications and feeds are engineered to trigger impulse; the pause
is a human brake on a machine that wants you to tap now. The *critical-thinking* lens: the
pause is not suppressing feeling — it is the moment you run the check from Lesson 4. Keep
journal moments small and safe; never require disclosure of conflict that is still painful.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Lesson 4: what is the check question, and where does it happen in
   a reaction? Today we build the moment *before* the reaction: the pause.
2. **Meet the gap (10 min).** Look at the path. A **charged moment** triggers an **impulse**
   — a fast "hot" system that says *now*. But there is a gap before you act. In that gap you
   can **pause**, pick one **tool** — **breathe**, **name the feeling**, or **count to ten** —
   and then **choose your response**. Reacting is automatic; responding is chosen.
3. **Watch it modeled (10 min).** Worked example: *A teammate blames you for a lost point.*
   First impulse: snap back. The pause: breathe, name it ("I feel defensive and angry"), count
   to ten. Chosen response: "That stung — can we talk about the play?" Notice the feeling is
   still there; the *choice* changed.
4. **Practice (15 min).** With a partner, pick a low-stakes charged moment (a dropped ball, a
   missed turn). Say your first impulse aloud, then walk the path: pause, pick a tool, choose
   a response. Swap roles.
5. **Journal (10 min).** On your page, record one real, safe charged moment from this week:
   the moment, your first impulse, and the response you chose (or wish you had). Keep it.
6. **Close (5 min).** The impulse says "now." The pause gives you the choice back. It gets
   faster with practice — that is the whole point.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Give a two-option pause ("breathe or count") and sentence starters ("My first
  impulse was ___; instead I chose ___").
- **Extension:** Compare a first impulse with a chosen response and weigh which served *you*
  and *others* better — then name when acting fast was actually the right call, and why.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name a pause tool and describe choosing a
  response instead of reacting in a real (or role-played) moment?
- **Portfolio artifact:** The impulse journal entry, kept for the unit's impulse-management
  journal.
- **Self-assessment:** "Which pause tool worked for me, and what will I reach for next
  time?" — one written line.

## Home connection

Choose one pause tool to try this week (breathe, name, or count), and tell someone at home
about one moment you used it.
