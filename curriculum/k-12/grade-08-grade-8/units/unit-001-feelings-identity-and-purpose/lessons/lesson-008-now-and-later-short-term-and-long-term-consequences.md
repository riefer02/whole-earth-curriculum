# Lesson 8 — Now and Later: Short-Term and Long-Term Consequences

## Summary

Learners explain the difference between **short-term and long-term consequences**, meet the
idea of **present bias** — why the brain favors "now" — and sort everyday choices onto the
two time scales. This prepares the deliberate weighing in Lesson 9.

## Objectives

- Explain the difference between short-term and long-term consequences. _(D01.S3.08.02)_

## Connection

Every choice ripples outward twice. The snack is sweet *now*, but it shapes your body's
health *later*. The mean joke gets a laugh *now*, but it costs a friend's trust *later*. The
"now" is loud and close; the "later" is quiet and far — and the brain, built to survive
today, often reaches for the near thing. Today you learn to hear both ripples.

## Materials

- Now-and-later card
- Decision scenarios

## Preparation

- Copy the now-and-later card and decision scenarios.
- Retrieval: from Lesson 7, recall the pause. The pause is *where* the weighing happens; this
  lesson and the next are *what* you weigh.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **a consequence can land in
the short term or the long term, and the two often pull in opposite directions.** The
tendency to favor a smaller "now" over a larger "later" is called *present bias* or *delay
discounting*; the fast, emotional mind reaches for the near thing while the slower mind can
hold the far one (Kahneman's System 1/System 2, S-337). Teach the sort as a worked example
(Kirschner, Sweller & Clark 2006, S-011) before independent work. The *ethics* lens: the
long term is often where the *other person's* cost hides — the friend's trust, the shared
resource — so weighing "later" is partly an act of care for others. The *egalitarian* lens:
not everyone can afford to think far ahead — when today is scarce, the "now" is not greed, it
is survival; judge the *weighing*, never the person. The *global* lens: the now/later split
looks different everywhere — a family saving for a season, a community managing water — but
the *skill* of seeing both ripples is universal. The *environment* lens: many of our deepest
problems — a forest cut faster than it regrows, a fish taken before it can spawn — are
exactly this: a loud now and a quiet, far later. The *critical-thinking* lens: neither
"always choose later" nor "always choose now" is right; the skill is *seeing* both, which is
the first step to weighing them well. Distinguish evidence from values: present bias is a
documented pattern of human judgment (S-337); that we *ought* to weigh both fairly is a
value this curriculum holds — name it as one.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Lesson 7: where does the pause happen, and what does it give you?
   Today we ask: pause *and weigh what*? Answer: the now and the later.
2. **Meet the two scales (10 min).** Look at the card. A **consequence** is what follows a
   choice. It can land **short-term** (now) or **long-term** (later) — and often the same
   choice has both, pulling opposite ways. The brain has a habit called **present bias**: the
   near reward feels bigger than the far one, even when it is smaller.
3. **Watch it modeled (10 min).** Worked example: *You consider eating a whole bag of sweets.*
   **Now:** the taste. **Later:** your body's health over time. The now is loud and close; the
   later is quiet and far. Naming both does not *dictate* the choice — it makes it a real
   choice instead of a reflex.
4. **Sort (15 min).** With a partner, sort the scenarios into NOW and LATER columns. For
   each, name the short-term consequence and the long-term one. Notice which time scale your
   brain reached for first.
5. **Close (5 min).** Share one scenario and your two columns. The now is loud; the later is
   quiet but often bigger. Tomorrow you learn to *weigh* them.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Give a two-column template and sentence starters ("Now ___, but later ___").
- **Extension:** Explain *present bias* in your own words, and give one real example where a
  smaller "now" beat a bigger "later" — and what might have changed that.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name both the short-term and long-term
  consequence of a choice, and say which time scale the brain tends to favor?
- **Portfolio artifact:** One sorted scenario with both columns, kept for the unit's
  decision portfolio.

## Home connection

Pick one small household or family choice this week and name its "now" and its "later" aloud
with someone at home.
