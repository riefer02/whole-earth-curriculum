# Lesson 5 — Many Parts, One Me: Intersecting Identities

## Summary

Learners explain how **multiple and intersecting identities** — culture, gender, ability,
language, and more — shape a **whole self**. They build an overlapping-circle self-map and
discover that no single label is the whole story: identities meet, blend, and change with
context.

## Objectives

- Explain how multiple and intersecting identities, such as culture, gender, ability, and
  language, shape a whole self. _(D01.S2.08.01)_

## Connection

No one is a single label. You are not *just* a student, or *just* a speaker of one language,
or *just* one gender or one ability — you are all of these at once, and they shape each
other. A person who farms *and* signs *and* paints is not three people; they are one person
whose parts lean on and change each other. Today you map the parts that meet in you.

## Materials

- Intersecting-identities self-map
- Identity example cards

## Preparation

- Copy the self-map and example cards.
- Retrieval: from Grade 7, recall identity as a changing story and the lesson on labels that
  narrow a person. Today's question is how the *many* parts hold together as one.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **identity is made of many
intersecting parts — culture, gender, ability, language, and more — that meet in one whole
self, and no single part is the whole story.** The concept of *intersectionality* — that
overlapping identities shape a person's experience together, not separately — is named and
sharpened in Kimberlé Crenshaw's work (S-334), used here descriptively and age-appropriately.
The recognition itself — that a person is many parts at once, never a single label — is far
older and appears across many cultures, so it is no single tradition's property; Crenshaw's
term names it for careful social and legal analysis. Teach the
self-map as a worked example (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark 2006, S-011) before independent
work. The *ethics* lens: seeing a whole person, not a label, is how we avoid reducing someone
to a single trait — a matter of respect and fairness. The *egalitarian* lens: no identity is
above another; the lesson deliberately names ability and language alongside culture and
gender so every learner's parts count. The *global* lens: which identity feels largest
changes by place — a language that is dominant at home may be minor at school, and a person
may be read differently in different countries; identity is shaped by where we stand. The
*technology* lens: platforms sort us into categories and boxes that can flatten a whole
person into one label; the self-map is a reminder of the fuller self behind the profile.
The *critical-thinking* lens: hold the self-map as a living picture, not a fixed answer — it
will keep changing. Be sensitive: let learners share only what they choose, never require
disclosure of identity or ability, and frame every identity as equal in worth.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Grade 7: what did you learn about identity as a changing story,
   and about labels that narrow a person? Say it to a partner.
2. **Meet the idea (10 min).** Look at the map. Your **identity** is made of many parts —
   **culture**, **gender**, **ability**, **language**, **family**, **interests** — and they
   *intersect*: they overlap and shape each other. You are not a stack of separate labels;
   you are one whole made where they meet.
3. **Watch it modeled (10 min).** Worked example: *A person who farms, signs, and paints.*
   Farming shapes the hands; signing shapes how they tell stories; painting shapes how they
   see. None of the three is "the real one" — the *whole self* lives where they overlap.
4. **Map yourself (15 min).** On your self-map, fill your own circles — the identities that
   matter to you. In the center, write or draw the **whole me**. Then, for two of your
   parts, write one sentence about how they shape *each other* in you.
5. **Share (10 min).** With a partner, share only what you choose: one identity and how it
   meets another in you. Listen for how your partner is a whole, not a label.
6. **Close (5 min).** No single label is the whole story — of you, or of anyone.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Start with two circles ("I am ___ and ___") and a sentence starter ("These
  two meet in me when ___").
- **Extension:** Compare where the *same* identity feels large or small in different places
  (home, school, a team), and say what changes it.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name several identities and explain how at
  least two of them meet and shape each other in them?
- **Portfolio artifact:** The completed self-map, kept for the unit's identity portfolio.

## Home connection

Ask someone at home about two parts of *their* identity that meet in them — and share how
two of yours meet in you.
