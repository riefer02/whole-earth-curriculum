# Lesson 9 — Weighing It: A Decision in Two Time Scales

## Summary

Learners **weigh** short-term against long-term consequences in a real decision, completing a
two-pan **decision note** that names both consequences, does the weighing, and states a
decision with a reason. This is the unit's formal decision-portfolio artifact.

## Objectives

- Explain the difference between short-term and long-term consequences and weigh them in a
  decision. _(D01.S3.08.02)_

## Connection

Naming the now and the later is the first step; *weighing* them is the second, and it is the
difference between knowing and choosing. A balance scale does not tell you which pan to
prefer — it shows you how much is on each, so you can decide with your eyes open. Today you
put a real decision of your own onto the scale and choose deliberately.

## Materials

- Decision-note page
- Decision scenarios

## Preparation

- Copy the decision-note page and decision scenarios.
- Retrieval: from Lesson 8, recall the two time scales and present bias. Today you *weigh*
  them, not just name them.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **weighing short-term
against long-term consequences means naming both, saying what is given up either way, and
choosing with a reason.** This is a *skill*, so model the full decision note once (worked
example — Kirschner, Sweller & Clark 2006, S-011), then guided practice before independent
work. The *ethics* lens: a good weighing includes consequences for *other people*, not only
for oneself — the friend's trust, the family's need. The *egalitarian* lens: there is no
single "correct" weighing; two reasonable people can weigh the same now-and-later
differently, and both reasonings can be sound — what we assess is the *reasoning*, not the
answer. The *global* lens: what counts as "short" and "long" differs by life — for a family
planting a crop the long term is a season; for a student it may be a year — so the tool is
the same while the numbers differ. The *environment* lens: some of our most important
decisions — how much to take from a commons today — are now/later weighings whose "later" is
a whole community and a whole ecosystem. The *critical-thinking* lens: the decision note
makes the reasoning *visible* so it can be questioned and improved — your own, and a
partner's. Let learners choose small, safe decisions; never require disclosure of painful or
private choices, and offer a made-up decision as a fully acceptable option. Distinguish
evidence from values: that making a weighing explicit improves the quality of a decision is
a skill claim (S-011); that every learner is the rightful author of their own small
decisions is a value this curriculum holds — name it as one.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Lesson 8: what are the two time scales, and what is present
   bias? Say them. Today you *weigh*.
2. **Meet the balance (10 min).** Look at the note page: a balance with two pans — **now**
   and **later**. A **decision note** has four parts: the **choice**, the **now**
   consequence, the **later** consequence, and the **weighing** — which pan is heavier for
   you and why.
3. **Watch it modeled (10 min).** Worked example: *Should I have a hard conversation now or
   avoid it?* **Now:** it will be uncomfortable. **Later:** a small hurt can fester into a
   broken friendship. **Weighing:** the discomfort lasts minutes; the friendship lasts years
   — I weigh the later heavier. **Decision:** have the conversation, with care. Notice the
   note does not *dictate* the answer; it makes the reasoning visible.
4. **Guided practice (15 min).** With a partner, run a decision scenario through the note:
   choice, now, later, weighing, decision. Check each other: are *both* pans filled? Does the
   decision follow from the weighing?
5. **Independent work (10 min).** Complete a decision note for one real, safe decision of
   your own. Name the **trade-off** — what you give up either way. Write your decision as a
   concrete action ("I will ___"), not a wish.
6. **Close (5 min).** A decision made with both pans filled, and a reason for the weighing,
   is a decision you can stand behind — whatever you choose.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide sentence starters ("The choice is ___; now ___, but later ___; I
  weigh ___ heavier because ___; so I will ___") and a one-scenario limit.
- **Extension:** Add a fifth line to the note — *whose else's* consequence am I weighing? —
  and say whether it changes the decision.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Does the decision note fill both pans, name the trade-off, and
  give a decision that follows from the weighing?
- **Portfolio artifact:** The completed decision note, kept for the unit's decision
  portfolio.
- **Peer check:** A partner confirms both time scales were weighed and the reason matches the
  decision.

## Home connection

Take one small real decision home this week, run it through the four-part note with someone
at home, and compare weighings.
