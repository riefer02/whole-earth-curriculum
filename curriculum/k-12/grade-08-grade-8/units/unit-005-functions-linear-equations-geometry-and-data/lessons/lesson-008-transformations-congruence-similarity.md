# Lesson 8 — Transformations: Moving Shapes, Proving Congruence and Similarity

## Summary

Learners describe the four **transformations** — **translation** (slide), **rotation** (turn),
**reflection** (flip), and **dilation** (scale) — and use them to show **congruence** (rigid motions
keep size and shape) and **similarity** (a dilation keeps shape, changes size).

## Objectives

- Describe translations, rotations, reflections, and dilations, and use them to show congruence and
  similarity. _(D05.S3.08.01)_

## Connection

A tiled floor repeats one shape, slid and turned and mirrored to cover the whole room. A map is a
shape scaled down — every distance shrunk by the same factor, so it stays a faithful likeness. A
shadow grows and shrinks but keeps the same outline. Slide, turn, flip, and scale are the four moves
behind tiles, maps, architecture, and design in every culture — and they are also how we *prove* two
shapes are the same (congruent) or the same shape at a different size (similar).

## Materials

- Shape-tracing sheet
- Transformation cards

## Preparation

- Copy the shape-tracing sheet and transformation cards.
- Have worked examples ready: translate a triangle 3 units right; rotate it a quarter turn; reflect
  it across a line; dilate it by a scale factor of 2.
- Recall from Grade 7: scale drawings (D05.S3.07.02) — a dilation is the same idea for any shape.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The ideas to land: **(1) four moves — translation
(slide), rotation (turn), reflection (flip), dilation (scale); (2) the first three are rigid motions:
they keep size and shape, so the image is congruent to the original; (3) a dilation keeps shape but
changes size by a scale factor, so the image is similar to the original.** Because naming and applying
the moves is a foundational skill, use explicit instruction, worked examples, and guided practice
before independent work (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006). Watch for the slips of confusing
reflection and rotation and of calling a dilation "congruent." The global lens: repeating tiles and
scaled patterns are found across traditions — Islamic geometric tilings, Andean and West African
woven and beaded symmetries (S-024), and architectural scale drawings worldwide — mathematics as a
shared human art, held side by side (philosophy §11). The technology lens: computer graphics move
every image on a screen with exactly these four transformations — zoom is a dilation, mirroring is a
reflection. The egalitarian lens: a shape and a tracing are all anyone needs to *see* the move — no
special tool required. The critical-thinking lens: "same shape" is checkable (compare angles and
side ratios), not a guess.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** You can graph lines and find where they meet. Today you *move shapes* and
   prove when two are the same.
2. **Meet the four moves (10 min).** **Translation** slides a shape. **Rotation** turns it around a
   point. **Reflection** flips it over a line. **Dilation** scales it bigger or smaller from a
   centre.
3. **Trace the moves (15 min).** On your sheet, trace the base shape, then apply each move: slide it,
   turn it, flip it, and enlarge it by a scale factor of 2.
4. **Congruent or similar (10 min).** The slide, turn, and flip keep size *and* shape — the image is
   **congruent**. The dilation keeps shape but changes size — the image is **similar**.
5. **Match and label (5 min).** With a partner, match each transformation card to its result and
   label it congruent or similar. Check each other's labels.
6. **Close (5 min).** Share one move and what it keeps the same. Remember: **slide, turn, and flip
   keep size and shape (congruent); scale keeps shape, changes size (similar).**

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Start with only slide and flip on tracing paper; describe each move aloud for
  learners with low vision; label "same size" vs "different size" before the formal words.
- **Accessibility:** Rely on tactile tracing (paper or cloth shapes moved by hand) and narrate each
  move for learners who are blind or have low vision; for learners with dyscalculia, keep the labels
  verbal ("same size" vs "different size") and minimize measuring.
- **Extension:** Combine two moves and describe the single equivalent move, and explain why a
  dilation preserves angles but not lengths.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation/performance):** Can the learner apply each of the four transformations and
  correctly label the result congruent (rigid motion) or similar (dilation)?
- **Self-check:** The learner asks, "Did I slide, turn, flip, or scale correctly? Did the move keep
  size and shape (congruent) or only shape (similar)? Did I check angles and side ratios to be sure?"

## Home connection

At home, find a repeating pattern or a scaled image (a tile, a map, a photo, a fabric), and name
which of the four moves made it — slide, turn, flip, or scale.
