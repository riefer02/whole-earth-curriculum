# Lesson 1 — Healthy, Safe & Strong: Where We've Been, Where We're Going

## Summary

Learners **recall three earlier body-and-wellness ideas** — nutrients and food groups, body signals and
energy needs, and first aid and asking for help — and **preview this unit's two strands and four goals**.
They meet the unit's organizing idea: health comes from **what I eat and how I eat**, and from
**informed choices and a routine of care**.

## Objectives

- Begin to name what a balanced meal and safe food handling mean, as the unit's first goal.
  _(D03.S3.08.01)_
- Begin to name what substances do and what a self-care routine is, as the unit's other goal.
  _(D03.S4.08.01)_

## Connection

Think of one day you felt strong, alert, and well — and one day you felt run-down. The difference is
rarely one single thing; it is the whole pattern of a day: what you ate, whether you slept, how you
moved, and the choices you made. Your body is the one home you will live in your whole life. Today we
remember what we already know about keeping it healthy, safe, and strong — and see where we are going.

## Materials

- Unit map sheet
- Recall cards

## Preparation

- Copy the unit map sheet and recall cards.
- Retrieval: from earlier grades, recall (a) nutrients and food groups, (b) body signals and energy
  needs, and (c) first aid and asking for help. Today we link those to the new unit.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It is the unit's **opening and retrieval pass**, so the
goals are modest: **(1) recall three earlier ideas — food gives energy through nutrients from different
food groups; the body sends signals (hunger, fullness, tiredness) and energy needs differ between
bodies; and first aid and asking a trusted adult for help are how we stay safe; (2) preview the unit's
two strands — food & nutrition, and safety & self-care — and its four goals; (3) plant the organizing
idea: health comes from what I eat and how I eat, and from informed choices and a routine of care.** Do
not try to teach the four goals today; just name and locate them. This is a health unit, so hold its
values from the first minute: **every body, every pace, and every family's way of eating is honored; the
body is taught with dignity, never shame.** The ethics lens: knowing the facts about food and substances
is how we make choices that are right for us and safe for others. The global lens: people everywhere
keep well in different ways — different foods, different routines — and no one way is the only way. The
egalitarian lens: not everyone has the same access to healthy food, clean water, or care, and a fair
world works to change that. The critical-thinking lens: we will tell evidence from marketing and myth
all unit long (philosophy §5). The technology & environment lens: this unit also asks how tools and the Earth shape a body's health — screens and sleep, food systems and clean water, medicines and devices — a thread we return to across the unit. Keep questions open; nothing here is creed (philosophy §11). Spacing
note (S-039): this recall pass is what makes the unit's ideas stick later.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (10 min).** In a small group, look at three recall cards: **nutrients and food groups**,
   **body signals and energy needs**, **first aid and asking for help**. For each, say in one sentence
   what you remember from earlier grades.
2. **Connect the three (5 min).** What do all three have in common? (Each is about *caring for a body*
   — knowing what it needs, hearing its signals, and keeping it safe.)
3. **Meet the map (10 min).** Look at the unit map. Two strands: **food & nutrition**, and **safety &
   self-care**. Four goals: plan and prepare a balanced meal safely; explain how eating patterns shape
   health; explain how substances affect the body and brain; build and track a self-care routine. Read
   each goal aloud once; don't worry about understanding them yet.
4. **Plant the big idea (10 min).** The unit's idea in one breath: health comes from **what I eat and
   how I eat**, and from **informed choices and a routine of care**. Say it back in your own words.
5. **Wonder (10 min).** Write one question about each goal in the wonder box. Which question do you most
   want answered by the end of the unit?
6. **Close (5 min).** Today we remembered what we know and saw the road ahead. Next: we learn to keep
   food safe.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Give sentence starters for the recall round ("Food gives my body energy through…").
- **Extension:** Add a fourth earlier idea of your own (for example, reading food labels, or social
  media and wellbeing) and say why it will matter in this unit.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner recall each earlier idea in one sentence and name the
  unit's two strands and four goals?
- **Artifact:** The completed unit map (or the learner's written recall), kept in the portfolio.

## Home connection

Ask someone at home: what is one daily habit they do to stay well? Bring the answer (no names needed)
to the next lesson.
