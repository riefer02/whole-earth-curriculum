# Lesson 2 — Keeping Food Safe: Clean, Separate, Cook, Chill

## Summary

Learners explain **how to handle food safely** using the four keys — **clean, separate, cook, chill** —
and sort food-handling scenes into safe and unsafe. They learn that unsafe food can cause illness, and
that **most foodborne illness is preventable** with simple habits.

## Objectives

- Explain the four keys of safe food handling — clean, separate, cook, chill. _(D03.S3.08.01)_
- Sort food-handling practices into safe and unsafe and say why. _(D03.S3.08.01)_

## Connection

Have you ever heard of someone getting sick after eating food that sat out too long, or that touched a
raw ingredient? That kind of illness comes from tiny germs that can grow on food when it is handled the
wrong way. The good news: a few simple habits stop most of them before they ever reach you. Today you
learn four keys that turn an ordinary kitchen into a safe one — clean, separate, cook, and chill.

## Materials

- Four-keys food-safety sheet
- Scenario cards

## Preparation

- Copy the four-keys sheet and scenario cards; have soap and water ready for a real handwash.
- Retrieval: from earlier grades, recall handwashing and why it prevents sickness; from Grade 7, recall
  first aid and when to seek help.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The facts to land: **(1) unsafe food can carry germs
(bacteria, viruses, parasites) that cause foodborne illness — and unsafe food is a global problem that
affects everyone, rich and poor, in every country (S-372); (2) the four keys are clean (wash hands,
surfaces, and produce), separate (keep raw meat/poultry/seafood/eggs away from ready-to-eat food to stop
cross-contamination), cook (cook food thoroughly to a safe internal temperature), and chill (keep
perishable food cold and refrigerate leftovers promptly) — a common simplification of the WHO's five
keys to safer food, which add "use safe water and raw materials" (S-373); (3) most foodborne illness is
preventable through these habits (S-372).** This is a *skill*, so model each key once (a worked example
— wash hands for ~20 seconds, use a separate board for raw meat, check that food is cooked through, put
leftovers away) before the sort (explicit instruction carries novice skills — S-011). The egalitarian
lens: safe food is a right, and not everyone has clean water, refrigeration, or a safe kitchen — a fair
world works to change that, and the low-tech variants below honor it. The global lens: every cuisine on
Earth has its own food-safety wisdom (drying, fermenting, salting, quick cooking), and no single
tradition owns safety. The ethics lens: keeping food safe is a way of caring for the people we cook for.
The environment lens: chilling and cooking use energy; using what is fresh and local can mean safer food
with a smaller footprint. Keep it factual and calm — this is about germs, not fear; every body can
learn to handle food safely.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From earlier grades: why do we wash our hands before eating? Today we learn how
   to keep the *food itself* safe.
2. **Meet the four keys (12 min).** Look at the four-keys sheet. **Clean** — wash hands, surfaces, and
   produce. **Separate** — keep raw meat, poultry, seafood, and eggs away from food you eat uncooked, so
   germs don't cross over. **Cook** — heat food thoroughly so the inside reaches a safe temperature.
   **Chill** — keep cold food cold and refrigerate leftovers quickly (S-373).
3. **Model one key (8 min).** Together, model one: wash hands with soap for about 20 seconds (sing a
   short song or count). Watch how a real handwash works.
4. **Sort the scenarios (15 min).** In your group, sort the scenario cards into **safe** and **unsafe**.
   For each unsafe one, say which key would fix it.
5. **Write one rule each (5 min).** On your sheet, write one action for each key in your own words.
6. **Close (5 min).** You now hold four keys that protect every meal you will ever make. Next: we use
   them to plan a balanced meal.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Sort a smaller set of scenarios with a partner; match each unsafe card to the key that
  fixes it.
- **Extension:** Explain *why* heat and cold help — how cooking kills harmful germs and chilling slows
  their growth.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name the four keys and correctly sort at least three
  scenarios, naming the fix?
- **Portfolio artifact:** The completed four-keys sheet, kept in the portfolio.

## Home connection

At home, watch a meal being made and find one place the four keys are followed. Name one place they
could be followed even better — and, if it is safe to do so, offer to help.
