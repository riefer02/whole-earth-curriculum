# Lesson 3 — Planning a Balanced Meal

## Summary

Learners **plan a simple, balanced meal** by choosing foods from several groups — vegetables and fruit,
grains, protein, and water — using the foods their own family actually eats. They learn that a balanced
meal is a *mix*, not any one "right" food, and that many cultures have their own balanced plates.

## Objectives

- Plan a simple, balanced meal by choosing foods from several food groups. _(D03.S3.08.01)_
- Explain what makes the meal balanced, using foods the learner's own family eats. _(D03.S3.08.01)_

## Connection

Every day, people all over the world sit down to a meal — and the meals could not look more different:
rice and lentils in one home, maize and beans in another, bread and greens in a third, fish and fruit in
a fourth. Yet the *idea* behind a balanced meal is shared nearly everywhere: put several different kinds
of food together so the body gets what it needs. Today you learn that idea and build a plate from the
foods you already know and love.

## Materials

- Balanced-meal plate sheet
- Food cards

## Preparation

- Copy the plate sheet and food cards; gather real or pictured local foods if possible.
- Retrieval: from earlier grades, recall food groups and that food gives the body energy to grow, play,
  and learn.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **a balanced meal brings together
several food groups so the body gets a mix of nutrients — vegetables and fruit, grains (starchy foods),
protein foods (beans, lentils, eggs, fish, meat, nuts), and water to drink (S-038, S-120).** It is a
*mix*, not a formula: no single food is "the" healthy food, and no family's meal is wrong. The standard
does not ask for a specific diet, so honor every way of eating — vegetarian, vegan, halal, kosher,
gluten-free, and the many food-based dietary guidelines that differ by country and culture (S-118). Use
the plate as a *worked example*: build one plate together (rice + beans + greens + a fruit + water),
then let learners build their own from their own family's foods (explicit instruction carries novice
skills — S-011). The egalitarian lens: access to a full, varied plate is not equal everywhere, and food
security is a matter of justice (S-120); the lesson never shames a learner for what is or is not on
their table. The global lens: many cultures express "balanced" differently — combining a grain with a
legume (rice + beans, maize + beans, bread + chickpeas) is a worldwide pattern that makes protein
complete. The ethics lens: planning a meal for a family is an act of care. The environment lens: plant
foods and foods grown nearby often cost the Earth less — a thread we pick up more in Grade 9. Keep the
tone warm and practical; this is about nourishment, never weight or judgment.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From earlier grades: what food groups can you name, and why does food matter to a
   body?
2. **Meet the balanced plate (10 min).** Look at the plate sheet. A **balanced meal** brings together
   several groups: **vegetables and fruit**, **grains** (rice, bread, maize, noodles, yam), **protein**
   (beans, lentils, eggs, fish, meat, nuts), and **water** to drink (S-038).
3. **Model one plate (10 min).** Together, build a plate on the board: for example, rice + beans +
   greens + a piece of fruit + water. Name the group each food belongs to.
4. **Build your own plate (15 min).** On your sheet, fill the plate with foods *your own family eats* —
   one or more from each group. Draw or write them.
5. **Explain the balance (5 min).** Write one sentence: "My meal is balanced because it has ___ (give
   the groups)."
6. **Close (5 min).** There is no single "right" meal — only a *mix* that feeds a body. Next: we prepare
   a simple meal safely.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use a pre-sorted set of foods and match each to its group; build the plate with a
  partner.
- **Extension:** Plan a whole day's meals and name what each meal contributes that the others do not.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner build a plate with foods from at least three groups and
  name them?
- **Portfolio artifact:** The filled balanced-meal plate sheet, kept in the portfolio.

## Home connection

At home, notice one meal your family already makes that is balanced — and name the groups on the plate.
If you can, help plan the next meal.
