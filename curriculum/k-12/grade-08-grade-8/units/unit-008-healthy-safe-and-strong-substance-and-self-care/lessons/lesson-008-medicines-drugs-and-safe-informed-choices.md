# Lesson 8 — Medicines, Drugs, and Safe, Informed Choices

## Summary

Learners explain **how drugs affect the body and brain** — many change brain signaling and repeated use
can lead to dependence — and draw the line between **medicine used safely** (as directed) and **drug
misuse**. They practice safe, informed choices, including never sharing medicine and knowing who to ask.

## Objectives

- Explain how drugs affect the body and brain, and how this differs from using medicine safely.
  _(D03.S4.08.01)_
- Make a safe, informed choice about medicines and drugs, including when to ask for help.
  _(D03.S4.08.01)_

## Connection

Medicine is one of humanity's great gifts — it can stop an infection, calm a seizure, or steady a
heart. But the very same substance, taken by the wrong person, at the wrong dose, or for the wrong
reason, can harm. That line — between medicine used as directed and **misuse** — is one of the most
useful things a person can learn to draw. Today you learn to draw it, and to know exactly when to ask
for help.

## Materials

- Medicine-versus-misuse sheet
- Decision cards

- Copy the sheet and decision cards; preview any video before showing it.
- Retrieval: from Lessons 6 and 7, recall how nicotine and alcohol reach and change the brain.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It completes the substance strand with the most
important distinction a young teen can hold: **a medicine used as directed is help; misuse is harm.**
The facts to land: **(1) a medicine is a substance that prevents or treats illness when taken by the
right person, at the right dose, at the right time, as a trusted adult or clinician directs; (2) drugs
that affect the mind (psychoactive substances) change brain signaling — many flood the brain's reward
system with a chemical called dopamine, which is why repeated use can lead to dependence and addiction
(S-376, S-377); (3) addiction is a treatable health condition, not a moral failing — and help exists
(S-377); (4) the hard rules that keep a person safe: never take medicine that isn't yours, never take
more than the dose, never mix with alcohol or other drugs, and never use an unknown pill or plant
(S-376).** The critical-thinking lens: read the label — who it is for, the dose, the warnings — before
anything goes in your body. The egalitarian lens: access to safe, real medicine is deeply unequal around
the world, and the drug trade preys on that inequality — a justice matter. The global lens: humans in
every culture have used plant medicines and remedies, and modern medicine is one more chapter in that
long story; the rule everywhere is to know what you are taking and who it is for. The technology & environment lens: modern medicine is a technology — a triumph of human care — and it carries a footprint too; disposing of medicine carelessly can put it into shared water, and the drug trade damages both land and people, so a safe, informed choice keeps the body and the shared world well. The ethics lens: a
safe choice protects our own body and the people who care about us. Keep it **non-shaming and
harm-reduction-informed**: do not rank or judge people who use drugs; instead, teach the skills — read
the label, refuse a shared pill, ask a trusted adult, and know the emergency floor (if someone is
unresponsive or in trouble after using a substance, call for adult or medical help and stay with them).
**Do not name specific street drugs or describe methods of use** — at this age that detail is
unnecessary and can be counterproductive. A learner who has a family member using drugs is met with
compassion, never blame; addiction is an illness, and the family member deserves care too. Close by
pointing firmly to help: a trusted adult, a school counselor, a doctor, or a local helpline.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Lessons 6 and 7: how did nicotine and alcohol reach and change the brain?
   Today we draw the line between help and harm.
2. **Meet medicine (10 min).** A **medicine** prevents or treats illness — when taken by the **right
   person**, at the **right dose**, at the **right time**, as directed (S-376).
3. **Meet misuse (10 min).** **Misuse** is any of these broken: taking someone else's medicine, taking
   more than the dose, or using a substance for a different purpose. Many mind-affecting drugs change
   brain signaling — they can flood the brain's **reward system** with **dopamine**, which is why
   repeated use can lead to **dependence** (S-377).
4. **The hard rules (5 min).** Four rules keep a person safe: never take medicine that isn't yours;
   never take more than the dose; never mix with alcohol or other drugs; never use an unknown pill or
   plant (S-376).
5. **Sort the decisions (15 min).** With your cards, sort each scene as **safe** or **unsafe**. For each
   unsafe one, name the rule it breaks and the safe choice instead — including asking a trusted adult.
6. **Close (5 min).** Remember: **addiction is a treatable health condition, not a moral failing.** If
   you or someone you know ever needs help with a substance, tell a trusted adult — a parent, a
   counselor, a doctor. Help is real, and asking for it is strength. Next: we build a self-care routine.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Match each card to "safe" or "unsafe" with a partner and say one safe choice aloud.
- **Extension:** Explain, in your own words, how drugs change the brain's reward system and why that can
  lead to dependence — and why addiction is a health condition, not a character flaw.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner state the difference between medicine used as directed
  and misuse, and name at least two of the four safety rules?

## Home connection

At home, ask a trusted adult to show you one medicine label and read it together: who it is for, the
dose, and the warnings. Knowing how to read a label is a skill for life.
