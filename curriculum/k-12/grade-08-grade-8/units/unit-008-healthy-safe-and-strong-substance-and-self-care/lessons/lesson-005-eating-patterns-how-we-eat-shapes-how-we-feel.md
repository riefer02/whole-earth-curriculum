# Lesson 5 — Eating Patterns: How We Eat Shapes How We Feel

## Summary

Learners explain how **eating patterns** — when and how regularly we eat, whether we listen to hunger
and fullness, whether we eat with others or while distracted — affect **health, digestion, and
wellbeing**, not only *what* we eat. They build a picture of their own day of eating and honor the many
patterns of the world.

## Objectives

- Explain how eating patterns, not only what one eats, affect health, digestion, and wellbeing.
  _(D03.S3.08.02)_
- Describe one pattern in their own day and how it might help or hinder wellbeing. _(D03.S3.08.02)_

## Connection

Two people can eat the very same meal and feel completely different — one calm and satisfied, the other
rushed and uneasy. The difference is not the food; it is *how* it was eaten: regularly or after
skipping all day, slowly or while scrolling, alone or with people they care about. **How** you eat is
part of nourishment too. Today you learn to notice the pattern behind the plate.

## Materials

- A-day-of-eating sheet
- Pattern cards

## Preparation

- Copy the day-of-eating sheet and pattern cards.
- Retrieval: from Grade 7, recall noticing body signals of hunger, fullness, and energy needs; from
  earlier grades, recall that food gives the body energy.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **eating patterns matter as much as
food choice. Regular meals steady energy and support digestion; listening to hunger and fullness helps
us eat the right amount for our own body; eating with others supports connection and wellbeing; eating
while very distracted can lead us to miss fullness signals.** These are gentle, evidence-aligned,
wellbeing claims (S-038, S-261), *not* weight claims — and the lesson must never drift into dieting,
restriction, or judgment of any body. Grade 7 already established that restricting food harms a growing
body and that energy needs differ between bodies; build on that. The **global/anthropological lens is
central**: eating patterns differ wonderfully across cultures — communal shared meals, several small
meals, two larger meals, family meals at a set hour, seasonal and religious fasting (such as Ramadan),
and grazing — and each is honored as a way of living, never ranked (S-118). Hold fasting traditions
respectfully: they are meaningful practices, not "skipping meals." The egalitarian lens: regular, varied
meals are a matter of access, not virtue — a learner who is hungry because food is scarce is never to be
shamed; hunger is a justice issue (S-120). The social-emotional lens: eating with others is one of the
oldest human ways of belonging. The ethics lens: how we eat affects our own body and can be an act of
care for ourselves. The technology & environment lens: how we eat is shaped by tools and places too — a phone in hand while eating can blunt fullness signals, and where food comes from and what is wasted touches the Earth — so the pattern is never only personal. Keep it concrete and warm; the *connection* is the meal, the *concept* is the
pattern.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Grade 7: what does it feel like to be hungry? To be full? Name the body
   signals.
2. **Meet eating patterns (10 min).** A **pattern** is not what you eat but *how and when*. Look at the
   pattern cards: regular meals, skipping meals, eating with others, eating while distracted, eating
   slowly and noticing, late-night eating.
3. **Sort and discuss (12 min).** In your group, sort the patterns into "helps my body and mood" and
   "might make it harder," and say why for each. (There are no "bad people" here — only patterns that
   help or hinder.)
4. **Map your own day (13 min).** On your day-of-eating sheet, mark when you usually eat and how your
   body feels around each time. Circle one pattern that helps and one you might gently adjust.
5. **Honor the world's patterns (5 min).** Note: people everywhere eat differently — shared meals, small
   meals, two big meals, fasting traditions — and each is a real way of living, not a mistake.
6. **Close (5 min).** You are not just what you eat; you are *how* you eat. Next: we meet substances —
   tobacco, alcohol, and drugs.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Match each pattern card to a simple drawing (a clock, a family at a table, a phone) and
  name one that helps.
- **Extension:** Explain the difference between a healthy eating pattern and a restrictive or dieting
  pattern, and why restriction harms a growing body.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner explain how at least two patterns affect health,
  digestion, or wellbeing?
- **Portfolio artifact:** The completed day-of-eating sheet, kept in the portfolio.

## Home connection

At your next meal at home, notice the pattern: is it regular? Shared? Slow? Ask how it feels to eat
that way — and thank whoever made the meal.
