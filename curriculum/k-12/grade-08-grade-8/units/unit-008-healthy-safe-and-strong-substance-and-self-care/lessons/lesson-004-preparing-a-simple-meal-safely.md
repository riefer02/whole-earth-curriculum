# Lesson 4 — Preparing a Simple Meal Safely

## Summary

Learners **prepare a simple, balanced meal** using safe food-handling practices from start to finish,
following a recipe and a safety checklist. They link each step to the four keys — clean, separate, cook,
chill — and honor allergies and each person's needs.

## Objectives

- Prepare a simple, balanced meal using safe food-handling practices. _(D03.S3.08.01)_
- Connect each step of preparation to a safety key and to the needs of the people eating. _(D03.S3.08.01)_

## Connection

Planning a meal is a map; preparing it is the walk. The safest cooks are not the most skilled — they are
the most careful: they wash, they separate, they heat, they cool, and they think about who is eating
(including anyone with a food allergy). Today you take the meal you planned and make it real, one safe
step at a time. This is a skill you will use for the rest of your life.

## Materials

- Simple recipe card
- Safety checklist

## Preparation

- Copy the recipe card and safety checklist; arrange a safe workspace and clean water.
- Retrieval: from Lesson 2, recall the four keys — clean, separate, cook, chill.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It is the **performance lesson** for D03.S3.08.01: the
learner actually prepares a meal. The skill to land: **safe preparation is a sequence — wash hands;
wash produce; keep raw and ready-to-eat food separate (different boards/plates); cook food thoroughly
if heat is used; chill leftovers promptly; serve clean.** Emphasize **knife safety and adult supervision
for anything sharp or hot** — a young teen must never cook with heat or sharp tools unattended, and
**allergies are non-negotiable**: check with the person eating, and never share a dish with a known
allergen (S-372, S-373). This is a *skill*, so **model one full sequence once** (for example, assemble
a fruit-and-vegetable salad: wash hands → wash produce → clean board → cut → serve), then let learners
do theirs (explicit instruction + worked example carry novice skills — S-011). Choose a **no-cook or
low-cook recipe** so the lesson works with limited resources; the low-tech path uses whatever foods are
at hand. The egalitarian lens: not every learner has a kitchen, so frame this as a skill that belongs to
everyone, adaptable to any table — and never let it become a display of who has more. The community
lens: cooking for others is care, and sharing the food is the point. The ethics lens: safe handling is
how we protect the people we feed. The environment lens: wash with just the water you need, use what is
fresh and local, and avoid waste. The global lens: every cuisine on Earth has its own safe ways of preparing food — fermenting, steaming, pounding, raw preparations — and no single kitchen style owns safety; the four keys work in any kitchen, with any tradition. Assess by observing the *habits* (washing, separating, checking), not
the tastiness of the food. If a learner cannot prepare food in this setting, they can still demonstrate
the sequence with props or describe it step by step — the learning is the habits, not the dish. For
learners with limited hand or arm movement, adapt the workspace and tools — a seated workspace, a
non-slip mat or bowl stabilizer, and one-handed or no-knife tasks (tearing leaves, mixing, assembling)
— or let the learner direct each step while a partner performs it.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Lesson 2: name the four keys — clean, separate, cook, chill. Today we put
   them to work.
2. **Read the recipe and checklist (5 min).** Look at your recipe card and the safety checklist on the
   back. Notice where each of the four keys appears.
3. **Model the sequence (8 min).** Watch one worked example: wash hands, wash produce, use a clean
   separate board, cut or assemble, serve. Notice each safe step.
4. **Prepare your meal (25 min).** Follow your checklist step by step. Wash your hands first. Wash
   produce. Keep raw and ready-to-eat food separate. If anything is cooked, cook it through and let an
   adult handle heat. Chill any leftovers. Serve.
5. **Check your work (7 min).** Tick each step on your checklist. Ask: did I wash? separate? cook or
   chill? Does this meal include several food groups? Is it safe for everyone eating (allergies)?
6. **Close (5 min).** Share the meal if you can. You just did something real: you made food that feeds a
   body, safely. Next: we look at *how* we eat, not only what.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Handle a single safe task (washing, tearing, measuring) and narrate the sequence a
  partner carries out. Where hand or arm movement is limited, use a non-slip mat, a seated workspace,
  and no-knife tasks, or direct a partner through each step while you name it.
- **Extension:** Write your own recipe with a safety note for each step, then make it and explain the
  notes to someone.

## Assessment

- **Performance (observation):** Does the learner wash hands, keep raw and ready-to-eat food separate,
  and handle food safely from start to finish?
- **Portfolio artifact:** The completed safety checklist (and a drawing or photo of the meal), kept in
  the portfolio.

## Home connection

Prepare the same meal (or one like it) for your family at home, with a grown-up present for anything
sharp or hot. Ask them to watch your safe habits.
