# Lesson 9 — Building a Self-Care Routine: Sleep, Hygiene, Movement, Connection

## Summary

Learners **build a personal self-care routine** covering the four pillars — **sleep, hygiene, movement,
and connection** — choosing one realistic action for each that fits their own life. They learn that a
routine that is small and kept beats one that is grand and abandoned.

## Objectives

- Build a personal self-care routine covering sleep, hygiene, movement, and connection. _(D03.S4.08.02)_
- Choose actions that are realistic for the learner's own life and explain why each helps wellbeing.
  _(D03.S4.08.02)_

## Connection

Have you ever made a big plan — exercise every day, sleep early every night — and watched it fall apart
in three days? That is normal. A routine that works is not the one that is grand; it is the one that is
**small, realistic, and kept**. Self-care is not a treat for a bad day; it is the ordinary, repeatable
care of a body and a mind — sleep, washing, moving, and staying connected to people. Today you build one
that actually fits *your* life.

## Materials

- Self-care wheel sheet
- Routine builder cards

## Preparation

- Copy the wheel sheet and builder cards.
- Retrieval: from Grade 8 Unit 2, recall how stress affects the body and the healthy ways back to calm;
  from Grade 7, recall noticing body signals and setting social-media boundaries.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It opens the self-care strand. The idea to land: **a
self-care routine rests on four everyday pillars — sleep, hygiene, movement, and connection — and the
best routine is the one the learner can actually keep.** The evidence-aligned guidance: **sleep** — most
adolescents need about 8–10 hours a night (S-050); **hygiene** — daily washing, handwashing, tooth and
body care prevent illness (S-016, S-017); **movement** — regular physical activity benefits body and
mind, and any amount helps (S-069); **connection** — belonging and supportive relationships are central
to adolescent wellbeing (S-261, S-311). Emphasize **realistic over grand**: one small, specific action
per pillar is the whole lesson. This is a *skill-building* moment — model building one routine on the
board (worked example: "sleep — lights out by 10; hygiene — brush teeth morning and night; movement — a
20-minute walk after school; connection — call one friend this week"), then let learners build theirs
(explicit instruction carries novice skills — S-011). The social-emotional lens: self-care is not
selfish; a person who is well can be there for others. The egalitarian lens: a routine must fit the
learner's real life — their home, their chores, their water and privacy, their family's schedule — and
no routine is "better" because it costs more; dignity, not display. The global lens: people everywhere
practice care in different forms — a daily walk, a prayer, a bath, a family meal, a nap — and every
culture has its own wisdom; honor them all. Even tooth care takes many forms: a brush and paste in one
home, a chewing stick (miswak, neem, or another local twig) in another — both are real ways people keep
teeth clean, and neither is "better." The ethics lens: caring for one's own body and mind is a
responsibility we hold toward ourselves, and it makes us better able to care for others. The technology & environment lens: screens and light quietly shape sleep and mood, and a safe place to move plus clean water to wash with are environmental foundations of any routine — so a realistic routine works with the tools a learner has and the place they live in, never against them. Watch for
learners whose circumstances make some pillars hard (no quiet place to sleep, no clean water, no safe
space to move) — meet them with adaptation, never shame, and help them find what *is* possible. This
routine will be *tracked* in the next lesson, so keep it safe to track: habits and feelings, never
weight, appearance, or anything that could shame.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Grade 8 Unit 2: what does stress do to a body, and what helps it return to
   calm? Today we build the daily version of that.
2. **Meet the four pillars (10 min).** Look at the self-care wheel. Four sections: **sleep** (about 8–10
   hours for most teens), **hygiene** (washing, teeth, hands), **movement** (any movement that feels
   good), **connection** (the people who matter to you) (S-050, S-016, S-069, S-261).
3. **Model one routine (10 min).** Watch one built on the board: one small, specific action per pillar.
   Notice how *small* each action is — that is what makes it keepable.
4. **Build yours (15 min).** On your wheel, write one realistic action for each pillar — something that
   fits *your* day, your home, and your life. Make it small enough to keep.
5. **Pair check (5 min).** Read your four actions to a partner. Together, ask: is each one small enough?
   Does it fit your real life? Adjust if needed.
6. **Close (5 min).** You have built the bones of a routine. Next: we track it for a week and see what
   it does for you.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Choose one action for each pillar from the builder cards rather than writing your own.
- **Extension:** Set a small, specific goal for one pillar (for example, "lights out by 10 for five
  nights") and plan one thing that would help you keep it.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name all four pillars and give one realistic action for
  each?
- **Portfolio artifact:** The completed self-care wheel, kept in the portfolio (and tracked next
  lesson).

## Home connection

Share one pillar of your routine with someone at home and invite them to do it with you — a walk, a
shared meal, an earlier bedtime. Self-care grows when it is shared.
