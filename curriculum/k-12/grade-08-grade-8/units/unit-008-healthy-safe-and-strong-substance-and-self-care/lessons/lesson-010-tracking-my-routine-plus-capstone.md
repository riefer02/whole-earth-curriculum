# Lesson 10 — Tracking My Routine + Capstone: Healthy, Safe & Strong

## Summary

Learners **track their self-care routine** for a week and reflect on how it affects wellbeing, then
synthesize the unit's **four goals** into one web around **healthy, safe, and strong** — recalling each
goal, writing one sentence and drawing one picture for each, and choosing one to explain fully. This
spaced-retrieval pass closes the unit.

## Objectives

- Track a personal self-care routine and describe how it affects wellbeing. _(D03.S4.08.02)_
- Recall and connect all four goals: planning and preparing a balanced meal safely, eating patterns,
  substances, and self-care. _(D03.S3.08.01, D03.S3.08.02, D03.S4.08.01, D03.S4.08.02)_

## Connection

You began this unit by remembering what you already knew, and you have added four new ways of caring for
yourself — a safe, balanced meal; the pattern behind the plate; the truth about substances; and a
routine of sleep, hygiene, movement, and connection. Now you do two things at once: you set up a tracker
to watch your routine work over a week, and you weave the whole unit into one picture. A well person is
not someone who knows four facts; it is someone who lives them, day after day.

## Materials

- Self-care tracker
- Capstone map sheet

## Preparation

- Copy the tracker and capstone map sheet.
- Retrieval: this whole lesson is a retrieval and synthesis pass — the "science of learning" payoff of
  the unit (S-039). Have the learner's earlier portfolio artifacts on hand.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It is the **closing capstone and synthesis**, with one
forward-looking piece: the **self-care tracker**. Two jobs: **(1) set up a one-week tracker for the four
pillars — sleep, hygiene, movement, connection — that the learner will mark each day and reflect on
after the week; (2) synthesize the unit's four goals into one web around healthy, safe, and strong.** The
four goals are: (1) plan and prepare a simple balanced meal safely — clean, separate, cook, chill; (2)
explain how eating patterns, not only what we eat, affect health, digestion, and wellbeing; (3) explain
how tobacco, alcohol, and drugs affect the body and brain and make safe, informed choices; (4) build and
track a self-care routine of sleep, hygiene, movement, and connection. The tracker is about **habits and
feelings, never weight or appearance** — keep it safe and non-shaming; a missed day is information, not
a failure. This is a **retrieval and spacing** moment — having learners recall rather than re-read is
what makes the learning durable (S-039). Keep assessment **mastery, not ranking**: a learner who cannot
yet recall a goal revisits that lesson's artifact and tries again — "not yet" is information, never a
verdict. The lenses, together: the **critical-thinking** lens asks what evidence supports each
claim; the **ethics** lens asks what is right and who is affected; the **egalitarian** lens asks who
benefits and who is left out; the **global** lens asks how this looks in other places and cultures; the
**technology & environment** lens asks how tools and the Earth are touched by each goal; the
**social-emotional** lens asks how each goal makes a person *feel*. Celebrate the collective: each
learner's map is a thread in one class web, and each learner's routine is a quiet act of care. Note for
continuity: the tracker runs *after* this lesson — the learner returns to it over the week and reflects
(with a trusted adult or on their own); this is the unit's self-care strand made real.

## Procedure

1. **Recall, don't peek (8 min).** Without looking at notes, list the unit's four goals from memory.
   What can you remember? (That is the point — recalling *is* the learning.)
2. **Check and fill (5 min).** Compare with the capstone map sheet. For any goal you missed, open that
   lesson's artifact and add it. "Not yet" is not a failure; it is a map of what to revisit.
3. **Set up your tracker (10 min).** On the self-care tracker, write your four routine actions from
   Lesson 9 — one per row (sleep, hygiene, movement, connection). You will mark each day for one week
   and note how you felt. A missed day is just information, never a verdict. If writing or reading a
   chart is hard, track without it — by voice (tell a trusted adult each evening), by memory, or by
   touch (drop a pebble, bead, or seed into one of four cups each day, or tie a knot in a string).
4. **Build your web (15 min).** On the capstone map, write one sentence and draw one picture for each of
   the four goals. Connect them to the center: **healthy, safe, strong**.
5. **Choose a deep dive (12 min).** Pick the one goal that most matters to you and explain it fully to a
   partner — what it means, what evidence supports it, and one way it touches your real life. Then listen
   to your partner's.
6. **Close (5 min).** Look at your map and your tracker. You did not memorize four facts — you built a
   way of caring for yourself. Carry it with you. After one week, return to your tracker and reflect:
   how did your routine make you feel?

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Work from the capstone map with pictures; choose one goal to explain instead of four, and
  track just one pillar for the week.
- **Extension:** Choose one goal and explain how it connects to at least two others (for example: safe
  food handling and a self-care routine both protect the body; knowing about substances and tracking
  habits are both ways of choosing deliberately).

## Assessment

- **Summative (portfolio):** The completed capstone map — all four goals, each with a sentence and a
  picture — plus the set-up tracker, kept in the portfolio.
- **Self/peer:** Learners self-check each goal is present and give one specific "I understood this when
  you said…" to a partner.
- **Mastery, not ranking:** A learner missing a goal revisits its artifact and completes it; the map is
  finished when it is finished, not before.

## Home connection

Show someone at home your capstone map and your tracker, and invite them to do one pillar of the week
with you. Teaching it — and sharing it — is the surest sign you have learned it.
