# Lesson 5 — My Identity Is Many Parts

## Summary

Learners map their identity as **many connected parts** — family, culture, language,
interests, **beliefs**, place, and friendships. This grows the map they began in Grade 5
(family, culture, place, history) by adding **beliefs** — the ideas and values a person
holds. They honor that **every family shape, culture, language, and belief system is
ordinary and whole**, and celebrate the many different maps in the room as a shared
strength.

## Objectives

- Describe their identity as made of many parts that can shift by context, such as
  family, culture, language, interests, and beliefs. _(D01.S2.06.01)_

## Connection

Draw a dot in the middle and call it **me**. Now draw a ring of bubbles around it — the
people who care for you, the language(s) you speak, the food and music you love, the
place you call home, the things you are interested in, the beliefs that guide you. That
ring is your **identity**: not one single thing, but many parts at once. A child raised by
grandparents, a child in a blended family, a child who moved across the sea, a child who
speaks three languages — each has a map that is whole and true. No map is "more complete"
than another.

## Materials

- Many-parts identity map poster
- Blank many-parts map page
- Pencils and crayons

## Preparation

- Print or draw the many-parts identity map poster (center "me," ring of connected
  bubbles).
- Copy a blank many-parts map page for each learner.
- Arrange a space for a gallery walk at the end.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land is that **identity has
many connected parts, and every shape of family, culture, language, and belief is whole
and worthy**. This is the unit's deepest "honor every family" moment: be explicit that
families come in many shapes — two parents, one parent, grandparents, adoptive or foster
parents, siblings, chosen family, blended families — and that all belong; model adoption,
moving, and multilingualism as ordinary, not "special cases." **Beliefs are personal and
optional to share** — invite learners to include beliefs (values, faith, worldview) only if
they choose, and never rank any belief system. The gallery walk should celebrate
*difference*, never compare maps as better or worse. See `docs/facilitation.md` and
`docs/art-style.md`.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** In Grade 5 you drew parts of you: family, culture, place, history.
   Today you add a new part — **beliefs** — and draw the whole map again, with everything
   on it.
2. **Meet the map (10 min).** Look at the poster: a center **me** with bubbles around it —
   my family, my culture, my language, my interests, my beliefs, my place, my friendships.
   Worked example: one child's map has "my grandmother" (family), "my two languages"
   (culture), "the village by the river" (place), "football" (interests), and "I believe
   people should be treated fairly" (beliefs). Every bubble is a real thread. Beliefs are
   the ideas and values you hold — and they are *yours* to include or keep private.
3. **Draw your map (20 min).** On your page, write **me** in the center, then draw your
   own bubbles and fill each one. Add any part that matters — a friend, a faith, an
   ability, a dream. Your map is yours; there is no "right" way to be a family, to speak,
   or to believe.
4. **Gallery walk (10 min).** Walk quietly and look at the maps around the room. Notice:
   how many family shapes? How many languages? How many beliefs? Each map is whole and
   true. Find one thing on someone else's map you admire. (Learners who do not stand or
   walk can stay seated and have the maps passed hand to hand around the circle, or posted
   at seated height — everyone sees every map either way.)
5. **Close (5 min).** You are many parts, one whole self — and every person's map deserves
   honor. Carry that with you.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide pre-drawn bubbles with picture prompts to fill in; scribe labels
  as needed.
- **Extension:** Connect two bubbles with a line and write one sentence about how those
  two parts meet in your life (e.g., "my language and my beliefs meet at our family
  meals").

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner produce a map with several connected parts
  and name how at least one part shapes who they are?
- **Portfolio:** The identity map, kept as a growing artifact to revisit in Lessons 6 and
  10 and later in the year.

## Home connection

Show someone at home your map and invite them to add one bubble you may not have thought
of — a story, a place, a person, a belief, or a dream.
