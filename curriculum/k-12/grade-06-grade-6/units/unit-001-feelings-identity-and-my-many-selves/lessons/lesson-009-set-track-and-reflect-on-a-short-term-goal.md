# Lesson 9 — Set, Track, and Reflect on a Short-Term Goal

## Summary

Learners set a **short-term goal** they can actually reach, build a simple **tracker**, and
plan to **reflect** on what worked. They learn that a good short-term goal is **small and
specific** — something doable in days, not a wish — and that the cycle **set → track →
reflect** turns effort into learning. They begin the goal today and track it over the
following days, returning in Lesson 10 to reflect.

## Objectives

- Set a short-term goal, track their progress, and reflect on what worked.
  _(D01.S3.06.01)_

## Connection

Think of anything you have learned to do — riding a bicycle, cooking a dish, hitting a
note, clearing a level in a game. Almost nobody learned it in one leap. They set a small
goal ("ride to the end of the street," "make the rice without burning it"), practiced a
little each day, and noticed what worked. A **wish** is "someday I'll get better." A
**goal** is specific, small, and checkable: *what*, *how much*, *by when*. The people who
reach things do not wish harder — they set a goal and track it. That works in a city, on
a farm, or anywhere there is something you want to grow.

## Materials

- Goal-cycle poster
- My goal page
- Pencils

## Preparation

- Print or draw the goal-cycle poster (set → track → reflect, with a worked example).
- Copy a "my goal" page for each learner.
- Arrange a circle and a quiet space for individual work.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land is that **a reachable
goal is small, specific, and tracked** — not that a learner "failed" if they don't meet
it. Frame missing a goal as *information* ("what made it hard? what would I change?"), the
spirit of mastery learning: revisit and adjust, never a verdict. Let learners choose goals
in *any* domain — a school skill, a sport, a craft, a habit, a kindness — so no one's goal
is ranked against another's. The tracker is personal and private; progress is compared only
to the learner's own start. Plan for the goal to run over the days *between* this lesson
and Lesson 10, and prompt learners to touch their tracker daily. See `docs/facilitation.md`
and `docs/contexts.md` (mastery learning, TaRL).

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** You have studied your feelings, your triggers, and your many selves.
   Now the turn toward agency: setting a goal you can actually reach and learning from the
   try.
2. **Meet the cycle (10 min).** Look at the poster: **set → track → reflect**. Worked
   example: a wish is "get better at reading." A goal is *specific*: "read for 15 minutes
   every evening for five days." **Set:** name what, how much, by when. **Track:** mark a
   little each day — a check, a note, a number. **Reflect:** what worked? what was hard?
   what next? A specific goal can be checked; a wish cannot.
3. **Set your goal (10 min).** On your page, write one **small, specific** goal you can
   do in the next few days. Test it with a partner: can you answer *what, how much, by
   when*? If not, make it smaller or clearer. It can be about a skill, a habit, or a
   kindness — your choice.
4. **Build your tracker (10 min).** Design your tracker — five boxes, a line of marks, or
   a small chart. Decide *when* each day you will check in (right after school, before
   bed). Write one **reflection question** you will ask yourself in a few days: "What
   worked? What got in the way? What would I change?"
5. **Close (5 min).** Start today. A small goal, tracked a little each day, and reflected
   on honestly — that is how a growing person grows. Bring your tracker back next lesson.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a menu of small, specific example goals to choose from, and a
  pre-drawn five-box tracker; scribe as needed.
- **Extension:** Write two goals — a five-day one and a two-week one — and note how
  "specific" changes when the time is longer.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner state a goal that is small, specific, and
  time-bound (what, how much, by when), plus a tracker and a reflection question?
- **Portfolio:** The goal page, kept and revisited in Lesson 10.

## Home connection

Share your goal with someone at home and invite them to ask you, each evening, "How did
today's step go?" — a gentle check that helps you keep tracking.

## Resources

- Decades of research on goal-setting (Locke & Latham) show that specific, challenging
  goals with feedback improve performance; mastery orientation (Dweck) frames a missed
  goal as information, not failure. For classroom framing, see CASEL's self-management
  competency: <https://casel.org/fundamentals-of-sel/>
