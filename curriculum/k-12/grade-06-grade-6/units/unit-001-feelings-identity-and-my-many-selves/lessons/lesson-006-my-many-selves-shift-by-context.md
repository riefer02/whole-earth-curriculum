# Lesson 6 — My Many Selves Shift by Context

## Summary

Learners see that different **contexts** — home, school, with friends — bring different
parts of them forward, and that this **shifting is a skill, not a fake self**. Speaking a
home language with family while speaking another at school, or being careful at school and
playful with friends, are all one whole person. They map their own contexts and notice
which parts come forward where — and which parts (their values) stay the same everywhere.

## Objectives

- Describe their identity as made of many parts that can shift by context, such as
  family, culture, language, interests, and beliefs. _(D01.S2.06.01)_

## Connection

One child is quiet and careful at home, loud and bold on the football field, gentle with
a younger sibling, and the one who makes everyone laugh with friends. Is one of those the
"real" them? All of them are. Like different instruments in one song, different contexts
bring different parts of a person forward. A child who speaks one language at home and
another at school is not pretending — she is *fluent in many selves*, and that is a
strength, not a split.

## Materials

- Shifting-selves poster
- My contexts page
- Pencils

## Preparation

- Print or draw the shifting-selves poster (same person, three contexts, three parts
  forward).
- Copy a "my contexts" page for each learner.
- Arrange a circle for discussion.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land is that **shifting by
context is real and skillful — never "two-faced."** This matters deeply at age 11–12, when
learners first worry they are being "fake" by being different in different places. Name
it plainly: being different with a teacher than with a best friend is normal and healthy;
the alternative — being exactly the same everywhere — would be impossible and unkind.
**Honor multilingualism explicitly** as an ordinary, powerful form of shifting, and never
frame a learner's home language as a "private" or "lesser" self. If a learner reveals a
context that feels unsafe or in conflict, receive it calmly and note the one part that
stays true across contexts — their values. See `docs/facilitation.md` and `docs/contexts.md`.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Last time you drew your many parts. Today you see how those parts
   move: different **contexts** bring different parts of you forward.
2. **Meet the shift (10 min).** Look at the poster. The same person, three contexts: with
   family, they speak their home language; at school, they are the careful one; with
   friends, they are the one who makes jokes. Worked example: is any one the "real" them?
   All of them are. A **context** is a place and a group — home, school, a team, a
   gathering — and each one invites a different part of you forward. This is a skill, not
   a fake self.
3. **Map your contexts (15 min).** On your page, draw or write three contexts of yours
   (home, school, with friends — or others, like a place of worship, a team, a grandparent's
   house). For each, name the part of you that comes forward there. Check with a partner:
   do your two lists show different parts of the same whole person?
4. **What stays the same? (10 min).** Look at your three contexts. Is there something that
   stays with you in *all* of them — a value, a way you treat people? Write it down. That
   is your through-line, the part that holds the many parts together.
5. **Close (5 min).** You are many parts, one whole self — and the parts that shift by
   context are all really, truly you.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide picture cards of contexts to sort, and sentence starters ("At ___
   I am the one who ___").
- **Extension:** Name a part of you that stays the same across all contexts and explain
   why it matters to you that it does.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name two or three contexts and the part of
  themselves that comes forward in each, plus one part that stays the same?
- **Self-check:** The learner rereads their page and checks that they described *different
  parts* for different contexts, and that their through-line feels true.

## Home connection

Ask someone at home what part of *you* they see come forward at home — and share which
part of you comes forward elsewhere.
