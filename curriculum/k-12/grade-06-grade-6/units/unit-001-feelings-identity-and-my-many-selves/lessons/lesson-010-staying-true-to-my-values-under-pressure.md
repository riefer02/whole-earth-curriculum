# Lesson 10 — Staying True to My Values Under Pressure

## Summary

In this **capstone** lesson, learners name three pressures — **stress, distraction, and
peer pressure** — and practice a **pause → name → choose** strategy that keeps them true
to their **values** (built in Lessons 5–6) while handling the pressure itself (built in
Lessons 1–2). They rehearse a response, reflect on the goal they set in Lesson 9, and
close the unit with agency and hope.

## Objectives

- Use strategies to handle stress, distraction, or peer pressure while staying true to
  their own values. _(D01.S3.06.02)_
- Describe their identity as made of many parts that can shift by context, such as
  family, culture, language, interests, and beliefs. _(D01.S2.06.01)_
- Identify patterns in their own emotional responses and name what tends to trigger
  them. _(D01.S1.06.01)_
- Set a short-term goal, track their progress, and reflect on what worked.
  _(D01.S3.06.01)_

## Connection

Every life has the moment: a test tightens your chest (**stress**), a feed pulls your
eyes from what you meant to do (**distraction**), a friend presses you to do what the
group does even though something in you says no (**peer pressure**). The question is not
"Am I weak for feeling it?" — everyone feels it. The question is "**What will I do?**"
Your **values** — honesty, kindness, fairness, the beliefs and people you hold dear — are
your anchor. When pressure pushes, the anchor is not willpower alone; it is pausing long
enough to remember what you stand for, then choosing the act you can stand behind.

## Materials

- Values-under-pressure poster
- Pause–name–choose page
- Pencils

## Preparation

- Print or draw the values-under-pressure poster (a shield of values with three pressures
  pressing in).
- Copy a pause–name–choose page for each learner.
- Arrange a circle and space for paired role-play.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land is **agency, not
willpower shaming**: everyone feels pressure, and the skill is *pausing to choose*, not
*never feeling tempted*. Never frame a learner as "weak" for feeling peer pressure —
that is a normal, human pull, and this lesson makes it *nameable and manageable*, not
shameful. Connect the three pressures to earlier lessons: a pressure often *triggers* a
pattern (Lesson 2), and the *feeling behind* it can differ from what is shown (Lessons
3–4). Reflect on the Lesson 9 goal with a mastery frame — progress, not verdict. The
egalitarian lens asks: whose values win under group pressure, and is that fair? Keep the
discussion open and non-dogmatic; a learner's values are their own. See `docs/facilitation.md`
and `docs/philosophy.md` §11–12.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** This is your capstone. All unit you have traced your patterns,
   named your triggers, read what people show and feel, mapped your many parts, noticed
   the mirrors, and set a goal. Today it all comes together: **staying true to your values
   under pressure.**
2. **Meet the three pressures (10 min).** Look at the poster. Three arrows press on a
   person holding a shield labeled "my values." **Stress** — the worried, tight feeling
   when something is on the line. **Distraction** — attention pulled away from what you
   meant to do. **Peer pressure** — the push to do what the group does. None of these makes
   you weak; everyone feels them. The shield is your **values** — honesty, kindness,
   fairness, and the beliefs from your identity map (Lesson 5).
3. **Learn pause–name–choose (10 min).** Worked example. **Pause:** take one breath before
   acting. **Name:** "A friend is pushing me to copy homework — that's peer pressure, and
   honesty matters to me." **Choose:** "I'll say, 'I'll do my own work — but I'll help you
   study if you want.'" Notice: the choice is honest *and* kind — it stays true to my value
   without putting my friend down.
4. **Rehearse a response (15 min).** With a partner, pick one pressure (stress,
   distraction, or peer pressure) and act out pause–name–choose for a small, safe scene.
   Take turns. Then on your page, write the full response for a pressure that is real in
   your life. Check with your partner: does your choice stay true to *your* value — and is
   it also fair to the other person?
5. **Reflect on your goal (10 min).** Open your Lesson 9 goal page. Ask your reflection
   question honestly: what worked? what got in the way? what would you change next time?
   Write two sentences. If you met your goal, notice what helped; if not, that is
   *information*, not a verdict — adjust and try again.
6. **Close (5 min).** You are many parts, one whole self — and under pressure, you have an
   anchor. Pause, name what presses, and choose what stays true. That is how a growing
   person grows, and keeps growing.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide three picture cards (a test, a buzzing phone, a friend urging) and
  sentence starters ("I pause, I notice ___, my value is ___, so I choose ___"); scribe as
  needed.
- **Extension:** Weigh two pressures at once (stress *and* a friend urging) and write a
  response that holds your value under both.

## Assessment

- **Formative (performance):** In role-play, can the learner name a pressure, a value, and
  a chosen response that is true to their value *and* fair to the other person?
- **Self-check:** The learner rereads their pause–name–choose page and their goal
  reflection, and checks that the choice really reflects *their* value, and that the goal
  reflection is honest (not self-punishing).
- **Portfolio:** The pause–name–choose page and the completed goal reflection, kept as
  unit artifacts.

## Home connection

When a pressure shows up at home this week — a test, a screen, a friend — try pause, name,
choose, and notice how the day goes. Share what you chose with someone you trust.
