# Lesson 6 — Writing and Solving One-Step Equations

## Summary

Learners write a **one-step equation** for a real situation (a mystery number plus 3 equals 8 →
**x + 3 = 8**) and solve it by using the **inverse operation** on *both* sides, keeping the equation
**balanced**. They check every solution by substitution and connect the balance scale to the algebra.

## Objectives

- Write a one-step equation for a real situation and solve it using an inverse operation, then check the
  solution. _(D05.S2.06.01)_

## Connection

A vendor counts the day's take: some coins are already in the jar, and after adding 3 more, there are 8
coins. How many were there to begin with? The unknown is a number you can name *x*, and the story becomes
an **equation**: **x + 3 = 8**. To find *x* you *undo* the "add 3" by subtracting 3 — from *both* sides,
so nothing goes out of balance — and you find **x = 5**. Undoing to find an unknown is how shopkeepers,
cooks, builders, and scientists reason backward from a result to its cause, all over the world.

## Materials

- Balance-scale cards
- Equation recording page
- Small balance or pan scale (where available)

## Preparation

- Copy balance-scale cards for groups and the equation recording page for each learner.
- Have worked examples ready: x + 3 = 8 → x = 5 (subtract 3 from both sides); 5x = 20 → x = 4.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The ideas to land: (1) an **equation** says two things are
equal — like a balanced scale; (2) to **solve**, use the **inverse operation** (subtract to undo adding,
divide to undo multiplying) *on both sides* so the equation stays true; and (3) always **check** by
substituting the answer back. Because solving equations is a foundational skill, use explicit instruction
and worked examples before independent work (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006): model x + 3 = 8 step by
step, then guide 5x = 20 together. Two common slips: doing the operation to only one side, and picking the
wrong inverse (adding when you should subtract). Retrieval: ask learners to recall expressions from last
lesson (4t + 3) — an equation is two expressions with an equals sign between them, and solving finds the
value that makes them equal. The balance image is not a gimmick; it is the *definition* of equality. Note
the global and historical point: the word "algebra" comes from *al-jabr* in the title of al-Khwarizmi's
9th-century book, and the method of balancing both sides is a human achievement shared across cultures.
The critical-thinking lens: checking your answer rather than trusting a guess is honest reasoning
(`docs/philosophy.md` §7).

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Last time you wrote an expression — a recipe with a variable. Today you set two
   sides *equal* and find the mystery number that makes them match.
2. **Meet the equation (10 min).** An **equation** is a balance: the left side equals the right side.
   "A number plus 3 equals 8" is **x + 3 = 8**. Whatever you do to one side, you must do to the other, or
   the balance tips.
3. **Worked example — subtract (10 min).** Solve **x + 3 = 8**. Undo the "+ 3" by subtracting 3. Do it to
   *both* sides: **x + 3 − 3 = 8 − 3**, so **x = 5**. Check: 5 + 3 = 8 ✓. The answer is 5 coins in the jar
   to begin with.
4. **Worked example — divide (10 min).** Solve **5x = 20** ("5 times a number is 20"). Undo "times 5" by
   dividing by 5 on both sides: **5x ÷ 5 = 20 ÷ 5**, so **x = 4**. Check: 5 × 4 = 20 ✓.
5. **Practice with a partner (10 min).** Take your balance-scale cards: write each balance as an equation,
   solve it with the inverse operation on both sides, and check by substituting. Trade and check each other.
6. **Close (5 min).** An equation is a balance; solving it is undoing, on both sides. Undo add with
   subtract, undo multiply with divide — and always check.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use only addition and subtraction equations (x + 2 = 7) with a real balance to act them out;
  describe each step aloud so learners with low vision can follow by listening.
- **Extension:** Solve multiplication and division equations (5x = 20, x ÷ 3 = 6) and write a one-step
  equation for a real situation of the learner's own.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation/self):** Can the learner write a one-step equation from a situation and solve it
  with the correct inverse operation on both sides?
- **Self-check:** The learner asks, "Did I do the same operation to both sides? Did I use the right inverse,
  and did my answer check when I substituted it back?"

## Home connection

Invent a mystery-number story at home (some coins, add or take a few, arrive at a total) and write and
solve the one-step equation for it.
