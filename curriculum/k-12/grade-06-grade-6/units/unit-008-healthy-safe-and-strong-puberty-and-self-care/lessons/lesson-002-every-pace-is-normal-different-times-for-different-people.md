# Lesson 2 — Every Pace Is Normal: Different Times for Different People

## Summary

Learners turn from *what* the changes are to *when* they happen: puberty begins at
**different times for different people**, and **every pace is normal**. They meet a
**worked example** of the full range, **retrieve** the changes from Lesson 1, and write a
short, kind reflection about honoring their own pace and everyone else's — **never
comparing or ranking bodies**.

## Objectives

- Describe the basic changes of puberty and that they happen at different times for
  different people. _(D03.S1.06.01)_

## Connection

Think of a field of the same crop, planted on the same day. Some shoots come up first,
tall and green; others come up a week later; a few come up last of all. In the end the
whole field stands — the early and the late together. Puberty is like that field. Two
friends born in the same month can change at very different times: one may grow taller at
ten, the other not until fourteen. Neither is ahead and neither is behind, because there
is no race. Every body keeps its own clock, and every pace is normal.

## Materials

- Every pace is normal poster
- Pace reflection page
- Pencils

## Preparation

- Print or draw the "every pace is normal" poster (several same-age figures at different
  heights and stages, each with a different clock, and the caption "same age, different
  pace — and every pace is normal").
- Copy a pace reflection page for each learner.
- Recall Lesson 1: the basic changes of puberty (growth spurt, body hair, skin, voice,
  and reproductive changes).

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The one idea to land is that **timing
varies and every pace is normal** — and that **comparing or ranking bodies is both false
and unkind**. This is a tender subject at ages 11–14, when self-consciousness spikes, so
guard against any sorting, ranking, or "who is ahead" framing; never ask learners to
reveal or compare their own development. Use the **field of crops** image and the
**worked example** of a wide range to make "normal" concrete. Watch for teasing and name
it directly: making fun of someone's pace is unkind, and it is *wrong about the facts*,
because every pace is healthy. If a learner is worried about being early or late, respond
with calm reassurance and point them to a trusted adult if the worry feels big. See
`docs/facilitation.md` and `docs/development.md` (the 6–8 band on self-consciousness).

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Last lesson you learned *what* puberty changes are. Today you
   learn about *when* they happen — and the short answer is: at a different time for
   everyone.
2. **Retrieve (5 min).** Without looking back, name the changes you remember from last
   time: the growth spurt, body hair, oilier skin, the voice, and the reproductive
   changes. Say them to a partner or to yourself.
3. **Meet the range (10 min).** Look at the poster — a **worked example**. Four people,
   all the same age. One is still shorter, with no body hair yet. Another is tall. Another
   notices their voice changing. Another notices their skin changing. Each one has a
   different clock, but **all four are normal**. Puberty usually begins somewhere between
   about 8 and 15, so a room of same-age friends is *supposed* to look different.
4. **Why timing differs (5 min).** Why the differences? Many things shape the timing —
   family patterns, how the body is nourished, and other factors we are still learning
   about. It is not a race, and no pace is better than another.
5. **Write your reflection (15 min).** On your page, finish the sentences in your own
   words: "One change I have noticed in myself or in others is…" and "A kind and true
   thing to remember is…". Keep it kind and true: you are describing a normal, shared
   human change, not ranking anyone.
6. **Close (5 min).** Remember the field: the early shoots and the late shoots all stand.
   Your body is on its own schedule, and every pace — including yours — is normal, and
   worthy of respect.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer a sentence bank and picture prompts; scribe as needed; allow one
  drawing and one word instead of full sentences.
- **Extension:** Name several factors that can shape timing (family patterns, nutrition,
  and others) and write one sentence explaining why ranking bodies is both factually
  wrong and unkind.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner explain, in their own words, that puberty
  happens at different times for different people and that every pace is normal?
- **Self-check:** The learner reads their reflection and asks, "Is it kind? Is it true?
  Does it honor every pace, including my own?"

## Home connection

Ask a trusted adult at home: "When you were growing up, did you and your friends change
at the same time?" and share the idea that every pace is normal.

## Resources

- That puberty begins across a wide age range (most often between about 8 and 15) and
  unfolds at a different pace for each person is standard adolescent health science, as
  summarized in the World Health Organization's adolescent health and development
  resources (<https://www.who.int/health-topics/adolescent-health>). The lesson holds
  "every pace is normal" as both a fact (timing varies across the healthy range) and a
  value (bodies are never to be ranked). The reflection practice draws on the CASEL
  self-awareness competency: <https://casel.org/fundamentals-of-sel/>.
