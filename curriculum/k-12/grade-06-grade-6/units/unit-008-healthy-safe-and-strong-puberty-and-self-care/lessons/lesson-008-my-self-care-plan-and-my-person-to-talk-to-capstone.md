# Lesson 8 — My Self-Care Plan and My Person to Talk To (Capstone)

## Summary

The unit capstone brings self-care home: learners name **one trusted person to talk to**
and build a **one-page self-care plan** that also **reviews the whole unit** — the changes
of puberty and that **every pace is normal**, **one change to fuel the body**, the **food
journey and its effects**, and **safer choices in play, travel, and online life**. This is
**retrieval and synthesis**: the unit's learning, carried forward into a growing, caring
life.

## Objectives

- Identify signs of stress or distress in themselves and name one self-care action and one
  person to talk to. _(D03.S4.06.02)_
- Describe the basic changes of puberty and that they happen at different times for
  different people. _(D03.S1.06.01)_
- Analyze a typical day of food and drink and identify one change that would better fuel
  the body. _(D03.S3.06.01)_
- Explain how food is produced and transported and how this affects people and the
  environment. _(D03.S3.06.02)_
- Assess everyday risks in play, travel, and online life and choose safer actions.
  _(D03.S4.06.01)_

## Connection

A traveler does not walk a long road without a small map and a friend to call when the way
is hard. This unit has been building *your* map and *your* friend. The map is your
**self-care plan**: one **trusted person** to talk to, and one **self-care action** to
take when you notice stress. The friend is the plan itself — something you can carry with
you, out of this classroom and into your life. You do not need the plan to be perfect; you
only need to know it is **yours**, and where to reach for it.

## Materials

- My self-care plan poster
- My self-care plan page
- Earlier pages (Lessons 1–7)

## Preparation

- Print or draw the "my self-care plan" poster (three sections: my trusted person, my
  self-care action, and what I know now).
- Copy a "my self-care plan" page for each learner.
- Have learners gather their own earlier pages from Lessons 1–7.
- Arrange a quiet space for individual writing and a circle for sharing.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It has two jobs: (1) land the **trusted
person + self-care action** (D03.S4.06.02) and (2) **review the whole unit** into one
portfolio artifact. Treat the trusted person with care: it may be a parent, grandparent,
aunt or uncle, teacher, coach, health worker, or neighbor — whoever the learner genuinely
trusts and can reach. Normalize that **telling is strength, not weakness**, and reassure
learners that if a first trusted person does not help, they can tell a second. This is
**summative retrieval** — the learner recalls each idea rather than re-reading; if someone
struggles, point them to their *own* earlier pages, not the answer. Keep the review
**dignified and joyful**: no two plans will match, and that is the point — every body and
every life is honored. See `docs/facilitation.md` and the retrieval-practice evidence in
`docs/contexts.md`.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** You have traveled the whole unit: the changes of puberty and every
   pace being normal; a day of food and one change to fuel you; the journey food makes and
   its effects; safer choices in play, travel, and online life; and the signs of stress.
   Today you tie it all together into a **self-care plan** you can carry forward.
2. **Meet the plan (5 min).** Look at the poster — a **worked example** of the one-page
   plan, with three sections: **① my trusted person** (someone I can talk to); **② my
   self-care action** (what I do when I notice stress); **③ what I know now** (the unit's
   big ideas). Your plan will be your own.
3. **Build your plan (30 min).** Fill each section, *recalling* before you check your
   notes:
   - **My trusted person:** name one person you can talk to when something feels big or
     wrong. Write who they are and why you trust them.
   - **My self-care action:** name the action you chose last lesson, and add one sentence
     about *when* you would use it.
   - **What I know now:** write or draw one thing from each part of the unit — a puberty
     change and that every pace is normal; one change that fuels your body; one step of
     the food journey and one effect on people or the environment; and one safer choice in
     play, travel, or online life.
4. **Share one section (10 min).** In a small circle, each person shares **one** section
   and listens to the others. Notice how different — and how complete — the plans are.
5. **Close (5 min).** Healthy, safe, and strong. You know your growing body, you know how
   to fuel it, you know the journey behind your food, you know how to choose safely, and
   you know a person to talk to and an action that helps. That is a self in good care —
   carry it with you.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a partially filled plan with picture prompts and a word bank for
  each section; scribe as needed; allow one picture per section.
- **Extension:** Connect the five big ideas in one paragraph about how knowing, fueling,
  choosing, and caring work together — and name one new situation where your plan will
  help.

## Assessment

- **Summative (portfolio):** Does the plan name a trusted person and a self-care action,
  and does the review cover the unit's five big ideas (puberty and pace, one fuel change,
  the food journey, a safer choice, and stress signs) in the learner's own words or
  drawings?
- **Self-check:** The learner reads the plan back and asks, "If stress or something wrong
  arrives tonight, would I know who to tell and what to do — and could I teach one part of
  this to a friend?"

## Home connection

Show your self-care plan to a trusted adult at home, and tell them why you chose them —
and share one thing you now know about your growing body, your food, or your safety.

## Resources

- Naming a trusted adult to talk to is both a self-care skill and a child's right to
  protection under the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child, Article 19
  (<https://www.ohchr.org/en/instruments-mechanisms/instruments/convention-rights-child>).
  Gathering the unit into one plan is **retrieval practice**, which strengthens long-term
  memory (see the testing-effect literature and Dunlosky et al., *Improving Students'
  Learning With Effective Learning Techniques*, <https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266>),
  and supports the synthesis and identity work of ages 11–14. For SEL framing of
  self-awareness and self-management, see CASEL: <https://casel.org/fundamentals-of-sel/>.
