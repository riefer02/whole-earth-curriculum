# Lesson 6 — Biodiversity: Why Variety Keeps Ecosystems Strong

## Summary

Learners explain what **biodiversity** means and why **variety** helps an ecosystem stay
**healthy**. A worked example contrasts a **garden with many kinds** of life against a **field
with one crop**: when one kind fails in the many-kinds garden, others still grow, but when a
single crop is hit by disease, everything fails at once. Learners then trace the same
safety-net idea through a **coral reef, a forest, and a farm field**, seeing variety as
**resilience** — not decoration.

## Objectives

- Explain what biodiversity means and why a variety of living things can help an ecosystem
  stay healthy. _(D10.S3.06.02)_

## Connection

Imagine a rope made of many strands. Cut one strand and the rope still holds. Now imagine a
rope of a single strand — cut it and it snaps. An ecosystem with many kinds of living things is
the many-stranded rope: if one kind is harmed, the others still hold the system up. That
variety of life is called **biodiversity**, and it is a living thing's safety net.

## Materials

- Variety safety-net poster
- Biodiversity cards
- Biodiversity page
- Pencils

## Preparation

- Print or draw the variety safety-net poster.
- Copy biodiversity cards for each pair and a biodiversity page for each learner.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land is the **safety-net metaphor**:
more kinds of life means more ways for an ecosystem to survive a shock. Model the garden-vs-
field contrast fully as a worked example. Build on the previous lesson's communities and on
Grade 5's interdependence. Present variety factually and hopefully: biodiversity is strength,
and people everywhere — farmers who mix crops, communities who protect reefs and forests — are
already building that strength. Distinguish evidence from values: *that variety aids
resilience* is observable ecology; *that we should protect the variety of life* is a value this
curriculum holds. See `docs/philosophy.md` §4–5 and `docs/facilitation.md`.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Last lesson: populations and communities. Today, a quality of the whole
   community — *how many kinds* of life it holds — and why that number matters.
2. **Meet the two gardens (12 min).** Look at the poster. In the **many-kinds garden** —
   flowers, grass, a bush, a tree, a bee, a bird, a worm — if one plant fails, others still
   grow, and food and homes remain for many creatures. In the **one-kind field** — a single
   crop repeated — if a disease hits it, everything fails at once, and few creatures find food.
   That is **biodiversity**: the variety of living things, and the reason variety is a safety
   net.
3. **Trace your own safety net (18 min).** Take a card. The **coral reef** (many kinds: coral,
   fish, crabs, algae, turtles; if one kind of coral suffers, other corals and algae still
   shelter the fish — but a reef with only one coral can collapse at once). The **forest**
   (many kinds: tall trees, shrubs, birds, fungi, insects; if one tree species is diseased,
   others still shade and shelter — but a single-species plantation falls together). The
   **farm field** (many kinds: mixed crops, hedges, bees, birds, soil worms; if one crop
   fails, others still feed the family and the soil — but one crop alone is one point of
   failure). On your page, name the ecosystem, list its variety, and write one sentence on why
   that variety is a safety net.
4. **Check with a partner (10 min).** Say: "variety helps ___ because if one kind ___, the
   others still ___." Ask: did your partner show *why* variety, not just *that* variety?
5. **Close (5 min).** Variety is not extra; it is the system's strength. The more strands in
   the rope, the harder it is to break.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a card with the many kinds already listed and a sentence starter for the
  safety-net reason; work one card aloud first.
- **Extension:** Compare two real ecosystems — one rich and one poor in variety — and argue,
  with reasons, which could better survive a shock such as a drought or a disease.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner explain biodiversity and give the safety-net
  reason variety helps an ecosystem stay healthy?
- **Peer check:** Partners ask each other, "Did you say *why* variety helps — what happens when
  one kind fails?"

## Home connection

Find one patch of life near home. Count its different kinds, then ask: if one kind disappeared,
what would still hold?

## Resources

- Biodiversity — the variety of living things — and its link to ecosystem resilience are core
  ecology concepts documented by the UN Convention on Biological Diversity and in children's
  references (BBC Bitesize, Britannica Kids, National Geographic Kids); the single-crop risk
  (e.g., the Irish potato famine and crop-disease risk) is a standard, age-appropriate example.
  The safety-net metaphor and hopeful framing follow `docs/facilitation.md` and
  `docs/philosophy.md` §12.
