# Lesson 4 — Loaded Words and Emotional Images

## Summary

Learners identify a **persuasive technique** — a **loaded word** and an **emotional image** —
and explain how each tries to shape a view. A worked example dissects one drink advertisement:
the word **"PURE!"** makes the drink feel clean and safe, and the image of a **happy, healthy
child** makes you feel good about buying it. Learners then spot the loaded word and emotional
image in a **snack ad, a candidate poster, and a toy ad**, reading persuasion with awareness
rather than fear.

## Objectives

- Identify a persuasive technique, such as a loaded word or an emotional image, and explain how
  it tries to shape a view. _(D10.S2.06.02)_

## Connection

Have you ever wanted something the moment you saw it — before you even thought about whether it
was good or worth the price? Often that wanting is not your own idea; a picture or a single
word planted it. A loaded word makes you feel a thing is clean, strong, or safe; an emotional
image makes you feel happy, proud, or included. Today you learn to notice those feelings — and
to ask where they came from.

## Materials

- Persuasion-spotter poster
- Persuasion-spotter cards
- Persuasion-spotter page
- Pencils

## Preparation

- Print or draw the persuasion-spotter poster.
- Copy persuasion-spotter cards for each pair and a persuasion-spotter page for each learner.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The skill to land is the **persuasion spot**:
name the loaded word, name the emotional image, and say how each tries to shape a view. Model
the drink ad fully as a worked example. **Teach awareness, not fear or cynicism:** persuasion
is everywhere, some of it honest and useful; the goal is to *feel* the pull and still think
clearly — to be moved but not tricked. Keep all examples age-safe (snacks, toys, a school
candidate) and free of frightening content. Distinguish evidence from values: *which words and
images carry feeling* is observable; *that we should notice how we are persuaded* is a value
this curriculum holds. See `docs/philosophy.md` §5, §7 and `docs/facilitation.md`.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Last lesson: news, opinion, and ad. Today, a closer look at how an ad —
   or any message — tries to shape your view, using two tools: loaded words and emotional
   images.
2. **Meet the drink ad (12 min).** Look at the poster. The word **"PURE!"** is a **loaded
   word** — it carries a feeling, making the drink seem clean and safe. The image of a
   **happy, healthy child** is an **emotional image** — it makes you feel good about the drink,
   as if buying it would bring that happiness. Neither proves the drink is good for you; both
   *make you feel* it is. Ask the question aloud: "what is it making me feel, and why?"
3. **Spot your own (18 min).** Take a card. The **snack ad** ("MEGA-CRUNCH! the snack that
   makes you a champion") — loaded word: "champion"; emotional image: an athlete grinning with
   the snack; it makes you feel strong and cool. The **candidate poster** ("Vote for Ama — she
   will SAVE our school!") — loaded word: "save"; emotional image: Ama smiling with students;
   it makes you feel safe and hopeful. The **toy ad** ("the toy every friend will BEG to
   share") — loaded word: "beg"; emotional image: laughing children crowded around one toy; it
   makes you feel left out if you do not have it. On your page, name the loaded word, the
   emotional image, and how each tries to shape the view.
4. **Check with a partner (10 min).** Say: "the word ___ makes me feel ___, and the image ___
   makes me feel ___." Ask: did your partner name the feeling *and* the technique that caused
   it?
5. **Close (5 min).** Persuasion is not magic; it is a craft of feelings. When you can name the
   word and the image at work, you can feel the pull — and still decide for yourself.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a card with the loaded word already underlined and a sentence starter
  for the feeling; work one ad aloud first.
- **Extension:** Rewrite an ad using neutral words and a plain image, and explain how the
  feeling — and the pull — changes.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name a loaded word and an emotional image and
  explain how each tries to shape a view?
- **Peer check:** Partners ask each other, "Did you name the word, the image, and the feeling
  each one is trying to create?"

## Home connection

Next ad you see, name its loaded word and its emotional image, and say what feeling each is
trying to give you.

## Resources

- Loaded words (words chosen for emotional effect) and emotional images (imagery chosen to
  stir feeling) are standard concepts in media-literacy education, taught by Common Sense
  Media and the Center for News Literacy; the "awareness, not fear" framing follows
  `docs/philosophy.md` §7. Advertising's use of emotional appeals is documented in
  age-appropriate references (Britannica Kids, Common Sense Media). On modeling before
  practice, see Kirschner, Sweller & Clark (2006),
  <https://doi.org/10.1207/s15326985ep4102_1>.
