# Lesson 8 — Identity Shapes Belonging: Culture, Language, Ability

## Summary

Learners describe how **identity — culture, language, and ability — shapes belonging**. They
treat each identity thread as **ordinary and welcome**, not exotic or deficient, and practice
naming **one ordinary way a group can honor each thread** so everyone is at home, producing a
**belonging map**.

## Objectives

- Describe how identity, such as culture, language, and ability, shapes belonging.
  _(D02.S3.06.02)_

## Connection

A new learner arrives. Her home language is not the classroom's, she eats foods the others
don't know, and she uses a wheelchair. Whether she feels she **belongs** depends less on who
she is than on how the group receives her — whether her language is treated as a *gap* or a
*gift*, her food as *strange* or *welcome*, her chair as *ordinary* or *different*. Identity
shapes belonging not because some identities fit and others don't, but because **welcome is a
choice we make**. Every person carries threads — a language, a way of being, a body — and in
every human community, from a mountain village to a port city, belonging is built when those
threads are honored as ordinary.

## Materials

- Identity-and-belonging poster
- Identity cards
- Belonging map page

## Preparation

- Print or draw the identity-and-belonging poster (one circle, three identity threads, "all
  belong, and it is ordinary").
- Copy identity cards per pair and a belonging map page per learner.
- Arrange pairs; optionally prepare found objects for the low-tech variant.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land is that **identity is never
the problem — reception is**. Culture, language, and ability shape belonging because of how
groups treat them, not because some are "normal" and others "special." Guard the words
carefully: a home language is a *gift*, never a *gap*; a wheelchair or a hearing aid is
*ordinary*, never a *sad story*; a food is *welcome*, never *strange*. This is the
anti-deficit heart of the lesson and of the curriculum's egalitarian lens. Keep examples
global and varied — no single culture or region as the default "normal." If a learner
discloses their own identity, receive it warmly and never rank it. See `docs/facilitation.md`.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of one thread of who you are — a language, a food, a way your
   body works, a tradition. Today you explore how **identity shapes belonging**.
2. **Meet the circle (10 min).** Look at the poster. One circle holds four people, each with
   a thread: a drum and shared food (**culture**), two scripts in one bubble (**language**), a
   wheelchair and a hearing aid (**ability**). All are *inside* — because belonging is not
   about matching; it is about **being received**. Worked example: a learner whose home
   language differs. Treated as a gap: "she can't keep up." Treated as ordinary: the class
   learns to greet in her language too. Same learner, two very different belongings.
3. **Meet the threads (10 min).** Read the identity cards. For each — a home language, a
   festival, a way of moving or hearing — ask the card's question: "**what would make this
   person feel at home?**" Name one ordinary, doable thing (learn one word, share one food,
   make one space that fits).
4. **Map a belonging (15 min).** On your page, choose one identity thread — your own, a story
   character's, or one from the cards. Map it: what is the thread, how could it shape
   belonging (for better or worse), and one **ordinary** thing a group could do to honor it.
   Draw or write. Share one part with a partner.
5. **Close (5 min).** Nobody is "hard to include"; every person is *easy to welcome* once we
   treat their threads as ordinary gifts. Belonging is built by the small, daily choice to
   honor who people already are.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide one identity thread with the honoring move already written, and ask
  the learner to draw or act out the welcome.
- **Extension:** Analyze how the *same* trait (a home language, a way of moving) can be
  treated as ordinary in one place and as "different" in another, and write one sentence on
  why welcome is always a choice.

## Assessment

- **Formative (self/portfolio):** Can the learner name an identity thread, explain how it can
  shape belonging, and give one ordinary, honoring move — without framing the identity as a
  deficit?
- **Self-check:** The learner asks, "Did I treat the identity as ordinary and welcome, not as
  a problem to fix? Is my honoring move real and doable, not grand or pitying?"

## Home connection

Notice one identity thread someone around you carries — a language, a food, a way of moving —
and do one ordinary thing to honor it this week.

## Resources

- Belonging is shaped by how identity is received, and inclusive settings treat difference
  as ordinary rather than deficient (a core principle of inclusive education; UNESCO guidance
  on inclusion). The "gift, not gap" framing is a values commitment of this curriculum. For
  classroom SEL framing: <https://casel.org/fundamentals-of-sel/>
