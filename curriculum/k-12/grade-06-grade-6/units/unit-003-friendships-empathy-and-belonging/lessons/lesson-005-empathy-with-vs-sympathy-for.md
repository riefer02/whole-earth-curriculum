# Lesson 5 — Empathy (With) vs Sympathy (For)

## Summary

Learners distinguish **empathy — feeling *with* someone, shoulder to shoulder** — from
**sympathy — feeling *for* someone, from above and apart**. They sort real responses into the
two and write an **empathy note**, learning that "with" is felt as being *joined*, while
"for" can feel like being *pitied from a distance*.

## Objectives

- Distinguish empathy (feeling with someone) from sympathy (feeling for someone).
  _(D02.S2.06.01)_

## Connection

Your friend's grandparent is very ill. One person says, "Oh, you poor thing, I feel so sorry
for you" — and somehow your friend feels *smaller*. Another sits down beside them and says,
"That must be so scary — I'm right here." That second one is **empathy**: it stands *with*
the person, at their level, not *above* them. Sympathy reaches *down*; empathy reaches
*across*. The difference is felt in the body — and it matters in every place people comfort
one another, a hospital ward in one city or a neighbour's doorstep in another.

## Materials

- With vs for poster
- With-or-for sorting cards
- Empathy note page

## Preparation

- Print or draw the with-vs-for poster (beside vs above, with sample lines).
- Copy sorting cards per pair and an empathy note page per learner.
- Arrange pairs and space to stand in the two positions.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land is a *felt* distinction, not
a vocabulary quiz: **empathy is "with," sympathy is "for"** — one joins, the other can rank.
The egalitarian lens is central here: sympathy, even when kind, can quietly place the helper
*above* the helped, while empathy places them *beside*. Be gentle — sympathy is not wrong or
unkind; it is simply a different (and sometimes distancing) stance, and everyone uses it.
Use invented scenes or story characters, never real classmates in distress. The body carries
the meaning, so have learners physically try both positions. See `docs/facilitation.md` on
holding feelings safely and `docs/contexts.md`.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of a time someone comforted you. Did you feel *joined* or
   *looked down on* — even a little? Today you learn the difference between **empathy** and
   **sympathy**.
2. **Meet "with" and "for" (10 min).** Look at the poster. **Sympathy** stands *above*:
   "Oh, you poor thing" — feeling *for* someone, from a distance, a little above them.
   **Empathy** sits *beside*: "That must be so hard — I'm right here" — feeling *with*
   someone, at their level. Same caring heart; different body, different message.
3. **Feel the positions (10 min).** With a partner, physically try both. Stand apart and a
   little above, say "I feel sorry for you." Then sit side by side, shoulders level, say "I'm
   right here with you." Which one would you rather receive when you are hurting? Why?
4. **Sort with and for (10 min).** Take the sorting cards and place each response as
   **empathy** or **sympathy**: "I'm sorry that happened to you," "That must be so hard — I'm
   right here," "Oh, you poor thing," "I can't imagine — but I'm here." For each, ask: is this
   speaker *beside* the person or *above* them?
5. **Write an empathy note (10 min).** On your page, choose a scene (a friend failed a test,
   a neighbour lost a pet, a teammate made a mistake that cost the match). Write one
   **sympathy** line and one **empathy** line, then one sentence on what each would feel like
   to receive. Close by sharing your empathy line with a partner.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a scene with a sympathy line and an empathy line already written, and
  ask the learner to circle which is "beside" and which is "above."
- **Extension:** Explain *why* sympathy can feel distant or pitying, and write one rule for
  keeping your comfort "with" rather than "for" (e.g., sit down, don't stand over; say "I'm
  here," not "poor you").

## Assessment

- **Formative (self/portfolio):** Can the learner sort responses as empathy or sympathy and
  explain the "with vs for" difference in their own words, using body position?
- **Self-check:** The learner asks, "Is my comforting *beside* the person or *above* them —
  and would I want to receive what I am offering?"

## Home connection

Next time someone at home is sad or worried, try sitting *with* them — literally at their
level — and notice what you say and how it lands.

## Resources

- The empathy–sympathy distinction follows from empathy research (Batson's work on
  empathic concern vs personal distress; Decety & Cowell on empathic responding). The
  "with vs for" framing is a teaching simplification of that literature. For classroom SEL
  framing: <https://casel.org/fundamentals-of-sel/>
