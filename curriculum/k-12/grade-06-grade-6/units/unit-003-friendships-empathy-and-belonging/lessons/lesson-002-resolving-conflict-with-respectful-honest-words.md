# Lesson 2 — Resolving a Conflict with Respectful, Honest Words

## Summary

Learners turn the unit's first skill toward conflict: they learn a **four-step repair** —
**pause, say it with an honest I-statement, listen, and agree on a next step** — and rehearse
it on small, invented conflicts. **Respect** and **honesty** are framed as two hands of the
same move, not opposites.

## Objectives

- Resolve a conflict using respectful, honest communication. _(D02.S1.06.01)_

## Connection

A friend borrowed your pencil and returned it broken. Your first impulse might be to say
nothing (and seethe) or to snap, "You always wreck my stuff!" Both leave the conflict
unresolved. A third way exists: **pause**, say what *you* felt without blame — "I felt upset
when my pencil came back broken" — **listen** to what happened, and **agree on a next step**.
Honesty is saying the true thing; respect is saying it in a way the other person can hear.
Both together are what actually *repair* a friendship, in a classroom or a courtyard or a
kitchen anywhere in the world.

## Materials

- Four-step repair poster
- Conflict scenario cards
- Repair record card

## Preparation

- Print or draw the four-step repair poster (Pause, Say, Listen, Agree) with a worked example.
- Copy conflict scenario cards and a repair record card for each pair.
- Arrange pairs for role-play.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land is that **respect and honesty
are partners, not enemies** — you can say the true thing *and* say it so the other person can
hear it. Use **invented scenarios only**, never real classmates, so no learner is put on trial
in front of peers. Model the worked example fully before pairs begin; this is a skill
(Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006). Watch for the two slips: suppressing the honest thing
(which turns respect into silence) and blurting blame (which turns honesty into attack). The
I-statement is the bridge. Note that "conflict" here is ordinary disagreement, not harm or
bullying — if a learner raises something bigger, hold it privately and connect them to a
trusted adult. See `docs/facilitation.md` on holding difficult topics safely.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of a small conflict you have had — a broken thing, a missed
   plan, a sharp word. Today you learn a **four-step repair** that is both honest and kind.
2. **Meet the four steps (10 min).** Look at the poster. **Pause** (breathe; don't fire back).
   **Say it** with an I-statement: "I felt ___ when ___." **Listen** (repeat back what you
   heard). **Agree on a next step.** Worked example: "I felt upset when my pencil came back
   broken." Listen: "You didn't know it was fragile." Agree: "Next time I'll tell you it's
   fragile, and you'll ask before borrowing."
3. **Learn the I-statement (5 min).** Practice the frame aloud: "**I felt ___ when ___.**"
   Notice what it does *not* do — it names your feeling and the event, without "you always"
   or "you never." That is honesty with the blame left out.
4. **Rehearse a repair (15 min).** With a partner, take a scenario card. Walk the four steps
   together — Pause, Say, Listen, Agree. Record the I-statement, what each of you heard, and
   the next step you agreed. Swap and do a second scenario.
5. **Close (5 min).** Conflict is ordinary; *unresolved* conflict is what corrodes a
   friendship. Four steps — pause, say it honestly, listen, agree — turn a fight into a
   repair. You can be the one who begins.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a scripted four-step exchange to read through, and a fill-in
  I-statement frame with a feeling word bank.
- **Extension:** Rewrite two blame-statements ("you always…", "you never…") into honest
  I-statements, and explain why the rewrite invites repair instead of defense.

## Assessment

- **Formative (performance/peer):** Can the learner state their feeling with an I-statement,
  listen by reflecting back, and agree on a fair next step — without blame or silence?
- **Self-check:** The learner asks, "Was I honest *and* respectful? Did I name my feeling
  without blaming, listen, and end with a next step both of us could accept?"

## Home connection

Next time a small conflict comes up at home, try the four steps before anything else: pause,
say "I felt ___ when ___," listen, and agree on a next step.
