# Lesson 10 — Earth's Place in the Solar System

## Summary

Learners locate **Earth in the solar system** — the third planet from the Sun, one of
eight planets, each orbiting the Sun, many with **moons** of their own. They order the
planets by distance and grapple with **scale**: the Sun is vastly bigger than Earth,
and the distances between worlds are so large that no single drawing can show size and
distance to scale at once.

## Objectives

- Describe Earth's place in the solar system and the scale of planets, moons, and the
  Sun. _(D06.S4.06.01)_

## Connection

Look up at the night sky: the bright dots are other worlds. Our home, Earth, is one
planet circling a star — the Sun — along with seven others, and many of those worlds
carry moons. People have watched these wanderers from deserts, mountains, islands,
and plains for thousands of years: Polynesian wayfinders read the stars to cross the
open ocean, and Aboriginal star-lore in Australia encodes seasons and food in the sky
— knowledge held beside the astronomy you learn today, not beneath it. Today you learn
where Earth sits in that great family and just how small we are in it.

## Materials

- Solar-system diagram
- Planet cards
- Scale-model page

## Preparation

- Print or draw the solar-system diagram, planet cards, and scale-model page.
- Recall Grade 5: the Earth–Moon–Sun system, day length, moon phases, and eclipses.
- Have a big and a tiny object (a beach ball and a marble, or a big stone and a rice
  grain) ready for the scale feel.

## Facilitator note

Written to the learner ("you"). Two ideas to land: (1) **Earth is the third planet
from the Sun**, one of eight, each with an orbit, and most planets have moons; (2)
**scale** — the Sun is enormously bigger than the planets (about 100 times wider than
Earth), and distances are vast. The honest teaching move is to be explicit that no
single page can show size and distance to true scale at once — the diagram shows
*order*, while the inset shows *relative size*; a distance model needs a long space.
The Sun is a **star**, and stars are far bigger than planets. The fact that Earth is a
planet orbiting a star is evidence-based science (observation, measurement, and
spacecraft); the sense of wonder at our smallness can seed a global reflection, but
hold it as a value, not a science claim. Keep "how do we know" alive: we know the
distances by measurement, not by guess. See
[`docs/facilitation.md`](../../../../../../docs/facilitation.md).

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Name everything you can see in the night sky. Which of those
   is a planet, and which is a star? What do you already know about where Earth fits?
2. **Meet the solar system (10 min).** Look at the diagram. The **solar system** is
   the Sun and everything that orbits it. The **Sun** is a **star**, huge and bright.
   Eight **planets** orbit it in order: Mercury, Venus, **Earth**, Mars, Jupiter,
   Saturn, Uranus, Neptune. Earth is the **third planet**. Many planets have
   **moons** that orbit them. Point to Earth and name its neighbors.
3. **Order the planets (10 min).** With your cards, line up the Sun and the eight
   planets in order of distance. Which are the four **inner planets** (small and
   rocky, close to the Sun)? Which are the **outer planets** (huge and far)? Where
   does Earth sit on the line?
4. **Feel the scale (15 min).** Look at the size inset: the Sun is about 100 times
   wider than Earth. Hold a big object (the Sun) and a tiny one (Earth) side by side.
   On your scale-model page, record: if the Sun were this big, Earth would be this
   small — and the distance between them would be far larger than this room. Why can't
   one drawing show size and distance to scale at once?
5. **Close (5 min).** Earth is one world among eight, circling one star among
   billions. Knowing our place — small and precious — is the beginning of seeing the
   universe clearly.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Order pre-labeled planet cards by distance; focus on "Earth is third"
  and "the Sun is a star and much bigger."
- **Extension:** Compare the Sun's width to Earth's (about 100 to 1) and to Jupiter's,
  and explain why the outer planets are so different from the inner ones.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner place Earth as the third planet, order
  the eight planets, and state that the Sun is much larger than Earth and the
  distances are vast?
- **Self-check:** The learner asks, "Can I name Earth's place, order the planets, and
  say why a single drawing cannot show true scale?"

## Home connection

On a clear night (or with a star map), find a planet or the Moon. Tell someone where
Earth sits among the planets and how the Sun's size compares to ours.
