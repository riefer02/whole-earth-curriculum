# Lesson 7 — Forms of Energy and How Energy Changes

## Summary

Learners meet the five **forms of energy** — **kinetic** (motion), **potential**
(stored), **thermal** (heat), **chemical** (stored in matter), and **electrical**
(moving charges) — through everyday examples, and learn the key idea that energy can
**change from one form to another**. They sort examples, act out the forms, and hunt
for energy in the world around them.

## Objectives

- Describe the forms of energy (kinetic, potential, thermal, chemical, electrical) and
  how energy changes between them. _(D06.S3.06.01)_

## Connection

Energy is everywhere, wearing different clothes. The rolling ball is **kinetic**; the
stretched bow, ready to spring, is **potential**; the steaming bowl of soup is
**thermal**; the rice and fuel that power your day are **chemical**; the current that
lights a lamp is **electrical**. From a campfire to a charging phone to a river
turning a wheel, the same energy keeps changing its form. Today you learn to name all
five and watch them change.

## Materials

- Forms-of-energy diagram
- Energy form cards
- Energy detective page

## Preparation

- Print or draw the diagram, cards, and detective page.
- Recall Grade 4: energy moves from place to place (heat, sound, light) and is never
  destroyed; today we name its forms.
- Have a rubber band and a rolling object ready for the feel-it activity.

## Facilitator note

Written to the learner ("you"). Two ideas to land: (1) the **five forms** and a clear
example of each, and (2) **energy changes form** (transforms). Keep the forms concrete
and bodily: kinetic = moving, potential = stored by position or stretch, thermal =
heat, chemical = stored in food/fuel/batteries, electrical = moving charges in wires.
Model the worked example with the rubber band: stretched it is potential, released it
becomes kinetic, and (rub your hands) motion becomes thermal. The claim that energy
changes form without being destroyed (conservation) is introduced lightly here and
formalized in Grade 9 — keep the everyday meaning now. Global examples keep it
faithful to many lives: a river turning a water wheel, a charcoal stove, a solar
panel, a bicycle. Note technology's role honestly: the same energy that cooks a meal
also powers engines — a chance to name both benefit and care. See
[`docs/facilitation.md`](../../../../../../docs/facilitation.md).

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Where is energy in this room right now? Point to something
   moving, something warm, something plugged in. Write three guesses.
2. **Meet the five forms (10 min).** Look at the diagram. **Kinetic** energy is
   energy of motion. **Potential** energy is stored — a ball held high, a bow pulled
   back. **Thermal** energy is heat. **Chemical** energy is stored in food and fuel.
   **Electrical** energy moves through wires as moving charges. Point to each and say
   its example aloud.
3. **Feel it (10 min).** Stretch a rubber band (potential) and let it fly (kinetic).
   Rub your hands together fast — the motion becomes **thermal**. You just watched
   energy **change form**: potential → kinetic → thermal.
4. **Sort the cards (15 min).** With a partner, sort every card into one of the five
   forms: a rolling cart (kinetic), a raised bucket (potential), a hot stone
   (thermal), a candle or a bowl of rice (chemical), a lit bulb (electrical). Then
   for two cards, name a **change** — what form did it come from, and what did it
   become?
5. **Close (5 min).** Energy is one thing with five faces, always changing between
   them. Name a form, and you can start to see the changes happening everywhere.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use the five example objects in hand and a two-word label for each;
  focus on naming the forms before changes.
- **Extension:** Choose one object (a lamp, a bicycle, a cooking fire) and write a
  two-step chain of changes, naming each form in order.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name all five forms, match each to an
  example, and describe one energy change from one form to another?
- **Self-check:** The learner asks, "For any object, can I name its energy form now
  and one form it came from or will become?"

## Home connection

Be an energy detective at home: find one example of each of the five forms. Tell
someone what you found and one change you saw energy make.
