# Lesson 6 — Energy Flows, Matter Cycles Through an Ecosystem

## Summary

Learners trace two different journeys through an ecosystem. **Energy flows one way** —
from the Sun, into producers, through consumers, and out as heat, never to return —
while **matter cycles** — carbon and water are used, released, and used again. They
sort real processes into "flows" and "cycles" and draw both kinds of arrow in a local
ecosystem map.

## Objectives

- Describe how energy flows and matter (such as carbon and water) cycles through an
  ecosystem. _(D06.S2.06.02)_

## Connection

The water in your cup has been rain, a river, a cloud, and — long ago — part of
countless plants and animals. Water and carbon are the same atoms, borrowed again and
again: they **cycle**. But the energy that warmed you and grew your food came from the
Sun and will leave as heat and never come back the same way: it **flows**. One turns
in a circle; the other runs in a line. Today you learn to tell them apart in any
ecosystem, from a rice paddy to a coral reef to a forest.

## Materials

- Ecosystem energy-and-matter diagram
- Flow-or-cycle card set
- Ecosystem map page

## Preparation

- Print or draw the diagram, the card set, and a map page per learner.
- Recall Grade 5: matter and energy move through a food web; today we separate the two
  kinds of movement.
- Have string and tokens ready if using the body activity.

## Facilitator note

Written to the learner ("you"). The one distinction to land: **energy flows one way**
(Sun → producers → consumers → decomposers → heat out) while **matter cycles** (carbon
and water return and are reused). The classic mix-up is treating the two as the same,
so use the two-token activity: the energy token is passed down a line and leaves at
the end (never returned); the matter token goes around a circle and comes back. The
science is evidence-based (ecological measurement of energy loss as heat at each step,
and of carbon and water moving through reservoirs); the gentle value — "we are
borrowing matter that life has used forever" — can seed an environmental reflection,
but name it as a value, not a science claim. Keep "energy" in the everyday sense
introduced in Grade 5 (usable fuel); conservation of energy is formalized later.
See [`docs/facilitation.md`](../../../../../../docs/facilitation.md).

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Recall Grade 5: the arrow in a food web points from the eaten
   to the eater, showing where energy goes. Today's question: does *everything* move
   that way, or do some things come back around?
2. **Meet flow and cycle (10 min).** Look at the diagram. **Energy flows** one way:
   sunlight → plant → animal → decomposer → and out as **heat**. It does not loop
   back. **Matter cycles**: **carbon** and **water** move from air and soil into
   plants, into animals, and back again through breathing, decay, and evaporation.
   Say each one aloud: "energy flows through; matter cycles around."
3. **Sort flow or cycle (10 min).** With your card set, sort each process into one of
   two piles: **flows** (one-way — sunlight in, heat out, energy eaten) or **cycles**
   (comes back — water evaporated then raining, carbon dioxide breathed out then
   taken in by plants, nutrients returned to soil).
4. **Draw your ecosystem (15 min).** On your map page, draw a local ecosystem — a
   garden, a riverbank, a field. Draw a straight arrow for energy, pointing one way
   and leaving as heat. Draw a loop of arrows for carbon and for water, showing them
   returning. Label which is which.
5. **Close (5 min).** Every living place runs on two kinds of movement: energy
   running through it, and matter going around it. One is spent; the other is reused
   forever.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Sort cards into two labeled boxes first; use one-color arrows for
  energy and a drawn loop for matter.
- **Extension:** Follow a single water molecule from rain → root → leaf → animal →
  breath → air → cloud → rain, and explain where it "returns" and why it never runs
  out.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner state that energy flows one way and
  matter cycles, and correctly sort two example processes?
- **Self-check:** The learner asks, "For any arrow I draw, can I say whether it is
  energy leaving forever or matter coming back around?"

## Home connection

Trace one thing at a meal: where did the water in it come from and where will it go
(the cycle), and where did its energy come from and go (the flow)? Tell someone both
stories.
