# Lesson 1 — Use the Elements on Purpose: Line, Shape, Color, Balance, Contrast

## Summary

Learners meet the five building blocks of visual work — **line, shape, color, balance,
and contrast** — and learn to use them **deliberately**, not by accident. A worked example
builds a "calm" composition and a "storm" composition from the same five elements, then
each learner makes one deliberate piece to express a chosen idea or feeling and writes a
short **artist's statement** naming the choice and the reason.

## Objectives

- Use the elements and principles of art — line, shape, color, balance, and contrast —
  deliberately to express an idea or feeling. _(D07.S1.06.01)_

## Connection

Everywhere you look, someone made a choice about lines, shapes, color, balance, and
contrast — a food stall's sign with its big red circle and bold letters, a woven basket
with its even bands of light and dark, a game's start screen with its calm sky and a
sudden bright button. The makers didn't just throw marks at the page; they chose, on
purpose, what would make you feel a certain way. Today you become that maker: you pick a
feeling, then pick the lines, shapes, colors, balance, and contrast that carry it — the
way a storm cloud gathers dark sharp edges while a still pond holds long smooth ones.

## Materials

- Elements-and-principles poster
- Drawing paper and mark-makers
- Artist's-statement slip

## Preparation

- Print or draw the elements-and-principles poster, including the worked example (calm
  vs storm).
- Gather paper and mark-makers with at least a light and a dark value.
- Copy an artist's-statement slip for each learner.
- Recall Grade 5: learners planned and completed an artwork with a clear idea, choosing
  materials and techniques to fit the idea.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The one idea to land is **deliberateness**:
the elements are not decoration, they are *chosen* to express an idea or feeling, and a
learner can name the choice and its reason. Model the calm-versus-storm worked example
fully before learners work (explicit instruction for a novice skill — Kirschner, Sweller &
Clark, 2006). Keep "balance" and "contrast" concrete — "balance = is it even or off-center?"
and "contrast = how big is the difference between light and dark (or rough and smooth)?"
There is no wrong feeling to express, and no "better" element; the goal is that the choice
is *on purpose* and can be said aloud. These same five building blocks appear in art
traditions around the world (see `docs/facilitation.md` on honoring diverse traditions);
mention that artists everywhere choose from the same small toolkit, which is what makes
their work legible across places and times. See `docs/contexts.md` on small-group work.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Last year you planned an artwork with a clear idea and chose
   materials to fit it. Today you meet the five building blocks every visual maker uses —
   **line, shape, color, balance, and contrast** — and learn to use them *on purpose*.
2. **Meet the elements (10 min).** Look at the poster. Here is a worked example. To
   express **calm**, you might draw *long horizontal lines* (still, like a sleeping
   horizon), *soft round shapes*, an *even balance* (the same weight on both sides), and
   *low contrast* (light and dark close together, nothing shouting). Now flip the same
   five elements to express **storm**: *diagonal jagged lines* (energy, like wind),
   *sharp shapes* (strong, pointed), an *off-center balance* (weighted to one side —
   tension), and *high contrast* (very dark against very light — bold). Same five tools,
   opposite feelings. The difference is **deliberate choice**.
3. **Choose your idea (5 min).** Pick one idea or feeling to express — *calm, storm,
   celebration, loneliness, energy, pride, worry, home, danger, peace*. Say it to a
   partner in one word.
4. **Make it deliberately (15 min).** Draw your piece. For each element, ask: which
   *line* fits my feeling? which *shape*? which *color or value*? is my *balance* even or
   off-center — and why? how much *contrast* do I want? Make each choice on purpose.
5. **Write your artist's statement (10 min).** On your slip, finish two sentences: "My
   idea/feeling is ___" and "I chose ___ (element) to ___ (effect), because ___." Show a
   partner your piece and read your statement; the partner points to where they *see* the
   element doing the work.
6. **Close (5 min).** You did what every artist, sign-painter, and map-maker does: you
   chose, on purpose, how a picture would make someone feel. Keep your piece — this unit
   you will compare art, compose, perform, and design, all with the same deliberate hand.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a feeling word and a sentence frame — "I used ___ lines because
  ___ feels ___" — and limit the task to two elements used deliberately.
- **Extension:** Make a second version of the same idea (e.g., "energy" once bold, once
  calm) and write one sentence naming exactly which element you changed and why.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation/self):** Can the learner name at least one element they chose
  and connect it, with a reason, to the idea or feeling they meant to express?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The deliberate piece and its artist's statement, kept for the
  unit portfolio.
- **Self-check:** The learner asks, "Did I choose each element on purpose, and can I point
  to where it shows and say why?"

## Home connection

Find one designed image in your day — a sign, a wrapper, a poster, a screen — and name one
element (line, shape, color, balance, or contrast) the maker chose, and the feeling it
gives you.
