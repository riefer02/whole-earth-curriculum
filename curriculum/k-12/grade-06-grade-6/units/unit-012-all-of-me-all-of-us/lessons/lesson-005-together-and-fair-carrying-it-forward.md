# Lesson 5 — Together and Fair: Carrying It Forward

## Summary

Learners gather the year's work on **cooperation, competition, and fairness** into one closing
commitment — **cooperate when the goal is shared, compete only in fair play, and act so everyone
has enough**. They revisit **why some have more than they need** (systems and circumstances, never
worth) and write a **common-good commitment**, then celebrate the whole **year-end portfolio** and
look ahead to Grade 7.

## Objectives

- Compare cooperation and competition and explain when each helps or harms a group.
  _(D02.S4.06.01)_
- Explain why some people have more than they need while others lack, in age-appropriate terms.
  _(D02.S4.06.02)_

## Connection

A crew rows one boat together — **cooperation**, because the goal is shared. Two runners race to
one line — **competition**, fair and joyful, because the test is who is fastest. And down the
street, one pantry overflows while another is nearly empty — a question of **fairness**, because
the difference comes from systems and circumstances, never from anyone's worth. These three are
not separate subjects; they are three ways a person takes part in the world. The skill is
**choosing**: cooperate when we need each other, compete only when it is fair play, and act so
everyone has **enough**. That is the common good — and it is yours to carry into Grade 7, in a
city, a village, a team, or a shared Earth.

## Materials

- Together and fair poster
- My common-good commitment page
- Year-end portfolio folder or page
- Pencils and crayons

## Preparation

- Print or draw the together-and-fair poster (three panels and a banner).
- Copy a common-good commitment page for each learner.
- Gather the learners' year-end artifacts from Lessons 1–4 for the closing celebration.

## Facilitator note

This is the unit's **closing capstone and celebration**, written to the learner ("you"). It
**retrieves** cooperation-vs-competition and unequal-shares from Unit 3 (see L.06.003.09–10) and
gathers the whole year into one commitment — a spiral, not a repeat. Hold the ethical core
firmly and kindly: **unequal shares come from systems and circumstances, never from a person's
worth**, and fairness is something people build together. Never ask learners to disclose their
own family's resources. Keep the closing **celebratory, not ranked**: every portfolio is complete
and worthy; the measure is growth, not comparison (`docs/philosophy.md` §8). Model one commitment
of each kind first (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006). See `docs/facilitation.md`.

## Procedure

1. **Gather and remember (5 min).** Two questions you have carried this year: **when does
   cooperation help or harm a group, and when does competition?** And: **why do some have more
   than they need while others lack?** Quick recall, no page needed — say one answer for each.
   (Retrieval practice: recall before you look.)
2. **Meet the three panels (10 min).** Look at the poster. **Cooperation:** figures row one boat —
   the goal is shared, so it helps; it harms when people hide their best ideas. **Competition:**
   two runners to one line — it helps when it is fair play; it harms when winning is all that
   matters. **Fairness:** three piles, different sizes, and an arrow evening them out — the
   difference comes from where you were born, the work a family could find and its pay, illness or
   disability, war or displacement, unfair rules. **None of these is anyone's worth.** Fairness —
   sharing and fair rules — makes more people have enough. Worked example: a shared school garden
   (cooperate), a spelling match (compete fairly), a food drive or a fair rule so no one goes
   hungry (act fairly).
3. **Write your common-good commitment (15 min).** On your page, write three things you will do
   in Grade 7: (a) **one act of cooperation** — a goal you will work on *with* others; (b) **one
   commitment to fair play** — a way you will compete kindly or cheer others; (c) **one fair
   action** — a way you will share or speak so more people have enough.
4. **Gather your portfolio and celebrate (20 min).** Bring together your four year-end artifacts:
   your "who I am becoming" reflection, your goal-and-plan note, your values anchor card, and your
   belonging reflection. Add this commitment page. Look at the whole — this is *you*, a whole
   person, after a whole year. Share one thing you are proud of and one thing you will carry
   forward. (Learners who prefer may share in writing or to a partner instead of aloud.)
5. **Close (5 min).** Who am I? Many parts, one whole self, with goals, values, and belonging.
   Who am I becoming? Someone who cooperates, plays fair, and acts so everyone has enough. Carry
   it into Grade 7 — and keep carrying it.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide one ready-made commitment of each kind to choose from; ask the learner to
  say one in their own words.
- **Extension:** Connect cooperation, fair play, and fairness to one real situation in the world,
  and write two sentences on how the three work together for the common good.

## Assessment

- **Summative (portfolio):** Can the learner compare cooperation and competition (when each helps
  or harms), explain unequal shares through systems and circumstances (never worth), and produce a
  three-part common-good commitment?
- **Self-check:** The learner asks, "Did I name when each mode helps and harms? Did I explain the
  *systems*, not blame or shame any person? Did I end with a real commitment, not just a problem?"

## Home connection

Bring home one commitment you will carry into Grade 7 and invite someone at home to name one of
theirs — then celebrate the year together.

## Resources

  structural factors — where one is born, work and pay, inherited wealth, health, and conflict —
  rather than individual worth (World Bank and UN data on inequality and poverty). The statement
  that *no person's worth is measured by their resources* is a values commitment of this
  curriculum, not a scientific claim. For classroom SEL framing:
