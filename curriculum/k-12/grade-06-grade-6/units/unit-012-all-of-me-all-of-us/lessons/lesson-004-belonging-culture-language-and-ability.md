# Lesson 4 — Belonging: Culture, Language, and Ability

## Summary

Learners return to how identity — **culture, language, and ability** — shapes **belonging**, and
reflect on real moments of being included and left out. They name how a person's language,
culture, or ability can become a **gate**, and commit to **one act that opens the circle**,
producing a **belonging reflection** for the year-end portfolio.

## Objectives

- Describe how identity, such as culture, language, and ability, shapes belonging.
  _(D02.S3.06.02)_

## Connection

You are new in a room where everyone speaks a language you do not yet know — the words swirl
around you and you wait at the edge. Or a classmate uses a wheelchair and the doorway is too
narrow. Or your family's holiday food smells "different" to someone and they wrinkle their nose.
In each moment, **who you are — your culture, your language, your ability — shapes whether you
step inside the circle or stand outside it**. The good news: circles can be opened. A single
"you are welcome," a gap left in the circle, a door made wide, a shared bowl — these are small
acts that turn exclusion into belonging. Belonging is not luck; it is something people *do* for
each other.

## Materials

- Welcome circle poster
- My belonging reflection page
- Pencils and crayons

## Preparation

- Print or draw the welcome circle poster (a circle of diverse figures, one being welcomed
  through a gap).
- Copy a belonging reflection page for each learner.
- Arrange a circle for the closing share.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It **retrieves** the belonging work from Unit 3
(see L.06.003.07–08) and deepens it with reflection — a spiral, not a repeat. Handle with care:
learners may disclose real experiences of exclusion or of being made to feel "different." Never
ask anyone to perform their difference or to relive pain; invite, do not require, personal
sharing. The idea to land is that **difference is ordinary and whole**, and that **belonging is
built, not given** — so every learner has power to open the circle. Include ability naturally
(wheelchairs, hearing aids, prosthetics) as ordinary, never as a "special case." See
`docs/facilitation.md` and `docs/art-style.md`.

## Procedure

1. **Gather and remember (5 min).** Earlier this year you asked: who is in, who is out, and why?
   Quick recall, no page needed: name one thing about a person — their language, culture, or
   ability — that can make belonging easier or harder. (Retrieval practice: recall before you
   read.)
2. **Meet the welcome circle (10 min).** Look at the poster. Inside the circle: a drummer, a
   shared-food holder, a two-language speaker, a wheelchair user with a hearing aid. Each is
   **ordinary and whole**, and each brings something the circle needs. But notice the gap, and
   the figure outside with an open arm. Worked example: a new classmate speaks a different first
   language. Belonging is shaped by that — but one person says "you are welcome," another points
   to the food, a third makes room. The circle opens.
3. **Reflect on your own belonging (15 min).** On your page, write or draw: (a) one moment you
   felt you **belonged** — what opened the circle? (b) one moment you felt **left out** or saw
   someone left out — what closed it? and (c) **one act** you will do to open the circle for
   someone whose culture, language, or ability is different from yours.
4. **Share the welcome (15 min).** In pairs or small groups, share just what you choose — the
   welcome act, or a word of welcome from a language you know. Teach each other one "welcome"
   word or sign.
5. **Close (5 min).** Difference is ordinary. Belonging is built. You hold a piece of the door —
   keep it open for everyone.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide two picture prompts (a moment of welcome, a moment of being left out) and
  ask the learner to choose one and say one thing that opened or closed the circle.
- **Extension:** Analyze how one specific part of identity — a language, an ability, a cultural
  practice — can become a *gate*, and write one sentence proposing how a community could open it
  for good.

## Assessment

- **Formative (self):** Can the learner describe how culture, language, or ability shapes
  belonging, and name one act that opens the circle?
- **Portfolio:** The belonging reflection, kept as a year-end artifact.

## Home connection

Ask someone at home about a time they felt welcomed or left out, and what one person did (or
could have done) to open the circle — then share your own welcome act.
