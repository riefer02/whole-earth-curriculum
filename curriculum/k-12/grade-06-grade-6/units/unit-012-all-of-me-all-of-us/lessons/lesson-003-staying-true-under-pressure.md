# Lesson 3 — Staying True Under Pressure

## Summary

Learners consolidate the strategies they have practiced this year for handling **stress,
distraction, and peer pressure** into one personal **values anchor**. They work through three
short scenarios with the **pause–name–choose** move, then build a small **values-and-strategies
card** they will carry into Grade 7.

## Objectives

- Use strategies to handle stress, distraction, or peer pressure while staying true to their own
  values. _(D01.S3.06.02)_

## Connection

A test is tomorrow and your chest feels tight — that is **stress**. A phone buzzes while you are
trying to focus — that is **distraction**. Friends urge you to join something you know is wrong
for you — that is **peer pressure**. All three push on the same thing: your **values**, the ideas
about right and wrong you have chosen to live by. When pressure pushes, your values are the
**compass** that tells you which way is *your* way. The skill is not to feel nothing — it is to
**pause, name what presses, and choose what stays true**. That skill works in a busy city, a
quiet village, on a screen, or in a crowded corridor.

## Materials

- Values compass poster
- My values anchor card
- Pencils

## Preparation

- Print or draw the values compass poster (figure, compass of values, three arrows, pause bars,
  toolkit strip).
- Copy a values anchor card for each learner.
- Prepare three short scenario prompts to model and practice.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It **retrieves and consolidates** the
pressure-handling strategies from earlier in the year (see L.06.001.10 and the self-care work in
Unit 8) into a portable anchor — a spiral, not a repeat. This is a skill, so **model each
scenario step-by-step** before independent practice (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006). Name the
**technology** lens explicitly — distraction is often a device, and the strategy is to *choose*
when and how to use it, not to shame the tool. Values are personal; invite learners to share only
what they choose, and never rank any value system. Frame peer pressure carefully: peers are not
villains, and "staying true" includes saying no kindly and finding allies. See
`docs/facilitation.md` and `docs/contexts.md`.

## Procedure

1. **Gather and remember (5 min).** Quick recall, no page needed: name the three pressures we
   have studied this year — **stress, distraction, peer pressure** — and one strategy you have
   already used. (Retrieval practice: recall before you read.)
2. **Meet the compass (10 min).** Look at the poster. You stand in the middle holding a compass
   labeled **my values**. Three arrows press in: stress, distraction, peer pressure. Before each
   arrow reaches you there is a **pause** bar. The move is always three steps:
   **pause → name what presses → choose what stays true**. Worked example: a friend urges you to
   copy someone's work. Pause. Name it — "that is peer pressure, and it goes against my value of
   honesty." Choose — "no thank you, I will do my own and I can help you with yours."
3. **Practice the three pressures (15 min).** With a partner or alone, work through three
   scenarios: (a) **stress** — a big test tomorrow; (b) **distraction** — a phone buzzing while
   you read; (c) **peer pressure** — friends urging something against your values. For each, say
   or write: what presses, what value it touches, and one choice that stays true. Use the toolkit
   strip: pause, breathe, name it, ask for help, choose.
4. **Build your anchor card (15 min).** On your card, write two or three **values** you hold —
   honesty, fairness, kindness, courage, family, faith, the Earth — and next to each, one
   **strategy** for when pressure pushes. Keep the card small enough to carry.
5. **Close (5 min).** Pressure will come again. Your values are your compass, and the pause is
   your power. Carry the anchor with you — into the summer, into Grade 7, into the rest of your
   life.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide one pre-written value and one strategy to choose from for each pressure;
  ask the learner to say the three-step move in their own words.
- **Extension:** Write about a moment two pulls were in tension — a friend's urging against a
  value — and draft an assertive, kind refusal that keeps the friendship and the value.

## Assessment

- **Formative (self):** Can the learner name the three pressures, apply pause–name–choose to a
  scenario, and name at least one value and one strategy?
- **Portfolio:** The values anchor card, kept as a year-end artifact (the "values note").

## Home connection

Ask someone at home what *their* "values compass" points to and one way they stay true to it
when life pushes — and tell them yours.
