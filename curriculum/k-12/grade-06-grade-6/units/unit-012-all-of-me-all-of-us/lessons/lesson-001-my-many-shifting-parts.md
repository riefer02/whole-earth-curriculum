# Lesson 1 — My Many Shifting Parts

## Summary

Learners return to the **many-parts identity map** they began earlier in the year and now
see how those parts **shift by context** — at home, at school, at a celebration. They write a
**"who I am becoming" reflection** that names their parts, notices which parts come forward
where, and records **how they have grown this year**. This is the opening artifact of the
year-end portfolio.

## Objectives

- Describe their identity as made of many parts that can shift by context, such as family,
  culture, language, interests, and beliefs. _(D01.S2.06.01)_

## Connection

Think of one person you know well — maybe yourself. At home they are a grandchild, a sibling,
a cook of the family's food. At school they are a teammate, a question-asker, a keeper of fair
rules. At a celebration they are a dancer or a drummer, a speaker of the family's first
language, a believer in something that matters. It is **the same person** — but different
**parts** come forward in different places. That is identity: not one fixed label, but many
parts that **shift by context**, while the whole person stays whole. A child who moved across
the sea, a child who signs with their hands, a child who is new to the neighborhood — each has
a set of parts that is complete and true, and none is more "real" than another.

## Materials

- Shifting-parts poster
- My shifting parts reflection page
- Pencils and crayons

## Preparation

- Print or draw the shifting-parts poster (a center "me" connected to three contexts: at home,
  at school, at a celebration).
- Copy a "my shifting parts" reflection page for each learner.
- Arrange a space for a year-end gallery walk.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It opens the year-end unit by **retrieving** the
many-parts identity map learners built earlier in the year (see L.06.001.05) and adding the new
idea that parts **shift by context** — a spiral, not a repeat. The tone is celebration and
honoring: every family shape, culture, language, and belief system is ordinary and whole. Be
explicit that **beliefs are personal and optional to share**; never rank any belief system or any
family shape. The gallery walk must celebrate *difference*, never compare maps as better or
worse. Keep the "how I have grown" line private if a learner prefers; it is their reflection to
keep or share. See `docs/facilitation.md` and `docs/art-style.md`.

## Procedure

1. **Gather and remember (5 min).** Earlier this year you drew your identity as many parts —
   family, culture, language, interests, beliefs. Quick recall, no page needed: from memory, name
   three parts of you. (Retrieval practice: try before you look.)
2. **Meet "shifting by context" (10 min).** Look at the poster. It is the same **me** in three
   places. Worked example: at **home** my family and my language are in front; at **school** my
   interests and my beliefs (like "everyone should be treated fairly") come forward; at a
   **celebration** my culture and my interests show. Not one of these is the "real" me — they are
   all me, and the place decides which parts step forward.
3. **Draw your shifting parts (20 min).** On your reflection page, write **me** in the center.
   Then draw three context boxes — home, school, and one more of your choosing (a team, a
   celebration, online, a place of worship, a friend's house). In each box, write the parts of
   you that come forward there. Add any part that matters — an ability, a dream, a language you
   are learning. Then finish one sentence: **"This year I have grown by…"**
4. **Gallery walk (10 min).** Walk quietly and look at the reflections. Notice: how many
   languages? How many family shapes? How many beliefs? Each map is whole and true. Find one
   thing on someone else's page you admire. (Learners who do not stand or walk can stay seated
   and have pages passed hand to hand around the circle, or posted at seated height — everyone
   sees every page either way.)
5. **Close (5 min).** You are many parts, one whole self, and those parts shift with where you
   are. That is **who you are becoming** — and it deserves honor. Carry that with you.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide pre-drawn context boxes with a picture prompt in each; scribe labels as
  needed, and ask the learner to name just one part that comes forward at home.
- **Extension:** Write about a moment two of your parts felt in tension (e.g., "at school my
  belief in fairness and my wish to fit in pulled different ways") and how both can still be
  true.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name several parts and place at least one part in
  each of two contexts, showing the *shift*?
- **Portfolio:** The "who I am becoming" reflection, kept as the opening artifact of the
  year-end portfolio.

## Home connection

Show someone at home your reflection and invite them to name one part of you they have watched
grow this year — a skill, a kindness, a courage you may not have noticed yourself.
