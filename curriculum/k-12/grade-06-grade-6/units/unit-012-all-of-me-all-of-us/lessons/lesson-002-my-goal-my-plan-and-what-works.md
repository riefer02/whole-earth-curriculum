# Lesson 2 — My Goal, My Plan, and What Works

## Summary

Learners return to the **goal loop** — set a goal, try it and track, reflect on what worked,
adjust and try again — and set **one concrete short-term goal** for the summer or the start of
Grade 7, with a simple tracking plan. They reflect on what has helped them stay on track before,
producing a **goal-and-plan note** for the year-end portfolio.

## Objectives

- Set a short-term goal, track their progress, and reflect on what worked. _(D01.S3.06.01)_

## Connection

You decide you want to learn a song on an instrument, ride a bike without wobbling, or bake a
bread that rises. Wishing is not enough — a **goal** turns the wish into a plan: I will practice
ten minutes a day; I will try every morning; I will check off each day I try. Then you **reflect**:
which mornings worked best? What made the difference? That is the whole skill, and it is the same
for a farm task, a homework plan, a new language, or a summer project. The loop — set, try and
track, reflect, adjust — works in a city flat or a village field, and it is yours to carry into
Grade 7.

## Materials

- Goal loop poster
- My goal-and-plan note page
- Pencils

## Preparation

- Print or draw the goal loop poster (four steps around "my short-term goal").
- Copy a goal-and-plan note page for each learner.
- If possible, prepare a simple week-long tracking chart for the enriched variant.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It **retrieves** the goal-setting skill from
earlier in the year (see L.06.001.09) and applies it forward — a spiral, not a repeat. This is a
foundational self-regulation skill, so **model it as a worked example first** (Kirschner, Sweller
& Clark, 2006): name a goal, make it specific and small, show a tracking plan, then model
reflection. Keep the stance **mastery, not ranking** — a goal not yet reached is *information*
("not yet"), never a verdict. Goals should be the learner's own, realistic, and about growth, not
comparison. Encourage goals that are doable in a week or a month, and never require any learner
to disclose anything private. See `docs/facilitation.md`.

## Procedure

1. **Gather and remember (5 min).** Earlier this year you set and tracked a short-term goal.
   Quick recall, no page needed: what was the goal, and what was **one thing that worked**?
   (Retrieval practice: recall before you read.)
2. **Meet the goal loop (10 min).** Look at the poster. It is a circle with four moves:
   **Set a goal → Try it and track → Reflect: what worked? → Adjust and try again →** and around
   again. Worked example: goal — "read one chapter every evening for two weeks." Track — a
   checkmark each evening. Reflect — "I read best right after dinner, before I get tired." Adjust —
   "move reading to right after dinner." That small reflection is the whole trick.
3. **Set your goal (10 min).** On your page, write one **short-term** goal for the summer or the
   start of Grade 7. Make it **specific and small**: not "get better at everything" but "run a
   little farther each week" or "learn five new words of a language I want to speak." Then write
   **how you will track it** — checkmarks, a chart, stones in a row, a note in a notebook.
4. **Reflect on what works (10 min).** Think back: when you reached a goal before, **what
   helped?** Write one thing that has worked for you (a time of day, a person who reminds you, a
   small first step). That is your reflection to keep.
5. **Close (5 min).** A goal is a wish with a plan. Track it, reflect, adjust — and if you are
   not there yet, that is **information**, not a verdict. You are the author of your next step.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer two ready-made goal choices to pick from, and one tracking idea; ask the
  learner to say the goal in their own words.
- **Extension:** Make the goal measurable and time-bound, and plan a mid-point check where the
  learner will decide what to adjust.

## Assessment

- **Formative (self):** Can the learner state a specific short-term goal, a way to track it, and
  one reflection on what has worked?
- **Portfolio:** The goal-and-plan note, kept as a year-end artifact alongside the "who I am
  becoming" reflection.

## Home connection

Share your goal and tracking plan with someone at home and invite them to be your "check-in"
person once this week — and offer to be theirs.
