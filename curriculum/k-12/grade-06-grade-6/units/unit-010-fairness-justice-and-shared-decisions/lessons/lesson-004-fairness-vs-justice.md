# Lesson 4 — Fairness vs. Justice

## Summary

Learners learn to **distinguish fairness from justice**: fairness is about *what people
deserve* (a fair share, a fair turn, fair treatment), while justice is about *correcting
wrongs and protecting rights*. They sort a worked example, then example cards, and record
one of each from their own life.

## Objectives

- Distinguish between fairness (what people deserve) and justice (correcting wrongs and
  protecting rights), using examples. _(D09.S2.06.01)_

## Connection

Two different moments. In one, a harvest is shared so each family gets a fair portion —
that is **fairness**: everyone gets what they *deserve*. In the other, something was
stolen, and it is returned, and the harm is repaired, and a rule is put in place so it
does not happen again — that is **justice**: *correcting a wrong and protecting rights*.
Fairness is about the share; justice is about the wrong. They are cousins, not twins —
and telling them apart is how we see both clearly.

## Materials

- Fairness-vs-justice poster
- Example cards
- Comparison page

## Preparation

- Print or draw the fairness-vs-justice poster (two columns, each with a simple icon and
  an example).
- Copy example cards for each pair and a comparison page for each learner.
- Prepare one spoken example of each (fairness and justice) for the opening.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The distinction to land: **fairness** is
about *what people deserve* — a fair share, a fair turn, fair treatment; **justice** is
about *correcting wrongs and protecting rights* — responding when harm has happened, and
guarding what people are owed. They overlap (a fair share *is* part of justice) but they
are not the same, and the standard asks learners to tell them apart with examples. Model
the sort as a worked example (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006). Keep it **non-shaming**:
examples of correcting wrongs are about *repair*, not blame. Distinguish **evidence from
values**: *what happened and what was deserved or corrected* are observable; *that people
should get a fair share and that wrongs should be made right* are values held by this
curriculum and by the children it serves — name them as values. Hold the question open,
and attach any example of wrong to hope: wrongs can be, and are, corrected. See
`docs/facilitation.md`.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** You have weighed dilemmas and their consequences. Now two words
   people often use as if they were one: **fairness** and **justice**. Try to recall:
   what do you already think each one means? (Pause.) They are close — but different.
2. **Meet the distinction (10 min).** Look at the poster. **Fairness** is about *what
   people deserve*: a fair share, a fair turn, fair treatment. **Justice** is about
   *correcting wrongs and protecting rights*: when harm has happened, justice responds —
   returns what was taken, repairs the harm, and guards what people are owed so it does
   not happen again. Fairness asks "is the share right?" Justice asks "was a wrong done,
   and how is it made right?"
3. **Worked example (10 min).** Build the sort together. *Example:* "A shared harvest is
   divided so each family gets an equal portion." Fairness or justice? (Fairness — a fair
   share.) *Example:* "Someone's goat was taken; it is returned, the harm is repaired, and
   a rule is set so it cannot happen again." Fairness or justice? (Justice — correcting a
   wrong and protecting rights.) Say why each belongs where it does.
4. **Practice with a partner (10 min).** Take example cards. Sort each into **fairness**
   or **justice**, and for each say why: "This is ___ because ___." If one is *both*, say
   how the two overlap in it.
5. **Record your own (10 min).** On your page, write one real or invented **fairness**
   example (what someone deserved) and one **justice** example (a wrong corrected and a
   right protected), in your own words.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a card with one column already filled and a sentence starter
  ("Fairness is when ___; justice is when ___").
- **Extension:** Find an example that is *both* fairness and justice and explain in one
  sentence where the two overlap and where they differ.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner state, for a given example, whether it is
  fairness or justice, and say why using "deserve" or "correct a wrong / protect rights"?
- **Self-check:** The learner asks, "Is my fairness example about what someone deserved,
  and is my justice example about a wrong corrected and a right protected?"

## Home connection

Think of one moment at home or nearby that was fairness (a fair share) and one that was
justice (a wrong made right), and tell someone the difference.
