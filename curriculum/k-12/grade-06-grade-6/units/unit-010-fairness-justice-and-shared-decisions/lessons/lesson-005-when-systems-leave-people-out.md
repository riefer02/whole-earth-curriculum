# Lesson 5 — When Systems Leave People Out — Without Anyone Meaning To

## Summary

Learners explain **how rules, laws, and systems can treat some groups unfairly even when
no one person intends harm**. They trace a rule step by step to see *who is left out* and
*which step does it*, then trace system example cards and suggest one small change.

## Objectives

- Explain how rules, laws, or systems can treat some groups unfairly even when no one
  person intends harm. _(D09.S2.06.02)_

## Connection

A building's only entrance is a staircase. Nobody built it *wanting* to shut anyone out —
but a person who uses a wheelchair, or a parent pushing a cart, cannot get in. The harm
is real even though the *intent* was absent. Rules and systems do this quietly: a sign in
only one language, a meeting scheduled when some people work or pray, a test offered in
only one format. The unfairness is built into the *steps*, not into anyone's heart — and
that is why we have to look at the steps.

## Materials

- System-flow poster
- System example cards
- Trace-a-system page

## Preparation

- Print or draw the system-flow poster (one rule → several steps → an outcome, with a
  "who is left out?" box and "no one intended this" underneath).
- Copy system example cards for each group and a trace-a-system page for each learner.
- Prepare one real system example (a staircase, a one-language sign, a schedule) to trace
  together.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **a rule, law, or system
can treat some groups unfairly even when no one person intends harm** — the unfairness
lives in the *steps* of the system, not (necessarily) in anyone's intention. Teach the
tracing routine — *who is affected? who is left out? at which step? did anyone intend
it?* — as a worked example (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006). Keep it **non-shaming and
precise**: the point is *not* to blame designers (often there is no single villain), but
to see that *outcomes* can be unfair even when *intentions* are not. That is why the
egalitarian question "who is left out?" matters — we must ask it, because the system will
not tell us by itself. Distinguish **evidence from values**: *that a rule leaves some
people out, and at which step* is observable; *that leaving people out is wrong and
should be fixed* is a value held by this curriculum and its learners. Carry the
**technology & environment lens** honestly: systems include *built things* (a staircase,
a door, a screen, a form), and design choices — physical or digital — can include or
exclude; a small change (a ramp, a translation, a different time, a spoken option) often
repairs a lot. Attach every example to hope: unfair systems can be, and are, changed.
See `docs/facilitation.md`.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** You can now tell fairness from justice. Today a harder, quieter
   problem: unfairness that *no one meant*. Try to recall: what does "unfair" usually
   make you picture — a person doing something mean? (Pause.) Sometimes no one is mean,
   and it is still unfair.
2. **Meet the idea (10 min).** Look at the poster. A **system** is a set of steps, rules,
   or built things that people move through — an entrance, a sign, a schedule, a test. A
   rule can treat some groups unfairly **even when no one person intends harm**, because
   the unfairness lives in the *steps*. To see it, you trace: *who is affected? who is
   left out? at which step? did anyone intend it?*
3. **Worked example (10 min).** Trace one together. *Rule:* "The only way in is up these
   stairs." Step 1: a person using a wheelchair arrives. Step 2: there is no ramp or
   lift. Outcome: that person is left out. Did anyone intend harm? No — but the system
   still excludes. Then name the small fix: add a ramp, or a ground-level door.
4. **Practice with a partner (10 min).** Take a system example card — a sign in one
   language, a meeting time, a test in one format. Trace it: who is affected? who is left
   out? at which step? did anyone intend it? Then suggest **one small change** that would
   include them.
5. **Record your own (10 min).** On your page, trace one system you have seen or heard
   of, name who is left out and which step does it, and write one small change. No blame
   — just clear seeing.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a card with the steps already listed; the learner marks the step
  where people are left out and picks a fix from two options.
- **Extension:** Compare two systems and explain which leaves more people out, and how
  you can tell — then propose a change for each.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner trace a rule and name *who is left out*
  and *which step* does it, without needing a villain?
- **Peer check:** Partners check each other: "Did you find the *step* that excludes
  people — not just say 'that is unfair'?"

## Home connection

Notice one rule or built thing near home (a step, a sign, a time, a form) and ask who it
might leave out — then imagine one small change.
