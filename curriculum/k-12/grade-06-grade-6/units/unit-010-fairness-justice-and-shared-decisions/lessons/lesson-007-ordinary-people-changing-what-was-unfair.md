# Lesson 7 — Ordinary People, Changing What Was Unfair

## Summary

Learners describe **how ordinary people have worked together to change something
unfair**, using a historical or local example. They learn a four-part frame — the unfair
thing, the ordinary people, the action together, the change — trace a worked example (the
Green Belt Movement), then trace change-story cards and record one story of their own.

## Objectives

- Describe how ordinary people have worked together to change something unfair, using a
  historical or local example. _(D09.S3.06.02)_

## Connection

No hero with a cape — just neighbors, or students, or farmers, deciding *together* that
something unfair would not stay. A hillside being stripped of its trees; a road with no
safe place to cross; a village with no clean well. Ordinary people see it, join together,
and keep acting until it changes. That is **collective action** — and it has moved more of
the world than any single ruler ever did.

## Materials

- Before-and-after change poster
- Change-story cards
- Change-story page

## Preparation

- Print or draw the before-and-after change poster (two panels + the steps between).
- Copy change-story cards for each group and a change-story page for each learner.
- Prepare one told change story (the Green Belt Movement, or a local example) for the
  opening.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **ordinary people,
working together, can change something unfair** — and history is full of it. Teach the
four-part frame — *the unfair thing / the ordinary people / what they did together / what
changed* — as a worked example (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006). Use **true,
age-appropriate, non-shaming** examples: the Green Belt Movement (founded 1977 by Wangari
Maathai with other women in Kenya, planting trees against deforestation — later spreading
to other African countries and planting over thirty million trees; she won the 2004 Nobel
Peace Prize); or a local story your learners know (a crossing fixed, a well won, a space
reclaimed). The point is *how ordinary people do it*, not hero-worship — name the many,
not one name. Attach hope, not despair: unfair things *are* changed. Distinguish
**evidence from values**: *what happened, who acted, and what changed* are facts we can
check; *that people should act together against unfairness* is a value this curriculum
holds and offers to learners as a value, not a command. Carry the **environment lens**
honestly: some of the most famous collective actions protect the shared environment —
trees, water, land — because the environment is part of the common good. See
`docs/facilitation.md`.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** You have compared ways of deciding. Now: who actually *changes*
   things when they are unfair? Try to recall one time you saw a group of people change
   something together. (Pause.) Today you learn the pattern.
2. **Meet the four-part frame (10 min).** Look at the poster. Every change story has four
   parts: **the unfair thing** (what was wrong), **the ordinary people** (no single hero —
   many), **what they did together** (organize, act, keep at it), and **what changed**.
   Ordinary people change unfair things by *joining together* and *staying with it*.
3. **Worked example (10 min).** Trace one together. *The unfair thing:* a hillside being
   stripped of trees. *The ordinary people:* farmers and neighbors — mostly women. *What
   they did together:* in 1977, Wangari Maathai and others started the **Green Belt
   Movement**, planting trees together; it spread to other African countries and planted
   over thirty million trees. *What changed:* the land was re-greened, and the movement
   grew far beyond one hillside. (Maathai later won the 2004 Nobel Peace Prize.) Notice:
   ordinary people, acting together, over time.
4. **Practice with a partner (10 min).** Take a change-story card. Fill the four parts:
   the unfair thing, the ordinary people, what they did together, and what changed. Say
   it aloud in one sentence each.
5. **Record your own (10 min).** On your page, write one change story you know — from a
   card, from near home, or from a place you have heard of — using all four parts. This
   page is a portfolio piece for the unit.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a card with two of the four parts filled in; the learner completes
  the others with a sentence starter ("The ordinary people were ___; what changed was
  ___").
- **Extension:** Compare two change stories and explain what made one succeed where the
  other struggled (numbers, allies, persistence, timing).

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name all four parts of a change story —
  the unfair thing, the ordinary people, the action together, and the change?
- **Portfolio:** The change-story page, kept as the unit's collective-action artifact.

## Home connection

Ask someone at home about a time ordinary people (not rulers) changed something unfair —
nearby or in history — and name the four parts together.
