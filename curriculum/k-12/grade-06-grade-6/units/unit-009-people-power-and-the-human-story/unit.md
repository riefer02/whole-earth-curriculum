# Unit 9 — People, Power & the Human Story

## Overview

This unit steps into the wider human story with the historian's new tools, building
on Grade 5's timelines and comparison of civilizations. Learners explain the
difference between a primary source and a secondary source and use each to describe
one past event, and describe change and continuity over a period of about a hundred
years in one society, giving an example of each. They explain how knowledge is passed
from one generation to the next through oral tradition, written records, or
apprenticeship, using examples from different peoples, and describe how a technology
or idea spread between cultures and changed as it moved from place to place. They
explain how people shape their environment and how the environment shapes people, and
analyze how the movement of plants, animals, and people between world regions changed
environments and societies, using one historical example. They explain what power is,
who holds it in a community or society, and how it can be used fairly or unfairly,
and describe a movement for rights or freedom, such as a struggle against colonial
rule or for equal treatment, and what its people did. History is told as a global
story from many perspectives, never one people's triumph.

## Essential questions

- How do primary and secondary sources tell a past event, and what changes and what stays the same over a hundred years?
- How do knowledge, technology, place, power, and movements for rights shape the human story?
