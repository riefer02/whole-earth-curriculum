# Lesson 7 — People Shape the Land, the Land Shapes People

## Summary

Learners see **two directions at once**: how the environment shapes people and how people
shape the environment, through the **Andes** of South America — where steep mountains and
high altitude shaped farming, herding, and travel, and where people carved **terraces** and
**canals** into the slopes to farm. The two directions are held together, not opposed.

## Objectives

- Explain how people shape their environment and how the environment shapes people, using
  specific examples. _(D08.S3.06.01)_

## Connection

Look at where you live. The land does things to you — heat, cold, rain, hills, rivers shape
what you eat, wear, and how you travel. And you do things to the land — gardens, roads,
buildings, ditches. The relationship runs **both ways** at once. Today you watch it in one
place where the land is especially strong: the Andes mountains of South America.

## Materials

- People-and-environment poster
- Shape cards (eight items)
- People-and-environment note

## Preparation

- Print or draw the people-and-environment poster.
- Copy the eight cards and a note for each learner.
- Arrange a circle for the story and a flat space for sorting and writing.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The skill to build is **holding both
directions at once**: environment shapes people, and people shape environment. Model the
Andes example fully, then have learners sort and record. Guard against one-directional
thinking ("nature controls people" or "people conquer nature"): the point is a two-way
relationship. The Andes example honors Andean peoples' skill and knowledge — terraces and
canals are engineering, not mere "adapting to survive." A quick retrieval note (the Nile)
spirals back to Grade 5. See `docs/facilitation.md`.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** You have seen how knowledge and tools travel. Now you look at a
   relationship that is always with us: the one between people and the land.
2. **Meet the Andes (10 min).** The **Andes** are a great mountain range in South America,
   with steep slopes and thin, high-altitude air. People have lived there for thousands of
   years. The **environment shaped the people**: the steep, high land meant they grew hardy
   crops like potatoes and quinoa, raised llamas and alpacas (there were no large cattle),
   built from stone, and traveled on foot along mountain roads. And the **people shaped the
   environment**: they carved **terraces** — flat steps — into the steep slopes to farm, and
   dug **canals** to carry water to the fields.
3. **Worked example: sort the two directions (10 min).** Sort together: "steep mountains
   meant people farmed in steps" — that is the **environment shaping people**. "People cut
   terraces into the slopes" — that is **people shaping the environment**. Notice the two
   sentences point opposite ways, and both are true.
4. **Sort your own (15 min).** Take the eight cards and sort them into two columns:
   *the land shapes people* and *people shape the land*. Check with a partner.
5. **Record the two directions (5 min).** On your note, write one example in each direction
   from the Andes.
6. **Close (5 min).** People and the land are not enemies and not the same thing — they
   shape each other, everywhere on Earth.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Sort fewer cards with clear, single-direction statements; scribe the learner's
  note and read the story aloud slowly.
- **Extension:** Name a second place (for example, the Nile, from last year) and give one
  example of each direction there.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner sort statements into the two directions and
  give one example of each from the Andes?
- **Self-check:** The learner asks, "Can I name how the land shaped the people *and* how the
  people shaped the land — and explain which direction each one points?"

## Home connection

With someone at home, name one way the land where you live shapes how people live there, and
one way people have shaped that land — then share both.

## Resources

- Andean terrace agriculture and irrigation, including the terraces and canals of the Andes,
  are documented in Andean archaeology and UNESCO World Heritage listings (for example,
  Machu Picchu and the Qhapaq Ñan road system, <https://whc.unesco.org/>). The domestication
  of potatoes, quinoa, llamas, and alpacas in the Andes is documented in the archaeological
  record. These are presented as Andean peoples' achievements, in line with the curriculum's
  commitment to honor non-European knowledge and skill. Dates are approximations that
  scholars revise as new evidence appears.
