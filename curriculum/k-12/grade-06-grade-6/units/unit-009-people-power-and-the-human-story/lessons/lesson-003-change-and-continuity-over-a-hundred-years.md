# Lesson 3 — Change and Continuity Over a Hundred Years

## Summary

Learners meet **change** and **continuity**: what becomes different and what stays the same
in one society over about a hundred years. A worked example uses the **Songhai empire** and
the city of **Timbuktu** (about 1464–1591), where an empire rose and learning flourished
even as the Niger River and the griots' oral tradition endured.

## Objectives

- Describe change and continuity over a period of about a hundred years in one society,
  giving an example of each. _(D08.S1.06.02)_

## Connection

Think of your own town, village, or city across the time your grandparents can remember.
Some things changed — new buildings, new tools, new people arriving. Some things stayed the
same — a river, a market day, a song, the way a festival is kept. History is the same:
every society is always changing *and* always carrying things forward. **Change** is what
became different; **continuity** is what stayed the same. Today you look at one society
across about a hundred years and see both at once.

## Materials

- Change-and-continuity poster
- Change or continuity cards (eight items)
- Change-and-continuity chart

## Preparation

- Print or draw the change-and-continuity poster.
- Copy the eight cards and a chart for each learner.
- Arrange a circle for the story and a flat space for sorting and writing.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The skill to build is **holding change and
continuity in view at the same time** — not listing events. Model the Songhai example fully,
then have learners sort and record. Emphasize that *change and continuity are not opposites
to choose between*; they happen together. The Songhai example is deliberately West African
and non-European, and it honors oral tradition (the griots) as a real and enduring way of
keeping history — a thread that returns in Lesson 5. Guard against the idea that "continuity
= backward" or "change = progress": both are simply true, and whether a change is good or
bad is a separate question. See `docs/facilitation.md`.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** You have learned to read the past through sources. Now you learn to
   read it through **time**: what changed and what stayed the same over about a hundred
   years.
2. **Meet the society (10 min).** Listen to the story. Around the year 1464, in West Africa,
   the **Songhai** grew into a great empire along the **Niger River**, with the city of
   **Timbuktu** at its heart. For about a hundred years, until about 1591, Timbuktu became a
   famous center of learning, where scholars wrote and copied thousands of books in Arabic
   and local languages. Meanwhile, all through that time, the **Niger River** still shaped
   farming and trade, and **griots** — storytellers who keep a people's history by word —
   still passed the past from one generation to the next.
3. **Worked example: sort change and continuity (15 min).** **Change** is what became
   different. **Continuity** is what stayed the same. Sort the ideas together: "a new empire
   rose and grew strong" is **change**; "the Niger River still shaped farming and trade" is
   **continuity**; "Timbuktu became a center of learning" is **change**; "griots still told
   the people's history" is **continuity**. Notice: both were true at the same time.
4. **Sort your own (10 min).** Take the eight cards and sort them into two columns: *what
   changed* and *what stayed the same*. Check with a partner and explain any you disagreed
   on.
5. **Record the example (5 min).** On your chart, write one example of change and one
   example of continuity from the Songhai hundred years.
6. **Close (5 min).** A society is not "either changed or the same" — it is always both.
   That is what makes the human story rich.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Sort fewer cards with clear, single-idea statements; scribe the learner's
  chart and read the story aloud slowly.
- **Extension:** Name one change that *carried* a continuity inside it (for example,
  learning grew with new books — while the griots kept telling the older stories).

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner sort statements into change and continuity
  and give one example of each for the Songhai hundred years?
- **Self-check:** The learner asks, "Can I name what changed and what stayed the same — and
  explain why each one counts as change or continuity?"

## Home connection

Ask someone at home what has changed and what has stayed the same in your community across
the years they can remember, and sort their answers into two columns together.

## Resources

- The Songhai empire and Timbuktu as a center of learning, including its manuscripts, are
  documented in African history and by UNESCO; Timbuktu is a UNESCO World Heritage site
  (<https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/119/>), and its manuscripts are the subject of ongoing
  preservation work (see the Timbuktu Manuscripts Project and related scholarship). The
  griots of West Africa — hereditary keepers of oral history — are documented in West African
  oral tradition and scholarship (see the work of historians such as Djibril Tamsir Niane).
  Dates are approximations that scholars revise as new evidence appears.
