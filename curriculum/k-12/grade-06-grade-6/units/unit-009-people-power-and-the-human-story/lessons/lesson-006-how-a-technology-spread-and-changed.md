# Lesson 6 — How a Technology Spread and Changed: The Journey of Paper

## Summary

Learners trace how a **technology spreads and changes** as it moves between cultures,
through the journey of **paper** — invented in China, carried along the **Silk Roads** to
Korea, Japan, Central Asia, Baghdad, North Africa, and Europe, changing materials, mills,
and printing along the way. An idea example (the number **zero**) is interleaved for
contrast.

## Objectives

- Describe how a technology or idea spread between cultures and changed as it moved from
  place to place. _(D08.S2.06.02)_

## Connection

Think of a game, a food, or a style of clothing that started in one place and is now enjoyed
in many — and notice how it is a little different everywhere it went. That is how ideas and
tools travel: people meet, trade, learn from each other, and **change what they borrow** to
fit their own lives. Today you follow one such journey — a technology that began in China
and slowly traveled the world, changing at every stop.

## Materials

- Paper journey poster
- Spread-and-change cards (six stops)
- Spread-and-change note

## Preparation

- Print or draw the paper journey poster.
- Copy the six cards and a note for each learner.
- Arrange a circle for the story and a flat space for ordering the cards.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The skill to build is **describing spread
(path) and change (how it transformed at each stop)** — not memorizing dates. Model the
paper journey fully, then have learners order the stops and record. Emphasize that
borrowing is two-way and creative, not theft: each people made paper their own. The example
is deliberately non-Euro-led (the technology and the idea both began outside Europe) and
honors the Silk Roads as a web of exchange among many peoples. The number **zero** is
offered as a quick interleaved idea example for learners ready to extend. See
`docs/facilitation.md`.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** You have seen knowledge pass between generations. Now you follow
   knowledge and tools as they pass **between peoples** — and change on the way.
2. **Worked example: the journey of paper (20 min).** Follow the poster. **Paper was
   invented in China** around the year 105 CE (a court official named Cai Lun is remembered
   for refining it). From China it spread to **Korea and Japan**, where printing — first
   woodblock, later movable metal type — grew alongside it. It traveled west along the
   **Silk Roads** through **Central Asia**. In **Baghdad**, papermakers built **paper mills**
   and made paper from new materials — cotton and linen cloth instead of mulberry and hemp.
   From there paper moved across **North Africa** and into **Europe** by the 1100s, where
   water-powered mills and printing later transformed it again. Notice: at every stop,
   people changed how paper was made and what they did with it.
3. **Order the stops (10 min).** Take the six cards and put them in the order paper
   traveled. Check with a partner.
4. **Record the change (10 min).** On your note, write the technology (paper), its path
   (from China to the world), and **one way it changed** as it moved (for example, new
   materials, new mills, or printing).
5. **Interleave an idea (5 min).** An idea can spread the same way. The number **zero** and
   the decimal system were developed in **India** and carried by scholars to **Baghdad**,
   then into **Europe** — changing how people counted everywhere. Same story, different
   traveler.
6. **Close (5 min).** When people meet, they share — and what they share changes. That
   exchange is one of the great engines of the human story.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use fewer stops with picture cards; scribe the learner's note and read the
  journey aloud slowly.
- **Extension:** Trace the number zero from India to Baghdad to Europe and write one way it
  changed as it moved, comparing it with paper.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner order paper's journey and name one way it
  changed as it traveled?
- **Self-check:** The learner asks, "Can I describe the path *and* the change — and explain
  why a borrowed technology changes when it moves?"

## Home connection

Pick one thing your family uses that came from far away (a food, a tool, a game) and find
out with someone at home how it changed on its way to you.

## Resources

- The invention of paper in China (refined by Cai Lun around 105 CE) and its spread along
  the Silk Roads to the Islamic world and Europe are standard in the history of technology
  (see the British Library's history of writing materials, <https://www.bl.uk/>, and the
  Smithsonian, <https://www.si.edu/>). The spread of the Indian decimal system and the
  concept of zero through the Islamic world to Europe is documented in the history of
  mathematics (see the work of historians such as George Gheverghese Joseph, *The Crest of
  the Peacock*). Dates are approximations that scholars revise as new evidence appears; the
  spread-and-change idea, not the exact year, is what matters here.
