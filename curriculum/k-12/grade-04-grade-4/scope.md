# Grade 4 Scope

## How to read this scope

Each `unit_id` in the frontmatter resolves to a unit file under `units/`.
`start_day`/`end_day` map the unit onto the 180-day school year; the list is a
continuous sequence from day 1 to day 180 with no gaps or overlaps. `domain` and
`pillar` name the primary domain(s) and pillar(s) each unit serves. `domain_weighting`
expresses the intended balance of instructional time across the ten domains, in
percent of the year. `lesson_count` counts the core lessons; the days between lessons
carry flex time, assessment, integration, and celebration. `assessment_plan`
describes how a child's growth is seen and recorded at this level.

## Year at a glance

The Grade 4 year is a spiral that deepens what Grade 3 began. It opens inward.
*Feelings in Full Color & My Growing Self* builds on Grade 3's mixed feelings and
changing feelings: now children describe the *strength* of a feeling — a little, some,
or a lot — and what each level feels like in the body, recognize complex feelings such
as jealousy, guilt, and pride and connect them to real situations, explain how their
interests, abilities, and experiences shape who they are, and describe a family or
cultural tradition and how it connects them to a larger community. They plan the
several steps of a goal, try the plan, and adjust when a step does not work, and
practice self-control by staying with a task or rule even when it is hard to keep
going. *My Body at Work & at Rest* then reads the body with new precision: how
heartbeat, breathing, and warmth change with activity and rest and why, noticing and
respecting the many ways bodies sense and move — including people who use aids or
assistive tools — and learning the rules of simple cooperative games and sports while
playing fairly, applying balance, agility, and coordination to adapted and seated
versions of movement alike.

*Listening, Perspective & Belonging* turns outward into the practice of care. Where
Grade 3 learned to take turns and use "I" statements, Grade 4 learns to *actively*
listen — paraphrasing what a partner said before responding — and to negotiate a fair
solution to a disagreement with a peer with only minimal adult help. They explain why
a person might feel the way they do using clues about what happened, compare their own
point of view with a classmate's on the same event, identify a role they can play in
their class or neighborhood and carry it out, describe how communities welcome
newcomers and why belonging matters, organize a small act of care, and explain the
difference between sharing for the common good and taking more than one's share. The
heart of the year is given to the two great tools of school, now working with real
independence. *Reading, Writing & Many Languages* builds fluency and comprehension:
summarizing the main idea and key details of an oral presentation, presenting a short
talk with clear volume and pace, using context clues and word parts such as roots,
prefixes, and suffixes to figure out unfamiliar words, comparing and contrasting
themes, characters, or ideas within and across two texts, writing a narrative,
informative, or opinion piece organized into paragraphs, and carrying a piece through
the whole writing process — plan, draft, revise, edit, and publish — on their own.
They learn that languages belong to families and share roots, and show respect for a
peer's language by learning and saying their name and a few words correctly — always
in the learner's home language first, with multilingualism held as wealth. *Numbers,
Patterns, Shapes & Data* grows from Grade 3's place value to 1,000 into place value to
1,000,000, multi-digit addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division checked by
estimation, number and shape patterns that follow a given rule, a letter or symbol
standing for an unknown number in a simple equation, points, lines, line segments,
rays, and angles with shapes classified by them, the area and perimeter of rectangles
by formula, converting measurements within a system to solve problems with distance,
time, volume, and money, and making and interpreting line plots with fractional
measurements — mathematics as sense-making with real things, drawn from many
traditions, for everyone.

From there the year looks out at the world. *Controlled Experiments & the Living
World* turns wonder into a careful, repeated test: measuring with tools such as a
ruler, scale, thermometer, or magnifier and recording with units, comparing results
across repeated trials and explaining why repeating a test matters, describing how
animals' body parts and behaviors help them survive, how living and nonliving things
in an ecosystem depend on one another, how energy moves from one place or object to
another as heat, sound, and light and is never destroyed, building a simple circuit
and explaining conductors and insulators, the water cycle, and how fossils and rock
layers give clues about Earth's long history. *Making, Music & Movement* gives craft
to the imagination — depth and space in art through overlapping, size, and placement,
simple notation read and played, a dance or movement form from another culture, a
character built with voice, body, and a simple prop, and design for a specific user
with feedback to improve it — while meeting the arts of other cultures as living
traditions to honor with respectful questions, never as specimens. *Healthy, Safe &
Strong* returns to the body with growing independence — reading simple nutrition
information on food packages to make a healthier choice, honoring the many ways
families and cultures eat healthy foods, the basic safety rules for home, road, water,
and weather, and safe, respectful behavior online, including not sharing private
information and knowing where to get help.

The final third steps into the wider human story. *People, Places & Long Ago* places
events from two different places on a dated timeline and compares what was happening
at the same time, compares daily life in an earlier society with life today, describes
a knowledge tradition — herbal medicine, star navigation, or oral history — and how it
is kept and passed on, compares how two cultures explain the same natural event
through story and observation, uses maps to locate landforms and bodies of water and
how they shaped settlement, how the environment shapes how people get food, water, and
shelter and how people change it, how rules and leaders make decisions and what people
can do when a decision is unfair, and a peaceful way people have resisted unfair
treatment. *Rights, Fairness & Our Shared Rules* weighs two reasons for a choice and
decides which is stronger, names what a value is and one value of one's own, explains
dignity and where it is honored or denied, evaluates whether a rule or practice is
fair to everyone it affects, compares how communities decide and choose leaders with
one other place in the world, works on a shared project dividing tasks fairly, and
practices active listening, "I" statements, and restorative practice — repairing harm
rather than only punishing. *Tools, Media & Our Earth Home* meets the idea of design,
compares two tools for the same job, identifies the author, purpose, and audience of a
message, checks a claim against a second source, describes an ecosystem and the jobs
of producers, consumers, and decomposers, follows what happens to trash and the
"reduce, reuse, recycle" order, and investigates how one human action can help or harm
a local place — with a first, real sense of what we can do. The year closes by
gathering all of it together in *All of Me, All of Us*, celebrating each whole person
and the caring community, and looking ahead to Grade 5.

Throughout, the four pillars are interwoven. Intellectual awareness (P3) carries the
most weight — reading toward chapter books and non-fiction comprehension, multi-digit
number work, and repeated, measured experiments are the year's central work — but
emotional and social awareness (P1) and physical and somatic awareness (P2) remain
strong, for a known, cared-for, and capable body-and-self is still the ground all
formal learning stands on. Contextual and ecological awareness (P4) keeps growing:
history moves toward dated timelines, comparison, and peaceful resistance; civics
toward rights, dignity, fairness, and repairing harm; and ecology toward ecosystems,
the jobs of living things, and the first responsibilities of stewardship. No
high-stakes tests anywhere: a child's growth is seen through reading aloud, writing,
number work with real things, measured and repeated tests, and a portfolio of what
they make, say, and do.
