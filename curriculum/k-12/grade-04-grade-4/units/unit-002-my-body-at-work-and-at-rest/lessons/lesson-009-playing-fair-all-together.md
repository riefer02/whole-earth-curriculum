# Lesson 9 — Playing Fair, All Together

## Summary

Learners practice **fair play** as a set of doable acts — taking turns, being honest,
encouraging others, and winning or losing with grace. They sort scenes as fair or
unfair, play the cooperative game again with fair play in mind, and make one fair-play
rule of their own.

## Objectives

- Learn and follow the rules of simple cooperative games and sports while playing
  fairly. _(D03.S2.04.01)_

## Connection

Fair play is not just following the rules with your head; it is playing with a fair
heart. It is letting someone else have a turn, telling the truth when you touched the
line, cheering a teammate who is trying hard, and shaking hands whether your side won or
lost. Everywhere people play — a football pitch in Lagos, a chessboard in a Moscow
café, a skipping game on a Hanoi street — the game means more when it is played fairly.

## Materials

- Fair-play scenario cards
- A simple cooperative game card (reused from Lesson 8)
- A soft ball or rolled cloth

## Preparation

- Print or write the fair-play scenario cards (fair and unfair scenes).
- Reuse the game and rules from Lesson 8, and clear the play space.
- Prepare a way to gather for sorting (thumbs-up / thumbs-down).

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Fair play is a skill and a value — teach
it explicitly with worked examples (model each fair act), then let learners practice in
the game. Keep it non-judging: an "unfair" scene is about the *act*, never the *child*,
and a learner who has not yet got it revisits and tries again — "not yet" is
information. Watch for the learner who always lets others go first but never gets a
turn themselves; fair play includes taking your own turn too. Celebrate every honest
admission and every encouraging word out loud. This lesson deepens Lesson 8's rules
with the heart of fairness, and leads into the final lesson on movement skills.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Yesterday you learned the rules of a game. Today you learn the
   heart of a game: **fair play**. What does "playing fair" look like to you?
2. **Sort the scenes (10 min).** Look at each scenario card and decide: is it fair or
   unfair? Thumbs-up for fair, thumbs-down for unfair. Talk about why. Scenes might be:
   taking your turn, cutting in line, admitting you touched the line, cheering a
   teammate, gloating after a win.
3. **Learn the fair-play acts (10 min).** Say the four acts of fair play: **take
   turns**, **be honest**, **encourage**, **be gracious** — whether you win or lose.
   Watch each one modeled.
4. **Play with fair play (15 min).** Play the cooperative game again from Lesson 8. This
   time, notice your own fair-play acts. Take your turn, tell the truth, cheer a
   teammate, and be gracious at the end.
5. **Make your rule (10 min).** Write or draw one fair-play rule of your own — something
   you will remember the next time you play. Share it with a partner.
6. **Close (5 min).** Say it together: "I play fair, so everyone can play." How did it
   feel to play fairly — and to be encouraged?

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Sort two scenes at a time, and model one fair-play act for the learner to
  copy during the game.
- **Extension:** Ask learners to write a short "fair-play charter" of three rules for the
  class to keep.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation/self/peer):** Can the learner name and practice the acts of
  fair play — turn, honesty, encouragement, grace — during the game?
