# Lesson 3 — Breathing Deep and Easy

## Summary

Learners compare their breathing **at rest** and **at work**. They count breaths while
still, move hard, and count again, discovering breathing speeds up with activity. They
learn **why**: the body breathes **in** to take in oxygen for working muscles, and
breathes **out** to clear away carbon dioxide, the waste gas the body makes as it burns
fuel.

## Objectives

- Describe how breathing changes with activity and rest, and explain why the body needs
  more oxygen when it moves. _(D03.S1.04.01)_

## Connection

Every few seconds, without thinking about it, you pull air in and let it go. Sit very
still and your breath is slow and small — deep and easy. Run up a hill and your breath
comes fast and deep. You already knew this without being taught; today you notice it on
purpose. Divers who hold their breath, singers who fill their lungs, and children
running home from school all feel the same thing: breathing is a body signal that
changes with what you are doing.

## Materials

- A lungs picture chart
- A timer or a clock with a second hand
- A before-and-after breathing sheet (at rest / after moving)

## Preparation

- Print or draw the lungs chart.
- Copy the before-and-after breathing sheet.
- Clear a safe space to move, and have a feather, leaf, or scrap of paper ready.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Model counting breaths at rest, moving,
and counting again — **worked example first, then guided practice**. Count one full
breath as a breath in *and* a breath out. Keep the movement burst inclusive; seated
arm-pumping raises breathing too. Some learners breathe through a tube or in other
ways — all ways of breathing count, and this is stated with dignity, never as a
"special" case. The lungs picture chart should be simple and honest, not anatomical
clutter. This lesson retrieves Lesson 1–2 (pulse, rest vs. work) and adds the breathing
signal.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** You have read your heartbeat at rest and at work. Today you read
   a second signal: your **breathing**. Put a hand on your belly. Feel it rise and fall.
2. **Count at rest (10 min).** Sit very still. Count your breaths for 15 seconds — each
   breath is one **breath in** and one **breath out**. Write the number in the "at
   rest" box. Notice how slow and small it is.
3. **Move (10 min).** Stand and move hard for about two minutes — march, jump, or pump
   your arms. Choose your own way to move.
4. **Count after moving (10 min).** Stop and sit. Count your breaths for 15 seconds.
   Write the number in the "after moving" box. Compare your two numbers. Which was
   faster?
5. **Ask why (10 min).** Your working muscles needed more **oxygen**, so you breathed in
   faster and deeper. Your body also made a waste gas called **carbon dioxide**, so you
   breathed out to clear it. At rest, the body needs less, so breathing slows. Watch a
   feather near your mouth to see your breath move the air.
6. **Close (5 min).** Say it together: "when I work, my breathing works too." Two
   signals now — heartbeat and breathing — both change with what your body does.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** A partner counts breaths aloud while the learner rests, then moves; use
  "slow" and "fast" words instead of numbers.
- **Extension:** Count breaths at rest and after moving for a full 30 seconds, and
  describe how deep or shallow each felt.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner count breaths at rest and after moving,
  and explain that breathing speeds up to bring in oxygen and clear carbon dioxide?
