# Lesson 8 — Rules of the Game

## Summary

Learners learn and follow the rules of a simple **cooperative** game. They read three
rules, put them in order, and play — discovering that rules are not walls but
agreements that make a game fair and fun for everyone, and that a cooperative game is
won **together**, not against each other.

## Objectives

- Learn and follow the rules of simple cooperative games and sports while playing
  fairly. _(D03.S2.04.01)_

## Connection

Every game you love has rules — how to pass, where to stand, when it is your turn.
Without rules, a game falls apart: nobody knows what to do, and someone always gets
left out or hurt. Rules are not there to spoil fun; they are the agreement that makes
fun possible for everyone. The same is true everywhere: ball games in a Brazilian
favela, a running game in a Kenyan schoolyard, a board game on a family mat — all of
them work because everyone agrees to the rules.

## Materials

- A simple cooperative game card (three clear rules)
- A soft ball, hoop, or rolled cloth
- Paper and pencils

## Preparation

- Choose or write one or two simple cooperative games with three clear rules each.
- Clear a safe space and gather the game object.
- Plan how to split the group into small teams.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Teach the rules with explicit
instruction — **read the three rules aloud, model them, then play** — because rules are
foundational knowledge a novice needs spelled out (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006).
Choose cooperative games (a shared goal — "keep the cloth aloft together," "pass the
hoop around the circle without dropping it"), not elimination games, so there are no
losers to rank. If a learner breaks a rule, treat it as information, not a verdict:
gently restate the rule and invite them to try again. This lesson opens the unit's
third strand — cooperative games and fair play — and leads into Lesson 9 on playing
fairly.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** You have learned to welcome every body. Today you learn how a
   game *works*: through its **rules**. Why do you think games have rules?
2. **Meet the game and its rules (10 min).** Listen as the three rules are read aloud.
   Say them back in order. Watch them modeled. For a passing game they might be:
   **(1)** pass to a teammate, **(2)** stay inside the circle, **(3)** everyone gets a
   turn.
3. **Write the rules (10 min).** On your paper, write or draw the three rules in order.
   This is your own copy of the agreement.
4. **Play the game (15 min).** In your group, play the game and follow the three rules.
   Help each other remember them. The goal is shared — your team wins *together* by
   keeping the game going.
5. **Reflect (10 min).** What happened when everyone followed the rules? What happened
   when someone forgot one? Which rule helped the most?
6. **Close (5 min).** Say it together: "rules make a game fair, and we play together,
   not against each other."

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use two rules instead of three, and have a partner point to each rule
  card as it is followed during play.
- **Extension:** Ask learners to write a fourth rule that would make the game even
  fairer or more welcoming, and try it.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation/peer):** Can the learner state the game's rules and follow
  them during play, helping teammates do the same?
