# Lesson 5 — My Body Dashboard

## Summary

Learners gather the three body signals — **heartbeat**, **breathing**, and **warmth** —
into one "body dashboard," the way a driver reads several dials at once. They mark each
signal at rest, move, and mark again, then write **why** each changes. The finished
dashboard is their own guide to reading the body at work and at rest — the unit's
portfolio artifact.

## Objectives

- Describe how heartbeat, breathing, and warmth each change with activity and rest, and
  explain why. _(D03.S1.04.01)_

## Connection

A pilot watches many dials at once to know how the plane is doing; a gardener checks
soil, sun, and water to know how a plant is doing. Your body has its own set of dials —
heart, breath, warmth — and you have learned to read all three. Today you put them on
one dashboard, so you can read your whole body at a glance: how it works, and how it
rests.

## Materials

- A body-dashboard sheet (heart, breath, warmth — each with a rest and work side)
- Crayons or markers
- A clear space to move

## Preparation

- Print or draw the body-dashboard sheet for each learner.
- Set out crayons and clear a safe space to move.
- Prepare a spot to share finished dashboards.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. This is the unit's portfolio artifact — the "my body at work and
at rest" record named in the assessment plan — and it closes the body-signals strand.
Guide the marking and keep it gentle: there is no "correct" number, and no body is
praised or shamed for how fast or warm it gets. A learner's dashboard is a record of
their noticing, never a grade. Honor each body's own pace — a fast, slow, or differently
feeling heart is all fine. If a learner cannot feel a signal, they may describe it or
use a partner's help. Dashboards are shared only if the learner chooses.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** You have read three signals in the last four lessons: **heartbeat,
   breathing, warmth.** Today you will put them all on one dashboard.
2. **Recall the signals (10 min).** Say each signal, what it does at rest, and what it
   does at work, and why: heart → slow at rest, faster at work (muscles need fuel and
   oxygen); breath → small at rest, big at work (oxygen in, carbon dioxide out); warmth →
   cool at rest, warm at work (muscles make heat).
3. **Mark at rest (10 min).** On your dashboard, mark each of the three dials at its
   rest position. Sit still and quiet first, and really notice.
4. **Move and mark at work (15 min).** Move hard for two minutes, then mark each dial at
   its work position. Your dashboard now shows all three signals, at rest and at work.
5. **Write the why (10 min).** Beside each dial, write or tell one sentence about why
   that signal changes.
6. **Share and close (5 min).** Share your dashboard with a partner if you want. Say it
   together: "I can read my body at work and at rest." Every dashboard is right for its
   own body.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Mark two dials at a time, or have a partner scribe while the learner
  points and marks each dial.
- **Extension:** Add a fourth dial — something else the learner notices about their body
  at work and rest (such as tiredness or thirst) — and its "why."

## Assessment

- **Summative (portfolio/observation):** Can the learner mark all three signals at rest
  and at work and explain why each one changes?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The completed body dashboard, kept in the learner's
  portfolio.

Show your body dashboard to someone at home and read it to them — all three signals and
why each one changes. Ask them if their body does anything a little differently.
