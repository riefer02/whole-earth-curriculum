# Lesson 1 — My Body's Quiet Engine

## Summary

Learners meet their heartbeat at **rest**. They find their **pulse** — the throb of the
heartbeat they can feel through the skin — on the wrist or the side of the neck, count
it for 15 seconds while sitting still, and describe it. The heartbeat is the first of
three body signals this unit will read: heartbeat, breathing, and warmth.

## Objectives

- Describe how the heartbeat feels and changes with activity and rest, and why.
  _(D03.S1.04.01)_

## Connection

Every moment — even right now, while you sit still — a pump inside your chest is
working. It is your heart, squeezing about once every second, pushing blood around your
body the way a river carries water to every field. You cannot usually hear it, but you
can feel it. Health workers in every country listen to hearts with a stethoscope, and
you can feel yours with just two fingers. A heartbeat you can find and count is a body
signal you can read.

## Materials

- A pulse-finding chart (wrist and side of the neck)
- A timer or a clock with a second hand
- Paper and pencils

## Preparation

- Print or draw the pulse-finding chart (two places to feel a pulse: wrist and neck).
- Have a timer ready, or teach the steady-count clap as a low-tech timer.
- Arrange a quiet circle where learners can sit still.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Model finding the pulse on yourself before learners try —
**worked example first, then guided practice**. Remind learners to use two gentle
fingers, never a thumb (the thumb has its own pulse), and to press lightly. Some
children cannot easily find a pulse; that is normal and not a failure — offer the
chest (hand flat over the heart) as an alternative, or let them count their breathing
instead. Never press on the neck too hard or for long. Keep it calm and curious, not a
test: there is no "correct" number, only each body's own number. This begins the
body-signals strand of the unit and builds on Grade 3's body-signal map.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Last year you learned to read four body signals: hunger,
   fullness, thirst, and tiredness. This year you read three new ones: your
   **heartbeat**, your **breathing**, and your **warmth**. Today, the heartbeat.
2. **Meet the heartbeat (10 min).** Your heart is a muscle that squeezes and relaxes,
   about once every second. Each squeeze pushes blood through your body. You can feel
   that squeeze as a **pulse**. Watch the worked example: press two fingers gently on
   the inside of the wrist, just below the thumb — or on the side of the neck. Feel the
   soft tap-tap-tap. That is your pulse.
3. **Find your own pulse (10 min).** Try it. Use two gentle fingers on your wrist or
   the side of your neck. If you cannot find it, lay your hand flat over your chest and
   feel the thump there. Sit very still and quiet.
4. **Count it (10 min).** With a timer or a steady clap, count your heartbeats for
   15 seconds. Say the number to yourself. It might be near 20 or 25 when you are
   resting — every body has its own number.
5. **Record and close (10 min).** Draw a small heart and write your resting count next
   to it. Say it together: "my heart beats even when I am still." How did it feel to
   find your own pulse?

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use the chest method (hand over the heart) with a partner counting the
  beats aloud; count for a shorter stretch if needed.
- **Extension:** Count for a full 30 seconds and multiply by two to estimate beats per
  minute, then compare with a partner.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner find a pulse (or heartbeat on the chest)
  and describe it as a steady, quiet beat at rest?
- **Self-check:** The learner reads back their resting count and confirms it felt
  steady and even.

Tell someone at home what you felt — a quiet, steady engine that never stops.
