# Lesson 2 — Heartbeat at Work

## Summary

Learners compare their heartbeat **at rest** and **at work**. They count their pulse
while still, move hard for about two minutes, then count again — and discover the
heartbeat speeds up with activity. They learn **why**: working muscles burn fuel and
need oxygen, and the faster heart pumps more blood to carry both to the muscles.

## Objectives

- Describe how the heartbeat changes with activity and rest, and explain why working
  muscles need a faster heartbeat. _(D03.S1.04.01)_

## Connection

Think of the last time you ran hard to catch up with a friend, chased a ball, or
helped carry water home. Your heart was pounding. That is not a problem — it is your
body's engine revving. When muscles work hard, they ask for more fuel and more oxygen,
and your heart answers by pumping faster. A runner in a mountain village, a child
playing football in a city street, a farmer working a field — every working body's
heart speeds up, then slows again when the work stops.

## Materials

- A clear space to move
- A timer or a clock with a second hand
- A before-and-after record sheet (at rest / after moving)

## Preparation

- Clear a safe space to march, jump, or step in place.
- Copy the before-and-after record sheet (two boxes: "at rest" and "after moving").
- Plan a two-minute movement burst everyone can join — including seated versions.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Run the worked example first: model
counting at rest, moving, and counting again, then let learners do it. Keep the
movement burst inclusive — marching in place, arm circles, or seated stomping all raise
the heartbeat; a wheelchair user can wheel or press arms. Remind learners that a fast
heartbeat is healthy and expected, not scary, and that every body's number is different
— there is no "best" count and no ranking. If a learner cannot find a pulse, the
hand-over-chest method works, or they can describe the feeling ("thumping fast"). This
lesson carries retrieval practice from Lesson 1 (finding the pulse) and adds the "why"
the standard asks for.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Recall: yesterday you found your pulse at rest. What did it feel
   like? Today you ask: what happens when you move?
2. **Count at rest (10 min).** Sit still and quiet. Find your pulse again and count it
   for 15 seconds. Write the number in the "at rest" box.
3. **Move (10 min).** Stand and move hard for about two minutes — march fast, jump
   gently, or pump your arms. Choose the way your own body moves. Notice what you feel.
4. **Count after moving (10 min).** Stop and sit. Find your pulse right away and count
   for 15 seconds. Write the number in the "after moving" box. Compare your two numbers.
5. **Ask why (10 min).** Why did your heart speed up? Your muscles were working, so they
   needed more **fuel** and **oxygen** from your blood. Your heart pumped faster to
   deliver them. When you rest, the need drops, and your heart slows down again.
6. **Close (5 min).** Say it together: "when I work, my heart works too." Your heartbeat
   is a signal that changes with what your body is doing.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** A partner counts aloud while the learner moves, then together they
  compare "rest" and "work" words instead of numbers ("slow" vs. "fast").
- **Extension:** Estimate beats per minute at rest and after moving, and describe the
  difference with numbers.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation/performance):** Can the learner count a pulse at rest and
  after moving, and say which was faster and why?

Run or dance at home for one minute, then feel your heartbeat. Tell someone why your
heart is pounding — it is delivering fuel and oxygen to your working muscles.
