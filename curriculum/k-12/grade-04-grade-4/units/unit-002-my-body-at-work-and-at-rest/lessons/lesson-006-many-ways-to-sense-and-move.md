# Lesson 6 — Many Ways to Sense and Move

## Summary

Learners notice and respect the many ways bodies sense and move. They match common aids
and assistive tools — **glasses**, **hearing aids**, **wheelchairs**, **canes**,
**prosthetics** — with the sense or movement each supports, and learn that an aid is a
tool that extends a body's own ability, not something that makes a body "less."

## Objectives

- Notice and respect differences in how bodies sense and move, including people who use
  aids or assistive tools. _(D03.S1.04.02)_

## Connection

Every body senses and moves a little differently — and every body, everywhere, uses
tools to do more. A spoon extends a hand; a rope extends an arm; glasses extend the
eyes. Some people use a wheelchair to move, a hearing aid to hear, a white cane to feel
the way ahead, or a prosthetic to walk. These are called **aids** or **assistive tools**.
They are not "less than" — they are a body's own clever way of doing things. All over the
world, people live full, joyful lives with them.

## Materials

- A "many ways" picture card set
- A matching sheet (aid → sense or movement it supports)
- Pencils and crayons

## Preparation

- Print or draw the "many ways" picture cards.
- Copy the matching sheet for each learner.
- Gather one or two real aids (a magnifier, a crutch, an extra pair of glasses) if
  available.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The goal is dignity and accurate
language, not pity: an aid is a tool, and a person is never defined by it. Use
person-first language naturally ("a person who uses a wheelchair," not "a
wheelchair-bound person") and model respectful curiosity. Invite learners who use aids
to share only if they want to — never put anyone on the spot. Keep the frame global:
aids look different across cultures and resources, from a wooden walking stick to a
high-tech hearing aid, and all are worthy of respect. This lesson retrieves Grade 3's
sense work (two senses team up) and opens the unit's second strand: honoring diverse
bodies. It also prepares for the next lesson on welcoming every body into play.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** You have been reading your own body's signals. Now you look at
   how *different* bodies sense and move. How does your body move today? How might a
   friend's body move differently?
2. **Meet the aids (15 min).** Look at the picture cards. Name each aid and what it
   supports: **glasses** help eyes see; a **hearing aid** helps ears hear; a
   **wheelchair** helps a person move; a **white cane** helps a person who cannot see
   feel the way ahead; a **prosthetic** replaces and helps a limb. Say it plainly: an
   aid is a tool, and the person using it is whole.
3. **Match them (15 min).** On your matching sheet, draw a line from each aid to the
   sense or movement it supports. With a partner, check your lines and talk about one
   you have seen in real life.
4. **Draw or write (10 min).** Draw one aid you have seen — or would like to know more
   about — and write one sentence about what it helps with.
5. **Close (5 min).** Say it together: "every body is different, and every body belongs."
   A body that senses or moves differently is not less — it is just different, like every
   body.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use a smaller set of three cards and match them together aloud; scribe
  the sentence the learner says.
- **Extension:** Ask learners to list two more aids they know (a step stool, a cane
  seat, a communication board) and explain what each supports.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name several aids and match each to the
  sense or movement it supports, speaking about them with respect?

## Home connection

Look around home and neighborhood for aids and tools that help people — glasses,
ramps, canes, a step stool. Tell someone at home one aid you noticed and what it helps
with.
