# Lesson 10 — Balance, Agility, Coordination

## Summary

Learners apply three movement skills — **balance**, **agility**, and **coordination** —
at three stations, each with a standing and a seated version, then build a short
routine that uses all three. Every body moves the skills its own way, and every version
counts fully. The lesson closes the unit with a celebration of bodies at work and at
rest.

## Objectives

- Apply movement skills such as balance, agility, and coordination to a variety of
  activities, including adapted or seated versions. _(D03.S2.04.02)_

## Connection

When you walk a narrow log over a stream, you use **balance**. When you dart between
people at a busy market without bumping anyone, you use **agility**. When you toss and
catch a ball while running, you use **coordination**. These three skills show up in
every sport and dance on Earth — a surfer balancing on a wave, a footballer weaving
past players, a drummer coordinating both hands. And every one of them can be done
standing, seated, or with wheels.

## Materials

- A movement station card set (balance, agility, coordination — standing and seated)
- A clear space to move
- Soft objects to toss and balance

## Preparation

- Set up three stations: balance, agility, coordination — each with a standing and a
  seated version.
- Clear the space and gather soft objects.
- Plan the rotation so each learner visits all three stations.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Model each skill at its station before
learners try — **worked example, then guided practice** — and always offer the seated
or supported version as an equal choice, not a "lesser" one. Balance can be done on one
foot *or* seated with arms out; agility can be weaving on foot *or* wheeling or sliding
around marks; coordination can be tossing hand to hand standing *or* seated. Honor every
version fully. Keep it a celebration, never a competition. This lesson closes the unit
and pulls together the whole arc — reading the body's signals (Lessons 1–5), honoring
diverse bodies (6–7), and playing fairly (8–9) — into joyful, inclusive movement.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** You have read your body at work and rest, welcomed every body,
   and played fairly. Today you move with three skills: **balance**, **agility**, and
   **coordination**. Which of these have you used today already?
2. **Meet the three skills (10 min).** Watch each one modeled. **Balance** — stay steady
   on a line or one foot (or seated, arms out like wings). **Agility** — change
   direction quickly, weaving around marks (or wheeling/sliding around them). **
   Coordination** — toss a soft object from hand to hand, or toss and catch (standing or
   seated).
3. **Visit the stations (20 min).** Rotate through all three stations. At each one, try
   the version that fits your body — standing, seated, or with support. Do each skill
   slowly at first, then a little faster.
4. **Build a routine (10 min).** Put the three skills together in a short routine: a
   balance, an agility weave, a coordination toss. Practice it twice.
5. **Show and celebrate (10 min).** Show your routine to a partner or the group. Watch
   your friends and cheer every version — standing, seated, or with wheels. Say it
   together: "every body can move, and every body belongs."

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Try one station at a time with a partner's hand or a wall for support;
  accept a two-skill routine instead of three.
- **Extension:** Ask learners to link the three skills into a longer, smoother routine
  and add a fourth skill of their own.

## Assessment

- **Formative (performance/observation/self):** Can the learner perform balance,
  agility, and coordination each in a version that fits their body, and combine them
  into a short routine?
