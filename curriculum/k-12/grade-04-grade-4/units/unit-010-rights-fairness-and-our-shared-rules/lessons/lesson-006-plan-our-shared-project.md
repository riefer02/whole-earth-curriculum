# Lesson 6 — Plan Our Shared Project

## Summary

Learners plan a small shared project together, **dividing the tasks fairly and
listening to each person** before any work begins. Fair division means every voice is
heard, the load is shared, and each person takes a task they can do and want — not the
loudest person grabbing the easiest job. This begins the unit's collective-action
standard (carried out in Lesson 7).

## Objectives

- Work with classmates on a small shared project, dividing tasks fairly and listening
  to each person. _(D09.S3.04.02)_

## Connection

A group can do more than one person alone — but only if the work is **shared fairly**.
Think of building a kindness wall, tidying a shared path, or making a welcome poster:
someone must draw, someone must write, someone must hang, and someone must check with
everyone. If one person does everything and another is never asked, the project is not
really *shared*. Fair division begins with **listening** — hearing what each person
wants to do and can do, then spreading the load so no one is left out and no one is
crushed. People everywhere plan this way — a crew lifting a boat, a family harvesting
together, a class making a garden. Today you will plan your project and divide the
tasks fairly, with every voice heard.

## Materials

- A "project plan" sheet per group
- A talking stick or stone
- Crayons or pencils

## Preparation

- Prepare the "project plan" sheet (title line, task list, "who does what" column, and
  a "did everyone get a voice?" checklist).
- Decide on two or three small, safe, doable project ideas groups can choose from (a
  kindness wall, a welcome poster, tidying a shared space, planting seeds).

## Facilitator note

Written to the learner ("you"). This lesson teaches **fair task division and listening**
— the planning half of the shared-project standard. Model the move first as a worked
example: name a project, list its tasks, and show how to offer tasks and hear each
person ("What would you like to do? What can you do well?") before writing a name by
each task. The core fairness test is **listening**: every person is asked, every voice
counts, and the load is shared — not the quickest or loudest person deciding. Keep
projects small and blame-free; the goal is the *process*, not a perfect product.
Emphasize that fair does not mean "everyone does exactly the same amount" — it means
each person contributes in a way that fits them, and no one is left out or overloaded.
A learner who does not speak aloud points to a task or passes the stone to signal
their turn is done; a learner who is deaf or hard of hearing follows the plan through
the sheet and sign; a learner who is blind or low-vision hears the task list read and
chooses aloud; a learner with a motor difference can be the scribe's director or the
checker. A learner may always pass or take a small, quiet task. The aim is a group
that can say, in its own way, "we planned this together, every voice was heard, and
the tasks are fairly shared."

## Procedure

1. **Gather and remember (5 min).** Last time you compared how people decide and choose
   leaders. What was one way? Today you will decide something *yourself* — as a team.
2. **Meet "fair division" (10 min).** A **project** is something you make or do
   together. Dividing the **tasks** fairly means: **listen** to every person, ask what
   each can do and wants to do, and share the load so no one is left out and no one is
   overloaded.
3. **Choose and list the tasks (10 min).** As a group, pick your small project. List the
   tasks it needs. Pass the talking stone so **each person** names one task they could
   do or want to do.
4. **Divide the tasks fairly (15 min).** Write or draw who does what. Check the
   checklist: did every person get a voice? Is the load shared fairly? Does each person
   want their task? Change it until everyone says yes.
5. **Close (5 min).** A fair plan hears every voice and shares the load. Tomorrow you
   will carry it out — together.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Plan with a two-task list and a grown-up asking each person "what can you
  do?"; a non-speaking learner points to a task; a learner with limited movement takes
  a directing or checking role.
- **Extension:** Invite groups to plan for a task that no one wants (the "boring but
  needed" job) and find a fair way to share it.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Did the group divide tasks fairly and hear every person
  before assigning work?
- **Performance:** The completed project plan, with each task matched to a person who
  was listened to.

## Home connection

At home, plan one small family job together — dinner setup, tidying, watering — and
divide the tasks by asking each person what they can do and want to do, so every voice
is heard.
