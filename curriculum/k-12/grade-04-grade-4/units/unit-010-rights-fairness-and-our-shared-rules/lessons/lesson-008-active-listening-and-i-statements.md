# Lesson 8 — Active Listening and "I" Statements

## Summary

Learners practice the two moves that turn a disagreement into a fair talk: **active
listening** (facing the speaker, hearing, and reflecting back) and **"I" statements**
("I feel ___ when ___ because ___. I need ___."). Saying "I" instead of "you" names
feelings without blame, so both people stay heard. This deepens Grade 3's "calm down
and talk it through" into the two named skills.

## Objectives

- Practice active listening and "I" statements to solve a disagreement fairly.
  _(D09.S4.04.01)_

## Connection

When two people disagree, the words can turn into a blame match — "you always," "you
never" — and no one ends up heard. There are two tools that change this. First, an
**"I" statement**: "I feel ___ when ___ because ___," which names *your own* feeling
without pointing at the other person. Second, **active listening**: facing the speaker,
really hearing them, and reflecting it back — "I hear that you feel ___." Saying "I"
instead of "you," and listening before answering, is how people everywhere turn a
fight into a talk — two neighbors over a fence, two friends over a ball, two
caregivers over a plan. Today you will practice both moves, so you can solve a
disagreement fairly.

## Materials

- "I statement" frame cards ("I feel ___ when ___ because ___. I need ___.")
- Disagreement role-play cards (two friends want the same ball; one was interrupted; a
  borrowed pencil came back broken)
- Two puppets or soft toys

## Preparation

- Prepare the "I statement" and "active listening" frame cards.
- Prepare a few small, invented, blame-free disagreement cards.

## Facilitator note

Written to the learner ("you"). This is a **skill** lesson, so teach it with a worked
example and guided practice (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006): model the exchange
with puppets once, then have learners rehearse in pairs before any independent use.
Name the two moves clearly: **"I" statement** (I feel ___ when ___ because ___; I need
___.) and **active listening** (face the speaker, hear them, reflect back "I hear that
you feel ___"). Emphasize that "I" statements name *feelings and needs*, not blame —
they are not a magic script, and sometimes the first try is clumsy; practice makes
them real. Use only invented, gentle disagreements; never have learners role-play a
real, live conflict in the class (that could embarrass someone). A learner who does
not speak aloud fills the frame by pointing to feeling-word cards or signing; a learner
who is deaf or hard of hearing follows the frames through sign and the cards; a learner
who is blind or low-vision hears the frames read and practices aloud; a learner with a
motor difference can direct a partner or use a communication aid. A learner may always
pass. The goal is a learner who can, in their own way, say "I feel ___" and then
listen — the two halves of a fair talk.

## Procedure

1. **Gather and remember (5 min).** Last time you finished a shared project as a team.
   What helped you work together? Today we practice a skill teams need when they
   *disagree*.
2. **Meet the two moves (10 min).** Watch the puppets. First, an **"I" statement**: "I
   feel ___ when ___ because ___. I need ___." It names my feeling, not blame. Then
   **active listening**: face the speaker, hear them, and reflect — "I hear that you
   feel ___." No interrupting, no "but you..."
3. **Guided practice (15 min).** Pick a disagreement card. One partner speaks the "I"
   statement; the other listens and reflects. Then switch roles. Try all the frame
   lines, even if it feels a little silly at first.
4. **Try your own (10 min).** With a partner, invent a small, safe disagreement and run
   the whole exchange: "I feel...", "I hear that you feel...", then talk out a fair fix.
   If speaking is not your way, fill the frame by pointing to feeling words or sign.
5. **Close (5 min).** "I" instead of "you" names feelings without blame. Listening
   before answering keeps both people heard. That is how a disagreement becomes a fair
   talk.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Practice one line at a time with a partner and a single feeling word
  (sad, mad, left out); a non-speaking learner points to feeling-word cards; a learner
  with limited movement directs a partner.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to add the fair-fix step ("so what can we both do?")
  and practice a full three-turn exchange.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner use an "I" statement and active
  listening (reflect back) in a practiced disagreement?
- **Performance:** The modeled or role-played exchange, observed for both moves.

## Home connection

Tonight, if a small disagreement comes up at home, try one "I" statement ("I feel ___
when ___") and listen to the other person's turn before answering. Notice what
changes.
