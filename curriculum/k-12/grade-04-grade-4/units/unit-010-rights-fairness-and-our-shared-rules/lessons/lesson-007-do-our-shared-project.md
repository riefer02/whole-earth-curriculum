# Lesson 7 — Do Our Shared Project

## Summary

Learners carry out the project they planned in Lesson 6, **checking in and listening**
as they work, and close by reflecting on what each person did, how they listened to one
another, and what they would do better next time. The product matters less than the
*team* that made it.

## Objectives

- Work with classmates on a small shared project, dividing tasks fairly and listening
  to each person. _(D09.S3.04.02)_

## Connection

A plan is only a promise until the team makes it real. Carrying out a shared project —
the kindness wall, the tidy path, the planted seeds — is where fair division becomes
visible: each person doing their task, and everyone **checking in** so no one is stuck
or left out. A good team does not just split the work and walk away; it watches for the
person who needs a hand and asks "are you stuck? can I help?" Farmers harvest in teams
this way, fishers pull a net together, and builders pass bricks down a line. Today you
will carry out your plan and finish as a team.

## Materials

- The project plan from Lesson 6
- Project supplies (paper, markers, tape, or found objects)
- A "how did we work together?" reflection sheet per learner

## Preparation

- Return each group's completed plan and gather the supplies for each project.
- Prepare the reflection sheet (what I did; how we listened; what I would do better).

## Facilitator note

Written to the learner ("you"). This is the **doing** half of the shared-project
standard. Keep the emphasis on the *team process*, not the product: praise the check-in
("you noticed they were stuck and helped") as much as the finished work. Rotate among
groups and model the check-in question aloud ("are you stuck? can I help?") so it
becomes the group's habit. Watch for the quiet or slower worker and make sure they are
neither left out nor silently overloaded — a checker role helps. The reflection is the
assessment: it asks *what each person did* (so no one's contribution is invisible) and
*how the group listened*. Never rank projects against one another; every finished
project is a win for its team. A learner who does not speak aloud reflects by pointing
to a picture or a task on the plan; a learner who is deaf or hard of hearing reflects
through sign and the sheet; a learner who is blind or low-vision reflects aloud while
the sheet is read back; a learner with a motor difference can describe their
contribution while a partner scribes. A learner may always pass or reflect privately.
The aim is a team that can say, in its own way, "we did it together — every person
contributed, and we listened to each other."

## Procedure

1. **Gather and remember (5 min).** Look at your plan from last time. What project did
   you choose, and who does what? That was your fair plan — now make it real.
2. **Do the work (25 min).** Each person does their task. As you work, **check in**:
   ask "are you stuck? can I help?" and listen to the answer. If a task is too big or
   too small, pause and re-share the load so it stays fair.
3. **Finish and share (5 min).** Step back and look at what you made together. Name one
   thing each person did — so no one's work is invisible.
4. **Reflect (5 min).** On your sheet, finish the three lines: "what I did," "how we
   listened to each other," and "what I would do better next time." Share one line with
   the group.
5. **Close (5 min).** You did it — together. A fair team shares the work *and* checks in
   on each other.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Do the task beside a partner; reflect with a spoken sentence frame ("I
  did ___; we listened by ___"); a non-speaking learner points to their task on the
  plan; a learner with limited movement directs a partner.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to plan one improvement for next time — a task that
  was unfair or a person who was not heard — and say how they would fix it.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Did each person carry out their task, and did the group
  check in and listen to one another while working?
- **Performance / portfolio:** The finished project (photographed or drawn) plus the
  reflection sheet, kept as a portfolio record of fair teamwork.

## Home connection

Finish one shared family job this week, checking in on each other as you go. Then ask
together: "what did each of us do, and how did we listen to each other?"
