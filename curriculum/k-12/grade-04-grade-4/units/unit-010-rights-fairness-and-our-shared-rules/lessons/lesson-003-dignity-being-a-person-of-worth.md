# Lesson 3 — Dignity: Being a Person of Worth

## Summary

Learners learn that **dignity** is being treated as a **person of worth**, and they
give an example of dignity being **honored** (greeted by name, listened to, given a
seat) or **denied** (ignored, talked over, left out). Dignity belongs to every person —
it is not earned, and it cannot be taken away, only denied. This deepens Grade 3's
"left out and made more fair" into the fuller idea of a person's worth.

## Objectives

- Explain what dignity means (being treated as a person of worth) and give an example
  of it being honored or denied. _(D09.S2.04.01)_

## Connection

Everyone wants to be seen as a person who **matters**. When someone greets you by your
name, listens to what you say, and makes room for you, you feel your own **worth** —
that is your **dignity** being honored. When someone ignores you, talks over you, or
leaves you standing outside while others sit, you can feel your worth being pushed
down — that is your dignity being **denied**. Dignity is not something you have to earn
by being clever or strong or rich; every person is born with worth. People honor it
everywhere — a host offering a guest water and a seat, a class that saves a chair for
the new child, a speaker who waits for the quiet person's answer. Today you will learn
what dignity means and name a moment it was honored or denied.

## Materials

- A "dignity honored / dignity denied" picture strip
- Two puppets or soft toys
- Paper and drawing tools

## Preparation

- Prepare the two-panel dignity strip (one scene, treated two ways).
- Set up a small space for acting out honored and denied moments.

## Facilitator note

Written to the learner ("you"). This lesson teaches **dignity** — the worth every
person has, simply by being a person. Hold it with care and **never as shame**: the
"denied" examples are *invented and gentle* (ignored, talked over, left standing), and
the point is the *act*, never the *person*. No learner is ever asked to confess a time
they denied someone's dignity, and no learner is ever labeled as having denied it.
Frame both panels as things that happen in the world, and hold "what does each way of
treating someone do to their worth?" as a question. Emphasize that dignity cannot be
*taken* — it can only be *denied* — so the learning ends in agency and hope: we can
choose to honor it. A learner who does not speak aloud points to the honored panel or
signs; a learner who is deaf or hard of hearing follows the strip through sign and
caption; a learner who is blind or low-vision hears both panels described and names
which honors and which denies worth, and why; a learner with a motor difference can
direct a partner to act the scene. A learner may always pass. The aim is that every
learner can say, in their own way, "dignity means being treated as a person of worth —
here is a moment it was honored, and here is one it was denied."

## Procedure

1. **Gather and remember (5 min).** Last time you named a value — something you care
   about. What is a value? Today we look at something every person has: their worth.
2. **Meet "dignity" (10 min).** **Dignity** means being treated as a **person of
   worth**. It is **honored** when you are greeted by name, listened to, and given a
   place. It is **denied** when you are ignored, talked over, or left out. Dignity is
   not earned — every person is born with it.
3. **Compare the two panels (15 min).** Look at the strip: one scene, two ways. In the
   "honored" panel, name what shows worth (the greeting, the listening, the seat). In
   the "denied" panel, name what pushes worth down. Wonder together: what does each way
   do to the person? Could *you* choose to honor it?
4. **Act it (10 min).** With the puppets, show a small moment of dignity honored (a
   warm "hello, come sit with us"), then one denied (being talked over). A learner who
   does not move the puppets can point to the honored or denied panel or direct a
   partner.
5. **Close (5 min).** Dignity is being treated as a person of worth. It is yours, and
   it is everyone's — and we can choose to honor it.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Sort two picture cards into "honored" and "denied" with a grown-up
  model; a non-speaking learner points to the honored panel; a learner with limited
  movement directs a partner.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to wonder what to do when they see someone's dignity
  being denied — a small, safe action that honors it instead.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner explain what dignity means and give an
  example of it being honored or denied?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The learner's drawing of one honored or denied moment, with
  a one-line explanation.

## Home connection

At home, notice a small moment of dignity honored (a greeting, a seat offered, being
listened to) and name it together: "that was someone's worth being seen."
