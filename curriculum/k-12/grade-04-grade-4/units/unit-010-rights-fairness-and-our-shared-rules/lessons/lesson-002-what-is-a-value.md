# Lesson 2 — What Is a Value? Naming One of Mine

## Summary

Learners learn that a **value** is something a person cares about that guides how they
choose and act, and each learner names **one of their own values** with a "because I
care about..." A value is not a test answer — it is a real care, held as a question and
a guide. This builds on Grade 3's reasons and needs toward the deeper idea of what a
person holds dear.

## Objectives

- Describe what a value is (something a person cares about) and name one of their own
  values. _(D09.S1.04.02)_

## Connection

Everyone has things they **care about** — things that make them say "that matters to
me." You might care about fairness, honesty, your family, learning, nature, or being
kind. When you care about something deeply enough to let it guide your choices, that is
a **value**. People everywhere hold values — a fisher who returns a fish too small to
keep is acting on care for the river's future; a friend who tells the truth even when
it is hard is acting on honesty; a family that shares its last bread is acting on
care for each other. Today you will learn what a value is, and name one value that is
really yours.

## Materials

- Values cards (fairness, honesty, kindness, family, learning, nature, health, courage,
  safety, friendship)
- A "my value" sheet per learner
- Crayons or pencils

## Preparation

- Prepare the values cards, or decide which values to act out aloud.
- Prepare the "my value" sheet with a heart or star outline and a "because I care
  about..." line.

## Facilitator note

Written to the learner ("you"). This lesson introduces **values** gently — as things a
person genuinely cares about, not as a list to be graded. Model naming a value yourself
first ("I value honesty — because I care about people being able to trust my word"),
then invite learners to name their own. A value is personal, and there is no correct
answer: a learner may name anything from "my family" to "fairness" to "the river."
Hold values as *questions and guides*, never as a ranking — no learner's value is
"better" than another's, and we never shame a value that differs from our own. Some
values conflict (honesty vs. kindness), and that is worth wondering about later in the
unit. A learner who does not speak aloud points to a values card or draws their value;
a learner who is deaf or hard of hearing follows the cards through sign; a learner who
is blind or low-vision hears each value described and names their own aloud; a learner
with a motor difference can direct a partner to circle their value. A learner may
always pass, or name a value privately to a grown-up. The goal is a learner who can
say, in their own way, "this is something I care about — this is a value of mine."

## Procedure

1. **Gather and remember (5 min).** Last time you compared two reasons and decided
   which was stronger. What helped you decide? Today we look at what *lies under* our
   reasons: the things we care about.
2. **Meet "value" (10 min).** A **value** is something a person cares about that guides
   how they choose and act. It is not a fact you memorize; it is a care you hold. Look
   at the values cards and name each one: fairness, honesty, kindness, family, learning,
   nature, health, courage, safety, friendship.
3. **Sort and choose (10 min).** Which cards show things *you* care about most? Put
   them in order for yourself. Wonder: how do you know you care about it? (It makes you
   feel something; you would act on it.)
4. **Name your value (15 min).** On your sheet, draw or write **one value** that is
   yours, and finish the sentence: "I value ______ because I care about ______." Share
   with a partner if you wish. If drawing or writing is not your way, act it out, point
   to a card, or have a partner scribe while you direct.
5. **Close (5 min).** A value is something you care about that guides you. Today you
   named one of yours — and no one can take that from you.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer a smaller set of cards and a spoken sentence frame ("I care about
  ___ so I value ___"); a non-speaking learner points to a card; a learner with limited
  movement directs a partner.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to name a time they *acted on* their value, and wonder
  how it felt.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner describe what a value is (something a
  person cares about) and name one of their own with a "because"?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The learner's "my value" sheet, kept in their portfolio.

## Home connection

Ask a grown-up or older family member: "What is one thing you care about a lot, and
how does it guide what you do?" Listen, then share your own value with them.
