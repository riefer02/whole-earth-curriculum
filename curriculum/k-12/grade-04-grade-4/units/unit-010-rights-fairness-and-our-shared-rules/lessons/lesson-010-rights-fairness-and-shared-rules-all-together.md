# Lesson 10 — Rights, Fairness & Our Shared Rules, All Together

## Summary

Learners gather everything from the unit into one eight-part "fairness" page: stronger
reason, my value, dignity honored or denied, a rule on trial, decisions here and there,
our shared project, "I" statements and listening, and restorative practice. They share
their page and build a class "Rights, Fairness & Our Shared Rules" book.

## Objectives

- Weigh two reasons for a choice and decide which is stronger and why. _(D09.S1.04.01)_
- Describe what a value is and name one of their own values. _(D09.S1.04.02)_
- Explain what dignity means and give an example of it being honored or denied.
  _(D09.S2.04.01)_
- Evaluate whether a rule or practice is fair to everyone it affects and explain the
  judgment. _(D09.S2.04.02)_
- Describe how people make decisions and choose leaders in their community and compare
  with one other place in the world. _(D09.S3.04.01)_
- Work with classmates on a small shared project, dividing tasks fairly and listening
  to each person. _(D09.S3.04.02)_
- Practice active listening and "I" statements to solve a disagreement fairly.
  _(D09.S4.04.01)_
- Describe restorative practice and why it can work better than punishment alone.
  _(D09.S4.04.02)_

## Connection

Rights and fairness are not one big thing — they are many small things you can do every
day: weigh two reasons and judge which is stronger, name a value you hold, honor
dignity, test a rule against everyone it affects, see how decisions are made here and
elsewhere, share a project fairly, listen and speak with "I," and repair harm instead
of only punishing. When you put them together, you are practicing how to live fairly
with other people — at home, in class, and anywhere on Earth people are together. Today
you will put them all together.

## Materials

- An eight-part "fairness" page per learner
- Crayons and drawing tools
- Stapler, string, or clips for a class book

## Preparation

- Prepare an eight-box page for each learner (or one large shared sheet): "stronger
  reason," "my value," "dignity honored or denied," "a rule on trial," "here and
  there," "our shared project," "I statements and listening," and "restorative
  practice."
- Set up a gathering place and a spot to assemble the class book.

## Facilitator note

Written to the learner ("you"). This is the unit's capstone: each learner authors their
own small statement of rights, fairness, and shared rules. Keep all eight boxes
optional in depth — a word, a mark, or a drawing counts. This is a celebration of what
learners can *do*, not a test of what they know; there is no "correct" page. Celebrate
each page as a real contribution, and never rank pages against one another. Hold the
dignity and restorative boxes with the same care as in Lessons 3 and 9: no shaming, no
forced confession, no performed apology — invented examples and "always may pass"
apply here too. A learner who does not speak aloud shares by pointing to a box and a
picture, or by signing; a learner who is blind or low-vision describes their page aloud
and the boxes are read back to them; a learner with a motor difference can have a
partner draw while they choose and direct. Every voice matters — not just the loudest
or fastest — and a learner may always pass. The page stays live after this lesson, so
the unit's habits continue beyond the paper. Questions stay questions: rights,
fairness, and shared rules are practiced, not preached.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Remember the unit: stronger reason, my value, dignity, a rule on
   trial, decisions here and there, our shared project, "I" statements and listening,
   and restorative practice. You have practiced all of it.
2. **Fill the eight boxes (20 min).** On your page, draw or write one thing in each box:
   two reasons with the stronger one marked and why; one value of yours; a moment of
   dignity honored or denied; a rule judged fair or unfair to everyone; how your
   community decides and one other place's way; your shared project and its fair tasks;
   an "I" statement with a listening reflection; and the three restorative steps.
3. **Share (10 min).** With a partner or small group, show your page and tell about one
   box you are proud of. Listen to theirs.
4. **Make the class book (5 min).** Add your page to the "Rights, Fairness & Our Shared
   Rules" book or wall. Now everyone's fairness is together.
5. **Close (5 min).** Rights and fairness are many small things you can do every day.
   Your choices matter — to your friends, your community, and you.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Complete one box at a time with a grown-up scribe; a single word or mark
  in each box counts. A non-speaking learner points to pictures for each box.
- **Extension:** Invite learners to add a ninth box: "tomorrow" — one fair or caring
  thing they will do next.

## Assessment

- **Summative (portfolio):** The eight-part "fairness" page shows a weighed choice, a
  named value, a dignity example, a judged rule, a here-and-there comparison, a shared
  project, an "I" statement with listening, and the restorative steps.
- **Observation:** Does the learner connect each box to the right idea from the unit and
  share it in their own words?

## Home connection

Take your "fairness" page home and share one box with your family. Ask them what
fairness means to them, and listen.
