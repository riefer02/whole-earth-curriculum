# Lesson 1 — Depth in Art

## Summary

Learners discover that a flat page is not really flat to an artist. Three tricks —
**overlapping** (one shape covers part of another), **size** (near things look big,
far things look small), and **placement** (near things sit low on the page, far things
sit high, near the **horizon**) — make a picture feel deep. Learners practice each
trick, then make one artwork that uses all three.

## Objectives

- Create an artwork that shows depth or space by overlapping, size, or placement.
  _(D07.S1.04.01)_

## Connection

Stand at one end of a street, a path, or a field and look far away. The tree next to
you looks big; the same kind of tree far down the road looks tiny. The road itself
seems to get narrower the farther it goes. A flat picture has no real distance, so
artists copy these clues onto the page — big and low for near, small and high for far,
one thing tucked in front of another — and our eyes read it as depth. In a busy city,
in a mountain village, or on the open sea, the same three tricks make a drawing feel
like you could walk into it.

## Materials

- Paper and drawing tools (crayons, colored pencils, or markers)
- A few pictures that show depth (landscapes, street scenes, seascapes)

## Preparation

- Print or display the depth guide graphic and a few pictures that show depth.
- Have paper and drawing tools ready for each learner.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Showing depth is a **skill**, so model
each trick as a worked example on the board — draw a circle in front of a square, a
big tree and a small tree, a low tree and a high mountain — before learners try their
own (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006). A learner who is blind or has low vision can
feel depth in a **collage**: layered cut shapes (overlap), a big and a small shape
(size), and a shape placed low or high on a page (placement) with a partner naming
each. A learner with limited fine motor may direct a partner who places the shapes.
The goal is that a viewer can *name* where each trick appears, not that the drawing is
polished.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Last year you used pattern and mood in your art. Today you learn
   something new: how to make a flat page feel **deep**. Artists use three tricks.
2. **Meet the three tricks (10 min).** Look at the guide graphic.
   - **Overlap:** one shape covers part of another. The covered shape looks *behind*.
   - **Size:** near things look **big**, far things look **small**.
   - **Placement:** near things sit **low** on the page; far things sit **high**, near
     the horizon line where sky meets ground.
   Look at the example pictures and point to where you see each trick.
3. **Practice each trick (10 min).** On your paper, try all three: draw a circle that
   overlaps a square; draw a big tree and a small tree; draw a low tree and a high
   mountain with a horizon line between them.
4. **Make your artwork (15 min).** Make one picture — a street, a path, a harbor, a
   field, a room, anything you choose — that uses **all three tricks** to feel deep.
   Put your near things big and low, your far things small and high, and let some
   shapes overlap.
5. **Share and close (5 min).** Show a partner. Can they point to your overlap, your
   size change, and your placement? Remember: a flat page can hold a whole deep world.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer a pre-drawn horizon line and three ready shapes (a big tree, a
  small tree, a mountain) to place; focus on one trick at a time.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Build depth as a collage of layered, different-sized
  shapes placed low and high, with a partner naming each trick.
- **Support (fine motor):** Direct a partner who places the shapes, or use big cut
  shapes instead of drawing.
- **Extension:** Add a middle-ground object between the foreground and background, and
  explain which trick each part uses.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Does the learner's artwork use overlap, size, or
  placement, and can they point to where each appears?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The depth artwork (or a photo of it), kept in the portfolio.

## Home connection

With someone at home, look down a real street or path together and notice what looks
big and near versus small and far. Then draw it, using the three tricks you learned.
