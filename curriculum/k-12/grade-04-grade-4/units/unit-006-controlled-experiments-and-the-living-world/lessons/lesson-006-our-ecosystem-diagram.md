# Lesson 6 — Our Ecosystem Diagram

## Summary

Lessons 4 and 5 gave learners two living-world ideas — **adaptations** (body parts and
behaviors that help survival) and **ecosystems** (living and nonliving things depending on
each other). Now they join them into one labeled **diagram**. Learners draw an ecosystem
with living and nonliving parts, arrows showing who depends on whom, and at least one
animal **adaptation**, then self-check against a short checklist. The finished diagram
becomes a portfolio artifact — one picture that shows how the living world fits together.

## Objectives

- Describe how animals' body parts and behaviors help them survive in their environment.
  _(D06.S2.04.01)_
- Explain how living and nonliving things in an ecosystem depend on each other (such as
  soil, water, and sunlight). _(D06.S2.04.02)_

## Connection

A map helps a traveler see a whole place at once. A **diagram** does the same for ideas.
When you draw a pond with its plants, its frog, its soil, and its sunlight, and you draw
**arrows** from the frog to the plant it eats and from the plant to the sun that feeds it,
you have made a picture of dependence that anyone can read. Adding the frog's strong
**legs** shows how its body helps it survive there. Diagrams are how people everywhere —
farmers planning a field, fishers reading a coast, doctors mapping a body — put the pieces
together so others can understand.

## Materials

- Large paper and crayons or colored pencils
- The example ecosystem diagram
- The animal and ecosystem card sets from Lessons 4 and 5

## Preparation

- Display the example ecosystem diagram where everyone can see it.
- Have the card sets ready for planning.
- Prepare the diagram checklist (or write it on the board): living parts, nonliving
  parts, arrows, and an adaptation.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It is a synthesis lesson, so use a
**worked example first** — draw one diagram together on the board, pointing to each part,
arrow, and label — then let learners draw their own (guided practice) and self-check
against the checklist. Feedback is built in three ways: the checklist (self), a
partner-check (peer), and your glance (observation). Keep the egalitarian lens: the
diagram must show that the nonliving parts and the small creatures matter as much as the
big ones — a worm or a patch of soil belongs in the picture as much as a lion. Encourage
ecosystems from many places (a rice paddy, a coral reef, a desert, a city park) so every
learner can draw a home that is theirs (global lens). This artifact is kept for the unit
portfolio; celebrate each diagram, and let a learner who is not finished return to it —
"not yet" is information, never a verdict.

## Procedure

1. **Gather and remember (5 min).** You have two ideas: adaptations and ecosystems. Today
   you will show both in one picture — a **diagram**.
2. **Watch the example (10 min).** Look at the example diagram. Point to a **living**
   part, a **nonliving** part, an **arrow** showing dependence, and an **adaptation**. Say
   what each arrow means: "The frog depends on the plant."
3. **Plan your diagram (10 min).** Choose an ecosystem you know or can imagine — a pond, a
   field, a forest, a reef, a garden. List what you will draw: at least two **living**
   parts, two **nonliving** parts, and one **adaptation**.
4. **Draw and label (20 min).** Draw your ecosystem. Label each part **living** or
   **nonliving**. Draw **arrows** to show who depends on whom. Label one animal's
   **adaptation** and write how it helps it survive.
5. **Check your work (10 min).** Use the checklist: living parts? nonliving parts?
   arrows? an adaptation? Swap with a partner and ask: can they follow your arrows? Close
   by remembering: every part of the web matters.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Draw a three-part diagram (plant, water, sun) with a partner, and label
  "living" and "nonliving" aloud.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Draw the parts and point to the dependence arrows; a partner
  writes the labels.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Build the diagram with found objects (a leaf, a stone, a
  cup of water, a feather) and describe each part and arrow aloud.
- **Extension:** Add a second ecosystem and a note about what would happen if one
  nonliving part (the water) were removed from both.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Does the diagram include living and nonliving parts,
  arrows of dependence, and one labeled adaptation?
- **Self:** The learner checks the checklist and marks anything they want to improve.
- **Peer:** A partner reads the arrows and confirms they make sense.
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The finished labeled ecosystem diagram, kept for the unit
  portfolio.

## Home connection

At home, draw a quick diagram of one real place with a grown-up — the kitchen garden, the
balcony, or the backyard — and add two arrows showing what depends on what.
