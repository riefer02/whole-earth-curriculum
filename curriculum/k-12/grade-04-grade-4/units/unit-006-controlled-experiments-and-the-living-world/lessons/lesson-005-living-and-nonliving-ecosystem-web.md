# Lesson 5 — Living and Nonliving: An Ecosystem Web

## Summary

An **ecosystem** is everything — living and nonliving — in a place, and they all depend on
one another. Learners sort the parts into **living** (a plant, a worm, a fish, a bird) and
**nonliving** (**soil**, **water**, **sunlight**, **air**), then build a string **web**
that shows the dependence: the plant needs soil, water, sunlight, and air; the animal
needs the plant and the water; the worm returns life to the soil. Take one part away and
the whole web pulls. Learners see that a worm and a river are as vital as a lion.

## Objectives

- Explain how living and nonliving things in an ecosystem depend on each other (such as
  soil, water, and sunlight). _(D06.S2.04.02)_

## Connection

Think of the place where you live — a field, a garden, a riverbank, a city park. The
plants there do not grow alone. They need **sunlight** to make food, **water** to drink,
**soil** to stand in, and **air** to breathe. The animals need the plants and the water.
And when living things die, tiny living things and **soil** break them down so new plants
can grow. That is an **ecosystem** — living and nonliving things depending on each other,
the way the people in a village depend on the well, the soil, and each other. Pull out one
part and everyone notices.

## Materials

- Ecosystem picture cards (Sun, water, soil, air, a plant, a worm, a fish, a person or bird)
- A ball of string
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Prepare and shuffle the picture cards.
- Clear a circle of floor or ground for the string web.
- Keep a large sheet ready to draw the finished web together.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Teach with a **worked example** — model one
connection ("the plant depends on sunlight") — then let learners build the web together
(guided practice) before drawing their own. The central value: an ecosystem is a *circle
of dependence*, not a ladder; the nonliving parts — soil, water, sunlight, air — are not
"background" but full partners in keeping life alive (the egalitarian, ethics, and
environment lenses). Use ecosystems from many places — a rice paddy, a coral reef, a
desert, a forest — so no single landscape is the default (global lens). This is evidence
from ecology, gathered by observing many places over many years; hold it with wonder and
care. The web activity doubles as a kinesthetic and social lesson: everyone holds the
string, and everyone matters.

## Procedure

1. **Gather and remember (5 min).** Yesterday you saw how an animal's body parts help it
   survive. But an animal never survives alone. Today: who does everything depend on?
2. **Sort living and nonliving (10 min).** An **ecosystem** is all the living and
   nonliving things in a place. Sort the cards: which are **living** (they grow, move, and
   need food)? Which are **nonliving** (**soil**, **water**, **sunlight**, **air**)? Say
   each name as you sort.
3. **Build the web (15 min).** Hold the cards in a circle. Pass the string from each thing
   to what it depends on: the plant to the sunlight, the plant to the water, the plant to
   the soil; the worm to the plant; the fish to the water; the bird to the fish. Say each
   connection aloud.
4. **Feel the web (10 min).** Everyone pulls gently on the string. Now imagine one part is
   gone — the water dries up, or the soil washes away. Let that part drop the string. What
   happens to the web?
5. **Close (5 min).** Living and nonliving things depend on each other. Every part of the
   web matters — the soil and the sunlight as much as the lion. Tomorrow you will draw
   your own ecosystem.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Sort just three cards — one living, two nonliving — and say who depends on
  whom with a partner.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Pass the string from a living card to a nonliving card it
  needs; a partner names the connection.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Feel soil, water, and a leaf, and describe what each
  gives to a plant; build the web by passing the string and listening for names.
- **Extension:** Choose a second ecosystem (a desert or an ocean) and tell how its living
  and nonliving parts depend on each other differently.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name one nonliving thing a plant depends on
  and one living thing that depends on the plant?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The learner's ecosystem web drawing with living and nonliving
  parts labeled, kept for the unit portfolio.

## Home connection

At home, find one small ecosystem with a grown-up — a windowsill plant, a garden patch, or
a puddle — and name two living and two nonliving things in it and how they depend on each
other.
