# Lesson 7 — Energy Moves: Heat, Sound, Light

## Summary

Energy is the ability to make things happen, and it never simply disappears. Learners
trace three kinds of energy — **heat**, **sound**, and **light** — as each moves from a
**source** to a **receiver**. A warm stone passes heat to a cool one; a drum sends sound to
an ear; a lamp sends light to a page. In every case the energy goes somewhere and does
something: it is **never destroyed**, only moved or changed. Learners record the source,
what moves, and the receiver for three transfers they find themselves.

## Objectives

- Describe how energy moves from one place or object to another (such as heat, sound, and
  light) and is never destroyed. _(D06.S3.04.01)_

## Connection

Touch a cup that has been sitting in the sun. Warm, isn't it? That warmth is **energy**
that traveled as **light** from the Sun, landed on the cup, and became **heat**. Clap your
hands and hear the **sound** travel through the air to your ear. Shine a light on a wall
and watch the **light** leave the lamp and land on the wall. Energy is always moving from
one place to another — from the fire to the pot, from the drum to your ear, from the Sun
to the Earth — and it is never destroyed. It only changes place or form, which is why a
sunny window, a ringing bell, and a warm fire are all the same story: energy on the move.

## Materials

- A small drum or tin and a drumstick
- A flashlight or a lamp
- A warm object and a cool object (a warmed stone and a cool one)
- Paper and pencils

## Preparation

- Warm one stone in the sun or in warm (not hot) water, and keep a second stone cool.
- Set out the drum, flashlight, and stones where learners can safely touch and use them.
- Prepare a record space: **Source | What moves | Receiver**.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Teach the transfer idea with a **worked
example** — model one transfer ("the lamp is the source, light moves, the wall is the
receiver") — then let learners find their own transfers (guided practice) before recording
independently. The key science to protect: energy is **never destroyed**; when it seems to
"disappear" (the sound fades, the stone cools), it has only spread out into the
surroundings (the conservation-of-energy idea, held simply and honestly — evidence, not
dogma). Keep the technology and environment lenses alive: every light, machine, and fire
we use is energy on the move, and that is why we care where our energy comes from and
where it goes. Keep hands safe — warm, never scalding. Invite wonder alongside fact:
energy's endless journey is something scientists describe from centuries of careful
measurement, and it is also a thing to marvel at.

## Procedure

1. **Gather and remember (5 min).** Last year you met solids, liquids, and gases, and how
   heating and cooling change them. Today: what *is* that warmth, and where does it go?
2. **Meet energy (10 min).** **Energy** is what makes things happen — it warms, it makes
   sound, it lights. It moves from a **source** (where it starts) to a **receiver** (where
   it lands). Say the three kinds: **heat**, **sound**, **light**.
3. **Trace three transfers (20 min).** First, hold the warm stone near the cool one — feel
   the **heat** move from warm to cool. Next, tap the drum — hear the **sound** travel to
   your ear. Last, shine the flashlight — see the **light** land on the wall. Each time,
   say the **source**, what moves, and the **receiver**.
4. **Record your own (15 min).** Find three energy transfers around you — a lamp, a voice,
   warm hands, the Sun through a window. For each, write the **source**, what moves, and
   the **receiver**.
5. **Close (5 min).** Energy is never destroyed. When it seems to vanish, it has only
   spread out. Tomorrow you will build a path for one kind of energy — electricity — to
   move along.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Trace one transfer (light from lamp to wall) with a partner and say the
  source and receiver.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Point to the source and the receiver of a transfer; a partner
  names what moves.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Feel heat move from a warm stone to a cool one and hear
  sound travel; describe each transfer aloud.
- **Extension:** Explain in one sentence where the energy "goes" when a sound fades or a
  stone cools, and why it is not destroyed.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name a source and a receiver for one energy
  transfer and say what moves?
- **Self:** The learner checks their record: "Did I name a source, what moves, and a
  receiver for each?"

## Home connection

At home, trace one energy transfer with a grown-up — a kettle heating water, a phone
lighting a page, a voice reaching across a room — and say the source and the receiver.
