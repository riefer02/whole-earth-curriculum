# Lesson 9 — The Water Cycle

## Summary

Water is not used up — it is reused, endlessly. Learners follow the **water cycle**: the
Sun warms oceans, lakes, and puddles and **evaporation** turns water into invisible vapor
that rises into the air; the vapor cools and **condensation** gathers it into clouds; when
the drops grow heavy, **precipitation** returns the water as rain, snow, or hail; and
**collection** gathers it in rivers, lakes, and soil, to begin again. Learners watch a
small warm-water model and draw and label the four stages, discovering that the same water
has been traveling this loop for billions of years.

## Objectives

- Describe the water cycle and how water moves and is reused around the Earth.
  _(D06.S4.04.01)_

## Connection

Have you ever watched a puddle dry up on a sunny day and wondered where the water went? It
did not vanish — it rose into the air as invisible vapor. High in the sky it cooled into a
cloud, and later it fell somewhere else as rain. That is the **water cycle**: water
**evaporates** up, **condenses** into clouds, **precipitates** back down, and **collects**
to begin again. It is why a river keeps flowing, why clouds form over the sea and rain over
the land, and why the water you drink today may once have been rain on a mountain, snow in
a forest, or a wave in the ocean. The Earth reuses its water, over and over, for every
living thing.

## Materials

- A clear bowl or cup of warm water
- Clear plastic wrap or a lid and an ice cube
- Paper and crayons
- A kettle or warm-water source (facilitator only)

## Preparation

- Prepare one clear bowl of warm water per group, and have plastic wrap and an ice cube
  ready.
- Keep a large sheet ready to draw the four stages together.
- Have a kettle of warm (not boiling) water available, handled by the facilitator only.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Teach the cycle with a **worked example** —
run the warm-water model and name each stage as it appears — then let learners draw and
label their own cycle (guided practice). Handle warm water yourself, never boiling, and
never left where a learner could spill it. The core idea to protect: water is **reused**,
not used up — the same water cycles for billions of years (evidence from Earth science),
which carries a quiet ethics and environment message: water is shared and precious, and
caring for it is caring for the whole loop (the ethics and environment lenses). Use water
from many places — a rice paddy, a desert oasis, a mountain spring, a city tap — so every
learner can see their own water in the cycle (global lens). Invite wonder alongside fact:
the water in the bowl may once have been part of a cloud, a river, or an ancient sea.

## Procedure

1. **Gather and remember (5 min).** You have traced energy moving and built a circuit.
   Now follow a different loop: where does water go when a puddle dries up?
2. **Meet the stages (10 min).** The **water cycle** has four steps: **evaporation** (water
   rises into the air as vapor), **condensation** (vapor cools into clouds),
   **precipitation** (water falls as rain or snow), and **collection** (water gathers in
   rivers, lakes, and soil). Say them in order.
3. **Watch the model (15 min).** Watch the warm water. Over the plastic wrap, small drops
   form — that is **condensation**. When the drops grow heavy, they drip — that is
   **precipitation**. Where is the water going next? Back to **collection**. Name each
   stage as you see it.
4. **Draw and label your cycle (15 min).** Draw the loop: water on the ground, an arrow up
   for **evaporation**, a cloud for **condensation**, rain for **precipitation**, and
   arrows back to the water for **collection**. Label all four.
5. **Close (5 min).** Water is never used up — it is reused, over and over, for every
   living thing. Tomorrow you will meet the long, long story that water and rock can tell.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Order four picture cards of the stages with a partner and say each name.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Point to each stage on a diagram as a partner names it, then
  arrange picture cards in order.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Feel condensation form on the plastic and the drip of
  "rain," and describe each stage aloud in order.
- **Extension:** Explain where the water in your own tap or well may have come from before
  today, using the four stages.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name the four stages in order and say what
  happens at each?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The labeled water-cycle diagram, kept for the unit portfolio.

## Home connection

At home, leave a shallow dish of water in the sun with a grown-up and check it over a day
or two. Where did the water go? Talk about which stage of the cycle you watched.
