# Lesson 8 — The Environment Gives, People Change It

## Summary

Learners see the relationship between people and place as a two-way street: the
**environment** shapes how people get **food, water, and shelter** in cold, desert,
river-valley, and coastal places — and people in turn **change the environment** to meet
their needs, terracing slopes, digging irrigation canals, and sinking wells. Both
directions matter, and every people's answer is held with respect.

## Objectives

- Explain how the environment affects how people get food, water, and shelter in
  different regions, and how people change the environment to meet their needs.
  _(D08.S3.04.02)_

## Connection

Think of where you get your food, water, and shelter today. Some of it comes straight
from the environment around you — soil, rain, rivers, sunlight. And some of it comes
because people *changed* the environment to get more: fields were flattened, canals were
dug, wells were sunk, homes were built. Every place asks something of its people, and
every people answers by adapting — and by reshaping the land to live well.

## Materials

- "Environment and needs" picture cards (cold, desert, river valley, coast)
- "People change the environment" cards (terrace, canal, well, floating garden)
- Pencils and crayons

## Preparation

- Prepare the needs cards. **Cold place:** fish and sea food, warm parka, snug home.
  **Desert:** dates and herded milk, light robe, airy tent. **River valley:** grain and
  garden food, irrigated fields, brick home. **Coast:** fish and shellfish, boats, home
  by the shore.
- Prepare the change cards: a **terraced hillside** (flattened steps to farm slopes), an
  **irrigation canal** (channels to carry river water to fields), a **dug well** (a shaft
  to reach water underground), a **floating garden** (chinampa — reed beds for crops on a
  lake).

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). **Model the two directions with a worked
example first** (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006): "The environment gives — a river
valley gives water and rich soil, so people farm grain; people change — they dig an
irrigation canal to carry that water farther." Then guide the other places and changes.
**Keep the framing respectful and non-ranking:** terracing, canals, wells, and chinampas
are all ingenious human answers — the Andes terraces, the Nile and Mesopotamian canals,
desert qanats, and Tenochtitlan's floating gardens — never "primitive" and never "less
advanced" than today. Both directions matter: the environment shapes people, and people
shape the environment, with care and with consequences. Honor every body: describe the
cards in words for a learner who cannot see them; a learner who does not use a speaking
voice points or acts. If learners can name how their own place has been changed (a
field, a well, a road, a garden), welcome it.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Name three things you need every day — food, water, and shelter.
   Where do yours come from?
2. **The environment gives (15 min).** Sort the needs cards by place:
   - **Cold place:** fish, warm parka, snug home.
   - **Desert:** dates, light robe, airy tent.
   - **River valley:** grain, irrigated fields, brick home.
   - **Coast:** fish, boats, home by the shore.
   For each, say how the environment shaped the answer.
3. **People change (15 min).** Meet the change cards and say what each does:
   - **Terraced hillside:** people cut steps into a slope to farm it.
   - **Irrigation canal:** people dig channels to carry river water to fields.
   - **Dug well:** people dig down to reach water beneath the ground.
   - **Floating garden:** people build reed beds on a lake to grow crops.
   For each, say which need it helps and which place it fits.
4. **Draw one change (10 min).** Choose one way people changed the environment and draw
   or write it, with the need it served.
5. **Close (5 min).** The environment gives, and people change it — with knowledge and
   care, in every place on Earth.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Match one environment to its food, water, and shelter with heavy picture
  support and one shared sentence said together.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Feel a warm wrap and a light cloth, and model a terrace
  or canal in clay or sand, describing each aloud.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Sort the cards into "environment gives" and "people
  change" and point, instead of speaking.
- **Support (Deaf/HH):** The cards are visual — sort them and sign "food," "water,"
  "shelter," and "change" as you go.
- **Support (motor-difference):** Use larger cards and a stable board; point or eye-gaze
  while a partner moves each card.
- **Extension:** Choose a place you know and name one way its environment shapes life
  today and one way people have changed it.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner match each environment to how people get
  food, water, and shelter there?
- **Formative (oral/performance):** Does the learner explain one way people change the
  environment to meet a need — by speaking, signing, writing, or acting?

## Home connection

Walk around your home or neighborhood and find one way people changed the environment
to meet a need — a well, a pipe, a field, a garden, a roof. Draw or describe it and say
which need it serves.
