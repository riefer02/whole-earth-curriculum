# Lesson 3 — A Day Long Ago, A Day Today

## Summary

Learners step into the daily life of a **child in Mohenjo-Daro**, a planned city of the
**Indus Valley** (about 2500 BCE, in what is now Pakistan), and compare it with their
own day. They find **one thing that changed** — how people get water, from wells to
taps — and **one thing that stayed the same** — children still play and learn. The
lesson teaches that change and sameness always sit side by side, and that children
long ago were real people, not curiosities.

## Objectives

- Compare daily life in an earlier society with life today and identify one thing that
  changed and one thing that stayed the same. _(D08.S1.04.02)_

## Connection

Every day you do ordinary things — wake, wash, eat, play, learn, rest. Children have
done these same ordinary things for thousands of years, but *how* they did them
changed with place and time. A child in Mohenjo-Daro drew water from a brick well and
played with a small clay cart; you may turn a tap and play a different game. The need
to wash, eat, play, and learn is the same; the *way* changes. Both parts of the story
matter.

## Materials

- A "then and now" comparison chart (one per learner or pair)
- Picture cards of daily life in Mohenjo-Daro
- Pencils and crayons

## Preparation

- Prepare the chart with two columns and five rows: **home** (brick house with flat
  roof and courtyard / your home); **water** (a brick well / a tap or pump); **food**
  (flatbread, dates, vegetables / your meals); **play** (clay toy carts and dice / your
  games); **learning** (from elders and family / your school or learning place).
- Prepare simple picture cards for the long-ago side.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). **Comparing is a foundational skill, so
model one full row first** (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006): "Water — long ago, a
child drew water from a brick well; today, many people turn a tap. That *changed*. But
children still need water every day — that *stayed the same*." Then guide the other
rows. **Hold the earlier society with respect:** Mohenjo-Daro was a real, clever city —
brick houses, covered drains, wells, and a grid of streets — not "primitive." Do not
rank "today" as better in every way; some things today are more convenient, and some
things long ago were also wise and beautiful. Honor every body: describe the cards in
words for a learner who cannot see them; a learner who does not use a speaking voice
points or acts. Use the present for the learner's life and the past for the long-ago
city.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of your own morning: what do you do, from waking to
   breakfast? Name three things.
2. **Meet a child in Mohenjo-Daro (10 min).** About 4,500 years ago, a city of brick
   houses, wells, and straight streets stood by the Indus River. A child there lived in
   a brick home, drew water from a well, ate flatbread and dates, played with clay toy
   carts, and learned from elders.
3. **Compare then and now (15 min).** Fill each row of the chart:
   - **Home:** brick house with a courtyard / your home.
   - **Water:** a brick well / a tap or pump.
   - **Food:** flatbread and dates / your meals.
   - **Play:** clay carts and dice / your games.
   - **Learning:** from elders / your learning place.
   For each row, say what **changed** and what **stayed the same**.
4. **Name one change and one same (10 min).** Choose one thing that changed and one
   thing that stayed the same. Say or write each in a sentence: "People once drew water
   from wells; today many turn a tap. But children still need water every day."
5. **Close (5 min).** Daily life changes across time — and much stays the same. A child
   long ago and you are both part of the same long human story.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Compare only water and play, with heavy picture support and one shared
  sentence said together.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Feel a clay cup (well) and a small toy, and describe
  how each part of the day changed or stayed the same aloud.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Sort the picture cards into "long ago" and "today" and
  point to changed/same instead of speaking.
- **Support (Deaf/HH):** The cards are visual — sort them and sign "long ago," "today,"
  "changed," and "the same" as you go.
- **Support (motor-difference):** Use a large, stable chart and bigger cards; point or
  eye-gaze while a partner moves each card.
- **Extension:** Choose a different earlier society you have heard about and name one
  way its daily life changed and one way it stayed the same.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner match daily-life items to "long ago" and
  "today"?
- **Formative (oral/performance):** Does the learner name **one thing that changed** and
  **one thing that stayed the same** — by speaking, signing, writing, or acting?
