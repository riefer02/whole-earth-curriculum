# Lesson 7 — Land, Water, and Where People Settled

## Summary

Learners read a simple map to locate **major landforms** — mountains, deserts, river
valleys, coasts — and **bodies of water** — oceans, rivers, lakes — and explain how each
shaped **where and how people lived**. Rivers gave fresh water and fertile soil; coasts
gave fish and sea trade; mountain slopes became terraced fields; desert oases made
settlement possible. The lesson shows that people and place are always connected.

## Objectives

- Use maps to locate major landforms and bodies of water and explain how they shaped
  where and how people lived. _(D08.S3.04.01)_

## Connection

Look at any map and you will see the land is not all the same: there are mountains,
deserts, rivers, and seas. People do not settle just anywhere — they settle where the
land and water give them what they need. A river brings water to drink and rich soil to
farm; a coast brings fish and boats for trade. Reading a map is reading the answer to
the question, "why did people make their home *here*?"

## Materials

- A simple landforms-and-water map (one per learner or pair)
- Settlement reason cards (fresh water, fish and trade, terraces, oasis)
- Pencils and crayons

## Preparation

- Prepare the schematic map with labeled features: a **mountain range**, a **desert**, a
  **river** running through a **valley** to the sea, a **coast**, and an **ocean**. Leave
  blank circles for settlement dots.
- Prepare the four reason cards.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). **Map reading is a foundational skill, so
model one settlement fully first** (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006): "The river — people
settled here because the river gives fresh water to drink and fertile soil to farm, and
boats can travel on it." Then guide the coast, mountains, and oasis. **Keep the framing
global and egalitarian:** river valleys (the Nile, the Indus, the Tigris–Euphrates, the
Yellow, the Niger), coasts and islands (Pacific and Indian Ocean peoples), mountain
terraces (the Andes), and desert oases are all real, clever human answers — never
"better" or "worse" places, and never "advanced" vs. "backward." Every place asks
something of its people, and every people answers with knowledge. Honor every body:
describe the map in words for a learner who cannot see it; a learner who does not use a
speaking voice points or places a dot. If learners live near a landform or water body,
anchor the lesson there first.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of the place where you live: is there a river, a sea, a
   mountain, or flat land nearby? What does it give the people there?
2. **Meet the map (10 min).** Look at the map. Point to each feature and name it: the
   **mountains**, the **desert**, the **river** and its **valley**, the **coast**, and the
   **ocean**.
3. **Place the settlements (20 min).** One by one, decide where people settle and why:
   - **By the river:** fresh water to drink, fertile soil to farm, boats to travel.
   - **On the coast:** fish to eat and boats for trade across the sea.
   - **On the mountain slopes:** people cut flat **terraces** into the slopes to farm.
   - **At a desert oasis:** a spring of water makes life possible in the dry land.
   For each, place a dot and match the reason card.
4. **Name one place and its reason (10 min).** Choose one landform or water body and say
   or write how it shaped where people lived and how.
5. **Close (5 min).** Land and water shaped the human story. People read their landscape
   and made their homes where it could care for them — and they cared for it in return.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Place just the river and coast settlements with heavy picture support and
  one shared sentence said together.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Feel a raised map or use real objects (a stone for a
  mountain, a bowl of water for a river) and describe where people settle and why.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Place the dots and point to the reason cards, instead of
  speaking.
- **Support (Deaf/HH):** The map is visual — place the dots and sign "river," "coast,"
  "mountain," and "desert" as you go.
- **Support (motor-difference):** Use a large, stable map and bigger dots; point or
  eye-gaze while a partner places each dot.
- **Extension:** Add a second settlement to one landform (a port town *and* a fishing
  village on the coast) and say how each lives differently.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner locate the landforms and bodies of water
  on the map and place settlements sensibly?
- **Formative (oral/performance):** Does the learner explain how one landform or water
  body shaped where and how people lived — by speaking, signing, writing, or acting?

## Home connection

Look at a map of your region (paper or on a screen) and find the nearest river, coast,
mountain, or lake. Ask someone: "Did people settle here because of it?" Talk about how
the land and water shaped your place.
