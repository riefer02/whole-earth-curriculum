# Lesson 2 — At the Same Time

## Summary

Learners use a dated timeline to ask a historian's second great question: **what was
happening in different places at the same time?** They focus on one band of time —
around 250–200 BCE — and discover three things happening at once: Emperor Ashoka
raising kindness pillars in India, workers joining walls into the Great Wall in China,
and scholars gathering scrolls at the Library of Alexandria in Egypt. Comparing across
places at one moment shows that no single place is "the" story; many peoples were
building, writing, and thinking at once.

## Objectives

- Use a timeline with dates to compare what was happening in two or more places at the
  same time. _(D08.S1.04.01)_

## Connection

Have you ever wondered what a child in another part of the world was doing while you
were eating your breakfast? History asks the same question, but with bigger steps:
while one people built a wall, another carved a message into stone, and a third copied
books — all around the same years. When we line up dates, we see that the human story
was never one people taking turns; it was **many peoples acting at once**, each with
their own idea worth honoring.

## Materials

- A "same time" comparison strip (one per learner or pair)
- Three event cards for the worked example (India, China, Egypt)
- Pencils and crayons

## Preparation

- Draw the timeline strip with dates, and highlight the band around 250–200 BCE.
- Prepare three cards: **Ashoka's pillars** (India), about 250 BCE; **the Great Wall
  joined** (China), about 220 BCE; **scrolls at the Library of Alexandria** (Egypt),
  about 250 BCE.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). **Comparison is a foundational skill,
so model the first comparison fully** (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006): "Around 250
BCE, in India, Ashoka raised pillars telling people to be kind; around the same years,
in China, workers joined walls into the Great Wall — two places, one moment, two
different works." Then guide the third place. **The point is many-at-once, never
"who was first" or "who was best."** Each of the three events matters in its own
right: India's message of kindness, China's great wall, Egypt's meeting place of many
peoples' learning. Dates are approximate ("about") — say so, and model the honesty.
Honor every body: describe the cards aloud for a learner who cannot see them; a
learner who does not use a speaking voice points or acts. Hold gently that "around the
same time" means within the same span of years, not the same day.

## Procedure

1. **Gather and wonder (5 min).** Think of two things you did at the same time today
   (you ate while someone else was reading). History can compare places the same way.
2. **Meet the band of time (10 min).** Look at the timeline. One band is highlighted:
   around 250–200 BCE, about 2,250 years ago. What was happening then?
3. **Place three places at once (15 min).** Put the three cards on the band:
   - **India:** Emperor Ashoka raised stone pillars telling people to treat one another
     with kindness and respect.
   - **China:** workers joined many walls into the **Great Wall** to protect the land.
   - **Egypt:** scholars gathered scrolls at the **Library of Alexandria**, a meeting
     place of learning from many peoples.
   For each, say: "At the same time, in…"
4. **Compare two places (10 min).** Choose two of the three. Say or write one sentence:
   "While people in China were joining the Great Wall, people in India were…" — and one
   sentence about how the two works were different.
5. **Close (5 min).** No place waited for another. At the same time, in three places,
   people were building, writing, and thinking. The human story is many stories moving
   at once.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Compare just two places with heavy picture support and one shared
  sentence said together.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Feel three distinct objects standing on one band of
  the line and say or sign "at the same time" as a partner names each.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Place the three cards on the band and point to each as
  its name is said, instead of speaking.
- **Support (Deaf/HH):** The cards are visual — place them on the band and sign
  "same time," "India," "China," and "Egypt" as you go.
- **Support (motor-difference):** Use a large band and bigger cards; point or eye-gaze
  while a partner moves each card.
- **Extension:** Add a fourth place from your own timeline (from Lesson 1) that falls
  near the same years, and say what it was doing "at the same time."

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner place the three events on the same band
  and name what each place was doing at the same time?
- **Formative (oral/performance):** Does the learner compare two places in one sentence
  ("while X was…, Y was…") — by speaking, signing, writing, or acting?

## Home connection

Ask someone at home: "What was happening in our family the year I was born, in two
different places at the same time?" Write the two events with their dates and notice
they happened at once.
