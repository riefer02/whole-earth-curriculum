# Lesson 1 — Designing a Tool to Meet a Need

## Summary

Learners explain that to **design** a tool is to make something to meet a **need**, and they
describe **one step** in designing. They meet the design steps — *find the need, imagine
ideas, choose and make, test, and improve* — through a worked example (a scoop for grain),
then name a need of their own, draw an idea, and label one design step.

## Objectives

- Explain what it means to design a tool (making something to meet a need) and describe one
  step in designing. _(D10.S1.04.01)_

## Connection

Think of something that was hard to do lately: carrying water a long way, reaching a high
shelf, or sweeping a dusty floor with no broom. A **tool** is anything people make to make a
job easier, and **design** is the thinking-and-making work of creating it: you notice a
**need**, you imagine something that could meet it, you make it, you test it, and you improve
it. People have always done this — a woven basket, a clay pot, a bicycle, a water pump all
began as someone's answer to a need.

## Materials

- Design steps chart (Need → Imagine → Make → Test → Improve) with a worked example
- Paper and pencils

## Preparation

- Prepare the design steps chart, with the worked example "a scoop for grain": the need
  (lifting grain one handful at a time is slow), an idea (a curved cup on a handle), making
  it, testing it (does it hold grain?), and improving it (make the cup deeper).
- Set out paper and pencils.
- Prepare a few simple needs from daily life to offer as starting points (carrying water,
  shading from sun, holding things together, keeping food cool).

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). This is the Grade 4 upgrade of Grade 3's
"name a simple machine": now learners name what **design** is (making something to meet a
need) and can **describe one step** in the process. Teach the steps with explicit instruction
and a worked example before learners work independently (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006):
model "find the need → imagine → make → test → improve" on the grain scoop once, then guide a
second example together. Keep it **global and egalitarian**: use needs and tools from many
lives — a water carrier, a sunshade, a basket, a pump — so no single, wealthy, modern home is
the default; design is a human skill, not a store-bought one. A light values thread: a good
design meets a need for *someone* — ask "who does this help, and who is left out?" Honor every
body: describe the chart aloud for a learner who is blind or has low vision; a non-speaking
learner points to a step or draws the idea; acting out the steps works for learners who learn
best by moving.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Every tool you have ever used — a spoon, a bag, a door — was **designed**:
   someone noticed a need and made something to meet it. Today you learn to do that thinking.
2. **Meet design (10 min).** **Design** is making something to meet a **need**. The steps:
   *find the need*, *imagine ideas*, *choose and make*, *test*, *improve*. Follow the worked
   example: the need is lifting grain quickly; the idea is a curved cup on a handle; then you
   make it, test it ("does it hold the grain?"), and improve it ("make the cup deeper").
3. **Name the steps together (8 min).** For a second need — carrying water a long way — name
   each step with a partner: what is the need? what could you make? how would you test it? how
   could you improve it?
4. **Your own need and one step (12 min).** Pick a need from your own life (or from the list).
   Draw your idea for a tool that could meet it, and write or say **one design step** and why it
   matters — for example, "I would test it by seeing if it holds water."
5. **Share (5 min).** Show your idea to a partner. Can they tell what need it meets? Name your
   one design step for them.
6. **Close (5 min).** Designing is a loop, not a one-time answer: you try, test, and improve.
   Anyone can design, because everyone notices needs.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Name the need and one step together aloud using the chart and the frame "My
  tool meets the need to ___, and one step I would take is ___."
- **Blind/low vision:** Hear each step described and say the need and one step by voice; a
  partner scribes or models the idea.
- **Deaf/hard of hearing / non-speaking:** Point to the design step on the chart or draw the
  idea and label one step with pictures.
- **Motor differences:** Direct a partner's hands to draw while the learner says or points to
  the need and the one design step.
- **Extension:** Describe a second need and two design steps, or explain how you would improve
  your own first idea after testing it.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner explain that designing means making something to
  meet a need, and describe at least one step in designing?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The learner's need-and-idea drawing with one design step labeled.

## Home connection

At home, notice one need — something that is hard or slow to do. Tell a grown-up what tool you
would design to meet it, and name one step you would take (imagine, make, test, or improve).
