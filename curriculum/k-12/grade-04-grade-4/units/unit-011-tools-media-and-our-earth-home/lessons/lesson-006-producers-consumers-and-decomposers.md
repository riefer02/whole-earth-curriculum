# Lesson 6 — Producers, Consumers, and Decomposers

## Summary

Learners explain the jobs of **producers**, **consumers**, and **decomposers** in an ecosystem,
with an example of each. A **producer** makes food from sunlight (grass, a tree); a **consumer**
eats other living things (a rabbit, a bird, a person); a **decomposer** breaks dead things down
back into soil (a mushroom, an earthworm). They sort roles, then draw the three jobs in a cycle
with one example of each.

## Objectives

- Explain the jobs of producers, consumers, and decomposers in an ecosystem, with an example of
  each. _(D10.S3.04.02)_

## Connection

Where does the food in your meal begin? With a plant. A **producer** — grass, a rice plant, an
apple tree — makes its own food from **sunlight**. A **consumer** — a rabbit, a bird, or you —
eats other living things to get energy. And when anything dies, a **decomposer** — a mushroom, an
earthworm, a tiny beetle — breaks it down and returns it to the **soil**, which feeds the plant
again. These three jobs keep every ecosystem going, in a circle with no waste.

## Materials

- Role cards (producer, consumer, decomposer)
- A "who does what" chart
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Prepare the role cards: producers (grass, a tree), consumers (a rabbit, a bird, a person),
  decomposers (a mushroom, an earthworm, a beetle).
- Prepare the chart: *producer — makes food from sunlight; consumer — eats other living things;
  decomposer — breaks dead things down into soil*.
- If possible, gather a small sample of leaf litter or compost to look at.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Grade 3 explained that plants make their own food
from sunlight and that animals eat plants or other animals; Grade 4 names the three **jobs**
explicitly — **producer, consumer, decomposer** — and gives an example of each. The key new idea
is the **cycle**: decomposers close the loop, turning dead things back into soil that producers
use, so an ecosystem has no true waste. Model the cycle with the chart and arrows, then guide
sorting before independent drawing (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006). Keep it **concrete and
gentle**: say "breaks dead things down and returns them to the soil," not gory detail; frame
decomposers as quiet helpers, not as "gross." Use examples from many biomes — grass and rabbit,
sea plants and fish, rice plants and people, forest leaves and mushrooms — so no single place is
the default. A light ethical thread: everything is food for something else, and nothing is
wasted in a healthy ecosystem — this prepares the "reduce, reuse, recycle" idea in Lessons 7–8,
where humans can choose to waste *less*, the way ecosystems already do. Honor every body:
describe the cards in words for a learner who is blind or has low vision; a non-speaking learner
sorts the cards and points the cycle arrows; acting out each role works for learners who learn
best by moving.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Every living thing has a job in the ecosystem. Today you learn the three
   big jobs and who does them.
2. **Meet the three jobs (10 min).** A **producer** makes food from sunlight (grass, a tree). A
   **consumer** eats other living things (a rabbit, a bird, a person). A **decomposer** breaks
   dead things down back into soil (a mushroom, an earthworm). Watch the cycle: the plant makes
   food, the rabbit eats the plant, and when either dies, the decomposer returns it to the soil
   that feeds the plant again.
3. **Sort the roles (8 min).** With a partner, sort the cards into the three jobs. Say one
   example of each: "Grass is a producer; a rabbit is a consumer; a mushroom is a decomposer."
4. **Draw the cycle (12 min).** Draw the three jobs in a circle with arrows: producer → consumer
   → decomposer → back to the soil and the producer. Label one example of each.
5. **Share (5 min).** Show your cycle to a partner. Can they name your producer, consumer, and
   decomposer, and follow the arrows all the way around?
6. **Close (5 min).** Producers make food, consumers eat, decomposers return everything to the
   soil. In a healthy ecosystem, nothing is wasted.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Sort the given cards and say the three jobs aloud, using the frame "The ___ is a
  ___; its job is to ___."
- **Blind/low vision:** Hear each card described and name the three jobs and an example of each by
  voice; a partner describes the cycle in words.
- **Deaf/hard of hearing / non-speaking:** Sort the cards and point to each job and an example,
  then point the cycle arrows.
- **Motor differences:** Direct a partner's hands to sort and draw while the learner says or
  points to the three jobs and their examples.
- **Extension:** Add a second consumer and a second decomposer to the cycle, or explain what
  would happen in a garden with no decomposers.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner explain the jobs of producers, consumers, and
  decomposers and give an example of each?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The drawn cycle with the three jobs labeled and one example of each.

## Home connection

At home, find one producer (a plant), one consumer (a person, pet, or animal), and one
decomposer (a worm, a mushroom, or a rotting leaf). Tell a grown-up each one's job in the
ecosystem.
