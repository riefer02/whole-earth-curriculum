# Lesson 9 — Our Care Message: Author, Purpose, Audience, and Checked Facts

## Summary

Learners bring the unit together by making an **honest care message** with a clear **author**,
**purpose**, and **audience**, and one **fact checked against a second source**. They model a
stream-care poster, check its fact, then create and share a message of their own that urges one
real helping action for a local place.

## Objectives

- Identify the author, purpose, and audience of a message and explain how they shape it.
  _(D10.S2.04.01)_
- Check whether a simple claim is true by finding a second source and comparing them.
  _(D10.S2.04.02)_
- Investigate how one human action, such as littering or planting, can help or harm a local
  place. _(D10.S4.04.02)_

## Connection

When people want to care for a place, they do not just act — they *tell others*, so the care
spreads. A good care message is honest: it names who made it (**author**), what it is for
(**purpose**), who it is for (**audience**), and it rests on a fact that has been **checked** in
more than one place. You now know how to do all of that — so today you make a message that could
really help the place you live.

## Materials

- A "care message" model poster
- A "message builder" checklist
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Prepare the model poster: "Keep our stream clean — litter can wash into the water and harm
  fish," labeled with *author* (a learner), *purpose* (to persuade people to care), *audience*
  (people who visit the stream), and *checked fact* (litter can wash into water and harm fish,
  checked in two sources that agreed).
- Prepare the checklist: *author, purpose, audience, one claim, source 1, source 2, do they
  agree?*
- Prepare a way to share finished messages (a wall, a window, or a spoken sharing circle).

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It is the unit's **synthesis** for media literacy
and stewardship: learners make a *real* message that joins author/purpose/audience (Lessons 3–4)
with a checked fact and a helping action (Lessons 7–8). Model the full build on the stream-care
poster first, then let learners work with the checklist (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006). Keep
the stance warm and empowering: the message is not "scold people" but "invite people to care" —
persuasion used for good, resting on truth. **Honor no-wealth and global contexts**: a care
message needs no screens or printing — it can be spoken, sung, drawn in chalk, or told around a
fire; oral sharing is a real and honored medium everywhere. A message can be in any language; let
learners write or speak in the language of their home. Hold the ethics thread lightly but
firmly: a message that urges care should itself be honest — no exaggerating, because checked
facts are stronger than loud ones. Honor every body: a learner who is blind or has low vision
may make a spoken or sung message; a non-speaking learner may make a drawn message and point to
its parts; a learner with motor differences may direct a partner's hands while they author the
words.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** You have learned to read messages with clear eyes and to care for a
   place. Today you join the two: you make an honest care message.
2. **Meet the model (10 min).** Look at the stream-care poster. Its **author** is a learner; its
   **purpose** is to persuade people to care; its **audience** is people who visit the stream.
   Its fact — "litter can wash into the water and harm fish" — was **checked** in two sources
   that agreed. That is what makes the message strong: it is clear *and* true.
3. **Choose your care message (8 min).** Pick one real helping action for a local place (picking
   up litter, planting, saving water, keeping a stream clean). Name your **author** (you or your
   group), your **purpose**, and your **audience**.
4. **Check your fact (10 min).** Write the one claim your message rests on. Check it in **two
   sources** — two books, a book and a person, two people, a book and a page. Note whether they
   agree.
5. **Make and share (10 min).** Make the message (poster, song, spoken notice, chalk drawing).
   Label its author, purpose, and audience, and note the checked fact. Share it with a partner or
   the group.
6. **Close (2 min).** An honest message can spread care far beyond one pair of hands. You now
   know how to make one.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Build the message together aloud using the frame "My message's author is ___, its
  purpose is to ___, its audience is ___, and its checked fact is ___ (checked in ___ and ___)."
- **Blind/low vision:** Make a spoken or sung message and say its author, purpose, audience, and
  checked fact by voice.
- **Deaf/hard of hearing / non-speaking:** Make a drawn or signed message and point to its
  author, purpose, audience, and checked fact.
- **Motor differences:** Direct a partner's hands to draw and label while the learner authors the
  words and the checked fact.
- **Extension:** Check a second fact and add it, or make a second version of the message for a
  different audience and explain what you changed.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner make a message with a clear author, purpose, and
  audience, and one fact checked against a second source?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The care message with its author, purpose, audience, and checked fact
  labeled.

## Home connection

Share your care message at home — say it, sing it, or show it — and tell a grown-up the fact you
checked in two sources. Ask if they will join the one helping action it asks for.
