# Lesson 10 — Our Earth Home: Design, Media, Living Systems, and Care

## Summary

Learners close the unit by **retrieving and connecting** all four strands — designing tools,
reading messages, understanding living systems, and caring for the Earth — into one picture of
"our Earth home." They sort their own portfolio artifacts into four corners, recall one idea
from each, and write a short "my steward plan" naming one real action they will take.

## Objectives

- Explain what it means to design a tool and describe one step in designing. _(D10.S1.04.01)_
- Compare two tools for the same job and say which works better and why for a given person or
  situation. _(D10.S1.04.02)_
- Identify the author, purpose, and audience of a message and explain how they shape it.
  _(D10.S2.04.01)_
- Check whether a simple claim is true by finding a second source and comparing them.
  _(D10.S2.04.02)_
- Describe an ecosystem as living and nonliving parts that depend on each other, and name two of
  its parts. _(D10.S3.04.01)_
- Explain the jobs of producers, consumers, and decomposers in an ecosystem, with an example of
  each. _(D10.S3.04.02)_
- Explain what happens to trash after it is thrown away and name "reduce, reuse, recycle" in
  order of what is best to do first. _(D10.S4.04.01)_
- Investigate how one human action, such as littering or planting, can help or harm a local
  place. _(D10.S4.04.02)_

## Connection

This unit asked two big questions: *how are tools designed, and how do I read a message's
purpose and audience?* and *how do living systems work, and how can I care for my place on
Earth?* Today you look back and see that they are really one question: how do I meet the made
world and the living Earth with **clear eyes and a caring hand**? A designer meets a need; a
careful reader names author, purpose, and audience; a thinker checks a claim with a second
source; a steward knows the living system and acts to help it. These are not four separate
skills — they are one way of being a person on this one, shared Earth home.

## Materials

- Your own unit work from Lessons 1–9
- A "four corners" review chart (Design, Media, Living systems, Care)
- Paper and pencils

## Preparation

- Gather each learner's portfolio artifacts from Lessons 1–9.
- Set up four corners (or four spots outside) labeled *Design, Media, Living systems, Care*.
- Prepare a short list of recall prompts, one per corner.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It is the unit's **culminating review** — retrieval
practice and connection, not new content. The science of learning says recalling and organizing
is what makes learning last (retrieval practice; spacing); this lesson does that deliberately.
Guide the four-corner sort, then let learners recall one idea per corner and write a **"my
steward plan"** — one real, doable action — so the unit ends in agency, not a test. Keep it
**hope-full and global**: the steward plan should cost nothing and work anywhere (pick up litter
safely, plant or water a seed, carry a reusable bag, mend something, check a claim before
sharing it, tell others kindly). Emphasize that these habits are practiced by people everywhere,
with or without wealth — stewardship is not a product to buy. Honor every body: a learner who is
blind or has low vision recalls ideas by voice; a non-speaking learner points to artifacts and
corners; standing in four spots works for learners who learn best by moving; a learner who
struggles with recall is invited to pick up their own artifact as a memory cue rather than
answer from memory alone.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** You have traveled through four big ideas: designing tools, reading
   messages, understanding living systems, and caring for the Earth. Today you gather them all
   into one picture of *our Earth home*.
2. **Sort your work (10 min).** Look at your own work from this unit. Place each piece under one
   of four corners: **Design**, **Media**, **Living systems**, or **Care**. (Your tool drawing
   goes under Design; your message and two-source check under Media; your ecosystem and cycle
   under Living systems; your trash path, help-or-harm sheet, and care message under Care.)
3. **Recall one idea each (12 min).** Visit each corner and recall, from memory if you can: one
   design step; one way to check a claim; two parts of an ecosystem; the order of reduce, reuse,
   recycle. Say it to a partner or write it down.
4. **Connect (8 min).** How do the four corners fit together? Talk with a partner: how does
   *reading a message carefully* help you *care for the Earth*? (Answer: so you can check claims
   before you believe or share them, and so you can spread true, helpful messages.)
5. **Write your steward plan (8 min).** Write one real action you will take this week for a local
   place — small, free, and doable. Name who or what it will help.
6. **Close (2 min).** Clear eyes and a caring hand: that is what this unit is. You carry it
   wherever you go.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Sort artifacts with a partner and recall each corner using the frame "In the ___
  corner, I learned ___."
- **Blind/low vision:** Recall ideas by voice and have a partner describe each artifact as you
  sort it.
- **Deaf/hard of hearing / non-speaking:** Point to artifacts and corners, and write or draw one
  idea per corner and the steward plan.
- **Motor differences:** Direct a partner's hands to sort and write while the learner says or
  points to the ideas and the steward plan.
- **Extension:** Add a second idea per corner, or teach one concept from the unit to a younger
  learner or family member.

## Assessment

- **Summative (portfolio):** The assembled unit portfolio, sorted into the four corners, shows
  the learner's growth across all four strands.
- **Self:** The learner's own recall and "my steward plan" note — a check of what they can
  retrieve and apply.
- **Performance:** The learner shares one idea per corner and one real action they will take.

## Home connection

Show your unit work to someone at home. Tell them one thing from each corner — Design, Media,
Living systems, and Care — and the one action in your steward plan. Invite them to do it with
you.
