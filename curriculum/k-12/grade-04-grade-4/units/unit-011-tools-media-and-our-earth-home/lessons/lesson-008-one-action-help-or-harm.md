# Lesson 8 — One Action: Help or Harm

## Summary

Learners **investigate how one human action — such as littering or planting — can help or harm a
local place**. They compare a littered stream bank with a clean, planted one, name who and what
each action helps or harms, and record their own investigation: one action, its observed effects,
and what they would do instead or again.

## Objectives

- Investigate how one human action, such as littering or planting, can help or harm a local
  place. _(D10.S4.04.02)_

## Connection

One small action can ripple through a whole place. Drop a wrapper by a stream, and rain may carry
it into the water, where it can harm fish and birds. Plant a tree on the same bank, and its
shade cools the water and its roots hold the soil, helping everything that lives there. People
are part of the place, not separate from it — so every action, small as it looks, is a choice to
**help** or to **harm**.

## Materials

- Before-and-after picture pairs (littered vs. planted stream bank)
- A "help or harm" investigation sheet
- Paper and pencils

## Preparation

- Prepare the picture pairs and captions naming who and what is helped or harmed.
- Prepare the investigation sheet: *the action, what I observe, who or what is helped or harmed,
  what I would do instead or again*.
- If you can and have permission, plan one real helping action (plant a seed, water a plant, or
  safely pick up litter with gloves or a stick).

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Grade 2 followed litter to the right place; Grade 4
**investigates** how one action helps or harms a **local** place — a step toward stewardship with
agency. Model the before-and-after comparison once, then guide learners to observe or recall a
real local action before they record (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006). **Keep it
stewardship-framed and hope-full**: the point is not to shame anyone for litter (people often lack
bins, time, or systems) but to show that each of us *has* power to help — and that many small
helps add up. Connect to the ecosystems lessons: an action that harms the water harms the fish,
the birds, and the people downstream, because all parts depend on each other. **Honor no-wealth
and global contexts**: caring actions need no money — planting a seed, sweeping a path, keeping
trash out of a stream, watering a plant, telling others kindly — and people everywhere practice
these. If the local reality is that trash has no bin to go to, say so plainly and without blame,
and focus on what *is* possible. Honor every body: describe the pictures aloud for a learner who
is blind or has low vision; a non-speaking learner points to help or harm and to who is affected;
acting out the two actions works for learners who learn best by moving.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** One small action can ripple through a whole place — for good or for harm.
   Today you investigate one.
2. **Compare help and harm (10 min).** Look at the two pictures of the same stream bank. On the
   left, litter: rain can carry it into the water, harming fish and birds. On the right, a
   planted tree: its shade cools the water and its roots hold the soil, helping everything there.
   Name who and what each action helps or harms.
3. **Name effects together (8 min).** With a partner, think of a second action — trampling a
   garden bed, or watering a dry plant. Say what you would observe, and who or what is helped or
   harmed.
4. **Investigate your own action (12 min).** Choose one real action in a local place (or one you
   remember). Fill the sheet: *the action, what I observe, who or what is helped or harmed, and
   what I would do instead or again*.
5. **Share (5 min).** Tell a partner your investigation. Did they see an effect you missed? Did
   you see one they missed?
6. **Close (5 min).** You are part of the place. Every action is a choice to help or to harm —
   and helping is something you can do, starting today.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Complete one investigation together aloud using the frame "The action ___ helps/
  harms ___, because ___."
- **Blind/low vision:** Hear the pictures described and say help or harm and who is affected by
  voice; a partner scribes the sheet.
- **Deaf/hard of hearing / non-speaking:** Point to help or harm and to who or what is affected;
  the learner's investigation may be drawn and labeled with pictures.
- **Motor differences:** Direct a partner's hands to write and draw while the learner says or
  points to the action and its effects.
- **Extension:** Investigate a second action and propose one care action you could actually do,
  saying who it would help and how.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner investigate how one human action helps or harms a
  local place, naming who or what is affected?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The completed "help or harm" investigation sheet.

## Home connection

In your own neighborhood or home, watch one human action and its effect — litter, a planted
seed, a watered plant, a swept path. Tell a grown-up whether it helps or harms the place, and who
or what is affected.
