# Lesson 7 — Safe in Weather and Storms

## Summary

Learners name and practice basic weather and storm safety rules: go inside and away from
windows in a storm, "when thunder roars, go indoors," stay out of flood water, drink
water and rest in heat, and layer up and stay dry in cold. Calm, clear, and empowering.

## Objectives

- Name and practice basic safety rules for weather and storms. _(D03.S4.04.01)_

## Connection

The sky changes every day — sun, rain, wind, snow, thunder, heat, and cold. Weather is
not something to fear; it is something to understand and prepare for. When you know the
simple rule for each kind of weather, a storm becomes a moment to be safe and calm, not
scared. Today you will learn those rules.

## Materials

- Weather safety rule cards
- The growing "my safety rules" chart
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Prepare the weather rule cards and add a weather column to the chart.
- Prepare a simple, local weather danger list (what weather actually happens where you
  are) so the rules feel real, not abstract.
- Keep the tone calm; the message is "you can be ready," never "be afraid."

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). **Teach readiness, not fear.** Storm rules:
in a storm, **go inside** and stay **away from windows**; for lightning, the saying is
"**when thunder roars, go indoors**"; and **stay out of flood water**, which can be deeper
and dirtier than it looks. Heat rules: **drink water** and **rest in the shade** when it
is very hot. Cold rules: **layer up** (wear layers of clothes) and **stay dry** when it is
very cold. Adapt to the learner's own climate — some places face monsoons and floods,
others heat and dust, others snow and ice, and a learner should learn the rules for the
weather they actually meet, while still hearing that weather differs worldwide. Never
make the lesson frightening; frame storms as things people have safely weathered in every
land for all of human history. A learner who uses a wheelchair learns the nearest
accessible shelter and the heat rule of drinking water and resting; a learner who is
blind or has low vision learns each rule by hearing and by being guided to the shelter; a
learner who is deaf or hard of hearing follows the cards and demonstration from the
front, since every rule is shown, not only told. The storm, heat, and cold rules follow
widely used weather-safety guidance from public-health and meteorological organizations.
This lesson completes D03.S4.04.01 and leads into the unit's online-safety capstone.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** You already know the rules for home, fire, road, and water. Now
   add the rules for the sky: weather and storms.
2. **Storm rules (15 min).** In a storm, **go inside** and stay **away from windows**. For
   lightning, remember: "**when thunder roars, go indoors**." And **stay out of flood
   water** — it can be deeper, faster, and dirtier than it looks. Point to each rule card
   and act out moving to shelter.
3. **Hot and cold rules (10 min).** When it is very hot, **drink water** and **rest in
   the shade**. When it is very cold, **layer up** — wear layers of clothes — and **stay
   dry**. Act out drinking water in the "heat" and putting on layers in the "cold."
4. **Match weather to rule (5 min).** Which rule for which weather? "Go inside and away
   from windows" is for a storm; "drink water and rest" is for heat; "layer up and stay
   dry" is for cold. Point to each rule card.
5. **Close (5 min).** Say it together: "In a storm I go inside; in heat I drink and rest;
   in cold I layer up and stay dry. I can be ready for any weather."

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Teach one weather type at a time and act it out; accept spoken, drawn, or
  gestured answers.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Order the weather cards and act out each safe action.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Say each rule aloud in order and be guided to the
  nearest shelter.
- **Extension:** Describe the weather danger your own region faces most and the exact
  safe place your family would shelter.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name and act out the storm, heat, and cold
  rules — with "go inside" and "stay out of flood water" included?
- **Self:** The learner names one weather rule and the weather it goes with.

## Home connection

With a grown-up, name the weather your home faces most and decide together the safe place
to shelter in a storm. Practice saying "when thunder roars, go indoors."
