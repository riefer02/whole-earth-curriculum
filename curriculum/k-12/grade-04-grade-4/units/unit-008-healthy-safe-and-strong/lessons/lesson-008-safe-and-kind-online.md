# Lesson 8 — Safe and Kind Online

## Summary

The unit's capstone teaches safe and kind behavior online. Learners sort what is private
from what is okay to share, learn never to share private information, practice being kind
online, and learn to stop and tell a trusted adult when something feels wrong. The tone
is calm and empowering — the internet is a tool, and they hold the power to use it well.

## Objectives

- Explain safe and respectful behavior online, including not sharing private information,
  and where to get help. _(D03.S4.04.02)_

## Connection

The internet can feel like a big, exciting world — games, videos, chats, and messages.
Just like the real world, it is mostly full of good things, and it also has a few simple
safety rules. You would not tell a stranger on the street your full name and address;
online you use the same good sense. Today you will learn what to keep private, how to be
kind, and exactly what to do if something feels wrong.

## Materials

- "Private or not?" sorting cards
- A "kind online" reminder chart
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Prepare the "private or not?" cards (full name, home address, school's name, password =
  private; favorite game, favorite color = okay to share).
- Prepare the "kind online" chart and the "tell a trusted adult" script.
- Read the facilitator note fully; keep the tone calm and empowering, never frightening.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). **Empower, never frighten.** Teach the
rules simply and positively:

- **Keep private information private.** Private information is what could let a stranger
  find or pretend to be you: your **full name**, **home address**, **school's name**,
  **password**, and your **photo** (decide with your grown-up). Things like a favorite
  color or game are usually okay to share. A **password** is a secret word that guards
  your accounts — never share it with anyone but a trusted grown-up.
- **Be kind online.** Treat people online as kindly as you would face to face. If someone
  is unkind, do not answer back with cruelty; tell a trusted adult.
- **Stop and tell.** If anything online feels wrong, scary, or confusing — a stranger
  asking private questions, an unkind message, a request to meet — **stop**, **close it**,
  and **tell a trusted adult**. Never meet an online stranger alone, and never keep a
  secret that makes you feel bad.

Frame all of this as *power*, not danger: the learner is the boss of their information
and their words. Do not share frightening stories of online harm; if a learner asks,
answer briefly and calmly and return to the rules. Honor that many learners have no
device at home — the rules apply to any screen, at home, school, or a friend's. If a
learner discloses that someone has asked them for private information or to meet, follow
your local safeguarding procedure calmly and immediately. A learner who is blind or has
low vision sorts by hearing each item and saying "private" or "okay"; a learner who does
not use a speaking voice points or signs; a learner who is deaf or hard of hearing
follows the cards and demonstration from the front, since every rule is shown, not only
told. This guidance reflects widely used online-safety education for children (keep
personal information private, be kind, and tell a trusted adult), as promoted by UNICEF
and child-safety organizations. This lesson completes D03.S4.04.02 and closes the unit.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** The internet is a tool, like a road or a pool — wonderful when
   you know the rules. Today you learn the rules for being **safe and kind online**.
2. **Private or not? (15 min).** Some things are **private information** — details that
   could let a stranger find you or pretend to be you: your **full name**, **home
   address**, **school's name**, and **password**. Some things are okay to share: your
   favorite game or your favorite color. Sort each card: private, or okay to share?
   Remember: **never share private information online**.
3. **Be kind online (10 min).** Online, words reach real people. Treat people as kindly
   as you would face to face. If someone is unkind, do not be cruel back — walk away and
   tell a trusted adult. Practice a kind message: "That was a fun game! Thanks for
   playing."
4. **Stop and tell (10 min).** If anything online feels wrong — a stranger asking private
   questions, a scary message, a request to meet — **stop**, **close it**, and **tell a
   trusted adult**. You are never in trouble for telling, and you should never meet an
   online stranger alone. Practice saying it: "I will tell a trusted adult."
5. **Make your pledge (5 min).** Draw or write your online-safety pledge: "I keep my
   private information private. I am kind online. If something feels wrong, I stop and
   tell a trusted adult."
6. **Close (5 min).** Say it together: "I am the boss of my information and my words. I
   am safe and kind online, and I tell a trusted adult when I need help."

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Sort a few cards first (full name, favorite color) and model the
  "tell a trusted adult" phrase; accept spoken, pointed, or drawn answers.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Point to the "private" and "okay" areas and to the
  trusted-adult picture for each item.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Hear each item and say "private" or "okay"; repeat the
  pledge aloud.
- **Extension:** Write three things that are private and explain why a password must
  never be shared, and name two trusted adults to tell in order.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner sort private from shareable information
  and say, in their own way, what to do if something feels wrong online?
- **Portfolio:** The online-safety pledge, kept in the unit portfolio.
- **Self:** The learner names one piece of private information and one trusted adult they
  would tell.

## Home connection

With a grown-up, name the things your family keeps private online and who you would tell
if something felt wrong. Agree together on one kind rule for your family's screens.
