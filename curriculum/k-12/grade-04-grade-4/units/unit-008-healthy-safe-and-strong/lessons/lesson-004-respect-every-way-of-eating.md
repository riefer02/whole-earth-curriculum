# Lesson 4 — Respect Every Way of Eating

## Summary

Learners explore why people eat differently — culture, religion, health or allergy,
preference, and access — and practice treating every way of eating with respect. They
respond kindly to scenarios about different, restricted, and scarce diets, and make a
group "respect promise." Dignity and joy, never shame.

## Objectives

- Describe how different families and cultures eat different healthy foods, and treat
  every way of eating with respect. _(D03.S3.04.02)_

## Connection

Two friends can sit at the same table and eat completely different foods — and both are
right. One might not eat a certain food for their **religion**, another because of an
**allergy**, another because that is what their family has and can afford, and another
just because they prefer it. None of these is better than another. Today you will
practice the skill that makes every table a kind table: **respect**.

## Materials

- "Respect at the table" scenario cards
- A "ways of eating" reminder chart
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Prepare the scenario cards (a friend who cannot eat a certain food, a family that
  fasts, a friend whose family has little food, a friend who eats different foods) and a
  kind response for each.
- Prepare the reminder chart with five reasons people eat differently: culture, religion,
  health/allergy, preference, and access.
- Read the facilitator note fully before teaching; keep the tone warm and protective.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). **The heart of the lesson is kindness, not
judgment.** Teach the five reasons people eat differently — **culture**, **religion**,
**health or allergy**, **preference**, and **access** (what is available and affordable)
— and that every one of them deserves respect. Be especially careful with **food
scarcity**: a child whose family has little food, or whose meals differ from the class
"norm," must never be made to feel less. Never ask learners to disclose their own hunger
or scarcity, and never single anyone out; if a child shares something hard, respond with
quiet care and follow your local safeguarding practice. Model kind words ("That looks
delicious for your body," "What do you like about it?", "I'd love to learn about your
food") and kind silence when a friend does not want to talk about food. A learner who is
blind or has low vision hears each scenario and the kind response described; a learner
who does not use a speaking voice points to the kind response picture or signs; a learner
who is deaf or hard of hearing follows the cards and demonstration from the front, since
every response is shown, not only told. The framing that diets vary by culture, religion,
health need, preference, and access reflects WHO and FAO accounts of dietary diversity
and food security. This lesson completes D03.S3.04.02 and turns to the unit's safety
strand next.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** People eat different foods for many reasons, and every reason
   deserves **respect** — treating another person's way as good and worthy, even when it
   is not your own.
2. **Meet the five reasons (10 min).** Look at the reminder chart. Some people eat
   differently because of **culture** — the foods of their people. Some because of
   **religion** — foods they choose to eat or not eat for their faith. Some because of
   **health or allergy** — a food that could make their body sick. Some because of
   **preference** — they simply like or dislike it. And some because of **access** — what
   their family has and can afford. Every one is okay, and every one deserves respect.
3. **Choose the kind response (15 min).** Read each scenario card and pick the kind
   response. "My friend can't eat that food — I'll say 'no problem, eat what works for
   your body,' and I won't tease." "My friend's family is fasting — I'll be patient and
   not push food." "My friend's family has little food — I'll share if I can and never
   make fun." "My friend eats different foods than me — I'll ask kindly, 'what do you
   like about it?'"
4. **Make a respect promise (10 min).** Each learner says or draws one kind word or action
   for a different way of eating, and everyone repeats it together.
5. **Close (5 min).** Say it together: "People eat different foods for many reasons, and
   every reason deserves respect. I treat every table with kindness."

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use two scenarios first and model the kind response word for word; accept
  spoken, pointed, or drawn answers.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Point to the kind-response picture for each scenario and
  draw your own respect promise.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Hear each scenario and its kind response described and
  repeat the kind words aloud.
- **Extension:** Write a short note welcoming a new friend whose food is different, and
  name one question you would ask to learn about their food kindly.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner match a different way of eating to a kind
  response and, in their own way, explain why respect matters?
- **Self:** The learner names one reason people eat differently and one kind word or
  action they can use.

## Home connection

With a grown-up, name one reason a person might eat differently from your family, and one
kind thing you could say to them. If your family has a food rule or tradition, share what
it means to you.
