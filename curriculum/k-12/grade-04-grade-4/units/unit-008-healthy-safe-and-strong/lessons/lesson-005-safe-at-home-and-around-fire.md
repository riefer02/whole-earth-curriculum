# Lesson 5 — Safe at Home and Around Fire

## Summary

Learners open the safety strand by naming and practicing basic home and fire safety rules:
never touch matches or lighters, tell an adult about smoke or fire, **stop, drop, and
roll** if clothing catches fire, crawl low under smoke, and know two ways out. The tone is
calm and empowering, never frightening.

## Objectives

- Name and practice basic safety rules for home and fire. _(D03.S4.04.01)_

## Connection

Home is where you should feel safest — and a few simple rules help keep it that way.
Most home dangers are easy to prevent: a clear floor, matches kept away from children, a
smoke alarm that warns everyone early. Knowing what to do means you can act calmly
instead of freezing if something does happen. Today you will learn and practice the
rules.

## Materials

- Home and fire safety rule cards
- A "my safety rules" chart
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Prepare the rule cards and the "my safety rules" chart (this chart will grow across the
  next safety lessons).
- Prepare to demonstrate "stop, drop, and roll" and crawling low under smoke.
- Read the facilitator note fully; keep the tone calm and reassuring.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). **Empower, never frighten.** Teach the
rules plainly and practice them so they become automatic: never touch matches, lighters,
or a cooking flame without an adult; if you see smoke or fire, **tell an adult** and get
out; if your clothes catch fire, **stop, drop, and roll** (stop moving, drop to the
ground, cover your face, roll back and forth); if there is smoke, **crawl low** under it;
and **know two ways out** of every room. Stress that these are grown-up jobs first — a
child's job is to get out and get help, not to fight a fire. Adapt every motion for every
body: a learner who uses a wheelchair practices the "two ways out" plan and how to alert
an adult; a learner with limited movement learns to "drop and cover, then call" in a way
that works for them. A learner who is deaf or hard of hearing must know a smoke alarm
signal they can feel or see (a flashing strobe alarm or a trusted person); a learner who
is blind or has low vision practices the route hand-over-hand and says the rules aloud.
Practice the motions in the open, never near a real flame. The fire-safety steps follow
widely taught guidance (stop-drop-roll, crawl low under smoke, know two ways out), as
recommended by fire-safety and Red Cross organizations. This lesson opens D03.S4.04.01
and leads into road and water safety.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** A **safety rule** is a simple "what to do" that keeps you from
   getting hurt. Today you will learn the rules for home and fire.
2. **Home rules (10 min).** Keep floors clear so no one trips. Never touch matches,
   lighters, or a cooking flame without an adult. If you see smoke or fire, **tell an
   adult** right away. Point to each rule card as we name it.
3. **Stop, drop, and roll (10 min).** If your clothes ever catch fire, **stop** moving,
   **drop** to the ground and cover your face, and **roll** back and forth until the fire
   is out. Practice the three motions in the open, in order.
4. **Crawl low and get out (10 min).** In a fire, smoke rises, so the clean air is near
   the floor. Practice **crawling low** under the "smoke" (a raised cloth or imagined
   smoke). Then learn **two ways out** of this room — a door and a window or second door.
   Trace both ways.
5. **Grown-ups help (5 min).** Remember: firefighting is a grown-up's job. Your job is to
   stay safe, get out, and get help. A child never has to fight a fire alone.
6. **Close (5 min).** Say it together: "At home I stay safe: no matches, tell an adult,
   stop-drop-roll, crawl low, and know two ways out."

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Practice one rule at a time and model each motion; accept spoken, drawn,
  or gestured answers.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Order the rule cards and show each motion with the body.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Say the rules aloud in order and practice the motions
  hand-over-hand with a guide.
- **Extension:** Draw a floor plan of one room at home with two ways out, and name the
  smoke-alarm sound your family should listen for.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name and act out the home and fire safety
  rules, with "tell an adult" and "two ways out" included?
- **Self:** The learner names the rule they practiced best and the first thing to do if
  they see smoke or fire.

## Home connection

With a grown-up, find two ways out of one room in your home and check whether your home
has a smoke alarm. Practice "stop, drop, and roll" together — without any real fire.
