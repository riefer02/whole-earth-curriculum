# Lesson 3 — Many Healthy Tables

## Summary

Learners discover that there is no single "right" healthy meal: families and cultures
around the world eat many different healthy foods. They meet several world cuisines, add
their own family's foods to a class chart, and learn to describe and honor many ways of
eating well.

## Objectives

- Describe how different families and cultures eat different healthy foods, and treat
  every way of eating with respect. _(D03.S3.04.02)_

## Connection

Picture a healthy meal. Maybe you pictured rice and beans, or noodles and greens, or
bread and lentil stew, or fish and vegetables. There is no *one* right answer — and that
is the wonderful thing. Every family and culture has its own healthy table, built from
the foods of its land, its history, and its traditions. Today you will see many of them.

## Materials

- "Many healthy tables" picture cards (varied healthy meals from around the world)
- A large "our healthy tables" chart
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Prepare the picture cards showing varied healthy meals (a rice-and-vegetable bowl, an
  injera platter with lentils, tortillas with beans, a fish-and-vegetable stew, noodles
  with greens) — all different, all healthy, none ranked above another.
- Prepare the class chart and set a warm, respectful tone for sharing family foods.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). **There is no single correct healthy
meal, and no culture's food is better than another's.** Teach that what makes a meal
healthy is that it feeds the body well — a mix of grains or roots, vegetables or fruits,
and protein — and that every cuisine has its own beautiful way of doing this. Honor that
some families grow, gather, fish, or herd their food, and some buy it; all are worthy.
Be careful never to let "healthy" become a weapon: do not shame any food a learner's
family eats, and remember that scarcity is real — a family may not always have a full
"balanced" plate, and that is a question of access, never of worth. A learner who is
blind or has low vision learns the meals by having each one described and named; a
learner who does not use a speaking voice points to a card or draws their family's food;
a learner who is deaf or hard of hearing follows the cards and the chart from the front,
since every food is shown, not only told. The framing that healthy diets appear in many
food cultures reflects FAO and WHO accounts that all regions have healthy traditional
dietary patterns. This lesson opens D03.S3.04.02 and leads into treating every way of
eating with respect.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of one healthy meal your family makes. There are millions of
   different answers, and every one of them can be right.
2. **Meet the world's tables (15 min).** Look at each picture card and name what you see:
   a **rice and vegetable bowl**, an **injera platter** with lentils, **tortillas with
   beans**, a **fish and vegetable stew**, **noodles with greens**. These come from
   different lands and different **cultures** — the shared foods, stories, and ways of a
   people. Each one gives the body grains or roots, vegetables or fruit, and protein. All
   are healthy; none is better than the others.
3. **Why are they all healthy? (10 min).** A healthy meal feeds the body well. Point to
   the grain or root in each picture, the vegetable or fruit, and the protein. Say what
   you notice: different foods, same idea — feeding the body well. Tell a partner one
   meal and what makes it healthy.
4. **Add your own table (10 min).** Draw one healthy meal your own family or culture
   eats. Then add it to the class chart — our room now holds many healthy tables from
   many homes.
5. **Close (5 min).** Say it together: "There are many healthy tables in the world. Every
   family's way of eating deserves respect."

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Look at two or three cards first and name one food in each; accept spoken,
  drawn, or pointed answers.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Point to a food on a card and to your drawing of your own
  family's meal.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Hear each meal described in words and name the grain,
  vegetable, and protein in each.
- **Extension:** Name the country or community a card's meal comes from, and describe one
  more healthy dish from that same culture.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner describe two different healthy meals from
  different cultures and say, in their own way, why both are healthy?
- **Portfolio:** The learner's drawing of their family's healthy meal, kept in the unit
  portfolio.
- **Self:** The learner names one new food they saw today and one healthy meal from their
  own home.

## Home connection

At your next meal, name what is on your family's table and tell a grown-up how it feeds
your body — the grain or root, the vegetable or fruit, and the protein. Then ask a
grown-up about a meal from their own childhood.
