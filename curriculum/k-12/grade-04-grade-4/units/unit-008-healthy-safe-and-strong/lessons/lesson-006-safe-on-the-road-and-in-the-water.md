# Lesson 6 — Safe on the Road and in the Water

## Summary

Learners name and practice basic road and water safety rules: stop, look left-right-left,
and cross at a safe place; walk and wear a helmet; swim only with an adult or lifeguard
and never alone; and reach or throw to help someone in the water rather than going in.
Calm, clear, and empowering.

## Objectives

- Name and practice basic safety rules for road and water. _(D03.S4.04.01)_

## Connection

Every day you are a **pedestrian** — a person on foot — near roads, and many days you are
near water, whether a pool, a river, a lake, or the sea. Roads and water are wonderful
parts of life, and a few simple rules let you enjoy them safely. Today you will learn and
practice those rules.

## Materials

- Road and water safety rule cards
- The growing "my safety rules" chart
- Paper and crayons

## Preparation

- Prepare the rule cards and add road and water columns to the "my safety rules" chart.
- Prepare to demonstrate a safe crossing and the "reach or throw" action.
- Keep the tone calm and encouraging; this is about capability, not fear.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). **Teach and practice; do not frighten.**
Road rules: **stop, look left-right-left**, and cross at a safe place (a crosswalk or
corner) with an adult or when it is clear; **walk, don't run**; and **wear a helmet**
when cycling. Water rules: **swim only with an adult or lifeguard** and **never swim
alone**; and if you see someone in trouble in the water, **reach or throw** something
(a stick, a rope, a float) and **call an adult** — never go in yourself, because the
person in trouble could pull a child under. Honor that roads and water look very
different around the world — busy city streets, quiet lanes, rivers, seas, and wells —
so adapt the specific "where to cross" and "where to swim" to the learner's own place. In
places where cars drive on the **left** (much of Asia, Africa, and the United Kingdom,
for example), reverse the look order: stop, look **right-left-right**, then cross —
always look both ways and look again toward the nearest oncoming traffic. A
learner who uses a wheelchair practices looking and a safe crossing route and the
"reach or throw" rule with a rope; a learner who is blind or has low vision practices
the steps by listening for traffic and being guided through the motions; a learner who
is deaf or hard of hearing follows the demonstration and cards from the front, since
every rule is shown, not only told. The road rules follow widely used pedestrian-safety
guidance (look left-right-left, cross at a crosswalk), and the water rules follow
lifesaving guidance (swim with a lifeguard, reach or throw — don't go in), as promoted
by the World Health Organization and water-safety organizations. This lesson continues
D03.S4.04.01 and leads into weather safety.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Remember the safety rules you already know from home and fire. Now
   add the rules for two more places you go all the time: roads and water.
2. **Road rules (15 min).** To cross a road, **stop** at the edge, **look left, right,
   and left again**, and cross only at a safe place — a crosswalk or corner — with an
   adult or when it is clear. (In places where cars drive on the **left**, look
   **right, left, and right again** — always look both ways and toward the nearest
   traffic.) **Walk, don't run.** If you cycle, **wear a helmet**.
   Practice the crossing steps with an imagined road: stop, look left, look right, look
   left, cross.
3. **Water rules (15 min).** In water, the biggest rule is **never swim alone** — swim
   only with an **adult or a lifeguard** watching. If someone is in trouble in the
   water, **reach or throw** something to them (a stick, a rope, a float) and **call an
   adult** — never go in yourself. Practice the reach-and-throw action with an imagined
   rope or stick.
4. **Match rule to place (5 min).** Which rule is for the road, and which for the water?
   "Wear a helmet" is road; "reach or throw" is water; "look left-right-left" is road;
   "never swim alone" is water. Point to each rule card.
5. **Close (5 min).** Say it together: "On the road I stop, look left-right-left, and
   walk. In the water I swim with an adult and reach or throw — never go in. I know my
   safety rules."

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Practice one rule at a time with a partner; accept spoken, drawn, or
  gestured answers.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Order the rule cards and act out each rule with the body.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Say the steps aloud in order and practice the motions
  with a guide.
- **Extension:** Describe the specific safe place to cross near your own home or school,
  and name who watches when you swim.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name and act out the road and water rules
  — with "look left-right-left" and "swim with an adult" included?
- **Self:** The learner names the rule they practiced best and one rule they will use
  this week.

## Home connection

With a grown-up, find the safest place to cross the road near your home and practice
stop-look-left-right-left. Name who must be with you before you swim.
