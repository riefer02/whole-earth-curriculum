# Lesson 10 — Points, Lines, Segments, Rays & Angles

## Summary

Learners draw and identify the basic building blocks of geometry: a **point**, a
**line**, a **line segment**, a **ray**, and an **angle**, and name their parts — an
**endpoint**, a **vertex** — and the three kinds of angle: **right**, **acute**, and
**obtuse**. Geometry begins with a few simple figures you can see, point to, and make
with your own body.

## Objectives

- Draw and identify points, lines, line segments, rays, and angles, and classify
  shapes by their lines and angles. _(D05.S3.04.01)_

## Connection

Look around: the horizon is a line, the edge of a book is a line segment, a sunbeam
or the beam of a flashlight is a ray, and the corner where two walls meet is an angle.
Geometry is not something invented in a textbook — it is the shapes of the world,
named. People in many places and times have measured fields, built roofs, and mapped
stars with these same figures; the words we use today come from a long human
conversation about shape and space.

## Materials

- A straightedge (ruler or straight stick) (per learner)
- Paper or slate and a drawing tool

## Preparation

- Give each learner a straightedge.
- Prepare (or plan to draw) the five figures as a worked example.
- Clear space to move and act out the figures.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It opens the **Geometry** strand for
Grade 4 — the new skill is the precise vocabulary of points, lines, segments, rays,
and angles, and the three angle sizes. Teach each figure with a **worked example**
(philosophy §13) and let learners act it out with their bodies (Van de Walle, Karp &
Bay-Williams, 2019). The key distinctions are *finite vs infinite* (a segment has two
endpoints; a ray has one; a line has none) and *angle size* (right is a square
corner, acute is smaller, obtuse is larger). **Geometry vocabulary is learned at each
learner's own pace; naming a figure quickly is not a measure of smartness, and a
shape is a fact about the world, never about a person.** Geometry is a human and
cross-cultural achievement — land-measuring, building, and star-mapping appear across
many traditions, not one. Use the home language's words for these figures; all are
correct. For learners who are Deaf or hard of hearing, sign each figure; for learners
who are blind or have low vision, feel string and sticks and trace each figure by
touch. Learners with limited movement direct a partner to draw. Mastery arrives at
its own pace.

## Procedure

1. **Gather — shapes everywhere (5 min).** Point to something straight in the room.
   Point to a corner. Geometry is the study of shapes like these.
2. **Meet the five figures (12 min).** Learn and draw each, one at a time:
   - A **point** is an exact spot — no size, just a place.
   - A **line** is straight and goes on forever in both directions (draw arrows on
     both ends).
   - A **line segment** is part of a line with two **endpoints**.
   - A **ray** starts at one **endpoint** and goes on forever in one direction.
   - An **angle** is two rays that meet at a point called the **vertex**.
3. **Act it out (8 min).** Make each figure with your body: a point is a fingertip; a
   line is both arms stretched wide; a segment is a fist to an elbow; a ray is one arm
   from a fist pointing outward; an angle is a bent elbow.
4. **The three angles (10 min).** Make an angle with your elbow. A **right angle** is
   a perfect square corner; an **acute** angle is smaller (narrower); an **obtuse**
   angle is bigger (wider). Find one of each in the room.
5. **Check with a partner (5 min).** Trade drawings. Can your partner name each figure
   and its parts? Point to the vertex and endpoints together, kindly.
6. **Close (5 min).** Remember: a point is a place, a line goes on forever, a segment
   stops, a ray starts and keeps going, and an angle is two rays meeting. These five
   are the alphabet of shapes.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Learn point, segment, and angle today; add line and ray another day.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Feel string and sticks to trace each figure and its
  endpoints.
- **Extension:** Find and classify five angles in the room or outdoors as right,
  acute, or obtuse, and justify each.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner draw and name a point, line, segment,
  ray, and angle, and identify endpoints and the vertex?
- **Self:** Can the learner classify an angle as right, acute, or obtuse and tell how
  they know?

## Home connection

At home, find one of each figure — a point, a line, a segment, a ray, and an angle —
in your house or street, and tell a grown-up which is which and how you know.
