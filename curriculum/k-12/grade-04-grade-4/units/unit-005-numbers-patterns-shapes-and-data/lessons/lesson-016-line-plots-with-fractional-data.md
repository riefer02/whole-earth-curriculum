# Lesson 16 — Line Plots with Fractional Data

## Summary

Learners measure objects to the nearest **half** or **quarter** unit and record the
measurements on a **line plot** — a number line with one mark per object — then answer
questions about the data: which length is most common, which is least, and how many
in all. This closes the unit: data becomes a picture you can read, and every skill
from the unit — measuring, fractions, comparing — comes together in one honest chart.

## Objectives

- Make and interpret line plots with fractional measurements and answer questions
  about the data. _(D05.S4.04.02)_

## Connection

When many things are measured and the numbers are a little different each time, a
list is hard to read. A **line plot** lines the measurements up in order and stacks a
mark for each one, so the shape of the data appears: you can see at a glance which
length is common and which is rare. Rain trackers, garden keepers, and scientists
everywhere use plots like this to watch how things grow and change. Recording data
honestly is a small act of truth-telling — you show what you measured, not what you
wished it said.

## Materials

- A ruler marked in halves and quarters (per pair)
- A handful of small objects to measure (leaves, strips, sticks) (per pair)
- Paper or slate and a drawing tool

## Preparation

- Give each pair a marked ruler and objects to measure.
- Prepare (or plan to draw) a worked example line plot.
- Clear space to measure and mark.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") and closes the unit. The new skill is
building and reading a **line plot with fractional measurements** — a data
representation that combines measuring, fractions, and comparing (Van de Walle, Karp
& Bay-Williams, 2019; National Research Council, 2009). Model the plot once as a
**worked example** (philosophy §13): measure several objects, mark each above its
length on the number line, then read the plot together. This lesson is also the
unit's **spaced retrieval** payoff — ask learners to recall earlier skills (measuring
from Lesson 14, fractions and comparing from Lessons 1–2 and 12–13) as they build the
plot. **A measurement that is a little off is not a failing; it is honest data, and
every learner's data has a place on the plot. A plot shows facts, never ranks people.**
Data-keeping is human and cross-cultural — people have recorded rainfall, harvests,
and heights in many traditions, each an act of careful attention. Count in the home
language. For learners who are Deaf or hard of hearing, sign each value; for learners
who are blind or have low vision, use a tactile plot with raised marks. Learners with
limited movement direct a partner to measure. Mastery arrives at its own pace.

## Procedure

1. **Gather — many little differences (5 min).** Measure several leaves or sticks. The
   numbers are close but not the same. How do you show them all at once? A line plot.
2. **Worked example — build a line plot (10 min).** Draw a number line marked in
   halves. Measure each object to the nearest half unit, and for each object put one
   **X** above its length. If two objects are the same length, stack the X's. Now read
   it: the tallest stack is the most common length; the lone marks are the rarest.
3. **Collect and plot your own (15 min).** Measure your own set of objects to the
   nearest half or quarter unit. Draw your number line, mark each measurement, and
   stack the X's.
4. **Answer questions (8 min).** Read your plot: Which length is most common? Which is
   least? How many objects in all? What is the difference between the longest and
   shortest?
5. **Check with a partner (3 min).** Trade plots. Can your partner answer the same
   questions about your data? Check together, kindly.
6. **Close — our data (4 min).** Put your plot in your portfolio. Remember: a line plot
   is data you can see, and honest data is a small act of truth-telling — you show what
   you measured.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Measure to the nearest whole unit first; add halves and quarters
  another day.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Build a tactile plot with raised marks and read the
  stacks by touch.
- **Extension:** Measure to the nearest eighth of a unit and plot the finer data,
  explaining the new detail it shows.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner measure to the nearest half or quarter
  unit, record each measurement as a mark on a line plot, and answer questions about
  the data?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The completed line plot, kept as evidence of the unit's
  data skill.

## Home connection

At home, measure a set of small things (spoons, leaves, fingers) to the nearest half
unit, draw a line plot, and tell a grown-up which size is most common and how you
know.
