# Lesson 6 — A Small Act of Care

## Summary

Care is not only a warm feeling — it is something you **organize and do**. Learners plan
a small, real act of care (clean a shared space, water a shared plant, help a neighbor)
by deciding **what, where, who, and when**, then carry it out. The act is recorded and
becomes a centerpiece of the closing celebration — proof that the class turned care into
action.

## Objectives

- Organize a small act of care, such as cleaning a shared space or helping a neighbor.
  _(D02.S4.04.01)_

## Connection

Think of a place you share — a classroom, a path, a garden, a bus stop, a stairwell.
When someone cares for it, everyone who uses it benefits; when no one does, everyone
loses a little. Care is often a **small act**: a floor swept, a plant watered, a heavy
bag carried, a litter-free path. Small acts, done together, add up to a community that
takes care of itself. Today you don't just *feel* like caring — you organize one small
act and do it.

## Materials

- A "small act of care" plan sheet (what / where / who / when / what we'll need)
- Simple supplies for the chosen act (cloth, bin, watering can, etc.)

## Preparation

- Prepare the plan sheet with the four questions: what will we do? where? who will
  help? when will we do it?
- Choose, with learners, one shared act of care the class can actually do this lesson,
  and have its simple supplies ready.
- Ensure safety: no task should involve unsafe heights, sharp objects, or hazardous
  waste; litter-gathering uses gloves or a safe tool.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It moves from **planning** (Lesson 3's
plan-try-adjust) and **roles** (Lesson 4) into **organized care** — the unit's action
heart. The commitment: **care is organized, not random, and it serves the common good,
not just ourselves.** Choose an act that is real, free, and safe — tidying a shared
space, watering a class plant, wiping tables, helping carry for a neighbor, clearing a
path. Model the planning as a worked example first (what/where/who/when) before
independent work, since organizing is a new procedural skill. Keep it egalitarian: every
task within the act matters equally, and no one's part is "more important" because it is
more visible. Handle ability gently — find a part for every body (a learner with limited
mobility can be the "checker" or "caller" who verifies each part is done). The
"environment" lens is natural here: caring for a shared space is also caring for the
living world. For a learner who is blind or has low vision, assign a part that uses touch,
hearing, or verbal checking. For a non-speaking learner, accept acting, pointing, or a
partner voicing the plan. Close by recording the act — the celebration (Lesson 7) will
show what care looked like.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Care is a feeling *and* an action. What small ways have you seen
   people care for a shared space or a neighbor this year?
2. **Watch a worked example (5 min).** Read or watch this plan together: *What — tidy the
   reading corner. Where — our classroom. Who — Amara sorts books, Jo wipes the shelf,
   Mei sweeps. When — right after lunch today.* See how "what, where, who, when" turns a
   wish into a plan?
3. **Plan your act (10 min).** As a class (or on your own), choose one small act of care.
   On your plan sheet, fill in: **what** will we do? **where**? **who** will help (and
   what is each person's part)? **when** will we do it? **What will we need?**
4. **Carry it out (20 min).** Do the act of care together, each person doing their part.
   Notice how it feels to *do* care, not just plan it. Work safely and help each other.
5. **Reflect (5 min).** Sit back and look at what you did. Ask: *who benefits?* The
   answer is *everyone who uses this space or lives near it* — including you. That is the
   **common good**.
6. **Record it (5 min).** Write or draw what you did on your plan sheet — the act, and
   who it helped. This page will be shown at the celebration.
7. **Close (5 min).** Small acts, done together, add up. You just made your community a
   little better. Add your page to your portfolio.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer a menu of safe, concrete care acts to choose from; pair learners so
  each has a clear, small part.
- **Support (mobility/sensory):** Assign a part that fits each body — checking, calling,
  sorting by touch, or counting what's done.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Act out the part or point to it; a partner voices the
  reflection.
- **Extension:** Write the full plan, lead the act, and reflect on one thing that worked
  and one thing you'd change next time.

## Assessment

- **Formative (performance + portfolio + observation):** Can the learner contribute a
  concrete part to a planned act of care (or plan a solo act with what/where/who/when)
  and carry it out — by doing, showing, signing, or a partner voicing?

## Home connection

Choose one small act of care for a place you share at home or in your neighborhood this
week — sweep, water, carry, clear — and do it. Tell your class what changed.
