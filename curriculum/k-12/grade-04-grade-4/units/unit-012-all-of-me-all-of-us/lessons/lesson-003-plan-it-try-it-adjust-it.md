# Lesson 3 — Plan It, Try It, Adjust It

## Summary

A goal is not a promise to get everything right the first time — it is a plan you are
willing to **adjust**. Learners set one real, reachable goal, break it into steps, try
the first step right away, notice honestly what happened, and — when a step does not
work — change it and try again. The "plan, try, adjust" page joins the portfolio and
carries the year's agency work forward into the summer and Grade 5.

## Objectives

- Plan the steps to reach a goal, try the plan, and adjust when a step does not work.
  _(D01.S3.04.01)_

## Connection

Every builder, cook, athlete, and gardener knows the same secret: the first plan is
almost never the last plan. A cook tastes the soup and adds salt; a gardener sees a
seedling leaning and turns it toward the light; a builder finds a wobbly stone and puts
a wedge under it. The people who reach their goals are not the ones who never fail —
they are the ones who **adjust**. Today you make a plan, try it, and learn the grown-up
skill of changing course when a step doesn't work.

## Materials

- A "plan, try, adjust" page (plan / what happened / adjustment)
- Writing and drawing supplies

## Preparation

- Prepare the page with three labeled sections: "my plan" (numbered steps), "what
  happened when I tried," and "my adjustment."
- Have writing and drawing supplies ready.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It spirals up from Grade 3's "set a goal
and name the first step" to the fuller cycle — **plan, try, adjust**. Two commitments
matter. First, **the adjustment is the lesson, not a failure**: normalize it explicitly
with your own example ("I planned to read every night, but evenings were too tired, so I
moved it to mornings"). Second, **the goal is the learner's own and costs no money** —
some learners live with scarcity, so coach toward concrete, free, near goals (read one
new book; learn a new knot; water the class plant for a week; greet three people kindly
each day). Model the plan-try-adjust sequence as a worked example *before* independent
work — this is a new procedural skill, and novices need to see it done (Kirschner,
Sweller & Clark, 2006). Give the sentence frames: "My first step is…" / "When I tried,
I noticed…" / "So I will change…". For a learner who is blind or has low vision, do the
cycle aloud or with objects in a line. For a non-speaking learner, accept pointing,
drawing, signing, or a partner voicing. Keep the tone light: adjusting is clever, not
shameful.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Last year you set a goal and its first step. This year you learn
   the next grown-up power: when a step doesn't work, you **adjust** and try again.
2. **Watch a worked example (5 min).** Watch or read this example together: *Goal — learn
   to water the class plant without spilling. Plan — (1) fill the can halfway, (2) pour
   slowly near the stem. Try — the can was still too heavy and splashed. Adjust — (1) fill
   it one-quarter full and use two hands.* See how the plan changed after trying?
3. **Set your goal and plan (10 min).** Write one real goal on your page. Then write the
   steps, in order. Make the first step small enough to try *today*.
4. **Try it (10 min).** Do the first step now — or, if the goal is for later (like "read
   one book"), do a tiny practice of it right now. Notice carefully: *what actually
   happened?* Write or draw it in the "what happened" section. Be honest, not harsh.
5. **Adjust (10 min).** Look at what happened. If the step worked, write what you'll do
   next. If it did not work, change it: make it smaller, get help, switch the order, use
   two hands. Write your **adjustment**.
6. **Share and promise (5 min).** Tell a partner your plan, what happened, and your
   adjustment — and listen to theirs. Promise yourself: *I will try my adjusted step.*
7. **Close (5 min).** Adjusting is not failing; it is how every goal is actually
   reached. Add your page to your portfolio.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer a menu of small, free goals with pre-written steps to choose from;
  scribe the "what happened" and "adjustment" lines.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Point to or act out each step; a partner voices the
  adjustment.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Lay one object per step in a line and move one to show
  the adjustment; the plan lives in words and objects.
- **Extension:** Write a three-step plan, try the first two steps, and record *two*
  adjustments over the week.

## Assessment

- **Formative (self + portfolio + observation):** Can the learner state a goal, list
  ordered steps, describe honestly what happened on a try, and name one concrete
  adjustment — by word, drawing, sign, or a partner voicing?

## Home connection

Take your plan home and do your adjusted step once before the next lesson. Tell someone
how the adjustment changed what happened.
