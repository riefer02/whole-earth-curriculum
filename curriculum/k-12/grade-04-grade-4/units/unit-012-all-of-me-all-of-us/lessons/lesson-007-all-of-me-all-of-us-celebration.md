# Lesson 7 — All of Me, All of Us: Our Celebration

## Summary

The year ends as it began — with who we are and who we are to each other. Learners
display their six unit pages — the **me map**, the **tradition web**, the **plan-try-
adjust**, the **role card**, the **welcome plan**, and the **act of care** — equally,
share what each one shows about who they are becoming, and name one goal (with its first
step) for Grade 5. Then they celebrate. A celebration, never a contest.

## Objectives

- Explain how their interests, abilities, and experiences shape their sense of self.
  _(D01.S2.04.01)_
- Describe a family or cultural tradition and how it connects them to a larger
  community. _(D01.S2.04.02)_
- Plan the steps to reach a goal, try the plan, and adjust when a step does not work.
  _(D01.S3.04.01)_
- Identify a role they can play in their class or neighborhood community and carry it
  out. _(D02.S3.04.01)_
- Describe how communities welcome newcomers and why belonging matters. _(D02.S3.04.02)_
- Organize a small act of care, such as cleaning a shared space or helping a neighbor.
  _(D02.S4.04.01)_

## Connection

All over the world, people gather to mark an ending and a beginning — a harvest, a
season, a graduation, a year's work. At the heart of every good celebration is **care**
and **belonging**: someone set a place, someone remembered a name, someone helped a
smaller one reach the table. Today you gather the whole year into one place — who you
are becoming, the tradition you carry, the goal you planned, the role you played, the
welcome you learned, and the care you gave — and you celebrate it, together. Then you
look ahead. You are not finished; you are only beginning Grade 5.

## Materials

- Every learner's six unit pages
- A space to display the work equally
- A shared celebration (song, clap, movement)

## Preparation

- Gather each learner's six pages: me map, tradition web, plan-try-adjust, role card,
  welcome plan, and act of care.
- Set up the display so every learner's work is shown equally — side by side, none
  higher or more special than another.
- Arrange a circle for sharing, the goal-ahead, and a simple shared celebration.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It is the unit's and year's **capstone**,
with two commitments: **celebration, not competition**, and **every child belongs.** No
best-work awards, no winners, no ranking — every learner's work is displayed equally, and
every child shares or passes as they choose. Honor every family shape, tradition, and
identity: a child's caring circle may be parents, one parent, grandparents, extended kin,
guardians, foster or group care, a chosen family, or a trusted adult — and a learner may
share with any of these, or pass. Handle adoption, loss, and mobility gently: the
tradition and "what I have lived" pages are the learner's own, and a pass is a full
share. In the circle, sharing is by choice; help learners name *concrete* things (the
role they actually did, the care act they actually carried out) so the standards are met
in the everyday, not the grand. Keep the Grade 5 goal small and real, with a first step —
the habit from Lesson 3. For a learner who is Deaf or hard of hearing, make the
celebration visual — clapping, signing, a flag wave, a visual "bravo." For a learner who
is blind or has low vision, offer a spoken or tactile tour of the gallery. Keep the tone
warm and generous: the class has grown together, and everyone belongs.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Today is a celebration of all of you — all of me, all of us.
   There are no winners and no rankings, just everyone, together.
2. **Set up the gallery (10 min).** Lay out or hang every learner's six pages — the me
   map, the tradition web, the plan-try-adjust, the role card, the welcome plan, and the
   act of care. Every piece is shown equally — no one's is higher or more special. If a
   learner chooses not to show a piece, that choice is honored.
3. **Walk the gallery (10 min).** Walk around and notice: each page is a piece of a
   whole person. Look for the interests, abilities, experiences, traditions, goals,
   roles, welcomes, and acts of care — all side by side, all belonging.
4. **Circle of sharing (15 min).** Sit in a circle. Each learner may share one thing
   from their pages — who they are becoming, the tradition they carry, the role they
   played, or the care they gave — or pass. A pass is a full share.
5. **Look ahead (10 min).** From your plan-try-adjust page, name one goal for Grade 5
   and its very first step. Say it to the circle — or hold it in your heart. You are not
   finished growing; you are only beginning.
6. **Celebrate together (10 min).** Make a shared celebration — a song, a clap, a
   movement the class creates together. You grew all year, and you did it by caring for
   each other.
7. **Close (5 min).** Look around. You are a whole person — and you belong to a caring
   community, all of you, together. That is worth celebrating.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Allow a learner to celebrate by pointing to their work or holding up a
  partner's hand; a pass counts as sharing.
- **Support (Deaf/hard-of-hearing):** Make the celebration visual — clapping, signing,
  a flag wave, or a visual "bravo."
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Describe work aloud or offer a spoken or tactile tour
  of the gallery.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Point, sign, or draw; a partner voices the share and the
  goal.
- **Extension:** Connect all six pages into one spoken reflection on who you are
  becoming and what you will carry into Grade 5.

## Assessment

- **Summative (portfolio + performance + observation):** The learner shares their six
  unit pages (displaying them or passing) and shows, in their own way, that they see
  themselves as a whole person — interests, abilities, experiences, tradition, goal,
  role, welcome, and care — and their class as a caring community they belong to, plus
  one goal with a first step for Grade 5 — by word, drawing, sign, or a partner voicing.

## Home connection

Bring your six pages to the people who care for you — your family, whatever shape it
takes — and tell them one thing you are proud of becoming, one tradition you carry, and
the one goal and first step you have set for Grade 5.
