# Lesson 1 — The Me I'm Becoming

## Summary

The year ends by looking at the person each learner is becoming. Learners build a
"me" map with three branches — **what I love doing** (interests), **what I can do**
(abilities), and **what I have lived** (experiences) — and explain, in their own
words, how those three things shape their sense of self. The page joins their
portfolio and becomes the foundation for the closing celebration.

## Objectives

- Explain how their interests, abilities, and experiences shape their sense of self.
  _(D01.S2.04.01)_

## Connection

Think of a favorite food you learned to love because someone in your family made it —
that is an **experience**. Think of a game or subject you can't stop wanting to do —
that is an **interest**. Think of something you can do now that you could not do last
year, like swim farther, read harder books, or keep your temper a little longer —
that is an **ability**. All three mix together to make you *you*. Today you map the
recipe of yourself and notice the person it is making.

## Materials

- A "me" map page (three branches to a center "me")
- Crayons or colored pencils
- A personal portfolio folder

## Preparation

- Prepare the "me" map page: three labeled branches ("what I love doing," "what I can
  do," "what I have lived") that meet at a center circle labeled "me."
- Set out crayons or colored pencils.
- Have each learner's portfolio folder ready to receive the page.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). It is the unit's **opening reflection**,
and it sets the tone for the whole closing: **every child is becoming someone, and no
one is finished.** Two commitments matter most. First, keep the reflection *descriptive,
not ranked* — there are no "best" interests or abilities; a love of caring for a pet is
as real as a love of numbers. Second, hold every life story gently: a learner may have
moved homes, changed caregivers, or lost someone, so "what I have lived" must always be
the child's own choosing, never a demand to disclose. Give the sentence frame "I have
lived through ___ , and it taught me ___" only as an *option*, and honor a pass. The
phrase "sense of self" is the abstraction to unpack — model it aloud: "my sense of self
is the story I tell myself about who I am." For a learner who is blind or has low
vision, make the map in words or tactually (three folded corners or three raised dots).
For a learner who does not use a speaking voice, accept pointing, drawing, signing, or a
partner voicing. Keep the tone warm: this is wonder at a growing person, not a test.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** You have grown a whole year. Today you notice *who* you are
   becoming. Everyone is becoming someone, and no one is finished.
2. **Meet the three branches (10 min).** Look at your "me" map. It has three branches
   and a center. **Interest** = what you love doing. **Ability** = what you can do.
   **Experience** = what you have lived. Talk about one example of each, together, so
   the words are clear.
3. **Fill your map (15 min).** On each branch, write or draw two or three true things.
   What do you love doing? What can you do — now, this year? What have you lived that
   shaped you? These are *yours*; share only what you choose.
4. **Explain how it shapes you (10 min).** Now the center. Read your three branches and
   ask: *How do these make me who I am?* Write or draw one sentence in the center, or
   tell it to a partner. Example: "Because I love drawing and I practiced, I see myself
   as an artist."
5. **Look ahead (5 min).** Draw or imagine a small arrow past your map that says "the me
   I'm becoming." You are not finished. This map is a snapshot of right now.
6. **Close (5 min).** Put your map in your portfolio. It is the first page of the story
   of who you are becoming.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer a menu of example interests, abilities, and experiences to choose
  from; scribe the center sentence a learner says.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Point, sign, or draw each branch; a partner voices the
  center sentence.
- **Support (blind/low-vision):** Build the map in words or tactually; the three
  branches live in memory and speech, not pictures.
- **Support (experienced loss or change):** Honor a pass on "what I have lived"; the
  learner may write "I am still learning to say it."
- **Extension:** Write a short paragraph explaining how *one* experience changed how you
  see yourself, and what you predict you will become next.

## Assessment

- **Formative (self + portfolio + observation):** Can the learner name at least one
  interest, one ability, and one experience, and connect them to a sense of self — by
  word, drawing, sign, or a partner voicing?
- **Observation:** Does the learner speak of themselves with a "becoming," not-finished
  frame (e.g., "I am getting better at…")?

## Home connection

Take your "me" map to someone who cares for you and tell them one thing from each
branch — one thing you love doing, one thing you can do, and one thing you have lived
that shaped you.
