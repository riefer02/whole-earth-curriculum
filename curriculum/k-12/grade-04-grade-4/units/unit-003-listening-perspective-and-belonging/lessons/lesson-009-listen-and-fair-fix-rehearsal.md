# Lesson 9 — Listen and Fair Fix, Rehearsal

## Summary

Learners **rehearse** the unit's two interpersonal skills *together*: **active listening**
(say it back, check) and **fair negotiation** (name it, needs, options, choose, check).
They role-play a disagreement, paraphrase before responding, and then negotiate a
solution both sides can live with.

## Objectives

- Practice active listening by paraphrasing what a partner said before responding.
  _(D02.S1.04.01)_
- Negotiate a fair solution to a disagreement with a peer, with minimal adult help.
  _(D02.S1.04.02)_

## Connection

Real disagreements do not wait to be solved one skill at a time. You listen *and* you
negotiate, in one flow: first you say back what the other person needs so they know they
are heard; then you find a fix that gives both of you something. It is like two steps of
one dance — listen, then fix. A fisher listens to the water and then casts; a friend
hears you out and then helps you find a way. Today you rehearse the whole dance, slowly,
until it feels natural.

## Materials

- A combined-skills poster
- Role-play scenario cards
- A voice recorder (optional)

## Preparation

- Post the combined-skills poster: "listen (say it back, check)" then "fair fix (name it,
  needs, options, choose, check)".
- Prepare friendly role-play scenario cards with two clear sides.
- Arrange a "stage" space for role-plays.

## Facilitator note

This rehearsal combines and retrieves Lessons 1 and 2 — the heart of the unit's
interpersonal work. The evidence-based move here is **spaced practice with feedback**:
rather than teaching anything new, learners retrieve and *use* both skills together, and
get a peer-check on whether they used them (see `docs/contexts.md` on retrieval and
feedback). Run it as guided practice before any independent role-play (Kirschner, Sweller
& Clark, 2006). Protect the flow: *listen first, then fix* — a fair fix that skips the
paraphrase often misses what the other side actually needs. Watch for the learner who
jumps straight to "winning"; invite them back to "what did you hear them need?" Honor
every way of performing — sign, gesture, a written script, a puppet, a scribed line — all
are full rehearsals. This is egalitarian and ethical practice: both voices get heard, and
the fix must serve both. A learner who uses only one skill today is *not yet* at both —
name the one they did well, then invite the second. Read the procedure aloud.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** You have practiced two skills: **listen and say it back** (Lesson
   1) and **negotiate a fair fix** (Lesson 2). Today you put them together, like two steps
   of one dance.
2. **Recall the two skills (10 min).** Say them back without looking: **listen** — say it
   back in your own words, then check. **Fair fix** — name the problem, say what each
   side needs, think of options, choose one both can live with, check it feels fair.
   Watch a worked example: two volunteers run one whole flow for the class.
3. **Rehearse in pairs (20 min).** Draw a role-play card. One of you starts with the
   problem. The other **listens and says it back** before responding. Then together you
   **negotiate a fair fix** using the five steps. Switch roles and try a second scenario.
   Your partner checks you: "Did you say it back? Is the fix fair to both?"
4. **Swap feedback (5 min).** Tell your partner one thing they did well and one thing to
   try next time — kindly, like a coach, never like a judge.
5. **Close (5 min).** Listen, then fix — the whole dance. You have both steps now, and
   you can do them together, with almost no adult help. That is a skill for life.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer the poster as a two-step script and a scenario with only two
  options; a non-speaking learner points to each step as their partner performs it.
- **Extension:** Add a third role — a "checker" who watches and then tells the pair
  whether both skills were used and whether the fix served both sides.

## Assessment

- **Formative (performance/observation):** In the role-play, does the learner paraphrase
  before responding *and* then use the five fair-fix steps to reach a solution both sides
  accept?
- **Peer (check):** The partner (or checker) confirms: "They said it back" and "the fix
  was fair to both."

## Home connection

The next time a disagreement comes up at home, try the whole dance — listen and say it
back, then find a fair fix — and tell someone which step was hardest for you.
