# Lesson 7 — A Small Act of Care

## Summary

Learners **organize a small act of care** — cleaning a shared space or helping a
neighbor — by planning *what* they will care for, *why* it matters, and *how* (the
steps), then carrying it out and recording the difference it made.

## Objectives

- Organize a small act of care, such as cleaning a shared space or helping a neighbor.
  _(D02.S4.04.01)_

## Connection

A swept floor, a watered plant, a bag carried up the stairs for an older neighbor — these
are small acts, and together they are the quiet machinery that keeps a community
livable. In a village, clearing a shared path after a storm helps everyone; in a city
building, wiping the stair rail helps every hand that touches it. No act of care is too
small to matter, and none requires being asked. Today you plan one, do it, and watch a
shared space or a neighbor's day get a little lighter.

## Materials

- An act-of-care planner
- Cleaning or helping supplies (as available)
- A camera or tablet (optional)

## Preparation

- Copy an act-of-care planner for each learner.
- Gather simple supplies for likely acts of care.
- Identify a shared space or a neighbor who could use help (with permission where needed).

## Facilitator note

This is the unit's action lesson, building on Lesson 5's "my role." Teach the planner as
explicit instruction — *what, why, steps, who, what we need* — and model filling one out
(Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006). The value to protect: **care is chosen and organized
by the learners, not performed to be praised**, and the point is the *shared good*, not
an individual reward. This is egalitarianism and ethics in practice: everyone contributes,
and the benefit flows to the whole — including the natural world when the "shared space"
is outdoors. Keep the acts safe, doable, and within reach; a heavy or risky task is
scaled down so every learner can contribute a real step. Honor every ability: a learner
who cannot lift plans, counts, fetches, or holds the door — all are full acts of care.
Record the act for the portfolio and reflect on the *change*, not the praise. Read the
procedure aloud.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Remember Lesson 5: you found a role. Today you take the next
   step — you **organize a small act of care**, from start to finish, on your own.
2. **Plan the act (10 min).** Fill your act-of-care planner. **What** will we care for —
   a messy shelf, a dry plant, a neighbor's heavy bag, a littered path? **Why** does it
   matter — who does it help? **Steps** — what do we do, in order? **Who** helps? **What
   do we need?**
3. **Watch a worked example (5 min).** Model: "We will tidy the book corner. Why: so
   every child can find a book. Steps: one, sort the books; two, wipe the shelf; three,
   put books back spine-out. Who: the three of us. Need: a cloth." Notice the plan is
   small and doable.
4. **Carry it out (20 min).** Do the act of care. Work together; check your steps as you
   go. Then look at the *before* and the *after* — what changed? Record it on your
   planner as a portfolio piece.
5. **Close (5 min).** You just changed your community for the better with your own two
   hands. Small acts, done by many, are how the world is cared for. That power is yours.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer a two-step plan with pictures ("first sort, then wipe") and a
  partner; a non-speaking learner points to the steps and does one part.
- **Extension:** Organize a recurring act of care (a weekly tidy) and invite others to
  join, so the care lasts beyond today.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Did the learner plan an act of care (what, why, steps) and
  carry it out, and can they say what changed because of it?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The completed act-of-care planner with the before/after
  record.

## Home connection

Do one small act of care at home this week — a swept step, a cleared table, a carried
bag — without being asked, and tell someone what changed because of it.
