# Lesson 5 — My Role in My Community

## Summary

Learners identify a real need in their **class or neighborhood community**, match it to a
**role** they can play using their own **strengths**, plan the steps to **carry it out**,
and then actually carry it out — recording what they did as a portfolio artifact.

## Objectives

- Identify a role they can play in their class or neighborhood community and carry it
  out. _(D02.S3.04.01)_

## Connection

Every community runs because many people carry small roles: someone waters the plants,
someone tidies the shared shelf, someone reads to a younger child, someone helps carry a
heavy bag up the stairs. In a fishing village someone mends the nets; in an apartment
building someone watches the door. No role is too small to matter, and every role needs
a person. Today you find *your* role — the one that fits *your* strengths — and you
carry it out, not because an adult told you to, but because you are part of the whole.

## Materials

- A community roles chart
- A "my role" card for each learner
- Paper and pencils

## Preparation

- Post the community roles chart.
- Copy a "my role" card for each learner.
- Plan a short walk of the classroom or neighborhood to spot real needs.

## Facilitator note

This turns outward toward action, building on Grade 3's "contributing fairly to a group
goal." Teach the match as explicit instruction — *a role = a need + a strength* — and
model choosing one (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006). The core value to protect:
**roles are chosen by the learner from their own strengths, never assigned as a
ranking**, and no role is more important than another — the one who tidies and the one
who reads aloud both hold the community up equally. This is egalitarianism lived: care is
distributed, and everyone has something to give. Honor every ability: a learner who
cannot lift carries a lighter but no less vital role; a learner who does not speak aloud
can write, draw, or sign their role. Follow through matters — this lesson's role is meant
to be *carried out*, so plan a role-check later in the week and record it for the
portfolio. Read the procedure aloud.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** You have been listening, negotiating, and reading feelings. Now
   you turn your care into action: today you find **a role you can play** in your class
   or neighborhood — and you *do* it.
2. **Spot the needs (10 min).** Walk the room or neighborhood and look for real needs.
   Is there a mess that needs tidying? A plant that needs water? A younger child who
   wants a story? A heavy bag that needs carrying? List what you see.
3. **Match a role to a strength (10 min).** A role is a **need plus a strength**. Think:
   what can *you* do well? Match it to a need: if you are patient, you might read to a
   younger friend; if you are strong, you might help carry; if you are careful, you might
   tidy the books. Fill your "my role" card: name the role, what it helps, and the steps
   you will take.
4. **Carry it out (15 min).** Do it — right now if you can, or plan the exact time this
   week. Record what you did and how it helped. This is your portfolio piece.
5. **Close (5 min).** A community is not built by big heroes — it is built by many people
   carrying small, chosen roles. You found yours today. Carry it out, and watch the whole
   get stronger.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer a picture menu of possible roles and a two-step card ("first…,
   then…"); a non-speaking learner points to a role picture and shows the steps.
- **Extension:** Take on a role that helps more than one person, and check back after a
   week to see the difference it made.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Did the learner match a role to a real need and their own
  strength, and did they carry it out (or plan concrete steps to)?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The "my role" card with a record of the role carried out.

## Home connection

Look around your home or street for one need you can answer with a strength of yours —
and do it this week, then tell someone what you chose and why.
