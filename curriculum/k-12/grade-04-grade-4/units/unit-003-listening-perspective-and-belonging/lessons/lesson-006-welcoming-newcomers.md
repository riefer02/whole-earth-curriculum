# Lesson 6 — Welcoming Newcomers

## Summary

Learners describe **how communities welcome newcomers** — greeting by name, showing them
around, inviting them to play, learning their words — and explain **why belonging
matters**: people who feel they belong can learn, help, and be themselves.

## Objectives

- Describe how communities welcome newcomers and why belonging matters. _(D02.S3.04.02)_

## Connection

Think of the first day in a new place — a new school, a new street, a new town. You do
not yet know the rules, the faces, or where anything is. Then someone says your name,
shows you where to sit, and invites you to join. In that moment you stop being a
stranger and start to belong. Every culture on Earth has welcome customs: a shared meal,
a bow, a handshake, a cup of tea, a word in your own language. Belonging is not a small
thing — it is the ground a person stands on to learn, to help, and to be fully
themselves.

## Materials

- A welcome-ways chart
- Newcomer scenario cards
- Paper and pencils

## Preparation

- Post the welcome-ways chart.
- Prepare newcomer scenario cards (a new student, a new neighbor, a new family).
- Arrange a welcome circle for the closing.

## Facilitator note

This deepens Grade 3's "compare my community with one far away" and Grade 2's "what
makes a group feel welcoming." The two ideas to land: **welcome is something you do**
(specific, learnable acts), and **belonging matters because it lets people learn, help,
and be themselves** — not because it feels merely pleasant. Teach the welcome ways as
explicit instruction and model each with a greeting (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006).
Hold the global lens: welcome customs differ across cultures, and none is the "right" one
— honor them side by side. Hold the egalitarian lens: a community that welcomes everyone
is stronger for everyone; the newcomer is not a "guest to be tolerated" but a full member
with something to give. Notice the technology lens lightly: a newcomer might connect
online before arriving, and a kind message can be a first welcome — but a screen cannot
replace the seat offered or the name spoken. Keep this lesson warm and concrete, never a
lecture; the welcome circle is the heart of it.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Remember Lesson 5: a community is built of many small roles.
   Today you learn the first role every community shares — **welcoming the newcomer**.
   Have you ever been the new one? What did it feel like?
2. **Meet the welcome ways (10 min).** Learn the acts of welcome: **greet by name**,
   **show them around**, **invite them to play**, **learn their words**, **share a seat
   or a meal**. Watch a worked example: a new student arrives. You say, "Hi, I'm Amara —
   what should I call you?" Then you show them the water fountain and invite them to sit
   with you.
3. **Welcome the newcomer (10 min).** In pairs, take a scenario card — a new student, a
   new neighbor, a new family. Choose two or three welcome ways and act them out, each
   taking a turn as the newcomer and as the welcomer.
4. **Why belonging matters (15 min).** Now answer the big question: *why* does belonging
   matter? Because a person who belongs can **learn** (they know the rules), **help**
   (they join the roles), and **be themselves** (they are not hiding). Write or draw one
   reason, and share it in the circle.
5. **Close (5 min).** Everyone was once the newcomer, somewhere. When you welcome a
   newcomer — with a name, a seat, an invitation — you give them the ground to stand on.
   That is how communities are built, one welcome at a time.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer a two-picture choice ("greet by name" or "show around") and a
  sentence starter "They belong because…"; a non-speaking learner points to welcome-way
  cards.
- **Extension:** Write a full "welcome plan" for a newcomer that includes a greeting in
  their language and one thing the community could learn *from* them.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name at least two ways to welcome a
  newcomer and give one reason belonging matters (to learn, help, or be oneself)?
- **Peer (check):** In the role-play, did the newcomer get welcomed by name, and did both
  partners get a turn as welcomer and newcomer?

## Home connection

If a new person moves onto your street or into your building, greet them by name, show
them one useful thing, and invite them into something — then notice how the welcome
changes the day.
