# Lesson 1 — Feelings Have Strength

## Summary

Learners discover that every feeling has a **strength**, also called its **intensity**,
and that strength can be described as **a little**, **some**, or **a lot**. Using a
feeling thermometer, they name a real feeling and place its strength, learning that
noticing *how strong* a feeling is helps them know themselves and choose what to do
next.

## Objectives

- Describe the intensity of a feeling as a little, some, or a lot, and what each level
  feels like. _(D01.S1.04.01)_

## Connection

Two children can both feel *nervous*, but not the same amount. One feels a **little**
nervous before a spelling quiz — a tiny flutter. The other feels **a lot** nervous
before speaking in front of the whole school — a racing heart and shaky hands. Same
feeling, different strength. In a mountain village, a child feels a **little** worried
when clouds gather, and **a lot** worried when the river rises. Everywhere in the
world, feelings come in sizes. Knowing the size helps you know what kind of help you
might need — and that is a skill you can practice your whole life.

## Materials

- Feeling thermometer chart
- Blank feeling thermometer page
- Pencils and crayons

## Preparation

- Print or draw the feeling thermometer chart (three zones: a little, some, a lot).
- Copy a blank feeling thermometer page for each learner.
- Arrange a circle for gathering and a quiet space for individual work.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The one idea to land is that feeling
*strength* is a real, observable thing — not that some feelings are "good" or "bad."
Model the worked example aloud (a little / some / a lot of the *same* feeling) before
learners place their own. Watch for learners who choose a feeling that is very strong;
honor it calmly and name it with them, then move gently on. Never tell a learner their
feeling is "too big" — the point is *noticing size*, not judging it. See
`docs/facilitation.md` on holding big feelings safely.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Last year you learned that feelings change over time and that
   you can feel more than one feeling at once. Today you learn something new: every
   feeling has a **strength**. A feeling can be small, medium, or big — and you can
   notice the difference.
2. **Meet the feeling thermometer (10 min).** Look at the chart. It is a thermometer
   with three zones: **a little**, **some**, and **a lot**. Feelings rise on it the way
   heat rises in a real thermometer. Here is a worked example: *nervous*. A **little**
   nervous is a tiny flutter before a quiz. **Some** nervous is a warmer, tighter
   feeling before a race. **A lot** nervous is a pounding heart and cold hands before a
   big performance. Same feeling — three different sizes.
3. **Sort some feelings (10 min).** With a partner, name a feeling and decide its size:
   "I felt *mad* — a **little** mad, not a lot." Take turns. Try the same feeling at
   two different sizes and notice how the *word* stays the same while the *strength*
   changes.
4. **Place your own feeling (15 min).** Think of one real feeling you had recently.
   Draw or write it on your blank thermometer, and shade the zone that matches its
   strength — a little, some, or a lot. Underneath, write or tell what that level felt
   like in your body.
5. **Close (5 min).** You are the one who can measure your own feelings. Noticing the
   size of a feeling — a little, some, or a lot — is the first step to taking care of
   it. It is a skill you will grow all year.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer feeling word cards and a three-box "small / medium / big" frame to
  place a card in instead of writing; scribe the feeling name the learner says.
- **Extension:** Place two feelings on the same thermometer and compare their sizes
  ("my worry is *some*, but my excitement is *a lot*").

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name a feeling and place its strength on
  the thermometer as a little, some, or a lot?
- **Self-check:** The learner reads their thermometer back and checks whether the zone
  truly matches how the feeling felt.

## Home connection

Ask someone at home about a feeling they had today and how strong it was — a little,
some, or a lot — and share one of yours.
