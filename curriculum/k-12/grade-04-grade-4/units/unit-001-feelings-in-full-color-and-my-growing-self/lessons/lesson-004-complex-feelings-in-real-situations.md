# Lesson 4 — Complex Feelings in Real Situations

## Summary

Learners practice connecting the complex feelings of **jealousy**, **guilt**, and
**pride** to real situations, then learn the healthy next step each signal invites:
jealousy can be noticed and named, guilt can lead to making things right, and pride can
be shared and celebrated. The lesson turns a feeling into a source of information and
agency.

## Objectives

- Recognize complex feelings such as jealousy, guilt, and pride and connect them to real
  situations. _(D01.S1.04.02)_

## Connection

A feeling only makes sense inside a *situation*. Your friend wins the class prize and
you feel a hot, twisty pang — that situation makes **jealousy**. You promised to return a
borrowed book and forgot, and now your stomach feels heavy — that situation makes
**guilt**. You learned to ride a bike all by yourself after many falls — that situation
makes **pride**. The situation is the *where*; the feeling is the *what*. And every one
of these feelings carries a next step you can choose. Knowing which feeling you are in
lets you choose what to do about it — in a classroom, a market, a rice field, or a
harbor town alike.

## Materials

- Situation-to-feeling matching cards
- My complex-feeling story page
- Pencils and crayons

## Preparation

- Prepare situation cards and matching feeling cards (one set per pair).
- Copy a "my complex-feeling story" page for each learner.
- Arrange space for pair work and quiet individual writing.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The heart of it is the *signal → next
step* chain: jealousy → notice and name what I want; guilt → make it right; pride →
share and celebrate. That chain is what turns a feeling from something that happens *to*
a learner into something they can *use*. Keep the next steps gentle and real — "make it
right" might mean an apology, returning a thing, or simply deciding to do better next
time; it is not a punishment. Some learners may share situations that are heavy; stay
calm, name the feeling with them, and honor their right to pass. See
`docs/facilitation.md` on holding difficult topics.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Last time you met three complex feelings: jealousy, guilt, and
   pride. Today you practice spotting them inside *real situations* — and you learn what
   to do with the signal each one gives you.
2. **Match situations to feelings (10 min).** With a partner, look at the matching
   cards. Each situation goes with one feeling. A friend wins a prize → **jealousy**. You
   broke a promise → **guilt**. You learned a new skill → **pride**. Match them all, and
   check each other's work: what is the *clue* in the situation that tells you which
   feeling it is?
3. **Learn the next step (10 min).** Each feeling carries a **signal** and a **next
   step**. Jealousy signals *what I want* — the next step is to notice and name it, not
   to act unkindly. Guilt signals *what I did* — the next step is to make it right,
   however small. Pride signals *what I worked for* — the next step is to share it and
   celebrate, without putting anyone down.
4. **Write your own story (15 min).** On your page, fill the frame with one real
   situation: **situation → feeling → what it points to → what I can do**. Choose the
   feeling that fits, and a kind, honest next step.
5. **Close (5 min).** A feeling is information, not a command. You get to read the
   signal and choose your next step. That is what it means to be the author of your own
   actions.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer a word bank of next steps (notice, ask, apologize, return, fix,
  share, celebrate) and scribe the learner's story.
- **Extension:** Write a second story where a feeling showed up in a *group* and describe
  how the next step affected others.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner match each complex feeling to a real
  situation and name a healthy next step for at least one?
- **Self-check:** The learner checks that their next step is kind and honest, not harmful
  to themselves or others.

## Home connection

Think of one situation at home this week and name the complex feeling that went with it
— then tell someone what your next step was.
