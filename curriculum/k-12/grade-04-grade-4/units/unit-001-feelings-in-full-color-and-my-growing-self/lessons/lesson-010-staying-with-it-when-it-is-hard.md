# Lesson 10 — Staying With It When It Is Hard or Tempting to Quit

## Summary

Learners practice **self-control** as a skill: staying with a task or rule when it is
hard or tempting to quit. They meet three concrete strategies — **breathe**, **break it
small**, and make an **if-then plan** — and apply them to a real task of their own. As
the unit capstone, they also look back across the unit and name what they now understand
about their feelings, their growing self, and their goals.

## Objectives

- Practice self-control by staying with a task or rule even when it is hard or tempting
  to quit. _(D01.S3.04.02)_

## Connection

Every hard thing has a moment in the middle — the moment you want to stop. The page of
math you don't want to finish, the promise to help before you play, the rule about
waiting your turn, the screen calling you away from your homework. That moment is
**temptation**, and everyone meets it. **Self-control** is not about never wanting to
quit; it is about knowing what to do *when* you want to quit. You can breathe, break the
job into one small step, and make an **if-then plan**: "If I feel like quitting, then I
will do just one more small step." A child waiting out a long storm, a child finishing a
weaving, a child keeping a promise — all of them use the same quiet skill.

## Materials

- Climb-the-hill chart
- My stay-with-it plan page
- Pencils and crayons

## Preparation

- Print or draw the climb-the-hill chart.
- Copy a "my stay-with-it plan" page for each learner.
- Arrange space for practice and a closing circle.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"), and it closes the unit — so the closing
circle is also a **retrieval** and **capstone** moment: learners recall the feeling
thermometer, the complex feelings, the self-map, and the plan-adjust loop. Teach the
three strategies explicitly (worked example) — self-control is a skill, and explicit
strategy instruction beats vague "try harder" (see `docs/contexts.md` on when to
instruct). Note honestly that willpower is not infinite and the evidence for "building"
it like a muscle is mixed; the practical, well-supported levers are *strategies* and
*changing the situation* (removing the distraction, planning ahead) more than sheer
effort. Never shame a learner who struggled — "not yet" applies here too.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** This is the last lesson of our unit. You have learned to measure
   your feelings, meet complex feelings, map your growing self, and plan-and-adjust a
   goal. Today you learn the skill that ties it all together: **staying with it** when it
   is hard or tempting to quit.
2. **Meet the hard moment (10 min).** Look at the chart. A figure is partway up a hill.
   The flag — the goal — is at the top. But there is a **quit** sign on the side. That is
   the hard moment, and everyone reaches it. **Self-control** is knowing what to do when
   you reach it.
3. **Learn three strategies (10 min).** One: **breathe** — pause and take a slow breath
   before you act. Two: **break it small** — do not face the whole hill; face just the
   next step. Three: **if-then plan** — "If I feel like quitting, then I will do one more
   small step." These are tools, and you can practice them.
4. **Make your own plan (15 min).** On your page, write one real task or rule you want to
   stay with this week. Name the **temptation** to quit. Then write your three strategies:
   how you will breathe, how you will break it small, and your exact if-then plan. If you
   can, try one small step right now.
5. **Close — the capstone (5 min).** Look back: how strong can a feeling be? What do
   jealousy, guilt, and pride tell you? What makes you, you? What is your next step? You
   are the one who measures your feelings, knows your growing self, and chooses to stay
   with what matters. That is a big, true thing — and it is yours.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer a fill-in-the-blank if-then sentence and a picture of the three
  strategies; scribe the learner's plan.
- **Extension:** Practice a longer task with a partner using a timer, then reflect on
  which strategy helped most and why.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name a task or rule, the temptation to
  quit, and at least two strategies to stay with it?
- **Self-check:** The learner tries one strategy on a real task and notes whether it
  helped.
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The stay-with-it plan page, kept with the self-map and the
  goal-steps page.

## Home connection

Choose one task or rule at home to stay with this week, and tell someone your if-then
plan so they can cheer you on.
