# Lesson 6 — The Map of My Growing Self

## Summary

Learners draw a **self-map** that gathers their interests, abilities, and experiences
into one picture of who they are — and who they are becoming. A special **"not yet"**
branch reframes things they cannot do yet as growth in progress, turning the map into a
portfolio artifact that grows with them across the year.

## Objectives

- Explain how their interests, abilities, and experiences shape their sense of self.
  _(D01.S2.04.01)_

## Connection

A map shows you where you are *and* where you are headed. You can draw a map of yourself
the same way. In the center is **you**. Around you are the roads you have traveled and
the roads still ahead: what you **love**, what you are **good at**, what you have
**done**, what you are **still learning**, and your **next step**. A child in a desert
town maps the way to the well she can now find alone; a child in a port city maps the
knot he cannot tie *yet*. Every person's map is different, and every person's map is
unfinished — because you are still growing.

## Materials

- Self-map example chart
- My self-map page
- Pencils and crayons

## Preparation

- Print or draw the self-map example chart.
- Copy a blank self-map page for each learner.
- Arrange space for quiet drawing and a gallery walk.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The **"not yet"** branch is the heart of
it: research on mindset (Dweck) and on mastery learning treats "not yet" as information,
never a verdict — and that is the message to carry. Model your own self-map, including a
real "not yet" of your own, so learners see that adults are still growing too. Keep the
map honest, not performative; every branch is a real truth about the learner. This page
is a portfolio artifact — have learners keep it and return to it later in the year.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Last time you built a flower with three petals: interests,
   abilities, and experiences. Today you draw the whole **map** of your growing self.
2. **Meet the self-map (10 min).** Look at the chart. The center is **me**. The branches
   are **I love**, **I am good at**, **I have done**, **I am still learning**, and **my
   next step**. The "I am still learning" branch is special: we call it **not yet**. You
   are not "bad at" something — you are *not yet* able, and that is how every expert
   began.
3. **Hear a worked example (10 min).** "I love to sing. I am good at listening to
   friends. I have moved to a new town. I am still learning to swim — *not yet*. My next
   step is one swimming lesson." See how the map shows a whole person, including the part
   still growing?
4. **Draw your own map (15 min).** Fill your page with your own truth for each branch.
   Be honest, and remember: "not yet" is a strong thing to write, not a weak one.
5. **Close (5 min).** You are a whole person who is still becoming. Keep this map —
   later in the year you will look back and see how far you have grown.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer branch starter cards and scribe the learner's answers; let a learner
  draw pictures for each branch instead of words.
- **Extension:** Add a "how I got here" branch (who helped you) and a "where I want to
  go" branch with a first step.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner complete each branch of the map with
  something true, including a "not yet" that is stated as growth, not failure?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The self-map page, kept as a "my growing self" artifact.

## Home connection

Show your self-map to someone at home and tell them one thing on your "not yet" branch —
and ask them what they are still learning too.
