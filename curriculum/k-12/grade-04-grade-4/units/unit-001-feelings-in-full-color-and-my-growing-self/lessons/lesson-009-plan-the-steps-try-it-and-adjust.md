# Lesson 9 — Plan the Steps, Try It, and Adjust

## Summary

Learners move beyond the single first step of Grade 3 to plan **several ordered steps**
toward a goal, then learn the **adjust loop**: try the plan, check whether a step works,
and change that step when it does not. This is the core skill of reaching goals — a plan
is a living thing you improve as you go, not a fixed script.

## Objectives

- Plan the steps to reach a goal, try the plan, and adjust when a step does not work.
  _(D01.S3.04.01)_

## Connection

Think of something you want to reach — learn a song, grow a plant, save for a tool, run
faster, be kind more often. That is your **goal**. You do not jump to it; you take
**steps**, one after another. But here is the secret every builder, cook, and farmer
knows: the first plan is never perfect. A recipe needs more salt, a seed needs more
water, a wall needs a stronger brace. So you **try**, you **check**, and you **adjust** —
then try again. A fisher sets a net, finds a hole, mends it, and sets it again. Reaching
a goal is a loop, not a straight line.

## Materials

- Plan–try–adjust loop chart
- My goal steps page
- Pencils and crayons

## Preparation

- Print or draw the plan–try–adjust loop chart.
- Copy a "my goal steps" page for each learner.
- Arrange space for pair coaching and quiet writing.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The skill to coach is **adjustment** —
treating a failed step as information, not failure. This mirrors evidence that learning
is more effective when learners get feedback and act on it (mastery learning; Bloom
1984). Model a real adjustment from your own life first (worked example), and keep the
goal the *learner's own*. Watch for learners who fear a step "failing"; reframe loudly:
"the plan changed — that means the plan is working, because now you know more." This is a
portfolio artifact — have learners keep their goal-steps page for a follow-up check-in.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** In Grade 3 you set a goal and named its first step. Today you
   grow that into a full **plan** with several steps — and you learn the loop that makes
   any plan succeed: **plan, try, check, adjust**.
2. **Meet the loop (10 min).** Look at the chart. You **plan** your steps. You **try**
   the first step. You **check**: did it work? If not, you **adjust** that step — change
   it — and **try again**. The arrow loops back. That loop is the whole skill.
3. **Hear a worked example (10 min).** "My goal is to grow a plant. My steps: (1) find
   seeds, (2) plant them, (3) water them every day. I tried step 2, and the seeds did
   not sprout. I checked: the soil was too dry. I **adjusted**: I moved the pot to a
   shadier spot and watered more. Then they grew." A step did not work — so I changed it.
   That is not failure; that is the loop.
4. **Write your own plan (15 min).** On your page, write your goal and **three ordered
   steps**. Try the first step in your mind — or really, if it is small enough. Then
   write what you would **adjust** if that step did not work. Check with a partner: are
   your steps small and in a sensible order?
5. **Close (5 min).** A plan is a living thing. When a step does not work, you do not
   quit — you adjust. You are the one who makes the plan, and you are the one who can
   change it.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer goal idea cards and a three-step frame with pictures; scribe the
  learner's steps and adjustment.
- **Extension:** Add a way to measure success for each step, and a backup step to use if
  the first fails.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner write an ordered multi-step plan, try a
  step, and name an adjustment when a step does not work?
- **Self-check:** The learner checks that their steps are small, ordered, and adjustable.
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The goal-steps page, kept for a follow-up check-in.

## Home connection

Try your first step today, and tell someone at home what you would adjust if it does not
work.
