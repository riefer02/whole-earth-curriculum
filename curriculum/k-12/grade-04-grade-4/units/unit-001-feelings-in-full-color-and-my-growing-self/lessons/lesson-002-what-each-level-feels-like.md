# Lesson 2 — What Each Level Feels Like in My Body

## Summary

Learners connect each strength level of a feeling to what it feels like **in the body**.
A **little** might feel like a small flutter; **some** might feel warm and a bit faster;
**a lot** might feel hot, tight, or shaky. By scanning their own bodies, they learn that
body signals are honest clues to how strong a feeling is — an early step toward
regulating it.

## Objectives

- Describe the intensity of a feeling as a little, some, or a lot, and what each level
  feels like. _(D01.S1.04.01)_

## Connection

Your body is a quiet messenger. When you feel a **little** excited, your stomach may do
a tiny flutter. When you feel **some**, your cheeks get warm and your heart beats a bit
faster. When you feel **a lot**, your shoulders rise, your hands shake, and your breath
gets short. You can notice all of this without anyone telling you — because your body is
already telling *you*. A child by the sea feels a **little** flutter when a big wave
approaches, and a **lot** of pounding heart when the wave is taller than they are.
Bodies everywhere speak the same quiet language.

## Materials

- Body signal chart
- My body signal page
- Pencils and crayons

## Preparation

- Print or draw the body signal chart.
- Copy a "my body signal" page for each learner.
- Arrange a quiet space where learners can close their eyes and scan their bodies.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Body awareness (called *interoception* —
sensing signals from inside the body) is a real skill and grows with practice. Keep the
body scan slow and optional — some learners may not feel comfortable closing their eyes;
they may keep eyes open and that is fine. Model the three levels on yourself first
(worked example), then let learners find their own signals. A learner's body may not
match the "typical" list — every body is different, and that is the point: their body is
their own honest messenger. See `docs/facilitation.md` on holding big feelings safely.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Last time you learned that feelings have a strength — a little,
   some, or a lot. Today you learn *where* that strength shows up: in your own body.
2. **Meet the body signal chart (10 min).** Look at the three figures. **A little** feels
   like a small flutter — maybe in your stomach. **Some** feels warm, with a faster
   heartbeat in your chest. **A lot** feels hot, with tight shoulders and shaking hands.
   These are **body signals**: your body's way of telling you how strong a feeling is.
3. **Practice a worked example (10 min).** Together, pick the feeling *nervous*. Close
   your eyes or look down softly. Scan from your head to your toes: where do you notice
   something? A flutter? Warmth? Tightness? Notice how the signal gets bigger as the
   feeling gets stronger.
4. **Scan your own body (15 min).** Choose one feeling you felt recently. On your page,
   write or draw what **a little** of it feels like, what **some** feels like, and what
   **a lot** feels like — in your own body, with your own words.
5. **Close (5 min).** Your body tells you the truth about how strong your feelings are.
   The more you listen, the better you get at it. Next time a big feeling comes, you can
   pause and ask: *where do I feel this, and how strong is it?*

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer a menu of body-signal words (flutter, warm, tight, fast, heavy,
  hot) to choose from, and scribe the learner's own answers.
- **Extension:** Notice a second body signal at the same strength and describe how the
  two signals work together ("my heart beats fast *and* my hands shake").

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name one body signal for each strength
  level of a feeling (a little, some, a lot)?
- **Self-check:** The learner checks that their body signals grow with the strength of
  the feeling.

## Home connection

Ask someone at home where a feeling shows up in their body, and teach them the
little/some/a lot body check you practiced today.
