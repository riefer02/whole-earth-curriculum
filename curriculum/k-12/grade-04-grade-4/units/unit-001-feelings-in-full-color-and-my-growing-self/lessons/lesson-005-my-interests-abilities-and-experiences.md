# Lesson 5 — My Interests, Abilities, and Experiences Make Me, Me

## Summary

Learners build a **"three-petal me"** — **interests**, **abilities**, and
**experiences** — and see how each part shapes their **sense of self**. They name what
they love, what they can do, and what they have lived through, and begin to notice that
these parts combine into a person who is still growing and unique.

## Objectives

- Explain how their interests, abilities, and experiences shape their sense of self.
  _(D01.S2.04.01)_

## Connection

Who are you? Part of the answer is what you **love** — your **interests**, like drawing,
running, or caring for animals. Part is what you **can do** — your **abilities**, like
reading aloud, making people laugh, or fixing things. And part is what you have **lived
through** — your **experiences**, like moving to a new place, losing a tooth, or helping
a neighbor. Together these three petals make the flower that is *you*. A child who herds
goats on a mountainside, a child who swims in a warm sea, and a child who sells fruit in
a market are each shaped by what they love, what they can do, and what they have lived —
different petals, same three-part flower.

## Materials

- Three-petal "me" flower chart
- My three-petal flower page
- Pencils and crayons

## Preparation

- Print or draw the three-petal flower chart.
- Copy a blank flower page for each learner.
- Arrange space for sharing in pairs.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The goal is a *positive, true* sense of
self — not a boast and not a competition. Emphasize that every learner has all three
petals, and no petal is "better" than another: an ability to be a good friend is as real
as an ability to run fast. Model your own three petals first (worked example). Some
learners may have experiences that are heavy or private; let them choose what to share
and offer the option to keep a petal private. See `docs/facilitation.md` on holding
difficult topics safely.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** You have been reading your feelings. Now you turn to a bigger
   question: *what makes me, me?* Today you build a flower with three petals.
2. **Meet the three-petal flower (10 min).** Look at the chart. The center is **me**.
   The three petals are **interests** (what I love to do), **abilities** (what I can do
   well or am learning), and **experiences** (things I have lived through). Each petal
   shapes who you are. Together they make a person no one else is.
3. **Hear a worked example (10 min).** "My **interest** is caring for animals. My
   **ability** is reading aloud. My **experience** is helping my grandmother at the
   market every summer." See how the three petals combine into one whole self? Now think
   of your own.
4. **Build your flower (15 min).** On your page, fill each petal with your own truth —
   draw or write one interest, one ability, and one experience. If a petal feels hard,
   ask a partner: "What do you see me love? What do you see me do well?"
5. **Close (5 min).** You are not just one thing. You are your interests, your abilities,
   and your experiences — all growing together. And you are not finished: there is room
   for new petals as you grow.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer idea cards for each petal and scribe the learner's answers; let a
  learner draw instead of write.
- **Extension:** Add a fourth petal — what you *value* — and explain how it shapes the
  rest of the flower.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner name one interest, one ability, and one
  experience and say how each shapes who they are?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The three-petal flower page, kept for the "my growing self"
  page later in the unit.

## Home connection

Ask someone at home what they think one of your interests, abilities, and experiences
is, and see if it matches what you wrote.
