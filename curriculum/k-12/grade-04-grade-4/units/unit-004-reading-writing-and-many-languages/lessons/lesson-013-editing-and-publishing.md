# Lesson 13 — Editing and Publishing

## Summary

Learners **edit** their revised draft for **spelling**, **capitalization**, and
**punctuation**, then make a clean final copy and **publish** it by sharing with a real
**audience**. This completes the writing process — plan, draft, revise, edit, publish —
carried through on their own.

## Objectives

- Use a simple writing process — plan, draft, revise, edit, and publish — on their own.
  _(D04.S3.04.02)_

## Connection

A piece of writing is finished when it is ready to meet its reader. Editing is the last
tidying — making sure the words are spelled right, the sentences begin and end properly —
so nothing distracts from the idea. Publishing is the moment of giving: your thinking
leaves your hands and reaches another person. Every written thing you have ever loved was
once edited and published, just like this.

## Materials

- The revised draft
- An editing checklist
- Clean paper for the final copy

## Preparation

- Have each learner's revised draft ready.
- Prepare the editing checklist and final copy paper.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Editing is a taught step: model it on a shared paragraph with a
worked example — checking spelling, capitals, and end marks one by one (Kirschner,
Sweller & Clark, 2006). Remind learners of the difference: revision (last lesson) fixed
the *ideas*; editing fixes the *mechanics*. A spell checker or dictionary is a helper,
not a replacement for the learner's own careful eye — and a tool cannot decide for the
writer what to say. Honor editing in the learner's home language and writing system, and
honor editing by dictation, braille, or assistive technology. Publishing is the
celebration: give the piece a real audience — a partner, a group, a class collection, a
home reader — and honor every finished piece as a real accomplishment, never ranked
against others. This piece is the unit's portfolio centerpiece: plan, draft, revision,
and final copy together show the whole journey.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Your ideas are now clear. Today you will tidy the words so nothing
   distracts your reader, then share your work.
2. **Meet editing (5 min).** Learn what editing checks: **spelling**, **capitalization**,
   and **punctuation** (end marks, spaces). It is the mechanics — the last tidy before
   publishing.
3. **Watch a worked example (10 min).** The facilitator edits a shared paragraph, thinking
   aloud: "Does this word look right? Does this sentence start with a capital? Does it end
   with the right mark?" Watch each check and fix.
4. **Edit your piece (15 min).** Use the checklist on your draft. Fix spelling, capitals,
   and end marks, one kind at a time.
5. **Make the final copy and publish (10 min).** Write or type your final copy neatly.
   Then publish it: read it aloud (or sign it) to a partner or group, or add it to the
   class collection. Your work now has a reader.
6. **Close (5 min).** You carried one idea all the way from a plan to a published piece.
   That is what writers do — and now you are one.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Edit one check at a time (spelling first, then capitals, then end marks),
  with a partner reading along.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Edit by dictation, sign, or assistive technology, and
  publish by sharing through the learner's preferred mode.
- **Support (blind/low vision):** Edit from a draft in braille or large print, or by
  dictation, and publish by reading aloud or sharing a braille/large-print copy.
- **Extension:** Edit for sentence variety too — combine two short sentences or split one
  long one — and explain how the change helps the reader.

## Assessment

- **Summative (portfolio/performance):** Can the learner produce a final copy that
  completes the writing process — plan, draft, revise, edit, publish — with editing for
  spelling, capitalization, and punctuation, shared with a real audience?

## Home connection

At home, publish your piece to someone you love — read it aloud or give them a copy —
and tell them about the journey it took from plan to published work.
