# Lesson 6 — Prefixes and Suffixes

## Summary

Learners learn how **prefixes** (parts added to the *front* of a base word) and
**suffixes** (parts added to the *end*) change a word's meaning. They build words like
*un + help + ful = unhelpful* and take unfamiliar words apart to figure them out.

## Objectives

- Use context clues and word parts, such as roots, prefixes, and suffixes, to figure
  out unfamiliar words. _(D04.S2.04.01)_

## Connection

Think of a base word as a plain block, and of prefixes and suffixes as add-ons that
snap onto it and change what it does — like adding a roof or a porch to a house. "Help"
becomes "helpful" (full of help) or "unhelpful" (not helpful). Once you know a few
affixes, you can take apart a long word and read it piece by piece, like a builder
reading a plan.

## Materials

- A word-building strip (base word + spaces for prefix and suffix)
- Prefix and suffix cards (un-, re-, dis-, pre-, -ful, -less, -er, -ly)

## Preparation

- Prepare the word-building strip and affix cards.
- Choose a base word to model (for example, "help").

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you") so it works in a classroom and for a
self-directed learner. Affixes are a taught skill: model building and taking apart words
with a worked example, naming how the meaning changes each time (Kirschner, Sweller &
Clark, 2006). Teach the pattern, not a memorized list — a **prefix** goes before and a
**suffix** after, and each carries a meaning (un- = not, re- = again, -ful = full of,
-less = without). This is script- and language-agnostic: languages affix meaning in
different ways, and learners may share examples from their own languages. The goal is
meaning-making — a learner who can explain "unhelpful = not + full of help" has the
skill. Honor explanations in the learner's own language, and treat a digital tool as a
helper to check, not a substitute for thinking. Keep it playful: word-building is a
game, and every new word is a small discovery.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (5 min).** Think of a long word you know. Break it into parts in your mind.
   What does each part add?
2. **Meet prefixes and suffixes (5 min).** Learn the two kinds of add-ons: a **prefix**
   attaches before the base word, a **suffix** after. Each one changes the meaning in a
   small, predictable way.
3. **Watch a worked example (10 min).** The facilitator builds a word on the strip:
   **help** → **helpful** (full of help) → **unhelpful** (not helpful). Then they take
   **unhelpful** apart, piece by piece, naming what each part means. Watch the meaning
   change with each snap.
4. **Build and take apart words (15 min).** Use the cards to build new words from a base
   word. Write each one and explain what the prefix or suffix added. Then take an
   unfamiliar word apart and figure out its meaning from its parts.
5. **Share (5 min).** Read a partner one word you built, and have them tell you what it
   means from its parts.
6. **Close (5 min).** Affixes are snap-on meaning. Know a few, and long words stop being
   walls and become puzzles you can solve.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Build with one affix at a time on a single base word; the learner names
  how the meaning changed.
- **Support (non-speaking):** Point to the prefix or suffix and to a picture of the
  meaning, or sign the change.
- **Support (blind/low vision):** Hear the words built aloud, or read them in braille or
  large print, and explain each part's meaning orally.
- **Extension:** Take apart three unfamiliar words from your reading, name each affix and
  its meaning, and explain the whole word.

## Assessment

- **Formative (observation):** Can the learner take an unfamiliar word apart into prefix,
  base, and suffix, and use the parts to explain its meaning?

## Home connection

At home, find one long word and break it into its parts — prefix, base, suffix — and
tell someone what each part means.
