# Lesson 15 — Power, Inequality, and Technology

## Summary

Technology is never neutral: it reorders who can act, who profits, and who is left out.
Learners trace a phone's supply chain — the minerals inside it are sometimes mined in
conditions that harm and exploit workers, including children, far from the user (S-398)
— and weigh two faces of digital technology: the digital divide, where benefits spread
rapidly but unevenly (S-281), and mobile money, which grew where banks were scarce and
brought banking to people the old system had left out (S-210). The unit closes by
repeating its central question: *who gained, and who was left out?*

## Objectives

- Analyze the relationship between power, inequality, and technology in past and present
  struggles, and who gained and who was left out. _(D08.S4.11.02)_

## Connection

The device in your hand connects you to the world — and, through a long chain, to a
mine, a factory, and a worker far away. The same technology that includes you also
carries a chain of power behind it. Tracing that chain is how we see technology
honestly: both its gift and its cost.

## Materials

- Power-and-technology case sheet
- History journal

## Preparation

- Copy the case sheet; prepare the phone-supply-chain description (step 2).
- Retrieval: from earlier units, how technology's benefits and harms are distributed
  unevenly, and the life cycle of a designed object.

## Facilitator note

Hold technology in the unit's balance — neither worship nor fear. The **technology**
lens: tools reorder power; they do not erase it. The **egalitarian** lens: the benefits
of digital technology have spread fast but unevenly, and the digital divide is real
(S-281). The **ethics** lens: the minerals in our devices are sometimes mined in
conditions that harm and exploit workers, including children (S-398) — a harm borne far
from the user, which fairness requires us to see and act on. The **global** lens: mobile
money (M-Pesa) grew where bank branches were scarce and included people the old system
left out (S-210) — proof that technology can *also* widen access when designed for it.
The **critical-thinking** lens: the same tool can exclude and include at once; the
question is always *who* and *under what design*. Close the unit with agency: design,
governance, and fair access are choices, still open.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From the unit so far: name one movement's strategy and one
   climate response. Today we close with the tool that runs through all of them:
   technology.
2. **Trace a phone (12 min).** A phone's minerals — including cobalt — are sometimes
   mined in conditions that harm and exploit workers, including children (S-398). On the
   case sheet, trace the chain: mine → factory → port → hand. Mark *who gains* and *who
   is left out* at each stop.
3. **The digital divide (12 min).** Digital technologies have spread rapidly, but their
   benefits are unevenly distributed, and a digital divide in access remains (S-281).
   Fill the sheet: who gained access and who was left out, and what that difference
   means for power.
4. **Mobile money: technology that includes (12 min).** Where bank branches were scarce,
   mobile money such as M-Pesa let people send, save, and pay by phone — including people
   the old system had left out (S-210). Fill the sheet: who gained, and how design made
   the difference.
5. **Reconcile the two faces (10 min).** With a partner: how can the same technology
   both exclude and include? What decides which way it tips?
6. **Journal (4 min).** Answer the unit's question one last time: *"Technology reorders
   power — who gained, and who was left out?"* — with one example for each side.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use a two-column "gained / left out" sheet for a single case before
  writing.
- **Extension:** Argue one change in design or governance that would make a technology
  you use more inclusive, naming who it would help.

## Assessment

- **Formative (peer + self):** Can the learner name who gained and who was left out in
  each case, and explain how design decides which way technology tips?
- **Portfolio artifact (unit):** The completed case sheet and journal answer, as the
  power-inequality-technology analysis closing the unit portfolio.

## Home connection

Trace one object at home back a single step — where it came from, who likely made it,
what it cost them. Write one sentence on who gained and who was left out in that one
step.
