# Lesson 12 — Responding with Care and Justice

## Summary

When climate change reshapes where people can live, the question turns from *what is
happening* to *what should be done*. Learners weigh three broad responses — adapting in
place, cutting emissions (mitigation), and planned relocation — by asking who each helps
and who each burdens. They hold a role circle to feel the same response from different
seats, then argue for the response most rooted in care and justice.

## Objectives

- Evaluate responses to climate displacement, weighing them against care and justice and
  naming who each helps and burdens. _(D08.S3.11.02)_

## Connection

When a storm or a drought threatens a neighborhood, someone must choose: shore up the
homes where they are, change how we all live so the harm slows, or help people move
with dignity. Each choice helps some and burdens others. Weighing those choices fairly —
not just efficiently — is what care and justice ask of us.

## Materials

- Response evaluation sheet
- History journal

## Preparation

- Prepare the three response cards and role cards for the role circle (step 3).
- Retrieval: from Lesson 11, the climate pressures and who is affected.

## Facilitator note

The skill to land: **weigh a response by who it helps and who it burdens, then judge it
by care and justice.** The **ethics** lens: a fair response cares for the people most
affected, not only the most convenient. The **egalitarian** lens: the same response
lands differently on a coastal family, a farmer, a planner, and a distant neighbor; good
weighing names those differences. The **environment** lens: mitigation — cutting
emissions — addresses the cause, not only the symptom. The **global** lens: responses
vary by region and by resources, and solidarity across distance is part of the picture.
The **community** lens: relocation done *with* a community, not *to* it, preserves
belonging. Keep the role circle cooperative and respectful; no single answer is
required.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Lesson 11: name one climate pressure and who it affects
   most. Today: what should be done?
2. **Meet three responses (10 min).** (a) **Adapt in place** — build higher, plant
   trees, store water, change crops. (b) **Mitigate** — cut the emissions driving the
   change, everywhere, especially where they are highest. (c) **Planned relocation** —
   help people move with dignity, resources, and community intact. Each helps some and
   burdens others.
3. **The role circle (15 min).** Take a role — a coastal family, an inland farmer, a
   town planner, a person in a high-emitting country, a person in a low-emitting one.
   Weigh one response from each seat aloud. What does each role gain, and what does each
   give up?
4. **Evaluate (15 min).** On the sheet, weigh two responses against care and justice:
   who does each help? who does each burden? which is most rooted in care and justice,
   and why?
5. **Journal (8 min).** Write a short argument: *"The most just response is… because…"*
   naming the people it cares for and the burdens it fairly shares.
6. **Close (2 min).** Preview: we now turn from the land to *power* — the movements that
   have fought for rights, labor, and liberation.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Weigh just one response with a "helps / burdens" two-column sheet before
  writing.
- **Extension:** Argue the reverse case — defend the response you did *not* choose — and
  name its strongest point.

## Assessment

- **Formative (peer + self):** Can the learner weigh a response by who it helps and
  burdens, and argue for one response on care-and-justice grounds?
- **Portfolio artifact (unit):** The evaluation sheet and written argument, completing
  the climate-migration evaluation.

## Home connection

Consider one local risk — flood, drought, heat — and one response your community could
take. Ask: who would it help, and who would carry the cost?
