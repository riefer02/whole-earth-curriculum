# Lesson 10 — Forests and Water Across Regions

## Summary

Two resources connect every settlement to its environment: forests and water. Forests
cover about 31 percent of the world's land and are home to more than half of its land
animal and plant species; clearing them removes habitat and changes how water moves
(S-220). Meanwhile, about one in four people still lack safely managed drinking water
(S-065), and scarcity falls hardest on the least-advantaged. Learners trace both cases
through the loop and ask the unit's recurring question: *who is affected?*

## Objectives

- Analyze the relationship between human settlement, resource use, and environmental
  change, through deforestation and water scarcity across regions. _(D08.S3.11.01)_

## Connection

Cut a forest and the rain that once soaked into roots and soil now runs off hard ground,
carrying soil with it; the stream below changes, and so does the village that drinks
from it. Forest and water are not two separate problems — they are one loop, and people
live inside it.

## Materials

- Forest-and-water case sheet
- History journal

## Preparation

- Copy the forest-and-water case sheet.
- Retrieval: from Lesson 9, the settlement-resource-environment loop.

## Facilitator note

Keep the two cases concrete and connected, not a catalog of doom. The **environment**
lens: deforestation changes habitat and hydrology (S-220), and water scarcity follows
where water is overused or unevenly shared. The **egalitarian** lens: both burdens fall
unevenly — about one in four people lack safely managed drinking water (S-065), and the
poorest and most marginalized carry the heaviest share. The **global** lens: these are
worldwide patterns with local faces. The **critical-thinking** lens: distinguish
evidence (the figures) from values (that fair access and care for the land matter), and
always attach the difficulty to agency — stewardship, restoration, and fair sharing are
real and ongoing.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Lesson 9: what are the three parts of the loop? Today we
   trace two of its sharpest forms.
2. **Case one: deforestation (15 min).** Forests cover about 31 percent of the world's
   land and house more than half of its land species (S-220). When they are cleared for
   fields, pasture, or timber, animals lose homes, soil washes away, and water no longer
   soaks in as it did. On the sheet, fill cause, effect, and *who is affected*.
3. **Case two: water scarcity (15 min).** Water is essential to life and health, yet
   about one in four people lack safely managed drinking water (S-065). Scarcity grows
   where demand outruns supply, where water is polluted, and where access is unequal.
   Fill cause, effect, and who is affected.
4. **Link the two (10 min).** With a partner: how does clearing land change water? Why
   do the burdens of both fall unevenly? Where does the loop turn back on the people?
5. **Journal (8 min).** Write: *"Forests and water are one loop because…"* — then name
   one act of stewardship or fair sharing that could ease the loop.
6. **Close (2 min).** Preview: next we ask how a *changing climate* is reshaping where
   and how people can live.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use a cause/effect matching activity with pre-written cards for each case.
- **Extension:** Research one region's deforestation or water-scarcity case and add a
  fourth column: "one response underway."

## Assessment

- **Formative (peer + self):** Can the learner trace cause, effect, and who is affected
  in each case, and explain how the two connect?
- **Portfolio artifact (unit):** The completed forest-and-water case sheet and journal
  entry, added to the settlement-resource-environment analysis.

## Home connection

Notice one tree, stream, or water source near home. Ask an adult how it has changed in
their lifetime, and write one cause and one effect of that change.
