# Lesson 11 — A Changing Climate Reshapes Where We Live

## Summary

Climate change is not only a future threat; it is already reshaping where and how people
can live. Sea-level rise pushes people inland from coasts, drought strains farms and
cities, and shifting seasons change what land can grow. Learners evaluate these
pressures as documented drivers of movement within and across borders (S-383, S-005),
set alongside conflict and economy (S-493), and build a case table that asks the unit's
recurring question — *who is affected?* — before turning, next lesson, to responses.

## Objectives

- Evaluate how climate change is reshaping where and how people can live, naming the
  pressures, the changes, and who is affected. _(D08.S3.11.02)_

## Connection

Think of the lowest, driest, or most exposed place near you — the homes by the water,
the fields that depend on rain. When the water rises or the rain fails, it is those
places, and the people who live in them, that feel it first. Climate change moves the
map of where a life can be lived.

## Materials

- Climate-migration case sheet
- History journal

## Preparation

- Copy the case sheet; prepare one-paragraph case summaries (coastal, drought, shifting
  seasons) or use the ones here.
- Retrieval: from Lessons 9–10, the settlement-resource-environment loop.

## Facilitator note

Hold this with honesty and care, and never despair. The **environment** lens: the
climate system is changing, and the IPCC documents it authoritatively (S-005). The
**egalitarian** lens: the people who contributed least to emissions are often those most
exposed to the consequences (S-006), and displacement falls hardest on the
least-advantaged. The **global** lens: climate is one driver of movement among several —
conflict and persecution remain the largest documented causes of forced displacement
(S-493), with climate a growing driver (S-383). The **ethics** lens: how we respond to
people on the move is a care-and-justice question. Attach every fact to agency —
adaptation, fair sharing, and solidarity are real and already underway.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Lesson 10: how do forest and water connect? Today: what
   happens when the *climate itself* changes the terms.
2. **Name the pressures (10 min).** Climate change reshapes life through **sea-level
   rise**, **drought**, and **shifting seasons**. These are documented drivers of
   movement, within and across borders (S-383), and the underlying science is summarized
   by the IPCC (S-005).
3. **Build the case table (15 min).** For each case, fill the sheet: (a) a coastal
   community facing rising water; (b) a farming region facing drought; (c) a region
   whose seasons no longer match its crops. For each: *the pressure*, *the change in
   where/how people live*, *who is affected*.
4. **Set climate among other drivers (10 min).** Most forced displacement today is
   driven by conflict, persecution, and violence, with climate a growing driver (S-493,
   S-383). Discuss: why does it matter to name the causes carefully rather than call
   every mover a "climate refugee"?
5. **Who is affected? (10 min).** The places that emitted least are often those most
   exposed (S-006). With a partner, trace how this makes the burden uneven.
6. **Journal (5 min).** Write: *"Climate change reshapes where people can live
   because…"* — and name one group who is affected most.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Use one case (coastal) and a labeled picture to fill the table before
  adding the others.
- **Extension:** Distinguish climate as one driver among several (S-383, S-493) and
  write why precise naming matters for justice.

## Assessment

- **Formative (peer + self):** Can the learner name three climate pressures, the change
  each brings, who is affected, and why precise naming of causes matters?
- **Portfolio artifact (unit):** The completed case table and journal entry, as the
  first half of the climate-migration evaluation.

## Home connection

Ask an adult how the weather or seasons where you live have changed in their lifetime,
and how that has affected gardens, water, or travel.
