# Unit 9 — Global History: Transformation, Knowledge & Justice

## Overview

This unit gives Grade 11 its full pass through the long, many-voiced human story,
deepening Grade 10's hardest and most careful questions about time, culture, and
atrocity. Learners analyze one long-term transformation, such as industrialization or
the recent acceleration of human impact, and weigh what was gained and lost for
different peoples and the environment, and evaluate how the choice of what counts as
significant change shapes historical accounts, using contrasting examples. They
evaluate the relationship between dominant and marginalized knowledge traditions and
argue for how marginalized voices can be centered, and analyze how peoples have
maintained and revitalized their languages, traditions, and knowledge in the face of
pressure to assimilate. They analyze the relationship between human settlement,
resource use, and environmental change, such as deforestation or water scarcity,
across regions, and evaluate how climate change is reshaping where and how people can
live, weighing responses rooted in care and justice. They compare social movements
for civil rights, labor, and liberation across different countries and analyze their
strategies and effects, and analyze the relationship between power, inequality, and
technology in past and present struggles, and who gained and who was left out.
History is told from many perspectives, never as a single nation's triumph, and the
voices of those too often left out are centered with care and accuracy.

## Essential questions

- How do I analyze a long-term transformation and weigh what was gained and lost for different peoples and the environment, and how does what counts as significant change shape history?
- How do dominant and marginalized knowledge traditions relate, and how have peoples maintained and revitalized their languages and knowledge under pressure to assimilate?
- How do settlement, resource use, and climate change reshape where people can live, and how do social movements and technology shift power and inequality?
