# Unit 10 — Ethics, Justice & Political Economy

## Overview

This unit deepens Grade 10's critique and action into structures, justice, and
solidarity, building on ethical reasoning across frameworks and collective-action
design. Learners analyze how social and economic structures shape which choices
people can make, and argue about responsibility, and evaluate competing accounts of
what a good life or a good society means, drawing on more than one cultural
tradition. They analyze the relationship between justice, power, and law, and argue
for how an unjust law or system should be changed, and evaluate competing theories of
justice — those centered on freedom, equality, or care — and apply them to a current
issue. They analyze a global collective-action problem, such as climate change or
labor rights, and the coordination needed to solve it, and evaluate solidarity —
acting with others across difference and distance — and argue for what it requires of
citizens. They analyze a real or historical conflict from multiple perspectives and
propose a just and peaceful path forward, and evaluate the ethics of nonviolence and
of peacekeeping, arguing about when and how they can succeed. Questions are held as
questions, never answered as dogma.

## Essential questions

- How do social and economic structures shape which choices people can make, and what counts as a good life or a good society across traditions?
- How do justice, power, and law relate, and how do competing theories of justice apply to a current issue?
- What does solidarity require of citizens, and how do I analyze a conflict from multiple perspectives and argue for nonviolence and a just peace?
