# Lesson 9 — Three Lenses on One Issue

## Summary

Learners bring the three theories of justice — **freedom, equality, and care** — to
bear on one current issue (access to education, digital access, housing, health care,
or clean water). They run each lens's question across the same evidence, notice what
each reveals and hides, and defend a reasoned verdict about which lens is most
convincing for that issue — conceding a counterargument along the way.

## Objectives

- Evaluate competing theories of justice and apply them to a current issue.
  _(D09.S2.11.02)_

## Connection

A theory of justice is not a museum piece; it is a lens you can pick up and look
through. Take a live question — who gets a decent education, a home, health care, a
phone connection, clean water — and each theory asks a different question of it.
*Freedom* asks, "whose choices are respected?" *Equality* asks, "how are benefits and
burdens shared?" *Care* asks, "whose needs and relationships are met?" The same facts
look different through each lens, and a good argument tries all three before it
decides.

## Materials

- Three-lens application sheet
- Justice journal
- Issue cards (low-tech: paper)

## Preparation

- Copy or draw the three-column application sheet; prepare the issue cards with short
  fact bases.
- Retrieval: from Lessons 7 and 8, the three lenses — freedom (capability), equality
  (fair shares), care (meeting needs). Today we *apply* them.
- Prepare the issue fact bases accurately (general data authorities): education access
  and digital divide (World Bank, S-281), health and clean water (WHO, S-003 / S-049),
  inequality and poverty (UNDP, S-283).

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **theories of justice
are applied, not just admired — freedom, equality, and care each ask a different
question of the same issue, and each reveals something the others hide (Rawls S-459;
Sen S-460; SEP Distributive Justice S-388).** The methodological point: **application
is a skill — run the lens, cite the fact, state the verdict, concede the
counterargument — and it is modeled before it is assigned (worked examples, S-011).**
Keep the issues factual and non-sensational; the goal is reasoning, not outrage.

The **ethics** lens: each verdict is a moral judgment, opened and defended, not
imposed. The **egalitarianism** lens: the equality lens is applied *to* the others —
ask of every lens who it leaves out. The **global** lens: the issues (education, water,
digital access, health) are global in reach; learners may choose any, and none is the
default. The **technology** lens: digital access is one issue on the table — who gets
a connection shapes who can learn, work, and be heard (S-281). The
**critical-thinking** lens: distinguishing the *facts* (evidence) from the *verdict*
(value), and conceding a counterargument honestly. Preview: Lesson 10 scales from
individual issues to problems only the whole world can solve.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** Name the three lenses and each one's question: freedom
   ("whose choices are respected?"), equality ("how are shares shared?"), care ("whose
   needs are met?").
2. **Choose an issue (5 min).** Pick one issue card — education, digital access,
   housing, health care, or clean water — and read its short fact base.
3. **Model one lens (8 min).** Work a model together (S-011): run *one* lens on your
   issue — name the fact, state what the lens reveals, and what it misses.
4. **Run all three (15 min).** Fill the three columns: for each lens, what question
   does it ask, what fact does it highlight, and what does it hide?
5. **Write the verdict (12 min).** In your justice journal, write which lens is most
   convincing for your issue, with one reason and one counterargument you concede.
6. **Close (4 min).** Say, in your own words, why a just answer to a hard issue needs
   more than one lens — and what your verdict still left unsettled.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Run two lenses (freedom and care) and write a one-sentence verdict.
- **Extension:** Write a full short essay applying all three lenses to a second issue
  of your choice, with two counterarguments conceded.

## Assessment

- **Formative (peer + self):** Can the learner run each lens on the same evidence,
  name what each reveals and hides, and defend a verdict with a conceded
  counterargument?
- **Portfolio artifact (unit):** The completed three-lens application sheet with the
  written verdict — the unit's theories-of-justice application.

## Home connection

Ask someone at home what they think a hard local issue is, and which of freedom,
equality, or care they would put first in solving it. Write one sentence on their
answer and one on yours.
