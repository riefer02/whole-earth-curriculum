# Lesson 10 — The Gallery: Curating Our Voices Together

## Summary

Learners gather the unit's made and curated works — the artwork, curated exhibition,
composed piece, listening program, performance, directed program, prototype, and
life-cycle evaluation — into one coherent **showcase**, present them to an audience, and
write a **reflection** on what they intended and what actually happened. The lesson is a
spaced-retrieval pass over the whole unit, and a hand-off of a growing artistic voice to
Grade 12.

## Objectives

- Present the curated exhibition, listening program, and performance program together as
  one coherent showcase, with written reasoning for each, and reflect on intended versus
  actual effect. _(D07.S1.11.02, D07.S2.11.02, D07.S3.11.02)_

## Connection

A single picture is a voice; a whole show is a conversation. When you hang your artwork
next to someone's listening corner and someone else's performance, the room itself starts
to speak — about the issues you each chose, and about what a group of people, working
together, can make the world *feel*. Today you stop making and start showing, and you
notice that the showing is itself the last work of art.

## Materials

- Portfolio checklist
- Display and staging space
- Art journal

## Preparation

- Set the room (or outdoor path) with zones for the visual wall, the listening corner,
  the performance space, and the prototype/life-cycle table.
- Retrieval: this lesson is a **spaced-retrieval** pass — each artifact is recalled and
  re-explained, which is itself evidence-backed for learning (S-039).
- Prepare the portfolio checklist and a short reflective-statement frame.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **a portfolio is not a
pile; it is a curated whole — the learner selects, arranges, and explains their works as
one statement, then reflects on intended versus actual effect.** This is the unit's
capstone and its spaced-retrieval payoff (S-039); keep it a celebration of mastery, never
a ranking (philosophy §8).

The **intellectual** lens: the reflection asks the learner to compare *intention* with
*outcome* — the same evidence-versus-judgment discipline the unit has practiced. The
**community** lens: the gallery is *for* an audience; learners practice giving and
receiving feedback that is honest and kind. The **global** lens: learners see, side by
side, that their classmates' issues and traditions are many — and that a room of
different voices is stronger than a single one. The **egalitarianism** lens: every work
is shown, every voice heard; no work is "better," only different in what it intended.

Preview: Grade 12 (D07.S1.12.01, D07.S2.12.01, D07.S3.12.01, D07.S4.12.01) asks for a
*sustained* body of work with a coherent vision — the seed of that is planted today.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (10 min).** Use the checklist to collect your artifacts: the artwork and its
   explanation, the curated exhibition and its wall text, the composed piece and its
   notes, the listening program and its program notes, the performance and its
   explanation, the directed program and its staging reasoning, the prototype and its
   impact statement, and the life-cycle evaluation. Tick each and write its **intended
   effect** in one line.
2. **Arrange (15 min).** Place your works in the shared space: the visual wall, the
   listening corner, the performance space, the prototype table. Decide a simple order
   for a visitor to meet them, and set up so an audience can see and hear each one.
3. **Present and visit (20 min).** Take turns presenting and visiting. When you present,
   say your issue, point to one work, and state its intended effect. When you visit, ask
   only: *what did it make you feel or think?* and *what did you notice first?*
4. **Reflect (10 min).** In your journal, write a short **artist statement** for the
   whole unit: (a) the issue you carried through, (b) the one work you are proudest of
   and why, and (c) one place where the actual effect differed from what you intended —
   and what that taught you.
5. **Hand off (5 min).** Read one sentence of your statement aloud to the group. This is
   your voice, carried forward.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Present one favorite work with a sentence stem ("I wanted my audience
  to…"), and write a two-sentence reflection.
- **Extension:** Write a full reflective statement that names *two* places intention and
  effect diverged, and one skill you want to build in Grade 12.

## Assessment

- **Summative (portfolio):** Does the learner present a complete set of artifacts with
  written reasoning for each, and a reflection that honestly compares intended and actual
  effect?
- **Portfolio artifact (unit):** The completed checklist and reflective artist statement,
  closing the unit portfolio and handing it toward Grade 12.

## Home connection

Show your portfolio (or tell its story) to one person at home or in your community. Ask
them which work moved them most, and write down their answer beside your own intention.
