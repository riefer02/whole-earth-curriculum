# Lesson 5 — Curating a Listening Program Across Cultures

## Summary

Learners **curate a listening program**: they select works from **several cultures**
around a theme, decide an **order** that honors each tradition and makes the whole say
something more than any single work, and write **program notes** — the reasoning behind
the selection. They meet other cultures' music as **living heritage** carried hand to
hand, never as specimens to collect.

## Objectives

- Curate a listening program of works from several cultures and write the reasoning
  behind the selection. _(D07.S2.11.02)_

## Connection

A festival stage, a radio hour, a wedding, a funeral — every one of them is a *program*:
someone chose which songs or pieces would come first, which would follow, which would
close. A thoughtful program does not just play music; it *travels* — from one feeling to
another, and sometimes from one people's way of making music to another's. Today you are
that someone: the person who decides what a listener hears, in what order, and why.

## Materials

- Listening-program planning sheet
- Access to a small set of recorded works (6–10)
- Art journal

## Preparation

- Gather the candidate works (recordings or live performances), each with attribution —
  the name of the tradition and, where known, the performers and community.
- Retrieval: from Lesson 3, the two moves of curation (selection and order); from Grade
  10 (D07.S2.10.02), respectful engagement versus appropriation.
- Model one worked example: a three-work program with written reasons.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The ideas to land: **a listening program is
a *curated sequence across cultures*; the written reasoning must honor each tradition
(attribution, context) while explaining the selection and order.** Teach the two moves
with a worked example and guided practice (S-011). Hold the hard rule from Grade 10:
respectful engagement, not appropriation — music is met as a living tradition, credited
and asked about, never taken (philosophy §4).

The **intellectual** lens: program notes are argument — each choice is defended with a
reason about the music itself (its scale, rhythm, timbre, or mood), not just a feeling.
The **global** lens: learners meet real, named traditions — the **raga** of Indian
classical music, a melodic framework with its own scale, characteristic notes, and
associated mood or time (S-366); the **maqam**, the modal system of Arab, Turkish, and
Persian music with characteristic intervals (S-367); Andean **siku** panpipes played in
interlocking pairs and West African **djembe** drumming with layered rhythms and
call-and-response (S-188). Each is a living way of making meaning in sound. The
**egalitarianism** lens: no tradition is "better" or more "advanced" than another; a
program that places them side by side treats each as a full voice. The **community**
lens: where possible, invite the people of a tradition — classmates, families, neighbors —
to play or speak, so the program is *with* a community, not *about* it.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Lesson 3, what were the two moves of curation? Today you use
   them on *sound*, and across *cultures*.
2. **Meet the works, respectfully (12 min).** Listen to (or name) 6–10 works from
   several cultures. For each, record: *the tradition it comes from, one thing you
   notice in the music itself* (its scale, rhythm, timbre, mood), and *a respectful
   question you would ask its people*. Attribution is not optional.
3. **Select (12 min).** Choose 3–5 works that serve one theme (for example: water,
   home, grief, resistance, joy). On your sheet, write one line on why each earns its
   place — naming something *audible* in the music, not just a label.
4. **Order (12 min).** Arrange the chosen works so the sequence honors each tradition
   and makes a point. What should a listener hear first? What should close the program?
   Try two orders before deciding.
5. **Write the program notes (10 min).** Give the program a title and a two-sentence
   theme, then one sentence on the reason for each placement, with full attribution.
6. **Share (4 min).** Tell a partner your program's title, its works in order, and the
   one-sentence reason for the first and last choices.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Build a three-work program with sentence frames ("this work comes second
  because…"), and listen for one audible quality in each work.
- **Extension:** Add a *bridge* between two works — a note on what they share or contrast —
  and write it into the program notes.

## Assessment

- **Formative (peer + self):** Can the learner name each work's tradition, state the
  theme, and justify each selection and placement with a reason about the music itself?
- **Portfolio artifact (unit):** The planning sheet plus the written program notes, saved
  for the Lesson 10 gallery.

## Home connection

Ask a family member or neighbor to sing or play one piece from their tradition, or to
name one they love. Write down the tradition it comes from, one thing you notice in the
music, and one respectful question you would ask.
