# Lesson 6 — Performing a Response: The Body Speaks

## Summary

Learners **create a short performance** — movement, voice, and space — that responds to
their chosen issue, then explain its **intended effect** on an audience. They learn that
**gesture, posture, spacing, and level** form a language without words, and that the
moving body can carry a message as deliberately as any image or song.

## Objectives

- Create a performance that responds to a social or environmental issue and explain its
  intended effect on an audience. _(D07.S3.11.01)_

## Connection

Think of a moment you understood something with no words at all: someone's shoulders
dropping when they heard bad news, a whole crowd going still and silent, two people
standing far apart on a stage so you could *feel* the distance between them. The body is
always speaking — in posture, in space, in stillness. A performer does not wait for that
speech to happen by accident; they choose it, the way a composer chooses a tempo.

## Materials

- Performance-planning sheet
- Open floor space
- Art journal

## Preparation

- Clear a safe open space.
- Retrieval: from Grade 10 (D07.S3.10.01), devising an original piece with a clear
  concept and craft; from Lesson 1, each learner's issue and intention.
- Prepare one demonstration: the same simple movement at three *levels* (high, middle,
  low) so the class can *see* how level and posture change meaning.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The ideas to land: **gesture, posture,
spacing, and level are the body's vocabulary; a devised performance arranges them as
deliberately as an image arranges line and color; the written explanation names the
intended effect.** Teach the move with a worked example and guided practice (S-011),
drawing on devised-performance practice (S-260) and movement pedagogy that presents
traditions with respect (S-189).

The **physical** lens: the body is the instrument — learners attend to how a movement
*feels* from inside as well as how it reads from outside. The **critical-thinking**
lens: learners separate *observation* ("she stands still, then falls to a low level")
from *judgment* ("the fall reads as grief"). The **global** lens: performance carries
memory and meaning across cultures — the West African **griot** carries genealogies and
histories through oral performance (S-446); the Māori **haka** is a living posture-and-
chant tradition expressing identity, welcome, and remembrance (S-447). Meet these as
living traditions to honor with respectful questions, never to imitate carelessly. The
**community** lens: performance is made *for* someone — learners name their audience and
perform for the room, not just the mirror.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Lesson 1, your issue and intention. Today the body carries
   the same intention.
2. **Meet the body's words (12 min).** **Gesture** (a hand, a reach, a fist), **posture**
   (open, closed, upright, collapsed), **spacing** (near, far, together, apart), and
   **level** (high, middle, low) all carry meaning. Worked example: a performer showing
   a community divided walks two figures slowly apart across the space, then one figure
   sinks to the ground while the other stays standing — *spacing* plus *level* make the
   distance felt.
3. **Choose and plan (8 min).** On your sheet, choose **one movement idea** and **one
   other element** (voice, a sound, a prop) to carry your message. Sketch where bodies
   begin, move, and end.
4. **Devise it (15 min).** In your group (or alone), build a short performance of 60–90
   seconds that answers your issue. Rehearse it twice, watching for timing, spacing, and
   stillness — silence and stillness are as loud as movement.
5. **Explain the intended effect (8 min).** In your journal, write two sentences: *what
   you chose the body to do, and what you intended the audience to feel or think.*
6. **Perform and check (7 min).** Perform for the class or a partner. Ask: *what did the
   body say, and what did it make you feel?* Compare their answer to your intention.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Build one clear gesture or posture for the issue, then say in one sentence
  what it should make an audience feel.
- **Extension:** Add a second element and refine the *ensemble* — how the performers move
  relative to one another — and write how that relationship changes the meaning.

## Assessment

- **Formative (peer + self):** Can the learner name the movement idea and second element
  they chose, point to where each appears, and explain how each produces the intended
  effect?
- **Portfolio artifact (unit):** The performance (performed or recorded/described) plus
  its written intended-effect explanation, saved for the Lesson 10 gallery.

## Home connection

Watch any moment of performance — a game, a dance, a greeting, a ritual — and name one
thing the body is saying without words. Write down the gesture, posture, spacing, or
level you saw, and what it meant.
