# Lesson 2 — Making an Image Speak: Craft & Intended Effect

## Summary

Learners complete their issue-driven **artwork** by choosing **elements of art** (line,
shape, form, color, value, texture, space) and **principles of design** (balance,
contrast, emphasis, and more) *deliberately*, so the image steers what a viewer notices
and feels. They then write the reasoning that connects each choice to the **intended
effect**, and revise once after feedback — making, as artists do, with their heads as
well as their hands.

## Objectives

- Create an artwork that addresses a social or environmental issue, using elements and
  principles of art deliberately, and explain its intended effect on an audience.
  _(D07.S1.11.01)_

## Connection

Two posters say "save water." One is a gray wall of text; the other is a single cracked,
dry leaf drawn huge against an empty blue sky, with no words at all. The first tells you;
the second *shows* you — and your eye goes to the leaf before you can help it. That is
not magic; it is **composition**: the artist chose size, color, and placement so that
your eye and your feeling would land exactly where they wanted. Today you learn to make
those choices on purpose instead of by accident.

## Materials

- Artwork plan sheet
- Art-making supplies
- Art journal

## Preparation

- Copy the artwork plan sheet.
- Retrieval: from Lesson 1, each learner's issue and intention sentence; from Grade 10
  (D07.S1.10.01), a personal style built from ideas and influences.
- Prepare one demonstration: the same simple subject drawn twice, with one element
  changed (size, color, or contrast), so the class can *feel* the difference.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The ideas to land: **elements of art are
the building blocks (line, shape, form, color, value, texture, space); principles of
design are how they are arranged (balance, contrast, emphasis, and so on); and an artist
uses both to direct attention and feeling (S-365).** The skill — *make a choice, then say
why it produces the intended effect* — is taught with a worked example and guided
practice (S-011); the issue and the feeling stay the learner's own.

The **intellectual** lens: every visual choice is a *reason* in image form; the written
explanation makes that reasoning explicit. The **critical-thinking** lens: learners
separate *observation* ("the leaf is large and centered") from *judgment* ("it makes me
feel small and worried") — the move Grade 8 practiced (S-365). The **global** lens: the
vocabulary of elements and principles is a shared tool across traditions, not one
culture's property; an Akan adinkra cloth, a Māori kōwhaiwhai rafter pattern, and a
European engraving all make deliberate choices about line, shape, and repetition
(S-114, S-115). The **egalitarianism** lens: any material — paint or leaves, screen or
sand — can carry a serious message; no tool is more "real art" than another.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** Re-read your intention sentence from Lesson 1. It names your
   **intended effect**. Everything you make today serves that sentence.
2. **Meet the tools (12 min).** **Elements** are the building blocks: line, shape, form,
   color, value, texture, space. **Principles** are how you arrange them: balance,
   contrast, emphasis, movement, pattern, proportion, unity (S-365). Worked example: an
   artist making an image about loneliness places one small dark figure far to one side
   of a large empty field — *contrast* of size and *placement* create *emphasis* and a
   feeling of isolation.
3. **Plan your image (8 min).** On the plan sheet, choose **one element** and **one
   principle** to carry your message, and sketch a quick layout. Write, in one line, what
   you expect that choice to make a viewer feel.
4. **Make it (20 min).** Create the artwork. As you work, keep asking: *where will the
   eye go first, and what will it feel there?*
5. **Explain the reasoning (8 min).** In your journal, write three sentences: (a) what
   issue the work addresses, (b) the element and principle you chose and where, and (c)
   the intended effect on the audience.
6. **Peer check and revise (7 min).** Show a partner. Ask only two questions: *what did
   you notice first?* and *what did it make you feel?* If their answer differs from your
   intention, change one thing to close the gap. Art is revised, not just made.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Focus on a single principle (contrast or emphasis) with a single element
  (color or size), and give sentence frames for the written reasoning.
- **Extension:** Use two principles together, and write how the *combination* shapes the
  effect — then explain where a viewer might read it differently than intended.

## Assessment

- **Formative (peer + self):** Can the learner name the element and principle they chose,
  point to where each appears in the work, and explain how each serves the intended
  effect?
- **Portfolio artifact (unit):** The completed artwork plus its three-sentence written
  explanation, saved for the Lesson 10 gallery.

## Home connection

Look around home or your neighborhood for one image that is *doing* something to you — a
sign, a wrapper, a photo. Name the element and principle it uses, and write one sentence
about what it is trying to make you do.
