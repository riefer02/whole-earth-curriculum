# Lesson 5 — My Mind-Body Practice Plan

## Summary

Learners turn three tools — breathing, movement, attention — into one plan they will
actually use. They choose practices that fit their body and their life, set a
realistic dose ("a little, often"), and write, in their own words, why each works.
This is the first major portfolio artifact and a retrieval of everything in the
strand so far.

## Objectives

- Use breathing, movement, and mindfulness deliberately to regulate your own state,
  and explain why each works. _(D03.S1.11.01)_

## Connection

A tool you never pick up does not help. The difference between knowing *about*
breathing and actually breathing when you need to is a plan — small, specific, and
attached to your real days. Whether your days run on a school bell, a farm's rhythm,
a market's hours, or a caregiver's schedule, a plan that fits your life is the one you
will keep.

## Materials

- Practice-plan grid
- Practice log

## Preparation

- Print or draw the practice-plan grid.

## Facilitator note

This is a **consolidation and synthesis** lesson: retrieval of lessons 1–4, then
production of a plan. Give structure (the grid and sentence frames), then let learners
author their own plan — mastery, not ranking. **A "little, often" is better than a big
"sometimes"**; guide learners toward realistic doses they can actually keep, not
heroic ones they will drop.

**Spacing note.** The plan is meant to be *used* across the rest of the unit, not
finished and forgotten. Build in a check-in: invite learners to revisit their plan in
lesson 8 or 10 and mark what held up.

**Accuracy of explanations.** The "why it works" notes must be accurate and hedged:
breath → parasympathetic/relaxation response (S-340); movement → mood via several
mechanisms (S-408); attention → noticing and returning, with modest, still-growing
evidence (S-001, S-222). Correct overclaiming kindly: "cures anxiety" becomes "may
help me settle when I am anxious."

The **ethics** lens: a plan you can keep is a promise to yourself you can honor. The
**egalitarian** lens: every plan is different because every body and every life is
different — none is better. The **global** lens: practices come from many cultures;
using them with respect and a reason honors their roots. The **technology** lens:
decide whether a device or app helps your plan or adds pressure — you choose.

## Procedure

1. **Retrieve (10 min).** Without your log, write down: (a) the three tools, (b) one
   mechanism for each, (c) one caveat/hedge for each. Then open your log and correct
   or complete what you wrote. This recall is how the learning sticks.
2. **Choose your practices (10 min).** For each tool, pick one concrete version that
   fits your body and your place. Examples: *breath* — 4-4-6-2 before sleep; *move* —
   two-minute stretch after sitting; *attention* — one minute on sounds while
   waiting. Adapt every version to your body (seated, wheeling, lying, standing).
3. **Set the dose (10 min).** Fill the week grid: which practice, on which days, for
   how long, at what moment of your day. Keep it small enough to keep. "Three days,
   two minutes" beats "every day, an hour."
4. **Write why it works (10 min).** Under each practice, write one accurate,
   hedged sentence in your own words. Use the frames if you need them.
5. **Peer-check (10 min).** Trade "why it works" sentences with a partner. Check for
   accuracy and honest hedging — is anything overclaimed? Revise if needed.
6. **Commit and close (5 min).** Write one sentence to your future self: *"This week
   I will…"* Set a reminder — a note, a mark on the wall, a phone alert if you use
   one. This plan is now a living artifact.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide the sentence frames ("___ works because ___; it may help me
  ___"); build a two-day plan.
- **Extension:** Ask learners to add a "what if I miss a day?" recovery note — a kind
  plan for restarting without self-criticism.

## Assessment

- **Summative (portfolio):** The practice plan — a chosen practice, realistic dose,
  and accurate, hedged "why it works" explanation for each tool.
- **Self:** A one-line self-check at the end of the week ("what held up, what would I
  adjust").

## Home connection

Try your plan for real. At the end of the week, mark what you kept and what you would
change. Missing a day is information, not failure — the plan is yours to adjust.
