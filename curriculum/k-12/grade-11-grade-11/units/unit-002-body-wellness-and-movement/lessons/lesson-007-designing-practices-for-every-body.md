# Lesson 7 — Designing Practices for Every Body

## Summary

Learners turn the social model into design: they take one mind-body practice and
produce three or more adaptations so it honors a wide range of bodies — standing,
seated, wheeling, lying, low-energy, and different sensory needs. The result is the
strand's second portfolio artifact and a direct answer to the essential question of
inclusion.

## Objectives

- Design mind-body practices that honor a wide range of bodies, abilities, and needs.
  _(D03.S1.11.02)_

## Connection

A breathing exercise that can only be done standing on two legs quietly leaves out a
person who cannot stand. A "mindful listening" task that relies on hearing leaves out
a Deaf person. But the same practice, offered three ways, welcomes everyone. Design
is not a luxury or an afterthought — it is where dignity is either built in or quietly
withheld.

## Materials

- One-practice-many-bodies chart
- Practice log
- Paper for a design sketch

## Preparation

- Prepare the chart and, if available, the universal-design principles.

## Facilitator note

This is a **design-and-production** lesson, and it is the summative for the strand.
Give learners the seven **Principles of Universal Design** as a concrete checklist —
Equitable Use, Flexibility in Use, Simple and Intuitive Use, Perceptible Information,
Tolerance for Error, Low Physical Effort, and Size and Space for Approach and Use
(S-369) — then let them design. **Model one adaptation first** (worked example): take
the 4-4-6-2 breath and show it standing, seated in a chair, and while wheeling, and
note that the "hold" step is optional for anyone who finds it uncomfortable.

**No able-bodied default.** The chart deliberately shows no single "normal" body; the
seated and wheeling versions are not "special cases" but equal ways of doing the same
practice. Encourage learners to design for *low energy* (a lying-down version), *pain
or fatigue* (shorter, gentler), and *sensory difference* (a visual or tactile anchor
instead of an auditory one).

**Dignity and agency.** Let learners choose which practice to adapt and how many
versions to produce. Never grade a design by "how hard" the practice is; grade it by
how well it widens access. Avoid language that ranks bodies ("even someone in a
wheelchair can do it" is patronizing; "this version works while wheeling" is neutral).

The **ethics** lens: designing for the widest range is a form of respect. The
**egalitarian** lens: who is included by default, and who has to ask for a "special"
version? The **global** lens: adapted and seated practices exist in movement
traditions worldwide — inclusivity is not new. The **technology** lens: consider
whether a device (app, audio, video, screen reader) widens access or depends on
resources not everyone has.

## Procedure

1. **Retrieve (5 min).** In one sentence each: what is the social model? What is a
   barrier? Check your log.
2. **Study the worked example (10 min).** Look at the chart: the same breathing
   practice, three ways. Notice what changes (posture, position, effort) and what
   stays the same (the slow exhale). List two other ways the same breath could be
   adapted — lying down? shorter? with a hand on the belly instead of counting?
3. **Choose and design (15 min).** Pick one practice — breath, movement, or
   attention. Design at least three versions that welcome different bodies and
   needs. For each, write: *who it fits, what changes, what stays the same.* Sketch
   it if drawing helps.
4. **Check against the principles (10 min).** Against the seven universal-design
   principles, mark which of your versions is most flexible and which could be more
   flexible. Revise one version to widen access further.
5. **Peer review (10 min).** Exchange designs. Ask: *Is there a body or need still
   left out? What is one way to widen it?* Revise if you agree.
6. **Close (5 min).** One sentence: *"A practice is truly mine when it can be
   everyone's."* What does that mean to you now?

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a fill-in adaptation card ("___ position, ___ length, ___
  anchor"); design two versions instead of three.
- **Extension:** Ask learners to design an adaptation for a *specific* environmental
  constraint (small space, loud room, no equipment, outdoors).

## Assessment

- **Summative (portfolio):** A practice adapted three or more ways, each with a
  "who it fits / what changes / what stays the same" note and a universal-design
  check.
- **Peer:** A partner's check that the design widens access without ranking bodies.

## Home connection

Teach your adapted practice to one other person this week — a family member, a
friend, a younger learner. Notice what they need to change to make it theirs. Teaching
an adaptation is how it becomes real.
