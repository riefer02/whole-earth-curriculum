# Lesson 9 — Movement Traditions II: Dance, Sport, and Community

## Summary

Learners extend their cross-cultural comparison to dance and sport, and discover the
deeper offer of movement traditions: belonging. They play a simple, adapted game to
*feel* inclusion and exclusion, then synthesize a four-way comparison — yoga, martial
arts, dance, sport — as the strand's portfolio artifact.

## Objectives

- Compare movement traditions across cultures, including dance and sport, and what
  each offers for health and community. _(D03.S2.11.01)_

## Connection

Think of a celebration you know — a wedding, a festival, a victory, a farewell.
Almost always, people move: they dance, they play, they compete, they cheer. Movement
is how communities mark joy and grief, welcome and goodbye. A dance is not just steps;
a game is not just points. Each is a way a people says: *we are here, together.*

## Materials

- Dance-sport-community chart
- Practice log

## Preparation

- Prepare the chart and a simple passing or circle game with clear, adaptable rules.

## Facilitator note

This lesson completes the strand's **comparison** and adds its emotional heart:
**belonging**. Teach the game as *data*, not just fun: run a circle or passing game
once with rules that quietly exclude some bodies (for example, "stand to pass"), then
run it again with rules that include everyone (seated and standing, larger/slower
ball, no "out"). Debrief honestly about who could and could not join, and why. This
makes the abstract idea of inclusion physical and memorable.

**Dance traditions.** Name a few respectfully as living heritage: tinikling (a
Philippine folk dance stepping between bamboo poles; S-116), Bharatanatyam (South
India), capoeira (an Afro-Brazilian art of movement, song, and resistance born among
enslaved Africans; S-325, and UNESCO-inscribed in 2014 per S-056), haka (Māori),
and countless regional dances. **Sport traditions.** Name traditional games as living
heritage — kabaddi and kho-kho (South Asia), sepak takraw (Southeast Asia), and
others (UNESCO TSG; S-287) — alongside globally played sports. In every case,
emphasize: these are *living traditions practiced by real communities*, not museum
pieces.

**Fair play.** UNESCO's International Charter holds that sport and physical activity
are a fundamental right and that opportunities must be inclusive of every person
regardless of ability (S-342). Tie this to the game: fair play means *everyone can
play*.

The **ethics** lens: a game that excludes some bodies is not fair, however exciting.
The **egalitarian** lens: who gets to dance, to play, to belong — and who is left on
the edge? The **global** lens: dance and sport are human universals expressed in a
thousand local forms. The **technology** lens: broadcast sport and viral dance videos
spread traditions globally — sometimes amplifying, sometimes flattening their meaning.

## Procedure

1. **Retrieve (5 min).** Without your log, name the three traditions from last lesson
   and one health offer each. Then check.
2. **Meet dance and sport (10 min).** Study the chart. What is different about dance
   and sport compared to yoga or martial arts? (Rhythm, performance, competition,
   spectators, celebration.) Add your own community's dance or sport if you wish.
3. **Play, then notice (15 min).** Play the circle/passing game twice: first with a
   rule that only lets standing players pass, then with rules that welcome seated and
   standing players alike. After each round, write: *who could join? who could not?
   how did it feel?*
4. **Complete the comparison (10 min).** Finish your four-row table — yoga, martial
   arts, dance, sport — with origin, health offer, and community offer. Use hedged,
   respectful language.
5. **Synthesize (5 min).** One sentence: *"Movement traditions matter for health
   because… and for community because…"*
6. **Close (5 min).** Reflect: what is one tradition from your own culture or family
   that deserves the same respect you gave the others today?

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a completed-row example and sentence frames; allow drawing the
  comparison.
- **Extension:** Ask learners to add a fifth row for a tradition they researched
  themselves, with a source.

## Assessment

- **Summative (portfolio):** The four-way comparison table (yoga, martial arts,
  dance, sport) with origin, health offer, and community offer, written with respect
  and appropriate hedging.
- **Self + peer:** A reflection on how the two versions of the game felt differently.

## Home connection

This week, watch or join a movement gathering in your community — a game, a dance, a
sport, a festival — and note what it offers beyond exercise. Write one sentence about
what you saw people *share*.
