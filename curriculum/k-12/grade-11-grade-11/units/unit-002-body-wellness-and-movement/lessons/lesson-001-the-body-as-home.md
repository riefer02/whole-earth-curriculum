# Lesson 1 — The Body as Home: Dignity, Joy, and Noticing Your State

## Summary

Learners open the unit by agreeing on how they will talk about bodies — with dignity
and joy, never shame — then meet the idea of "state" (how the body feels right now)
and take a first baseline body-scan. They begin a practice log they will carry all
unit, so that by the end they can see how deliberate practice changed them.

## Objectives

- Begin to use mind-body practices deliberately to regulate your own state, starting
  with the foundational skill of *noticing* your state without judging it.
  _(D03.S1.11.01)_

## Connection

Think of the last time you noticed your heart pounding before speaking, or your
shoulders heavy after a hard day, or a warm lightness when someone made you laugh.
Your body is always sending signals — a stomach that tightens, a breath that slows, a
jaw that clenches. This unit is about reading those signals on purpose and learning
what you can do with them. You live in your body every minute of every day; it is the
one home you never leave.

## Materials

- Practice log (journal or notebook)
- State spectrum chart (printed or drawn)
- A small marker (stone, seed, or finger)

## Preparation

- Draw or print the state spectrum (Settled–Alert–Activated).
- Have practice logs ready to hand out or set up.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). Your job is to build a container of
**dignity and joy** that will hold three weeks of body work. Land these commitments
now and return to them every day: (1) **no shame about any body** — no weight talk, no
ranking bodies, no able-bodied default; (2) **every body is a real body** — disability,
illness, and difference are ordinary, not "special cases"; (3) **everyone owns their
own story** — learners may pass, be silent, or write privately at any time; and (4)
**practices are invitations, not tests.**

**Inclusion is non-negotiable.** Model "noticing without judging" by naming your *own*
state ("my shoulders are tense today") rather than pointing at learners'. Never
require a learner to name a diagnosis, disclose illness, or demonstrate a movement
that is not safe for them. If a learner's sharing suggests distress or need, follow
your setting's care procedure and connect them with a trusted adult or service.

The **ethics** lens: treating every body with dignity is the *right* way to begin. The
**egalitarian** lens: these agreements protect everyone equally. The **global** lens:
many cultures have their own practices for noticing the body; we honor them all. The
**social-emotional** lens: naming a state is itself a regulation skill.

## Procedure

1. **Gather and agree (10 min).** Sit comfortably. Read the four commitments: no
   shame about any body; every body is a real body; everyone owns their own story;
   practices are invitations. Add one agreement of your own that would make this a
   safe place to notice your body.
2. **Meet "state" (5 min).** Your *state* is how your body feels right now — heart,
   breath, muscles, energy. It is never "good" or "bad"; it is information. Look at
   the state spectrum: Settled, Alert, Activated. Where are you right now? Put a
   marker (or your finger) there.
3. **Body scan — your baseline (10 min).** Close your eyes or lower your gaze. Slowly
   travel from your feet to your head. Where is there warmth, tightness, ease,
   restlessness? Notice three things without trying to change them. This is your
   *baseline* — the starting point you will return to all unit.
4. **Record it (5 min).** In your practice log, write today's date and three words for
   your baseline (for example: "tense shoulders, warm hands, steady breath"). If
   writing is hard, draw it or record your voice.
5. **Why notice? (10 min).** With a partner or alone, answer: *What could you do with
   this information?* How might noticing a tight jaw early help you later? Share one
   idea aloud or in writing.
6. **Close (5 min).** Say or write one word for how you want to feel in this unit.
   Remember: your body is your home; you are learning to read its signals with care.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Offer a short list of state words to choose from; allow drawing or
  pointing instead of writing.
- **Extension:** Ask learners to note a *second* body-scan later the same day and
  compare the two states.

## Assessment

- **Formative (self + observation):** Can the learner place themselves on the state
  spectrum and name at least two body signals? Did they record a baseline?
- **Artifact (portfolio):** The first baseline entry in the practice log, kept
  private or shared by choice.

## Home connection

This week, notice one body signal at home — before a meal, after waking, or in a
moment of stress — and jot it in your log. No equipment, no one else involved. If you
prefer, notice a signal in a plant or animal you care for instead; reading bodies is a
skill that generalizes.
