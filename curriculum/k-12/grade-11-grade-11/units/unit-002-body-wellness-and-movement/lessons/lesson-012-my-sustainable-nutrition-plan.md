# Lesson 12 — My Sustainable Nutrition Plan

## Summary

Learners build a sustainable personal nutrition plan — a flexible way of eating that
fits their needs, culture, budget, and values — and then retrieve the whole unit into
one integrated understanding: body as home, practices as tools, food as both personal
and political. This is the unit's final portfolio artifact.

## Objectives

- Build a sustainable personal nutrition plan that honors your own needs, culture,
  budget, and values. _(D03.S3.11.02)_

## Connection

There is no single "correct" plate on Earth. A rice-and-lentil meal, a maize-and-bean
stew, a fish-and-greens dinner, a flatbread with vegetables — each can nourish a
body, and each belongs to a people. A sustainable plan is not a diet to endure; it is
a way of eating you can actually keep, made of foods you recognize, can afford, and
believe in.

## Materials

- Plate model chart
- Practice log

## Preparation

- Prepare the plate model and the four reminders.
- Gather (if available) the dietary-guidelines handouts and a simple budget worksheet.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is the unit's **capstone**, and it must land two things at once: a
concrete, personal nutrition plan, and a retrieval of the whole unit. **No shaming,
ever** — about weight, about "unhealthy" foods, about any family's or culture's way
of eating. The plan is about *sustainability and fit*, not moral purity.

**A plan, not a diet.** Guide learners to build a *dietary pattern* (what they usually
eat over a week) rather than a list of allowed/forbidden foods. WHO's healthy-diet
guidance — diversity across food groups; moderate free sugars, salt, and saturated
fat — is a *population-level* starting point, not a personal law (S-038). Energy needs
differ by age, sex, body size, and activity (FAO/WHO/UNU; S-285). The plan must fit
*this learner*: their needs, their culture's foods, their real budget, and their
values (religious, ethical, environmental). Food-based dietary guidelines exist in
many countries and are themselves culturally adapted (FAO; S-118).

**Retrieval at the close.** The final step asks learners to recall the whole unit from
memory — this spaced retrieval is the science-of-learning payoff (Dunlosky et al.;
S-039). Do not let learners skip to their notes first; recall, then check.

**Values vs. facts.** "Everyone deserves enough to eat" is a value; the plan honors it
personally, while the previous lesson's proposal aimed at it collectively. Keep both
visible.

The **ethics** lens: a plan built on self-respect, not shame, is a kind plan. The
**egalitarian** lens: no way of eating is above another; access is the real divide.
The **global** lens: every culture's staple foods are legitimate foods. The
**technology** lens: decide whether an app or tracker helps you or adds pressure — you
choose. The **environment** lens: a sustainable plan can also be lighter on the Earth,
but that is a value to weigh, not a duty to perform perfectly.

## Procedure

1. **Retrieve the unit (10 min).** From memory, write: (a) the three mind-body tools
   and one reason each works; (b) the social model of disability; (c) one movement
   tradition and what it offers; (d) one policy lever in the food system. Then open
   your log and correct or complete. This is how the unit becomes yours.
2. **Meet the plate model (5 min).** Look at the chart: flexible sections, no single
   "right" food, and four reminders — **needs, culture, budget, values.** A
   sustainable plan fits all four.
3. **Build your pattern (15 min).** Write a typical week of eating, using foods you
   actually eat and can afford. For each of the four reminders, add one line: *what
   my body needs, what my culture's foods are, what my budget allows, what my values
   ask of me.* Adapt as needed — some days are lean, and that is reality, not failure.
4. **Check for fit (10 min).** Ask yourself: *Could I keep this for a year? Is it
   flexible when money or season changes? Does it shame me, or sustain me?* Revise
   one part to make it more sustainable.
5. **Peer review (10 min).** Share only what you choose. A partner checks: *is this
   sustainable, culturally respectful, and free of shame?* Suggest one small
   improvement, kindly.
6. **Close (5 min).** One sentence to your future self: *"I will feed my body with…"*
   Remember the unit's deepest idea: your body is your home, and you have learned to
   care for it with dignity and joy.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a one-day plan template and a word bank of the four reminders;
  build a single day instead of a week.
- **Extension:** Ask learners to add an "if my circumstances change" note — a flexible
  fallback plan for lean or changed times.

## Assessment

- **Summative (portfolio):** A sustainable personal nutrition plan addressing needs,
  culture, budget, and values, plus the unit-wide retrieval in the practice log.
- **Peer:** A partner's check for sustainability, cultural respect, and absence of
  shame.

## Home connection

Share one thing you learned this unit with someone you trust — a practice, an idea, a
plan. Teaching it back is how you keep it. If you prefer, write it in your log for
your future self.
