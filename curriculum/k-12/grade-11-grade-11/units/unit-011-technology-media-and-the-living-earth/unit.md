# Unit 11 — Technology, Media & the Living Earth

## Overview

This unit gathers the outward year into governance, solidarity, and stewardship —
neither worship nor fear — building on Grade 10's data, surveillance, media
ownership, and sustainability work. Learners analyze how technologies are governed —
by law, standards, and markets — and evaluate a debate over regulating one, and
analyze how the benefits and harms of technology are distributed unevenly across the
world, arguing for fairer access. They analyze propaganda and information
manipulation in a historical or current case, and how a free press and literacy
resist it, and evaluate the responsibilities of creators and platforms when
information spreads harm, arguing for what should change. They trace a global cycle —
carbon, nitrogen, or water — through its parts and explain how human activity has
altered it, and explain the idea of the more-than-human world and why some traditions
treat land, water, and other species as beings owed care. They analyze global
environmental policy, such as a climate agreement, and the coordination and justice
questions it raises, and compare indigenous and other place-based stewardship
practices with industrial resource use, and what each teaches about sustainability.

## Essential questions

- How are technologies governed, and how are their benefits and harms distributed unevenly across the world?
- How do propaganda and information manipulation work, and what are the responsibilities of creators and platforms when information spreads harm?
- How do global cycles and the more-than-human world teach stewardship, and what do indigenous and place-based practices teach sustainability?
