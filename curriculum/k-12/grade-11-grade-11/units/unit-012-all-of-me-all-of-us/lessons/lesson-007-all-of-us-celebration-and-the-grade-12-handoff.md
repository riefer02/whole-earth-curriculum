# Lesson 7 — All of Us: Celebration and the Grade-12 Handoff

## Summary

The capstone closes by gathering every artifact of the week — the values–purpose compass,
the purpose story, the personal project plan, the belonging contribution, and the
mutual-aid effort plan — into one portfolio and one celebration. Learners share, appreciate
one another, and name what they will **hand forward into Grade 12**, the capstone year of
integration and life purpose. This is the unit's summative moment — warm, agentic, and
hopeful, never a test.

## Objectives

- Gather and celebrate my values, purpose, project, belonging plan, and mutual-aid effort,
  and hand them forward into Grade 12 as the author of my own life and a co-author of our
  shared future. _(D01.S2.11.01, D01.S3.11.02, D02.S3.11.02, D02.S4.11.02)_

## Connection

A year is not a score; it is a story you have been writing, a purpose you have been
leaning toward, and a set of hands you have reached toward. When people finish a harvest,
a build, or a long journey, they do not just stop — they gather, name what was made, thank
the hands that helped, and look toward the next field. That is what this lesson is: a
gathering at the end of a year, to see the whole cloth you have made and to step, together,
toward Grade 12 — where purpose becomes an integrated way of being and acting in the world.

## Materials

- Celebration-and-handoff graphic (SVG)
- The closing portfolio (all unit artifacts)

## Preparation

- Gather each learner's artifacts from Lessons 1–6. Arrange a circle for sharing.
- Retrieval/spacing: this lesson is a spaced retrieval pass across the whole unit (and the
  year) — recalling the artifacts now, once more, strengthens them for the future
  (Dunlosky et al., S-039).
- Prepare the Grade-12 handoff framing: the summit this unit points toward — an integrated
  sense of who I am, what I value, and how I will act (D01.S2.12.01); a meaningful life
  goal acted on with autonomy and accountability (D01.S3.12.01); my place in nested
  communities from family to the global community of all life (D02.S3.12.01); and a
  personal ethic of care, cooperation, and mutual aid (D02.S4.12.01).

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **the year's work gathers
into one whole — a purpose, a project, a belonging plan, and a mutual-aid effort — and that
whole is worth celebrating and worth handing forward into Grade 12.** This is the summative
close, but the tone is celebration, not grading. Assessment here is a portfolio and a shared
performance, in the spirit of the philosophy (assessment serves the learner; success is
collective — philosophy §§8–9).

Hold the lenses visibly. **Ethics:** naming a handoff intention — a promise to carry
something forward — is an ethical act, and it is kept small and doable. **Egalitarianism:**
the celebration honors every learner's artifacts as equally worthy; no one's purpose
outranks another's. **Global/anthropological:** gathering at a year's end to thank and look
ahead is a human pattern across cultures — let learners bring their own traditions of
marking endings and beginnings. **Environment:** the living Earth is in the circle itself —
the tree, the growing thing, the weather that carried the year — and stewardship is one of
the intentions learners may name. Distinguish evidence from values: "recalling and
celebrating work strengthens memory and meaning" is descriptive (Dunlosky et al., S-039);
"every person's purpose is worth honoring" is a value we hold.

Keep it spacious and warm. Let quiet learners share in writing or not at all; presence is
enough.

## Procedure

1. **Gather (8 min).** Set your artifacts out in a circle — the compass, the purpose story,
   the project plan, the belonging plan, and the mutual-aid effort plan. Walk the circle
   slowly and look at what you made this year.
2. **Recall and name (10 min).** For each artifact, say one line to yourself: one thing it
   taught you about your values, your purpose, your authorship, or how people care for one
   another. This final recall is what fixes the year in memory.
3. **Appreciate one another (12 min).** In a circle (or a gallery, or online), each person
   shares one artifact or one line — and leaves one true, specific appreciation for at
   least one other person's work. Appreciation, not ranking.
4. **Name the handoff (12 min).** Look at the celebration-and-handoff graphic: the circle
   around the living tree, the sun rising, the stepping stones toward "Grade 12." Write
   one intention you will hand forward into Grade 12 — to yourself, to a community, or to
   the Earth — and one first step. It can be tiny; a seed is enough. Tie it to one artifact
   you are proudest of.
5. **Celebrate and look ahead (8 min).** Read your handoff intention once, quietly or
   aloud. Then, together or alone, mark the year's end in a way that fits you — a shared
   meal, a song, a moment of quiet, a hand on a shoulder — and step toward Grade 12.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a sentence starter for the handoff ("In Grade 12 I will carry
  forward…, and my first step is…"); accept a spoken, drawn, or shown intention (an object
  or gesture may stand in for words).
- **Access (blind / low vision):** Describe the graphic aloud — a diverse circle around a
  living tree, a rising sun, stepping stones toward "Grade 12" — and invite the learner to
  place a stone or leaf for each thing they are grateful for, as in the outdoor variant.
- **Extension:** Write a short letter to your future self, sealed or saved, to open at the
  start of Grade 12.

## Assessment

- **Summative (portfolio + performance):** The completed closing portfolio — the compass,
  purpose story, project plan, belonging plan, and mutual-aid effort plan, plus the
  handoff intention — shared and appreciated in the celebration circle.
- **Self-assessment:** A short reflection — "What I am proudest of this year, and what I
  will hand forward into Grade 12." Mastery is individual; the celebration is collective.

## Home connection

Bring your portfolio home and share it with your family or community in whatever way fits —
a reading, a quiet look together, a meal. Let them add one appreciation, too.
