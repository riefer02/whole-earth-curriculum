# Lesson 2 — The Purpose Story, Carried into Grade 12

## Summary

Learners take the compass from Lesson 1 and shape it into a **purpose story** — a
narrative identity that links the past (what shaped their values), the present (where
their purpose now stands), and a Grade-12 chapter (where they mean to take it). They
practice revising it, discovering that a purpose is a living story they hold loosely,
not a verdict to defend. This is the handoff bridge into Grade 12, the capstone year of
integration.

## Objectives

- Shape my developing values, purpose, and sense of meaning into a revisable story with
  reasons for the Grade-12 chapter. _(D01.S2.11.01)_

## Connection

A purpose written in pencil can be revised; a purpose carved in stone cannot. Think of a
path you once thought led nowhere that later led somewhere — the direction was not wrong,
the map was just drawn too early. Your purpose is the same kind of thing: not a wall you
are trapped inside, but a trail you keep redrawing as you walk. The question this lesson
asks is not "what is my one fixed destiny?" but "where is my purpose leaning now, and
what chapter do I mean to write next year?" — knowing the chapter can surprise you.

## Materials

- Purpose-path graphic (SVG)
- Writing journal or portfolio

## Preparation

- Retrieval cue: from Lesson 1, the three-arm compass; from Grade 10's closing unit, the
  past–now–next story. Today we add the dimension of time *and* a named Grade-12 horizon.
- Prepare the three-part frame: **past** (what shaped my values), **now** (my purpose, in
  one or two sentences), **Grade 12** (one chapter I mean to write next year, and why I
  believe it — my evidence).
- Note the Grade-12 summit this strand builds toward: an integrated sense of who I am,
  what I value, and how I will act (D01.S2.12.01), and authoring my identity within
  global, cultural, and technological forces rather than being defined by them
  (D01.S2.12.02).

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **purpose is not a
verdict but a story — a narrative identity linking past, present, and an imagined future
— and the learner is its author, free to revise it.** Narrative identity (McAdams, S-404)
and "possible selves" (Markus & Nurius, S-399) are the descriptive research; a sense of
purpose grows by trying and reflecting, and only a minority of young people hold a clear
one, which is ordinary rather than deficient (Damon, S-400). Identity is a lifelong
process, not a finished fact (Erikson, S-155). Hold all of this as description; "you are
allowed to revise, and 'I don't know yet' is a complete answer" is a value we protect.

Hold the four lenses visibly. **Ethics:** revising your purpose is an act of honesty
toward yourself — it keeps your story true. **Egalitarianism:** no one's Grade-12 chapter
is more "correct"; a purpose may be quiet or loud, and a meaningful life can look many
ways. **Global/anthropological:** in many traditions the self is told as a story that
belongs to a people and a place, not only to an individual — invite learners to tell it
their way, solo or communal. **Critical-thinking:** the Grade-12 chapter must carry a
*reason* — evidence from what you have tried, not a wish — and distinguishing the two is
the thinking skill at stake. Model the three-part frame once (S-011), then let learners
draft. Keep it warm.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (7 min).** From Lesson 1, what was the closing line of your compass? Now add
   time: every purpose has a past (what shaped it), a now (where it stands), and a next
   (where it is going). Say which of the three feels easiest to name, and which hardest.
2. **See the path (8 min).** Look at the purpose-path graphic: past on the left, now in
   the middle, and a dashed summit on the right marked "Grade 12." The dashed line means
   the summit is open — you are allowed to climb toward it without knowing every step.
3. **Draft your purpose story (20 min).** Write three short paragraphs. **Past:** one
   thing — a person, a place, an experience — that shaped your values. **Now:** your
   purpose, one or two sentences, in your own words. **Grade 12:** one chapter you mean
   to write next year, and one reason you believe it (your evidence — something you have
   tried, noticed, or come back to).
4. **Revise it (8 min).** Read your story aloud once, quietly or to a partner. Find the
   line that felt least true, and change it. Notice that revising did not break the story —
   it made it more yours.
5. **Close (7 min).** Under your story write: "This purpose is mine, and mine to revise."
   Add it to your portfolio — it is your Grade-12 letter to yourself.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide three sentence starters — "My values were shaped by…", "My purpose
  now leans toward…", "In Grade 12 I want to write a chapter where…" — and ask for one
  line each.
- **Access (blind / low vision):** Describe the path aloud — past on the left, now in the
  middle, a dashed Grade-12 summit on the right — and offer a tactile path of three stones
  laid left, middle, and right that the learner can walk.
- **Alternative expression (non-writing, non-oral):** Tell the past–now–Grade-12 story by
  placing three found objects in a row and pointing to each, or by drawing three scenes; a
  partner may scribe only if the learner chooses.
- **Extension:** Add a counter-consideration — one way the Grade-12 chapter might surprise
  you, and how you would respond if it did.

## Assessment

- **Formative (self + peer):** Can the learner link past, present, and a reasoned Grade-12
  chapter, and revise the story when a line does not feel true?
- **Portfolio artifact (unit):** The three-part purpose story with the closing line, kept
  in the portfolio.

## Home connection

Tell one adult your Grade-12 chapter and the reason you believe it. Ask them what they
were leaning toward at your age — and whether their purpose, too, kept revising.
