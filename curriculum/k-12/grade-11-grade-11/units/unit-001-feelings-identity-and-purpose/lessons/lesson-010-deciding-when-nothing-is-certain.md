# Lesson 10 — Deciding When Nothing Is Certain

## Summary

Learners practice decisions made under **uncertainty** — where no option guarantees an
outcome. They add a three-part move to the decision loop: *what I know, what I value,
what I'll do if I'm wrong.* They learn that a wrong turn is repaired and reflected on,
not a verdict — and that a "good-enough" decision made honestly is often the best
available.

## Objectives

- Make decisions that align their actions with their values, even under pressure or
  uncertainty. _(D01.S3.11.01)_

## Connection

Sometimes you can see the whole board; sometimes you are choosing a path in fog. The
decision to start a project, to trust someone, to change direction, to leave a place —
these come with no guarantee. Waiting for certainty can be its own kind of trap,
because certainty may never come. The mature skill is not to *know* the outcome; it is
to *choose by your values and stay able to repair*. A compass works in fog. That is
what it is for.

## Materials

- Your journal
- A fog-of-uncertainty diagram (see the asset)

## Preparation

- Print or draw the fog-of-uncertainty diagram.
- Retrieval (spaced): from Lesson 9, recall the five steps of the decision loop.
  Today we add what to do when step 3 — "consider options" — is shrouded in fog.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **under uncertainty,
you cannot optimize for a certain outcome, so you optimize for honesty and
repairability — decide by your values, name what you know and don't know, and keep a
repair path open.** Teach the three-part move with a worked example (S-011).

The **ethics** lens: acting under uncertainty is where character shows — the question
is not "what will happen?" but "what kind of choice is this?" The **egalitarian**
lens: uncertainty is not distributed equally — some people can afford to be wrong, and
others cannot; a fair decision weighs who carries the downside of a wrong call. The
**global** lens: leaders, communities, and nations decide in fog all the time (a
harvest, an evacuation, a treaty), and the same discipline — name the unknown, decide
by values, keep repair open — applies at every scale. The **technology & environment**
lens: our sense of certainty is often shaped by screens that *feel* certain but are
not (a confident-sounding post, a smoothed-over prediction), and environmental
decisions are classic fog — you cannot know the full future of a climate choice, yet
you must choose. Naming what is unknown is itself an act of intellectual honesty.

**Emotional safety.** Normalize being wrong. The "repair" step exists precisely so a
miss is not a shame. Model a wrong decision of your own and how you repaired it. No
learner is required to share a high-stakes private dilemma; hypotheticals are always
available.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** Name the five steps of the decision loop. Which one gets
   hardest when the fog rolls in? (Usually "consider options" — because you cannot see
   the outcomes.)
2. **Meet the three-part move (15 min).** When you cannot know the outcome, run these
   three questions:
   1. **What do I know?** — the few solid facts, honestly separated from guesses.
   2. **What do I value?** — which values apply, and what would they point toward.
   3. **What will I do if I'm wrong?** — my repair path: how I'll notice, own, and
      fix a bad call.
   Worked example: deciding whether to speak up about an unfair rule, not knowing how
   people will react. *I know* the rule exists and who it hurts. *I value* fairness and
   courage. *If I'm wrong* about the reaction, I'll apologize for any misstep, learn,
   and try a better way next time. Copy the move into your journal.
3. **Run it on a real decision (20 min).** Choose one uncertain decision — starting
   something, trusting someone, changing course — and answer the three questions in
   writing. If you prefer, use this public one: *whether to join a community effort to
   repair a shared space, when you are not sure it will succeed.*
4. **Name the "good-enough" truth (10 min).** Write one sentence: *a decision made
   honestly in fog, with a repair path open, is usually better than a decision never
   made.* Then add today's entry to your decision log.
5. **Close (5 min).** Put the log entry in your portfolio. Remember: the compass works
   in fog — that is its whole job.

## Differentiation

- **Mode (expression):** Writing is one way, not the only way — respond by speaking, pointing to a word card, arranging found objects, drawing, or gesturing; a response that uses neither writing nor speaking is still a full response.
- **Support:** Provide the three questions as a fill-in template and one worked
  example to copy.
- **Extension:** Take a past uncertain decision you now see was wrong, and write what
  your repair path *would have been* — and what you learned.

## Assessment

- **Formative (self):** Does the log entry separate what is known from what is
  guessed, tie the choice to a value, and include a concrete repair path?
- **Portfolio:** The uncertain-decision entry, gathered toward D01.S3.11.01.

## Home connection

Ask an adult about a decision they made *without knowing* how it would turn out. What
did they hold onto, and what was their plan if it went wrong?
