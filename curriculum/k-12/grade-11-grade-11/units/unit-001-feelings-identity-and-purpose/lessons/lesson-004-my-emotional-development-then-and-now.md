# Lesson 4 — My Emotional Development: Then and Now

## Summary

Learners reflect on how their emotional life has grown over time — not to grade it,
but to *name* it. They identify one real area of strength and one honest area of
growth, and they write a then/now/next reflection that becomes a portfolio artifact.
The lesson is a celebration of a self in the making, never a test.

## Objectives

- Reflect on their own emotional development over time and name areas of strength and
  growth. _(D01.S1.11.02)_

## Connection

Think of a time, years ago, when a big feeling swallowed you whole — and notice how
you meet that same feeling today. Maybe you still feel it just as strongly, but now you
can name it faster, breathe through it, or ask for help sooner. You have been growing
as an emotional person this whole time, whether or not anyone measured it. Stopping to
see that growth — and to see where you are still growing — is how you become the
author of your own inner life.

## Materials

- Your journal
- A then/now/next map (see the asset)

## Preparation

- Print or draw the then/now/next map.
- Retrieval (spaced): from earlier grades, recall the skill of *emotional granularity*
  — having precise words for feelings — and the RULER practice of labeling emotions
  precisely (S-015). You will use that precision today on your *own* story.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **emotional
development is real, ongoing, and nameable — a learner can name a strength and a
growth area without judgment, because "not yet" is information, never a verdict.**
Model the reflection with a worked example of your own (S-011), then release learners
to write privately.

The **social-emotional** lens is the core: this is self-awareness applied over time
(CASEL, S-001), using the precise-labeling skill from RULER (S-015) and the idea that
a richer emotion vocabulary enables finer feeling and better regulation (Barrett's
"emotional granularity," S-401 — offered as one well-supported account, not a creed).
The **ethics** lens: naming our own growth honestly — strengths without arrogance,
gaps without shame — is a discipline of truthfulness toward oneself. The
**egalitarian** lens: growth looks different for everyone and depends on the support a
person had; no one's pace is a measure of their worth, and we never rank one
learner's story against another's. The **global** lens: what counts as emotional
maturity, and which feelings are shown or hidden, is shaped by culture — so honor your
own context rather than measuring yourself against a single standard. The
**technology & environment** lens: notice how screens and environments have shaped
your emotional life (what a feed does to your mood, what a quiet place does to your
body) — those are part of your story too.

**Emotional safety is the whole point.** This is private, vulnerable work. Never
require sharing; "pass" is complete. Frame the reflection as *celebration plus
curiosity*, never as a score. Remind learners that a growth area is not a defect — it
is a direction.

## Procedure

1. **Recall and anchor (5 min).** In your journal, list three precise feeling words you
   know now that you might not have known as a young child (for example: *grief*,
   *awe*, *resentment*, *relief*). That word-list is itself evidence of growth.
2. **Meet the map (10 min).** Look at the then/now/next map: a rising path with three
   points — *then* (the younger you), *now* (you today), and *next* (the you still
   growing). Worked example (your facilitator's, or this one): *Then* I felt anger as
   one big blur; *now* I can often feel it coming and name what it is about; *next* I
   want to pause one second longer before I speak.
3. **Find a strength (15 min).** Think of a moment, recent or far back, that shows an
   emotional strength you now have — staying calm when it mattered, noticing someone
   else's feeling, asking for help, bouncing back. Write it as a sentence that names
   the strength *and* the evidence: "I can ___; I know because ___."
4. **Find a growth area (15 min).** Now, with the same kindness, name one place you are
   still growing — a feeling that still floods you, a habit you want to shift. Write it
   the same way, without blame: "I am still learning to ___; I notice ___."
5. **Close (5 min).** Add the map and the two sentences to your portfolio. Read them
   once more and notice: strengths and growth areas are two sides of the same honest
   look at yourself. Neither is a verdict.

## Differentiation

- **Mode (expression):** Writing is one way, not the only way — respond by speaking, pointing to a word card, arranging found objects, drawing, or gesturing; a response that uses neither writing nor speaking is still a full response.
- **Support:** Offer sentence frames ("I used to ___, but now I ___"; "I am still
  learning to ___") and one filled example to copy.
- **Extension:** Write the "next" step as a small, specific intention — one thing to
  practice this week — and date it.

## Assessment

- **Formative (self):** Does the reflection name at least one strength and one growth
  area, each with a scrap of evidence, in a kind (non-blaming) voice?
- **Portfolio:** The then/now/next map, gathered toward D01.S1.11.02.

## Home connection

Ask a trusted adult or older sibling what *they* have noticed change in you over the
years — a way you handle things now that you didn't before. Receive it as information,
not a score.
