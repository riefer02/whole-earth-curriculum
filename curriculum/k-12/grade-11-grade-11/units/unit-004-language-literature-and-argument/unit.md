# Unit 4 — Language, Literature & Argument

## Overview

This unit grows from Grade 10's criticism and craft into the multilingual, critical,
and persuasive voice, building on rhetoric critique and extended reading. Learners
listen across differences of accent, dialect, and viewpoint, identifying assumptions
in what is said, and construct and deliver a persuasive speech that anticipates and
fairly addresses counterarguments. They compare texts across languages and
translations, noting how translation shapes meaning, and analyze how an author's
cultural and historical position shapes a text, reading it against other
perspectives. They write extended, original work that develops a sustained idea or
story, and adapt writing across languages and registers, including translation or
writing in a second language where possible. They evaluate language policies — such
as official languages or language in schools — for their effects on equity and
inclusion, and bridge between speakers of different languages in a real setting,
interpreting main ideas with accuracy and care. Always home language first, with
multilingualism held as wealth.

## Essential questions

- How do I listen across accents, dialects, and viewpoints, identify assumptions, and deliver a persuasive speech that fairly addresses counterarguments?
- How does translation shape meaning, and how does an author's cultural and historical position shape a text?
- How do I write extended original work, adapt it across languages and registers, and bridge between speakers of different languages?
