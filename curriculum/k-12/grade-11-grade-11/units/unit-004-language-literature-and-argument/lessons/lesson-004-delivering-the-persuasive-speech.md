# Lesson 4 — Delivering the Persuasive Speech, Fairly

## Summary

Learners turn their scaffold into a **delivery**: speaking the claim, reasons, and
evidence aloud, and — the mark of the whole lesson — naming the counterargument at
full strength and answering it honestly in front of others. Peers rate *fairness to
the other side*, and each learner scores their own delivery against a checklist.

## Objectives

- Deliver a persuasive speech that anticipates and fairly addresses counterarguments —
  with delivery and fair-mindedness working together. _(D04.S1.11.02)_

## Connection

Think of the difference between someone who talks *at* you and someone who talks *with*
you. The one who talks at you pretends there is no other view; the one who talks with
you says, out loud, "here is the strongest reason to disagree with me — and here is
what I think about it." That second person is not weaker for naming the objection;
they are stronger, because you stop guarding against the hidden trap and actually
listen. Delivery is not decoration — how you hold your voice and body either *builds*
that trust or spends it.

## Materials

- Delivery checklist
- Speech scaffold from Lesson 3

## Preparation

- Copy the delivery checklist.
- Retrieval: from Lesson 3, recall the six-box scaffold and the steelman principle.
  Today we speak it.
- Prepare one worked example: read a scaffolded argument aloud once *with* the
  counterargument named fairly, once *without* it, and compare how each lands.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **delivery — voice,
pacing, presence — is the vehicle, but fairness is the load; a persuasive speech that
fairly addresses its counterargument is delivered as much with honesty as with
technique.** Grade 11 learners are near-adult speakers; treat them as co-authors of
the standard (development.md 9–12 band). Delivery skills (pacing, emphasis, volume,
and — where comfortable — gaze) are explicit, coachable techniques — model them and
give guided practice
(S-011). But do not let polish outrank honesty: the single most important mark on the
checklist is *was the counterargument stated at full strength, and answered?*

Model once, then let learners deliver (worked example → guided practice →
independent/performance; S-011). The **ethics** lens is the spine: fairness to the
other side is the moral content of persuasion — a speech can be smooth and still be a
con. The **egalitarian** lens: a fair speech makes room for the objections of the
less powerful, not just the loud. The **social-emotional** lens: public speaking
raises real anxiety; name it, allow pauses, and treat a shaky delivery with a steady
claim as fully successful — the reverse is not. The **critical-thinking** lens: the
audience's job is to check whether the *counterargument* was a steelman or a straw
man, and whether the response actually met it. Use peer feedback for fairness, not for
ranking; celebrate mastery of the standard, not victory (philosophy §8).

**Access.** Delivery is *any* channel that carries the claim and the fair
counterargument. A learner may deliver in sign language (with a written summary for
hearing peers), through an AAC device or text-to-speech, or by sharing a written copy
that a partner reads aloud while the learner is present. The assessed skill is the
fairness of the steelman and the honesty of the response — never vocal ability. Eye
contact is never required: learners who find it uncomfortable may look at the horizon,
their notes, or a fixed point; gaze is offered as an option, not a mark.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Lesson 3: what makes a speech *honest*? (It names the
   best objection at full strength and answers it.) Today you speak it aloud.
2. **Meet the delivery marks (10 min).** Look at the delivery checklist. The marks:
   **claim clear**, **reasons given**, **evidence real**, **counterargument at full
   strength**, **response honest**, **voice paced**, **counterargument named before it
   is answered**. Watch a worked example: the same argument read twice — once hiding
   the objection, once naming it fairly. Feel the difference.
3. **Rehearse (12 min).** In pairs, read your speech aloud from the scaffold. Your
   partner listens for *one* thing only: was the counterargument stated at its
   strongest, or weakened? Swap roles and repeat.
4. **Deliver (15 min).** In circles of five or six, each learner delivers their
   speech. Listeners mark the checklist. After each speech, one listener names the
   strongest steelman they heard and whether the response truly answered it.
5. **Receive and give (8 min).** For each speech, give one piece of feedback in the
   form: "your counterargument was fair because ___" or "you could state it more
   strongly by ___." No ranking — only what served fairness and what could serve it
   better.
6. **Close (5 min).** Remember: delivery is the vehicle; fairness is the load. Score
   your own delivery against the checklist and add the sheet to your portfolio. Next
   time: we cross a border — the same text in two languages.

## Differentiation

- **Access (deaf, hard-of-hearing, non-speaking, autistic):** Deliver in sign, via AAC
  or text-to-speech, or by sharing a written copy (a partner may read it aloud). "Eye
  contact" and "voice paced" become "the delivery is clear and the counterargument is
  stated at full strength, in whatever channel you use" — gaze is optional, and fairness
  to the other side is the one non-negotiable mark.
- **Support:** Deliver a shortened version (claim, one reason, one counterargument,
  response) and receive feedback on fairness alone.
- **Extension:** Add a **concession** — one thing the other side is genuinely right
  about — before your response, and note how it changes the room's willingness to
  listen.

## Assessment

- **Performance + peer:** Can the learner deliver the speech with a counterargument
  stated at full strength and answered honestly? Peers mark the checklist; the
  learner self-scores.
- **Portfolio artifact (unit):** The scored delivery checklist (with a note of the
  speech's topic and the steelman used), added as the "delivered speech" entry — a
  spoken record may be substituted where recording is possible.

## Home connection

Deliver your speech to one person at home, but with a twist: ask them to interrupt
you with their own objection — and answer it on the spot, fairly.
