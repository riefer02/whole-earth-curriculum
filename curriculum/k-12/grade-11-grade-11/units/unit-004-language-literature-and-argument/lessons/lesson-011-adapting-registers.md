# Lesson 11 — Adapting Writing Across Registers

## Summary

Learners adapt one idea across three **registers** — formal, informal, and technical —
naming what changes (word choice, tone, formality) and what must stay (the idea
itself). They learn register is a tool for reaching an audience, not a ranking of ways
of speaking, and begin the unit's adaptation artifact.

## Objectives

- Adapt writing across languages and registers — beginning with registers: re-clothing
  one idea for different audiences without losing it. _(D04.S3.11.02)_

## Connection

You already do this every day. You describe the same headache one way to a friend
("my head is killing me") and another way to a nurse ("a throbbing pain above the left
eye since this morning"). The idea — the pain — stays the same; the register — the
words, the tone, the formality — shifts to fit the listener. Register is not about
sounding "better"; it is about being *understood* by the person in front of you. A
writer who masters register can carry one true idea into many rooms.

## Materials

- Register adaptation sheet (formal / informal / technical — and what changed)
- Learning journal

## Preparation

- Copy or draw the three-column adaptation sheet.
- Retrieval: from Grade 10, recall matching tone and register to purpose and audience
  (D04.S3.10.01). Today we adapt *one idea* across registers deliberately.
- Prepare one worked example: one idea, three registers.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **register is the
clothing an idea wears for its audience — the idea stays, while word choice, tone, and
formality shift — and no register is "better," only more or less fitted to the reader.**
This directly extends Grade 10's register work (D04.S3.10.01) into deliberate
*adaptation* of a single idea. Hold the anti-ranking commitment: formal is not superior
to informal, and a learner's home register is never "wrong" — the skill is *choosing* a
register for a purpose, not abandoning one's own (egalitarian lens; philosophy §1).

Model one adaptation, then let learners write the others (worked example → guided
practice → independent work; S-011). The **global** lens: what counts as "formal" or
"polite" differs across cultures — honorifics, address, and tone vary, so a register
that fits in one place may misfit in another (see Lesson 15 on politeness). The
**egalitarian** lens: technical jargon can *include* specialists and *exclude* everyone
else — ask who a register lets in and who it shuts out. The **intellectual** lens:
precision is the discipline — technical terms earn their place only when a plain word
would blur the meaning. The **critical-thinking** lens: read a register for what it
*does* — a formal register can signal authority, an informal one intimacy, a technical
one expertise — and each can be used honestly or to intimidate. Preview: Lesson 12
adapts writing across *languages*, not just registers.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Grade 10: you matched tone and register to audience. Today
   you adapt *one* idea across registers on purpose.
2. **Meet the three registers (10 min).** Look at the register adaptation sheet. Three
   columns: **formal** (a report, a letter to someone with authority), **informal** (a
   message to a friend), **technical** (a specialist explaining to another specialist).
   Watch a worked example: one idea written three ways. Name what changed — words,
   tone, formality — and what stayed.
3. **Choose your idea (5 min).** Pick one idea worth carrying — it can be your extended
   work's central idea from Lessons 9–10, or a simple fact you know well. Write it in
   one plain sentence in your **home language**.
4. **Write three registers (20 min).** Write the idea in each column. Formal: precise,
   measured, complete. Informal: warm, direct, everyday. Technical: using the exact
   terms of a field, if you know them. Keep the *idea* identical in all three.
5. **Name what changed (8 min).** In the bottom row, write what shifted in each column —
   one change of word choice, one of tone, one of formality — and *why* it fits that
   audience.
6. **Close (5 min).** Remember: register is a tool, not a ranking — the idea stays, the
   clothes change. Add this page to your adaptation artifact. Next time: the same idea
   crosses a *language* border.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide the formal version already written; ask learners to write the
  informal version and name one thing that changed.
- **Extension:** Write the technical version, then translate it *back* to plain
  language for a child — and note which meaning was hardest to carry without the
  technical term.

## Assessment

- **Formative (self):** Can the learner write one idea in three registers and name what
  changed (word choice, tone, formality) and what stayed (the idea)?
- **Portfolio artifact (unit):** The register adaptation sheet, added to the portfolio
  as the "across-register adaptation" entry.

## Home connection

Take one thing you said today — to a friend or a family member — and re-say it in your
head for someone in authority. Notice what changes, and whether the idea survives.
