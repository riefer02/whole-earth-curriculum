# Lesson 10 — Planning My Contribution to Belonging

## Summary

Learners stop analyzing and start contributing. They envision one real contribution that
strengthens **belonging** in a community they care about, test it against the question
"who benefits and who is left out?", and draft a concrete plan — what, who, steps,
timeline, and a first move small enough to actually take.

## Objectives

- Envision and plan a contribution that strengthens belonging in a community they care
  about. _(D02.S3.11.02)_

## Connection

A community is like a net: it holds people, but only where the threads are tied. A gap
in the net is a person falling through — the newcomer no one greeted, the neighbor no
one checks on, the quiet voice never asked. You do not have to weave the whole net. You
only have to add one thread, in one gap, in a place you actually care about. That is a
contribution. This lesson helps you plan yours so it is real enough to do, not just
wish.

## Materials

- Your journal
- A contribution-to-belonging diagram (see the asset)

## Preparation

- Print or draw the contribution-to-belonging diagram.
- Retrieval (spaced): from Lesson 9, recall the three questions — who has a seat, who has
  no chair, who holds the gate. A contribution is aimed at a specific gap: a chairless
  person, a locked gate, a missing thread. Today we plan the thread.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **a contribution to
belonging is a concrete, planned action aimed at a real gap — and it must be tested for
who benefits and who is left out.** Teach the plan structure with a worked example; keep
plans small enough to be feasible in the learner's real circumstances (no
resource-heavy requirements).

The **ethics** lens: contributing to belonging is a moral act — it treats another
person's being included as worth my effort. The **egalitarian** lens: test every plan
against *who benefits and who is left out* — a contribution that helps the already-seated
while ignoring the chairless is not yet a contribution to belonging (it may even widen
the gap). The **global** lens: contributions to belonging take endlessly varied forms
across cultures — hosting, sharing food, teaching a language, tending a shared space,
welcoming a newcomer — and none is ranked above another; the common thread is the
closing of a gap. The **social-emotional** lens: choosing *where* to care and acting on
it builds purpose and agency — the feeling of mattering.

**Emotional safety.** Plans should be chosen freely and may stay private. No learner is
required to commit publicly or to act outside their safety or capacity. A contribution
to a *small* community (a family, a study group, a friendship) is as valid as one to a
neighborhood or city; no scale is graded as better.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** What are the three questions from Lesson 9? Which "gap" matters
   most to you in a community you actually know? Hold it.
2. **Meet the plan (10 min).** Worked example, together. A learner notices that new
   students eat alone. Their contribution plan:
   - **What:** a weekly "welcome table" where new students are invited to sit.
   - **Who:** the learner + two friends; the new students; the lunch monitor's okay.
   - **Steps:** ask the monitor → make a sign → invite one person the first week.
   - **First move this week:** ask the monitor for permission.
   - **Test:** who benefits (new students), who is left out (students who eat elsewhere —
     could the table rotate?), and is that acceptable?
3. **Draft your plan (20 min).** In your journal, complete each part for *your*
   contribution: What? Who? Steps? First move this week? Then run the test: *who
   benefits, who is left out, and can I adjust to include more?*
4. **Peer check (10 min).** In pairs, share plans (only what you choose). Your partner
   asks two questions: "What would make it real?" and "Who is still left out?" Adjust
   your plan.
5. **Close (5 min).** Put the diagram and the plan in your portfolio. Say or write: *I
   can add one thread to the net — and I have a plan to do it.*

## Differentiation

- **Mode (expression):** Plan by writing, drawing a map or net, or speaking it aloud;
  expression is flexible.
- **Support:** Offer a template with sentence starters and a menu of small contribution
  ideas.
- **Extension:** Draft the plan at two scales — one small and immediate, one larger and
  sustained — and compare feasibility and reach.

## Assessment

- **Formative (self + peer):** Does the plan name a concrete contribution with a "what,
  who, steps, and first move," and does it state who benefits and who is left out?
- **Portfolio:** The contribution plan, gathered toward D02.S3.11.02.

## Home connection

Try the first move of your plan with your family's encouragement — or name a small gap
at home (a chore no one notices) and offer to be the thread that fills it.
