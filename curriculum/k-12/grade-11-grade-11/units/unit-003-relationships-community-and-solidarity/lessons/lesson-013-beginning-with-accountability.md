# Lesson 13 — Beginning with Accountability

## Summary

A plan is only paper until it begins — and it only *lasts* if it is held accountable.
Learners convert their mutual-aid or cooperative design into a first commitment and a
working **accountability loop**: agree on the goal and roles, do the work, check and
report honestly, adjust and do it again. They set roles, a check-in rhythm, and a way to
keep faith with one another — then take their first real step.

## Objectives

- Plan and begin a sustained mutual-aid or cooperative effort with accountability.
  _(D02.S4.11.02)_

## Connection

Most good intentions die quietly — not from lack of heart, but from lack of a loop. You
decide to help, then the week fills up, and no one asks, and it fades. Accountability is
not punishment or surveillance; it is the gentle structure that keeps a promise alive:
you say what you will do, you do it, you tell the truth about what happened, and you
adjust. Every lasting cooperative effort on Earth runs on some version of this loop.
This lesson is where yours starts turning.

## Materials

- Your journal
- An accountability-loop diagram (see the asset)

## Preparation

- Print or draw the accountability-loop diagram.
- Retrieval (spaced): from Lesson 12, recall your effort's design — need, members,
  tasks, deciding. Today we attach the loop that will keep it alive.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **beginning means
converting a plan into a first commitment wrapped in an accountability loop — agree, do,
check and report honestly, adjust — so the effort sustains.** Teach the loop with a
worked example; keep commitments small, voluntary, and within the learner's real
capacity.

The **ethics** lens: keeping a promise to others — and telling the truth when I fall
short — is the ethical core of accountability; it is what turns good feeling into
trustworthy action. The **egalitarian** lens: accountability is *mutual* — it binds the
powerful and the new equally, and "check and report" must be safe to do truthfully or it
becomes theater. The **global** lens: sustained cooperation across cultures runs on
shared expectations and honest reporting — from cooperative bylaws to communal-work
rotas — all versions of the same loop. The **social-emotional** lens: naming a small
commitment and following through builds self-trust and agency; honest self-reporting is
emotional honesty practiced in public, kindly.

**Emotional safety.** Beginning can be intimidating. Keep first commitments small and
real, never burdensome or unsafe. "Not yet ready to begin" is honored — a learner may
refine the plan instead. Check-ins must be blame-free: reporting "I couldn't do it" is
information for adjusting, never a reason for shame.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Lesson 12, what is your effort's design? What is its
   *smallest* first step — the one you could actually take this week?
2. **Meet the loop (10 min).** Worked example, together. A study-help circle's first
   week:
   - **Agree** — the goal (two sessions this week) and roles (who hosts, who reminds).
   - **Do** — each person does their part.
   - **Check and report** — "I did mine; I couldn't do mine" — honestly, without excuse
     and without shame.
   - **Adjust** — change the time, swap roles, or shrink the goal — and do it again.
   The loop, not a single heroic effort, is what makes it *sustained*.
3. **Build your accountability plan (15 min).** In your journal, complete:
   - **My first commitment:** one concrete, small thing I will do this week.
   - **Who checks with me:** a partner or the group — and when (a day, a moment).
   - **How we report honestly:** a word, a mark, a short message.
   - **How we adjust:** what we change if someone falls short, without blame.
4. **Begin (15 min).** Do the first real move *now* if you can — send the message, make
   the sign, ask the question, plant the marker. If not now, write the exact day and time
   you will, and tell your check-in partner.
5. **Close (5 min).** Put the diagram and the plan in your portfolio. Say or write: *I
   have begun — and I have a loop to keep it true.*

## Differentiation

- **Mode (expression):** Plan by writing, speaking, or drawing the loop; expression is
  flexible.
- **Access (deaf, hard-of-hearing, non-speaking):** State the commitment in writing,
  sign, or a shared written chart instead of aloud; a written, typed, or signed check-in
  works as well as a spoken one. Honest reporting in any medium keeps the loop true.
- **Support:** Offer a template and pair each learner with a check-in partner who will
  also be checked on.
- **Extension:** Design the full group accountability system — cadence, format, and how
  the group will celebrate honest reporting as much as completion.

## Assessment

- **Formative (self + peer):** Does the plan name a concrete first commitment, a check-in
  partner and time, an honest-reporting method, and a blame-free way to adjust? Has the
  learner taken (or scheduled) the first move?
- **Performance:** The begun effort, observed as an actual first step or a dated
  commitment with a named check-in.
- **Portfolio:** The accountability plan and first-move record, gathered toward
  D02.S4.11.02.

## Home connection

Tell someone at home your first commitment and ask them to check with you on one
specific day. Notice what changes when someone is watching kindly.
