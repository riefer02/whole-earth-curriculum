# Lesson 3 — Building Close, Trusting Relationships

## Summary

Learners see that **trust** is not a feeling that happens to them but a structure they
build — from **reliability** (doing what they say), **honesty** (telling the truth),
**care** (wanting the other's good), and **repair** (fixing it when they break it).
They map one relationship they maintain against these blocks and name one block they
want to strengthen.

## Objectives

- Build and maintain close, trusting relationships and offer the same support to
  others. _(D02.S1.11.02)_

## Connection

Think of a person you trust with something important — a secret, a hard day, a mistake.
Ask yourself *why* them. Almost certainly it is not because they are fun or clever,
though they may be both. It is because, over time, they did what they said they would,
told you the truth, showed they wanted your good, and — when they hurt you — made it
right. Trust is not magic and it is not found by luck; it is built, block by block, by
ordinary actions repeated over time. This lesson names the blocks so you can build them
on purpose.

## Materials

- Your journal
- A building-trust diagram (see the asset)

## Preparation

- Print or draw the building-trust diagram.
- Retrieval (spaced): from Lessons 1–2, recall the listening loop and the steelman.
  Trust is the ground those skills stand on; today we build it.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The idea to land: **close, trusting
relationships are built from concrete, repeatable actions — reliability, honesty, care,
and repair — not from luck or chemistry.** Teach with a worked example of one trust
block in action; keep the mapping private so no one is pressured to rank their real
relationships aloud.

The **ethics** lens: trust is a moral relation — it is the shape that keeping promises
takes between people, and breaking it has real consequences for another's safety. The
**egalitarian** lens: trust is reciprocal — it is built between equals, and it is as
owed to the quiet or less powerful person as to the loud one; no relationship form
(kin, chosen family, friendship, partnership) is ranked above another here. The
**global** lens: every culture has its own ways of building and marking trust — oaths,
shared work, hospitality, gift — but the underlying blocks (reliability, honesty, care,
repair) appear across them, in many forms. The **social-emotional** lens: naming the
blocks makes an often-invisible bond visible and therefore growable.

**Emotional safety.** Relationship topics touch personal experience. No forced
disclosure; mapping is private. Include a broad view of "relationship" — family,
friends, chosen family, co-workers, neighbors — so no learner's life is excluded or
ranked. Offer opt-outs and remind learners they may write only what they choose to
share.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** Think of one person you trust. In one line, *why* do you trust
   them? Hold that reason; today we give its parts names.
2. **Meet the blocks (10 min).** Worked example, together. Here is one block in action:
   **reliability** — a friend says they will help you move, and they show up. Now the
   full structure of trust has four blocks:
   - **Reliability** — do what I say I will do.
   - **Honesty** — tell the truth, even when it is awkward.
   - **Care** — genuinely want the other's good, not just my own.
   - **Repair** — when I break trust, own it and make it right.
   A relationship can survive a broken block *if* repair is strong; it is the block
   that lets the others be rebuilt.
3. **Map one relationship (20 min).** Privately, choose one relationship you maintain
   (family, friend, chosen family, neighbor, teammate). For each block, write one small,
   true thing: *how does this relationship show reliability? honesty? care? repair?*
   Then mark the block you most want to strengthen.
4. **Reflect (15 min).** In your journal: *Which block is strongest in this
   relationship? Which is the one I want to grow, and what is one concrete thing I
   could do this week?* Keep it private unless you choose to share.
5. **Close (5 min).** Put the diagram and your private reflection in your portfolio.
   Say or write: *trust is built, and I can build it.*

## Differentiation

- **Mode (expression):** Map the blocks by drawing, listing, or speaking into a
  recording; no single written form is required.
- **Support:** Offer sentence starters ("One way we show reliability is…") and allow
  learners to map a relationship they admire rather than one of their own.
- **Extension:** Compare two relationships — one where trust is strong and one where it
  is strained — and locate the difference in specific blocks.

## Assessment

- **Formative (self):** Does the map name a concrete example for each trust block in one
  relationship, and identify one block to grow with one next action?
- **Portfolio:** The trust map and reflection, gathered toward D02.S1.11.02.

## Home connection

With someone at home, ask: "What is one small thing I do that helps you trust me?"
Listen without defending — this is the repair and care block in action.
