# Lesson 14 — Comparing Cosmological Models, Weighing Evidence

## Summary

Learners compare **cosmological models**: the **Big Bang**, the once-competing
**steady-state** model that evidence set aside, and **cultural origin accounts**. They
weigh the evidence that favored the Big Bang, see **falsifiability** at work in the
history of cosmology, and hold scientific models and cultural accounts in respectful
relation — each answering a different question, none a dogma (philosophy §11).

## Objectives

- Compare cosmological models, weigh the evidence for each, and hold them all as models
  rather than certainties. _(D06.S4.11.01)_

## Connection

Not long ago, serious scientists disagreed about whether the universe had a beginning.
The **steady-state** model held that the universe had always been much the same; the
**Big Bang** model held that it began hot and dense and has expanded since. Evidence —
especially the discovery of the cosmic microwave background — settled the contest, and
the steady-state model was set aside. That is how scientific models work: they are
compared, tested, and revised. And beside these scientific accounts sit the origin
stories of the world's peoples, which answer a different question — not *how it
mechanically began* but *what it means and how we should live* — and which we hold with
the same respect, and the same honesty about what each is.

## Materials

- Cosmological-models comparison chart
- Science journal

## Preparation

- Copy or draw the comparison chart; prepare claim cards.
- Retrieval: from Lesson 13, the Big Bang model and its evidence. Today we place it
  beside its rivals and see *why* it won.
- Prepare a respectful example of a cultural origin account (e.g., Māori or
  Haudenosaunee), presented as a living tradition of a specific people.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The ideas to land: **cosmological
models are compared by evidence and falsifiability; the Big Bang beat the steady-state
model — a universe with no beginning or end, proposed by Bondi, Gold, and Hoyle
(S-497) — because the cosmic microwave background was observed (S-002), and a universe
with no hot, dense beginning could not account for it, so the steady-state model was
set aside (S-497); and cultural origin accounts answer questions of meaning and
relationship, which scientific models do not attempt, so the two are held side by
side, not ranked against each other (philosophy §11).** Present the cultural account as
a living tradition of a named people, never as a specimen or a "failed" science. Teach
the comparison with a worked example (S-011).

The **critical-thinking** lens: weighing models by evidence — and by what would falsify
each — is the heart of the lesson. The **global** lens: origin accounts exist in every
culture; a full picture includes them all, each on its own terms. The **ethics** lens:
honesty about the limits of each account — scientific or cultural — is a form of
respect for the truth and for the peoples who hold the stories. The **intellectual**
lens: distinguishing *kinds* of claim (mechanical how vs. meaning) is the mark of a
mature mind. Preview: Lesson 15 turns from the cosmos to the *coupled systems* of the
Earth we actually live on.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Lesson 13, what evidence supports the Big Bang? Today we
   see how it won out over a rival, and how it sits beside other kinds of account.
2. **Two scientific models (12 min).** The **steady-state** model held that the
   universe has no beginning and stays much the same; the **Big Bang** model held that
   it began hot and dense and has expanded since. Compare their claims on your chart.
3. **Weighing the evidence (12 min).** The **cosmic microwave background** — the faint
   glow of the early universe — is exactly what the Big Bang predicts (S-002), and it
   does not fit the steady-state model, so that model was set aside (S-497). This is
   how evidence decides between models: the one that cannot account for the
   observation is set aside. Record this on your chart.
4. **Models are revisable (8 min).** The steady-state model was once serious science;
   it was not wrong *people* but a *model that failed a test*. Even the Big Bang
   remains a model — the best we have, still open to revision. Write what "model" means
   in this sense.
5. **Cultural origin accounts (12 min).** Beside the scientific models, place a
   cultural origin account from a named people. It is not a failed science; it answers
   a different question — meaning, relationship, how to live. Hold the two side by
   side: one for *how the cosmos developed*, one for *what it means* (philosophy §11).
6. **Close (5 min).** In your journal, write one sentence on why the Big Bang won the
   scientific contest, and one on what a cultural origin account offers that a
   scientific model does not.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Retell the two scientific models and the one piece of evidence that
  decided between them, in your own words.
- **Extension:** State the exact prediction each model makes about the microwave
  background, and explain why the steady-state model's failure was a *falsification*
  rather than a mistake of measurement.

## Assessment

- **Formative (peer + self):** Can the learner compare two scientific models, state the
  evidence that decided between them, and explain how scientific models relate to
  cultural origin accounts — without ranking one above the other?
- **Portfolio artifact (unit):** The completed cosmological-models comparison chart.

## Home connection

Ask someone at home what story your family or community tells about beginnings — and
what that story is *for*. Write it down, and note what question it answers.
