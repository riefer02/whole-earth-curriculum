# Lesson 16 — Climate, Ecology, and Society

## Summary

Learners close the unit by reading **climate, ecology, and human society** as one
**coupled system**. They explain the **greenhouse effect** and its human strengthening
(S-438), see that the people who did least to cause change often feel it most
(S-005), and propose **hopeful, just responses** — **mitigation, adaptation, and
restoration** — carrying the unit's evidence-and-values discipline into a stance of
agency, never despair (philosophy §12).

## Objectives

- Explain how climate, ecology, and human society shape one another, and propose a
  hopeful, just response. _(D06.S4.11.02)_

## Connection

The same coupling you traced in the last lesson now has a third partner: **us**. We
burn fuels that warm the air; the warming air shifts the rains and the seasons; the
shifting seasons change what grows and where people can live; and those changes fall
hardest on people who contributed least to them — the very young, the poor, and
communities already at the edge. This is not a doom story; it is a *coupling* story,
and couplings can be steered. Understanding how the three shape one another is exactly
what makes a fair and effective response possible.

## Materials

- Climate-ecology-society synthesis sheet
- Science journal

## Preparation

- Copy or draw the synthesis sheet.
- Retrieval: from Lesson 15, the four coupled spheres and feedback; from Grade 10,
  the greenhouse effect (D06.S4.10.02). Today we add the human partner and the
  response.
- Prepare a short, real example of a hopeful response to ground the lesson in agency.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The ideas to land: **greenhouse gases
trap heat near the surface, and human burning of fossil fuels has strengthened this
effect (S-438); the resulting climate change is coupled to ecology and to human
society — it shifts what grows, where water is, and where people can live (S-005); the
burden is distributed unjustly — those who emitted least are often most exposed; and
the responses are mitigation (cutting emissions), adaptation (adjusting to changes),
and restoration (repairing ecosystems), which must be weighed for justice, not only
effectiveness.** Always connect difficulty to agency (facilitation): this lesson
ends in action, not despair.

The **environment** lens: the living Earth is the lesson's center. The **ethics**
lens: who should act, and who should be protected first, are moral questions. The
**egalitarianism** lens: a just response protects those least able to protect
themselves and does not let the powerful offload the cost. The **global** lens: the
coupling is planetary, and the response must be cooperative across every border. This
lesson also gathers the unit's portfolio into a whole — the closing artifact.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Lesson 15, how do Earth's four spheres couple? Today we add
   the human partner and the response.
2. **The greenhouse effect, strengthened (12 min).** Certain gases trap heat near the
   surface — this is the natural **greenhouse effect** that keeps Earth warm. Human
   burning of coal, oil, and gas has added more of these gases, strengthening the
   effect (S-438). State this as *evidence*: measured, not assumed.
3. **The three-way coupling (12 min).** Draw **climate, ecology, society** with arrows
   both ways: climate shifts what grows and where water is; ecology feeds back on
   climate (forests and oceans take up carbon); society both drives the change and
   suffers it. Trace one full loop end to end.
4. **Who feels it most (8 min).** The change is not distributed fairly: the people and
   places that emitted the least greenhouse gas are often those most exposed to its
   harms (S-005). This is a *justice* fact, not only an ecological one. Write it on
   your sheet.
5. **Responses with agency (12 min).** **Mitigation** cuts the cause (less fossil
   fuel); **adaptation** adjusts to the change (sea walls, new crops, early warnings);
   **restoration** repairs the living systems that cushion us (forests, wetlands,
   reefs). Propose one concrete response for the loop you traced — and one reason it is
   fair.
6. **Close (6 min).** In your journal, write one sentence on how the three shape one
   another, and one thing you — with others — could actually do.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Name the three partners and one care action in plain words, leaving the
  full loop to others.
- **Extension:** Weigh a mitigation action against an adaptation action on
  effectiveness, cost, and justice, and argue which a community should prioritize
  first.

## Assessment

- **Formative (peer + self) + summative:** Can the learner explain the strengthened
  greenhouse effect, trace the three-way coupling, name the unequal burden, and
  propose one hopeful, just response?
- **Portfolio artifact (unit):** The completed synthesis sheet, gathered with the
  whole unit's artifacts into the science portfolio.

## Home connection

Choose one small change in your household — one less car trip, one less wasted thing,
one native plant — and write how it touches the climate-ecology-society loop, however
small.
