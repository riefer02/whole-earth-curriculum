# Lesson 11 — Thermodynamics: Energy, Heat, and Order

## Summary

Learners meet the laws of **thermodynamics**. The first law: energy is **conserved** —
it changes form but is never created or destroyed (S-256). The second: in every real
change, some energy always **spreads out as heat** that cannot be fully gathered back
— the growth of **entropy**. Learners apply both to a real system, tracing **energy
in**, **useful work out**, and **heat lost**, and estimate an **efficiency**.

## Objectives

- Apply the laws of thermodynamics to a real system, explaining energy conservation,
  heat, and entropy. _(D06.S3.11.02)_

## Connection

Boil a kettle: electricity goes in, and the water gets hot — that is useful. But the
kettle's outside also gets warm, and heat leaks into the air; you can never gather
all of that leaked heat back to boil the next pot. Energy is never lost, but some of it
always *spreads out* and becomes useless for the job you wanted. That one idea — no
free lunch, some always spills as heat — explains why engines, chargers, and bodies
are never perfectly efficient, and it sits at the heart of every technology's cost.

## Materials

- Thermodynamics application sheet
- Science journal

## Preparation

- Copy or draw the thermodynamics application sheet.
- Retrieval: from Grade 10, energy conservation and transformations (D06.S3.09.01).
  Today we add the *second* law — the direction and cost of real changes.
- Prepare one real system to trace whole-class (a kettle, bulb, or engine) and a
  worked efficiency example.

## Facilitator note

This lesson is written to the learner ("you"). The ideas to land: **the first law of
thermodynamics says energy is conserved — it changes form but is never destroyed
(S-256, S-245); the second law says that in real changes some energy always spreads
out as heat that cannot be fully recovered — entropy increases — so no real process is
perfectly efficient.** Teach both laws explicitly with a worked example, then apply
them by tracing energy in, useful work out, and heat lost in a real device (S-011).
Keep the entropy idea qualitative and honest: it is *not* "things always get messier"
in a shallow sense, but that energy disperses in ways we cannot fully reverse.

The **intellectual** lens: two short laws organize an enormous range of experience —
the deep economy of energy. The **critical-thinking** lens: every "100% efficient"
claim is a red flag — the second law says to ask where the heat goes. The
**technology** lens: the second law is the quiet tax on every engine, charger, and
computer. The **environment** lens: because energy always partly spreads out as heat,
every technology has an irreducible material and energy cost (previewing Lesson 12).
Preview: Lesson 12 turns this into an evaluation of technology's energy and material
costs.

## Procedure

1. **Recall (5 min).** From Grade 10, what does "energy is conserved" mean? Today we
   add a second law about where energy *goes*.
2. **First law: energy is conserved (12 min).** In any change, the total energy stays
   the same — it only changes form: chemical to heat, electrical to light, motion to
   heat (S-256, S-245). Trace it in your system: how much goes in, in what form?
3. **Second law: some always spreads out (12 min).** In every real change, some energy
   spreads out as **heat** that cannot be fully gathered back. That spread is
   **entropy**, and it always grows overall. This is why heat flows from hot to cold
   and never, on its own, the other way. Worked example: a kettle turns almost all its
   electricity into heat, but only part of that heat goes into the water — the rest
   warms the kettle and the air, and is lost to the job.
4. **Efficiency (10 min).** **Efficiency** is the useful output divided by the input:
   useful work out ÷ energy in. Because of the second law, it is always less than 100%.
   Estimate it for your system: what fraction of the energy actually did the job you
   wanted?
5. **Trace your system (12 min).** On your sheet, draw energy in, useful work out, and
   heat lost for one real system, and write a rough efficiency. State what you had to
   assume.
6. **Close (4 min).** In your journal, write one sentence for each law in your own
   words.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Trace only "energy in" and "waste heat out" in one device, and name the
  two laws without computing.
- **Extension:** Quantify an efficiency from a labeled wattage (e.g., a bulb rated in
  watts and lumens), and explain precisely where the remaining energy goes.

## Assessment

- **Formative (peer + self):** Can the learner state both laws, trace energy in/work
  out/heat lost in a real system, and explain why efficiency is always less than 100%?
- **Portfolio artifact (unit):** The completed thermodynamics application sheet.

## Home connection

Choose one device at home (a kettle, a lamp, a fan). Write what energy goes in, what
useful work comes out, and where you can feel the waste heat.
