# Unit 6 — Science: Systems, Evidence & Our Origins

## Overview

This unit is the full pass through Grade 11 science, carrying Grade 10's
evidence-and-source discipline into the discipline of investigation, evolution, and
the deepest scales of matter and cosmos. Learners design and defend an investigation
that controls variables, includes controls and replicates, and reports uncertainty,
and compare how scientific, indigenous, and artistic ways of knowing generate and
test knowledge, and what each offers. They explain the evidence for evolution and
common ancestry, distinguishing this from beliefs about life's meaning, and analyze
the ethics of human relationships with other species and ecosystems, including
conservation and restoration. They explain how matter and energy behave at atomic and
quantum scales and why this challenges everyday intuition, and apply thermodynamics
to real systems, evaluating the energy and material costs of technology. They compare
cosmological models and weigh the evidence for each, holding them as models rather
than certainties, and explain how Earth's systems are coupled and how climate,
ecology, and human society shape one another. Origins are held as evidence-grounded
accounts among many others — never as dogma.

## Essential questions

- How do I design and defend an investigation that controls variables and reports uncertainty, and how do scientific, indigenous, and artistic ways of knowing differ?
- What is the evidence for evolution and common ancestry, and what is our ethical relationship with other species and ecosystems?
- How do matter and energy behave at atomic, quantum, and thermodynamic scales, and how are Earth's systems coupled with climate, ecology, and human society?
