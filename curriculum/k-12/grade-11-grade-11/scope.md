# Grade 11 Scope

## How to read this scope

Each `unit_id` in the frontmatter resolves to a unit file under `units/`.
`start_day`/`end_day` map the unit onto the 180-day school year; the list is a
continuous sequence from day 1 to day 180 with no gaps or overlaps. `domain` and
`pillar` name the primary domain(s) and pillar(s) each unit serves. `domain_weighting`
expresses the intended balance of instructional time across the ten domains, in
percent of the year. `lesson_count` counts the core lessons; the days between lessons
carry flex time, assessment, integration, and celebration. `assessment_plan`
describes how a learner's growth is seen and recorded at this level.

## Year at a glance

The Grade 11 year is a spiral that deepens what Grade 10 began. Where Grade 10 learned
to *examine, defend, and act on* what it synthesized, Grade 11 learns to
*articulate, commit, and stand for* it: a values-and-purpose statement, empathy for
distant and future others, and authorship of a personal project. Learners are co-authors
throughout, never students. The year opens inward, with three units consolidating the
self, the body, and the community.

*Feelings, Identity & Purpose* (U.11.001, D01) asks learners to articulate the emotional
climate of a group or society, to trace their emotional growth, and to carry a
values-and-purpose statement into a self-directed project. *Body, Wellness & Movement*
(U.11.002, D03) makes the body a deliberate practice and a shared public concern:
mind-body regulation explained, movement traditions compared across cultures, inclusive
activity designed, and nutrition read through policy, culture, and budget.
*Relationships, Community & Solidarity* (U.11.003, D02) turns care outward across
difference and distance — deep listening, sustained trust, empathy for people never met,
fairness weighed against empathy, and a mutual-aid effort begun with real
accountability.

The heart of the year is given to the three great tools of school, now wielded at
near-full maturity. *Language, Literature & Argument* (U.11.004, D04) becomes the
multilingual, critical, and persuasive voice: listening across difference, a speech that
answers counterarguments fairly, texts compared across translations, extended original
writing, and real interpreting — home language first, multilingualism as wealth.
*Mathematics & Reasoning* (U.11.005, D05) extends modeling into sequences and series,
vectors and matrices, periodic and exponential change, and the normal distribution,
closing with a survey the learner designs and critiques.
*Science: Systems, Evidence & Our Origins* (U.11.006, D06) carries evidence discipline
into defended investigations that report uncertainty, a comparison of scientific,
indigenous, and artistic ways of knowing, the evidence for evolution, and the quantum
and cosmological scales. *Creative & Aesthetic Expression* (U.11.007, D07) gives the
co-author's deliberate voice to art, music, performance, curation, and design, each
addressed to a social or environmental issue. *Health, Care & Access* (U.11.008, D03)
returns to the body with agency: reaching care, advocating for oneself, and designing
respectful support for chronic conditions and disabilities.

The final third steps into the full adult questions.
*Global History: Transformation, Knowledge & Justice* (U.11.009, D08) weighs one
long-term transformation and who gained and lost, argues for centering marginalized
knowledge traditions, follows peoples who kept their languages under pressure, and reads
climate and social movements — many-voiced, never one nation's triumph.
*Ethics, Justice & Political Economy* (U.11.010, D09) asks how structures shape the
choices people can make, what a good life and a good society mean across traditions, how
justice and power relate, and what solidarity requires of citizens.
*Technology, Media & the Living Earth* (U.11.011, D10) gathers the year into governance
and stewardship, neither worship nor fear: how technologies are governed, how their
harms fall unevenly, propaganda and the free press that resists it, and place-based
stewardship beside industrial use.

The year closes in *All of Me, All of Us* (U.11.012, D01 + D02), gathering the year's
purpose, community, and living Earth, and looking ahead to Grade 12, the capstone year.

Throughout, the four pillars are interwoven. Intellectual awareness (P3) carries the
most weight — the multilingual critical voice, the next rung of modeling, the
systems-and-evidence discipline of science, and the creative arts — while contextual and
ecological awareness (P4) holds a large and steady share, for Grade 11 faces the deepest
questions of power and justice. Emotional and social awareness (P1) remains the ground:
values and purpose are articulated and committed to, and deep listening, empathy for
distant others, and collective responsibility matter more than ever. A known, cared-for,
capable body-and-self (P2) is the foundation. The hardest truths — inequality,
ecological crisis, injustice, moral ambiguity — are met with intellectual tools and
agency, never nihilism.
