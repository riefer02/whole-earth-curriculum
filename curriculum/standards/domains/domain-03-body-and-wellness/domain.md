  with shame, and we honor the wide range of bodies and abilities humans have.

# Body & Wellness

This domain file is part of the standards tree. It declares the strands and
grade-level objectives for **Body & Wellness**. The full vertical ladder for
grades K–12 is declared here, so every lesson can trace to a standard
(see [`docs/roadmap.md`](../../../../docs/roadmap.md)).

## Strands

### D03.S1 — Body Awareness & the Senses

Knowing one's body, how it senses, and the signals it sends.

### D03.S2 — Movement & Motor Skills

Big and small movement, coordination, balance, and the joy of motion.

### D03.S3 — Health & Nutrition

Food, rest, and everyday habits that keep a body well.

### D03.S4 — Safety & Self-Care

Keeping oneself safe, caring for one's body, and knowing when to ask for help.

## Progression (spiral)

Each strand spirals from Kindergarten to Grade 12 with no leaps. **Body Awareness
& the Senses** moves from naming the five senses (K) through noticing body signals,
where feelings live in the body, growth and puberty, and the nervous system (1–8),
to integrating the body–mind connection and media's effect on body image into a
lifelong framework for embodied awareness (9–12). **Movement & Motor Skills** grows
from big and small movements (K) through sequences, coordination, cooperative
games, and skill-building (1–7) to personal fitness, the movement–mind link,
cross-cultural movement traditions, and inclusive, environmentally aware movement
design (8–12). **Health & Nutrition** rises from sorting everyday and sometimes
foods (K) through food groups, nutrients, and culturally varied ways of eating
(1–5) to food systems, marketing, nutrition science, environmental footprint, and
advocacy for food justice (6–12). **Safety & Self-Care** begins with routines and
asking for help (K) and moves through hygiene, first aid, consent, online safety,
substance awareness, and sexual and reproductive health (1–10) to independent
self-management and caring for the health of others across a lifetime (11–12).
Throughout, objectives honor the wide range of bodies and abilities and are
written through the lenses of ethics, egalitarianism, a global and anthropological
perspective, and the effect of technology on humanity and the environment.
