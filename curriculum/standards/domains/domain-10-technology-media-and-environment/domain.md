# Technology, Media & Environment

This domain file is part of the standards tree. It declares the strands and the full
K–12 grade-level objectives for **Technology, Media & Environment**.

## Strands

### D10.S1 — Technology & Society

Tools and technologies and how they change human life.

### D10.S2 — Media & Information Literacy

Reading media critically and creating it responsibly.

### D10.S3 — Ecology & Environmental Systems

Living systems and the interdependence of life and environment.

### D10.S4 — Sustainability & Stewardship

Caring for the Earth so all life can flourish.

## Progression (spiral)

In Kindergarten, children meet technology as helpful tools, media as story to be
questioned, and the Earth as a home to care for. In Grades 1–2, they name tools and
the jobs they do, tell fact from fiction in simple media, distinguish living from
nonliving things and what they need, and practice small acts of care such as saving
water and keeping litter out of the wrong places. In Grades 3–5, they meet simple
machines and the idea of design, learn that messages have purposes and audiences and
that claims should be checked against a second source, trace food chains, ecosystems,
and cycles of energy and matter, and connect human actions to their effects on a
local place with a sense of what they can do. In Grades 6–8, they analyze who designs
technology and who can and cannot use it, separate news from opinion from
advertisement and meet misinformation and disinformation, study populations,
biodiversity, symbiotic relationships, and how ecosystems respond to change, and
weigh everyday choices and footprints, learning the basic science of climate change.
In Grades 9–12, they analyze algorithmic power, surveillance, and the governance and
global distribution of technology, verify claims in a flood of information, trace
global biogeochemical cycles, feedback loops, and tipping points, and evaluate
solutions — renewable energy, circular economies, climate policy, and indigenous
stewardship — through the lens of justice across generations. By Grade 12, learners
analyze technology as a system shaping society, explain Earth as one interconnected
system pressing against planetary boundaries, and practice the habits of stewards
who create media honestly and act — individually and with others — for all life, with
critical judgment and inquiry, not worship or fear, as their guide.
