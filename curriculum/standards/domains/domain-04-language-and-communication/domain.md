# Language & Communication

This domain file is part of the standards tree. It declares the strands and
grade-level objectives for **Language & Communication**. The full vertical ladder
for grades K–12 is declared here, so every lesson can trace to a standard
(see [`docs/roadmap.md`](../../../../docs/roadmap.md)).

## Strands

### D04.S1 — Listening & Speaking

Listening with attention and speaking to be understood.

### D04.S2 — Reading & Literacy

Decoding and making meaning from written language.

### D04.S3 — Writing & Expression

Expressing ideas, stories, and feelings through written marks.

### D04.S4 — Multilingual & Intercultural Communication

Valuing many languages and communicating with respect across cultures.

## Progression (spiral)

Each strand spirals from Kindergarten to Grade 12 with no leaps. **Listening &
Speaking** moves from listening, retelling, and asking questions (K–3) through
summarizing, presenting, and participating in discussion (4–7) to analyzing
rhetoric, debating, and deliberating across many voices (8–12). **Reading &
Literacy** grows from recognizing letters and decoding (K–2) through fluency,
comprehension, text structure, and source credibility (3–8) to critical literacy,
translation, and reasoning across writing systems and languages (9–12). **Writing
& Expression** rises from drawing, scribbling, and simple sentences (K–2) through
paragraphs, the writing process, research, and argument (3–8) to mature,
multimodal, and multilingual composition for real audiences (9–12). **Multilingual
& Intercultural Communication** begins with pride in the home language and simple
greetings (K–2), moves through language families, writing systems, translation,
and language justice (3–9), and ends in bridging and advocating across languages
and cultures (10–12). Throughout, home language is honored first, multilingualism
is treated as a wealth, and objectives are written through the lenses of ethics,
egalitarianism, a global and anthropological perspective, and the effect of
technology on humanity and the environment.
