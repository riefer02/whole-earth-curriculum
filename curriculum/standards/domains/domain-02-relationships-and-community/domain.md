# Relationships & Community

This domain file is part of the standards tree. It declares the strands and
grade-level objectives for **Relationships & Community**. The full vertical ladder
for grades K–12 is declared here, so every lesson can trace to a standard
(see [`docs/roadmap.md`](../../../../docs/roadmap.md)).

## Strands

### D02.S1 — Interpersonal Skills

Communicating, cooperating, resolving small conflicts, and building healthy
relationships.

### D02.S2 — Empathy & Perspective-Taking

Sensing what others feel and think, and seeing a situation from another's point of
view.

### D02.S3 — Community & Belonging

Understanding the groups one belongs to and how communities are built and sustained.

### D02.S4 — Care, Cooperation & Mutual Aid

Helping others, sharing resources, and acting for the common good.

## Progression (spiral)

In Kindergarten, relationship is practiced through sharing, taking turns, and simple
kindness. By Grade 12, learners engage deep questions of belonging, solidarity, and
collective responsibility — from intimate friendship to global community — with the
same posture of care for the whole. Each strand spirals from K to 12 with no leaps.
**Interpersonal Skills** moves from turn-taking and kind words (K–2), through
active listening, I-statements, and fair negotiation (3–5), to boundaries,
repair, digital communication, and de-escalation (6–10), ending in deep listening,
intimate friendship, and deliberate growth as a communicator (11–12). **Empathy &
Perspective-Taking** grows from reading faces and simple feelings (K–2), through
retelling from another's view and cross-cultural perspective (3–5), to
distinguishing empathy from sympathy, analyzing bias, and empathy across distance
and media (6–10), ending in empathy for distant and future others woven into ethical
action (11–12). **Community & Belonging** begins with naming one's own communities
(K–2), deepens into how communities are built, welcomed, and meet shared needs
(3–5), turns to inclusion, exclusion, power, and online belonging (6–10), and
culminates in weighing solidarity and collective responsibility within nested
communities from family to the whole living Earth (11–12). **Care, Cooperation &
Mutual Aid** starts with helping and sharing (K–2), rises through fair division,
community care, and mutual aid across cultures (3–5), through cooperation versus
competition, systems of care, and the difference between charity and solidarity
(6–10), and ends in a personal ethic and lived practice of mutual aid connecting
local action to global solidarity (11–12). Every grade builds on the previous with
no unexplained gaps, growing in depth and maturity rather than jumping.
